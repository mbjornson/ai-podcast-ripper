# Zapier’s CEO shares his personal AI stack | Wade Foster — How I AI

## Summary
The episode features Wade Foster, co-founder and CEO of Zapier, who provides a practical, non-academic guide to integrating AI into a company's DNA. Foster argues that simply issuing AI memos is insufficient; leaders must actively create "play space" for employees to experiment and build comfort with the technology. He emphasizes that fear dissipates when people get their hands dirty, allowing them to move past the "boogeyman" narrative and focus on pragmatic application.

Foster’s core thesis revolves around shifting focus from *what* AI can do to *how* the organization can measure and reward AI fluency. He details a powerful method for diagnosing a company's true culture by using AI to analyze vast amounts of unstructured data—such as meeting notes and internal communications—and stress-testing that data against stated company values. This process provides a tangible, actionable roadmap for both internal optimization and external hiring.

## Key Points
- **Avoid the "Delegation Trap":** Leaders should not simply write an AI memo and expect results. This approach leaves ICs feeling overwhelmed and unsure if the initiative is viable for them or the organization.
- **Create "Play Space":** The most effective way to overcome AI anxiety is through structured, low-stakes experimentation (e.g., hackathons, "show and tells," or designated "messing around" time). This hands-on approach makes the technology feel pragmatic rather than terrifying.
- **AI Fluency is Measurable:** The most effective companies are building rubrics to define and measure AI fluency at different professional levels (e.g., for Product Managers). People naturally invest in skills that are rewarded and measured.
- **The Power of Unstructured Data:** The most valuable use of AI is taking the aggregate of all company communications (meeting notes, Slack threads, etc.) and running it against stated operating principles. This reveals the *actual* culture, highlighting areas where the company's actions contradict its stated values.
- **Hiring for AI Readiness:** When hiring, the focus should shift to anticipating future skill requirements. Candidates should be prepared to discuss how they will adapt to AI-driven processes, making the interview process itself a measure of AI fluency.

## Tools & Resources
- **Zapier:** The company's core product, demonstrating how AI can be integrated into workflows.
- **Granola:** A meeting recording/AI tool used to capture and analyze unstructured company communications (e.g., generating a "company culture handbook" from meeting transcripts).
- **ChatGPT:** Used as a secondary tool to take the output from data analysis (e.g., the "unspoken culture") and generate specific, actionable scoring prompts for interviews.
- **Grok:** Mentioned as an AI tool used in the context of finding and inspiring talent.
- **Brex:** An intelligent finance platform for founders, noted for running autonomous agents for finance management (cards, expenses, fraud).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement "Play Space":** Dedicate specific, non-goal-oriented time (e.g., a Friday afternoon "AI sandbox") for teams to experiment with new AI tools without fear of failure.
- [ ] **Audit Company Values:** Use a meeting recorder/AI tool (like Granola) to collect 30 days of unstructured communication data. Run a prompt to compare this data against your stated company values to identify cultural misalignment.
- [ ] **Develop Fluency Rubrics:** For your role (PM, Engineer, etc.), create a detailed rubric defining what "AI fluent" looks like at different seniority levels. Use this to guide training and performance reviews.
- [ ] **Practice AI Interviewing:** When interviewing candidates, structure questions around how they would adapt their current workflow using AI, rather than just asking about past projects.
- [ ] **Explore Autonomous Finance:** Investigate platforms like Brex to see how autonomous agents can run core business functions (like expense filing or fraud detection) in the background.
