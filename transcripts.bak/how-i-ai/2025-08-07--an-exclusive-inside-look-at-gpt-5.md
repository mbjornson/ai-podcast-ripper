# An exclusive inside look at GPT-5 — How I AI

## Summary
The episode provides an in-depth, comparative analysis of OpenAI's GPT-5 model, positioning it not as a single upgrade but as a specialized tool within a larger "AI team." The speaker, Claire Veau—a Product Leader and AI power user who runs her own product, Chat Purity—demonstrates deep expertise by comparing GPT-5 against established models like GPT-4 across complex workflows, specifically generating Product Requirements Documents (PRDs) and brainstorming features.

The central thesis is that the model's training dictates its functional style. GPT-5 is highly technical, behaving like an "engineer built by engineers for engineers." While exceptional at coding, refactoring, and diving into deep execution details, this focus means it often jumps straight to solutions ("What" and "How"). Conversely, older models (like GPT-4) maintain a more strategic, business-oriented perspective, focusing on the foundational discovery questions—the "Who" and "Why"—which is critical for aligning stakeholders.

## Key Points
- **Model Specialization > Model Upgrade:** Do not view LLMs as single upgrades; instead, treat them as specialized components in a workflow team. The optimal model depends entirely on the specific use case (e.g., GPT-5 for coding execution vs. GPT-4 for business alignment).
- **GPT-5 is Execution-Focused:** This model excels at technical implementation and generating code artifacts, even when prompted to write prose documents like PRDs (it often includes code blocks/technical comments). It operates from an "execution engineering point of view."
- **GPT-4 is Discovery-Oriented:** GPT-4 maintains a more general, business-centric lens. When brainstorming features, it prioritizes discovery questions related to metrics, personas, and core business goals ("Who" and "Why").
- **The Detail Trap (PRD Generation):** While GPT-5 generates significantly more detailed content than its predecessors, this level of technical precision can be a double-edged sword. For aligning non-technical stakeholders or product managers, excessive detail may obscure the primary message; for engineers/coding agents, it is highly beneficial.
- **The "What" vs. "Why" Shift:** The models' responses reflect their training bias: GPT-4 asks *why* a feature is needed (business impact); GPT-5 jumps to *what* needs to be built and *how* it should function (technical solution).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Model Selection Framework:** When tackling a complex problem (e.g., defining a feature), first identify your primary audience: If the audience is C-suite/Stakeholders, use a model biased toward *Why* and *Who*. If the audience is Engineering/DevOps, use a model biased toward *How* and *What*.
- [ ] **Practice Strategic Prompting:** When generating PRDs or feature ideas, explicitly prompt for both high-level business goals (the "Why") AND technical execution steps (the "How"). Do not rely on one model to cover both bases.
- [ ] **Experiment with Model Stacks:** Integrate multiple specialized AI tools/models into your workflow stack rather than relying on a single flagship LLM. Use the best tool for each specific stage of development (e.g., Claude for drafting, GPT-5 for code review).
- [ ] **Review Your Product Documentation Detail Level:** Be mindful of whether your required level of detail is "stakeholder-friendly" or "engineer-ready." Excessive technical detail can derail high-level alignment discussions.
