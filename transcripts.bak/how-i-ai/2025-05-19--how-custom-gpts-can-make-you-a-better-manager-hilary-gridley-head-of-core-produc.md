# How custom GPTs can make you a better manager | Hilary Gridley (Head of Core Product at Whoop) — How I AI

## Summary
The episode focuses on how custom GPTs can fundamentally change the role of a manager by allowing them to scale their personal expertise and systematize their evaluation criteria. Hilary Gridley, Head of Core Product at WHOOP, emphasizes that while AI will not replace good human management in the near term, it can dramatically reduce the time spent on foundational tasks (moving from 0% to 60-70% completion). This leverage allows managers to redirect their energy toward high-value strategic work or hands-on coaching. The core discussion revolves around using AI to solve one of the most difficult managerial challenges: articulating exactly what "good" or "excellent" performance looks like, which is often subjective and hard to define verbally.

Gridley introduces a powerful technique—a form of "reverse engineering"—where the manager trains the GPT by feeding it numerous examples of both poor work ("bad") and improved work ("good"). By doing this pattern matching, the AI can articulate the underlying criteria (e.g., clarity over jargon, one idea per slide) that define excellence, effectively building a digital proxy for the manager's own judgment.

## Key Points
- **The Managerial Bottleneck:** The most valuable skill a manager possesses is articulating clear standards of quality ("what good looks like"). GPTs can automate and codify this subjective knowledge, turning tacit expertise into explicit, repeatable criteria.
- **Reverse Engineering Your Preferences (The "Before/After" Method):** To train an AI on your personal standard of excellence, collect examples of artifacts (slides, emails, documents) in a structured "bad vs. good" format. This allows the AI to implicitly identify patterns and rules that define quality.
- **Prompting Strategy: Start Vague, End Specific:** Do not over-constrain the initial prompt. Begin by asking the AI to interpret your examples without bias (e.g., "What criteria are you using?"). Once the AI articulates general principles, then refine it with hyper-specific constraints ("Be 100 times more specific") to eliminate ambiguity.
- **The Value of Criteria:** The goal is not just getting feedback, but forcing the AI to output a structured set of *criteria*. These criteria are the true asset, as they allow the manager to communicate standards objectively and consistently across the team.
- **Leverage Through Automation:** By building these GPTs, managers create "bots of themselves." This allows them to scale their judgment and expertise without needing constant human oversight, freeing up time for strategic thinking.

## Tools & Resources
- **Custom GPTs:** The primary tool used to build specialized AI assistants that embody the manager's specific style and criteria.
- **PDF Format:** Recommended low-tech method for inputting "before/after" examples (e.g., side-by-side comparison of bad slides vs. edited good slides).
- **Orcus Conductor:** Mentioned as an enterprise platform for complex workflow orchestration, connecting APIs, microservices, human actions, and autonomous agents—relevant for scaling processes beyond simple GPT prompts.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a Personal Criteria Database:** Start collecting 10-20 examples of excellent and poor artifacts (slides, reports, emails) related to your core job function. Structure them in a "Before/After" format for later use.
- [ ] **Test the "Criteria Extraction" Prompt:** Upload your collected examples into an AI chat interface and prompt it: "Based on these 'bad' and 'good' examples, what are the top 5 criteria I am implicitly using to determine quality? Please explain your reasoning."
- [ ] **Practice Vague-to-Specific Prompting:** When refining a GPT, first ask for general observations (vague) before adding hyper-specific constraints (e.g., "The tone must be professional but witty," or "Use metric X and never mention Y").
- [ ] **Identify Time Sinks:** Map out the repetitive evaluation tasks in your current role (e.g., reviewing initial drafts, checking for consistency). These are prime candidates for GPT automation to gain immediate leverage.
