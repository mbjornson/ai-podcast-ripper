# How Webflow’s CPO built an AI chief of staff to manage her calendar, prep for meetings, and drive AI adoption | Rachel Wolan — How I AI

## Summary
The episode centers on the concept of the "AI native executive," moving the focus of AI adoption beyond just product managers and engineers. The guest, Rachel Wollan (CPO at Webflow), demonstrates how she has built a personalized "AI Chief of Staff" (CoS) using AI agents, cloud code, and personal software to manage her professional life. This system handles everything from generating executive summaries for impromptu meetings to performing deep calendar analysis to identify strategic blind spots (e.g., lack of customer contact).

Wollan's background highlights a critical shift in the modern professional landscape: the ability to build sophisticated, hyper-personalized tools (N=1) that were previously reserved for large engineering teams. She emphasizes that the most effective way to learn AI skills is not by starting with a work project, but by finding a personal, engaging problem—a "vibe coding" project—to build in a weekend. The AI CoS serves as a powerful example of how these self-built agents can provide "just-in-time" support, allowing an executive to be prepped for any context in minutes.

## Key Points
- **The AI Chief of Staff (CoS) Workflow:** The CoS acts as a proactive agent that connects to professional APIs (like Google Calendar and Gmail) to analyze patterns and provide strategic insights. For example, it can flag that a CPO is neglecting customer calls, even if the data is imperfect, providing a critical signal for energy reinvestment.
- **The Power of N=1 Software:** The most accessible and powerful software today is the personal, single-user application. This hyper-customization allows executives to build ephemeral widgets and agents that solve unique, immediate problems without needing a full product roadmap or team buy-in.
- **The "Just-in-Time" Executive Skill:** Because modern executives are constantly "back-to-back-to-back," the number one skill to master is improv. The AI CoS directly addresses this by providing structured, executive-level prep (e.g., generating three different ways to describe yourself and Webflow) in minutes.
- **Vibe Coding as a Gateway Skill:** The process of "vibe coding" (building personal, low-stakes apps) is presented as the most effective entry point into AI development. It is addictive and allows users to gain hands-on experience with complex tools without the pressure of corporate deliverables.
- **Technical Implementation:** The CoS is built by utilizing Google Cloud tokens and connecting APIs (Gmail, Calendar) with specific, limited authorizations (e.g., read-only access, draft creation only), demonstrating secure, functional automation.

## Tools & Resources
- **Webflow:** The company where the guest is CPO, demonstrating real-world application of AI tools in a high-profile tech environment.
- **Google Cloud/APIs:** The foundational technology used to build the CoS, specifically leveraging tokens and authorizations for Gmail and Google Calendar.
- **Vibe Coding Platforms:** General category for low-stakes, personal AI development environments (e.g., local LLM setups, agent frameworks).
- **Graphite:** Mentioned as an AI-powered code review platform designed to help engineering teams move through review cycles faster.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a Personal AI Project (N=1):** Identify a repetitive, time-consuming task in your personal or professional life (e.g., summarizing meeting notes, drafting difficult emails, analyzing personal spending) and commit to building a small, automated agent for it this week.
- [ ] **Master API Integration:** Learn how to connect personal accounts (like Google Calendar or Gmail) to a simple scripting environment using API tokens. Start with read-only access to minimize risk and maximize learning.
- [ ] **Practice "Just-in-Time" Prep:** Before any meeting or presentation, use an LLM to generate 3-5 different talking points or angles for the topic, forcing yourself to think on your feet.
- [ ] **Focus on the "Why" of AI:** Instead of asking, "What can AI do for my job?" ask, "What is the most repetitive, low-leverage part of my job that I wish I could automate?" This shifts the focus from capability to pain point.
