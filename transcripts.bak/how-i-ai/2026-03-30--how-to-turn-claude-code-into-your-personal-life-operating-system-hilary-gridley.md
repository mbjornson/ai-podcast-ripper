# How to turn Claude Code into your personal life operating system | Hilary Gridley — How I AI

## Summary
This episode of “How I AI” features a repeat guest, Hillary Gridley, a new mom and entrepreneur, sharing her “anti-system” approach to using Claude Code to manage her increasingly complex life and work. Gridley’s core philosophy centers around minimizing system maintenance and setup, prioritizing immediate problem-solving over elaborate automation. She advocates for a more stream-of-consciousness approach to AI integration, recognizing that not everyone thrives on highly structured, complex systems. The episode highlights the significant shift in Gridley’s life – from a product leadership role to entrepreneurship – and the resulting increase in cognitive load and logistical demands. The conversation touches on the opportunity cost of time, the challenges of fragmented attention, and the need to reclaim time for enjoyable activities. It’s a practical discussion focused on leveraging AI for efficiency, not building a sophisticated, maintainable system.

## Key Points
- **Opportunity Cost & Attention Fragmentation:** Gridley emphasizes the high opportunity cost of her time as a new mom and the significant impact of fragmented attention, leading to a feeling of being overwhelmed by logistics.
- **The “Anti-System” Philosophy:** Her core principle is to avoid complex, maintenance-heavy AI systems, opting instead for simple, immediate solutions. She advocates for a “plain text file” approach to task management, mirroring her personal preference.
- **Problem-Statement Driven Automation:**  She advises starting with a clear problem statement rather than attempting complex Python scripts. Learning by doing and iterative adjustments are key.
- **Spectrum of Productivity:** Gridley identifies a spectrum of productivity styles, ranging from highly structured (Kanban boards, linked assets) to a minimalist approach (a single text file). She positions herself as falling on the minimalist end of the spectrum.
- **Al Gore’s Desktop as a Metaphor:**  A humorous anecdote about Al Gore’s chaotic desktop serves as a relatable illustration of the feeling of being overwhelmed and the desire for a more streamlined approach.
- **Shortcut for Scheduling:** She demonstrates a simple iOS shortcut for quickly adding tasks to her inbox via voice dictation, highlighting the value of low-friction automation.
