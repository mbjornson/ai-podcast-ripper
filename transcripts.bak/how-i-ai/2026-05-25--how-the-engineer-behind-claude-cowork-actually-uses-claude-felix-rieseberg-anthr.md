# How the engineer behind Claude Cowork actually uses Claude | Felix Rieseberg (Anthropic) — How I AI

## Summary
The episode centers on the idea that the true frontier of AI is not in the raw capabilities of the models, but in the user's ability to frame complex, real-world problems for the tools. Felix Rieseberg, an engineering lead at Anthropic, details how his suite of products ("Clawed") is designed to handle "annoying things in the background" to free up human creative energy. He argues that AI should move beyond simple cursor automation and instead manage complex, multi-step workflows.

Rieseberg's expertise is rooted in building and optimizing these AI interfaces (Cowork, Code, etc.). He emphasizes that the most impressive use of AI is not when the model performs a superhuman task, but when the user demonstrates creativity and structure in their prompt engineering and workflow design. The discussion provides a practical framework for thinking about AI integration, moving from conceptual problem identification to iterative, production-ready solutions.

## Key Points
- **The Gap is Conceptual, Not Technical:** The biggest limitation in AI adoption is not the tools' capabilities, but "people being able to understand that almost any problem can go into these tools."
- **The Power of Contextual Input:** The most effective AI workflows involve feeding the model seemingly incomplete or "fake" data (e.g., a demo real estate folder with no units). The AI's value is in synthesizing this ambiguous input to derive missing information (like finding a permit that dictates garage size).
- **The Model Selection Heuristic:** When deciding between powerful models (like Opus) and reliable, efficient models (like Sonnet 4.6), the key metric is your **tolerance for the model not having thought through the exact problem.**
    - **Use Opus/Beefy Model when:** The problem is highly ambiguous, complex, or requires reinterpreting the user's underlying intent (e.g., legal, medical, or highly unstructured tasks).
    - **Use Sonnet 4.6 when:** The task is routine, specific, or involves structured data retrieval/manipulation.
- **The Speed Imperative in Product:** The industry pressure is moving toward a speed where features that once took weeks are now shifted in hours, requiring tools that can bridge the gap between high-fidelity design (prototypes) and engineering execution.

## Tools & Resources
- **Anthropic's Clawed Suite:** A collection of AI tools (Cowork, Code, Chrome, desktop apps) designed to manage complex, multi-step workflows and act as an AI co-pilot across different operating environments.
- **Magic Patterns:** A service that helps product teams move from idea to production using real design systems, allowing prototypes to maintain fidelity and connect directly to engineering AI advantages.
- **Claude/Sonnet/Opus:** Specific model tiers from Anthropic, useful for understanding the trade-off between raw power/ambiguity handling (Opus) and efficiency/reliability (Sonnet).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Ambiguity-Handling Skills:** When facing a complex problem, don't search for the perfect prompt. Instead, feed the AI a collection of related, seemingly disparate, or incomplete data points (like the fake house folder) and ask it to synthesize the missing context.
- [ ] **Master the Model Selection Heuristic:** Before running a prompt, ask: "Is this problem highly structured, or is it ambiguous?" Use the most powerful model only when the problem requires deep, interpretive reasoning.
- [ ] **Focus on Workflow, Not Single Prompts:** Design your AI interactions as multi-step processes (e.g., Step 1: Find data; Step 2: Use data to generate a plan; Step 3: Iterate the plan to a desired format).
- [ ] **Explore Productivity Tools:** Investigate AI prototyping tools like Magic Patterns to accelerate the transition from design concept to functional product.
