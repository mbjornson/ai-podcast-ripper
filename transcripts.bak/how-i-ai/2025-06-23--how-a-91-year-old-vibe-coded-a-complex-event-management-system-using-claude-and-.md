# How a 91-year-old vibe coded a complex event management system using Claude and Replit | John Blackman — How I AI

## Summary
The episode features John Blackman, a 91-year-old former electrical engineer and tech pioneer who demonstrates the power of modern AI agents for non-technical users. His credibility is immense, spanning roles from early AutoCAD adoption in the 1980s to working on major fiber optic projects like Google Fiber. He exemplifies a profound "growth mindset," showing that continuous learning remains possible well into one's senior years.

John’s project was building a complex event management system for his church's community service events ("impact weekends"). Previously, this process relied entirely on manual, handwritten paperwork. Using AI agents (specifically Claude), he transformed these simple paper processes into a sophisticated software blueprint. The resulting application is highly advanced, featuring multi-tenant architecture, detailed roles-based access control, and participant tracking via QR codes—all generated by prompting an LLM with basic requirements.

The discussion highlights that the barrier to building complex enterprise software has dramatically lowered. By using AI for product management (generating user stories, roadmaps, and technical specs) and then leveraging platforms like Replit Agents for coding, non-professional users can now tackle systems previously requiring dedicated development teams.

## Key Points
- **AI as a Product Manager:** The most powerful use case demonstrated is using Claude to take a manual process outline (a "paper workflow") and generate professional product artifacts: an MVP roadmap, detailed user stories, functional requirements, and even non-functional requirements. This bypasses the need for traditional business analysts.
- **Multi-Tenant Architecture Achievable by Non-Experts:** John designed a complex system with multiple layers of administration: System Admins (overall impact organizers) > Local Church Admins (individual churches) > Individual Users/Participants. Building this roles-based access control and data segmentation from scratch is typically an enterprise-level lift, but he achieved the blueprint using AI prompts.
- **The Power of User Stories:** The process emphasized structuring needs around user experiences ("user stories"). This product management technique ensures that development focuses on *how* a specific type of user (e.g., "a participant arriving from a Facebook ad") will interact with the system, making the software highly intuitive and functional.
- **Continuous Learning is Non-Negotiable:** John’s career arc—from electrical engineering to airplane mechanics to early AutoCAD adoption, and finally to AI coding at 91—serves as a powerful argument for adopting new technologies regardless of age or previous field expertise.

## Tools & Resources
- **Claude (Anthropic):** Used as the primary AI agent for requirements gathering, roadmap generation, user story creation, and structuring complex workflows from simple outlines.
- **Replit Agents:** The platform used to take the structured output from Claude and begin the actual development/coding of the application.
- **Impact Passport System:** A physical concept (the paper passport) that was successfully digitized into a functional, QR-code enabled digital system for event tracking and registration.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a 'Paper Workflow':** Identify a complex process in your current job or life (e.g., client onboarding, expense reporting) and write it out step-by-step on paper. This is your raw input for AI.
- [ ] **Prompt Engineering Practice:** Use Claude or ChatGPT to take that written workflow and prompt the AI to generate: 1) A Minimum Viable Product (MVP) roadmap; 2) At least five detailed user stories; and 3) A list of non-functional requirements (e.g., security, speed).
- [ ] **Explore Low-Code/No-Code Platforms:** Experiment with Replit or similar platforms to see how the structured output from an LLM can be translated into a functional prototype without needing deep coding knowledge.
- [ ] **Adopt the Growth Mindset:** Dedicate time each week to learning one new, non-core skill (e.g., basic Python, advanced Excel functions) to maintain professional agility and combat technological obsolescence.
