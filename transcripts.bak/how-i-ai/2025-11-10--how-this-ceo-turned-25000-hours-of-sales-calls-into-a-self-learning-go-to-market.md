# How this CEO turned 25,000 hours of sales calls into a self-learning go-to-market engine | Matt Britton (Suzy) — How I AI

## Summary
Matt Britton, CEO of Suzy, details how he built an automated "operating system" to transform raw, unstructured customer interaction data into a powerful go-to-market (GTM) engine. The core problem he addressed was that his sales team lacked deep, actionable insights into what customers truly cared about, despite having massive amounts of recorded call data. His solution leveraged 25,000 hours of historical customer calls, which he identified as the ultimate "source of truth" for understanding customer needs and pain points.

The process involves creating a sophisticated automation workflow that doesn't just analyze data, but actively uses it to generate multiple assets—from enriched data sources and tailored emails to new marketing content. Britton emphasizes that the key to successful AI implementation is not simply adopting the latest tools, but first taking a step back to define the single, core business problem that is actively holding the company back from growth.

Furthermore, he offers critical advice for leaders: it is insufficient to simply instruct engineers to build AI solutions. True leadership relevance in the AI era requires leaders to build accessible skills themselves, using no-code tools to understand the underlying mechanics of AI workflows and solve problems personally.

## Key Points
- **The Source of Truth:** The most valuable, untapped data asset is often recorded customer interactions (e.g., 25,000 hours of Gong transcripts). This raw data provides the perfect context for understanding customer needs, far surpassing survey data or gut feeling.
- **Problem-First Approach:** Before touching any tool, the focus must be on identifying the single, core business problem (e.g., "Our sales team doesn't know how to find what customers are interested in"). This prevents "tool-hopping" and ensures the automation drives measurable business value.
- **The Automation Stack:** The workflow is built on a trigger-based system: A new call occurs (the trigger) $\rightarrow$ Zapier/Browse AI scrapes the transcript using a unique Call ID $\rightarrow$ AI processes the raw text into actionable, structured data (GTM assets, enriched data, etc.).
- **Leadership Skill Shift:** Leaders must become proficient in building AI workflows themselves. This is a hard skill that increases relevance and allows executives to challenge expensive, over-engineered solutions (e.g., questioning a $4,000 fix when a $2,000 solution is possible).

## Tools & Resources
- **Gong:** Used for recording and transcribing every customer call, providing the raw, structured data source.
- **Zapier:** The central automation platform used to stitch together disparate tools and create the workflow backbone.
- **Browse AI:** Used as the scraping mechanism, allowing the system to pull the transcript content from a specific URL based on the unique Call ID.
- **Brex:** Mentioned as an example of an intelligent finance platform for founders, automating tasks like issuing cards, filing expenses, and stopping fraud.

## Quotable
> "It is not sufficient to instruct your engineers to build AI. You'll go nowhere."
>
> "The first step is figuring out what problem that you want to solve... what's holding me back from growing faster than I want to?"

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Data:** Identify the largest, most underutilized repository of unstructured data in your business (e.g., support tickets, meeting transcripts, Slack archives). Treat this as your "source of truth."
- [ ] **Define the Core Constraint:** Before implementing any new technology, write down the single, most critical bottleneck preventing your business from scaling. Focus all automation efforts on solving *that* problem first.
- [ ] **Build a No-Code Workflow:** Dedicate time to learning a no-code automation tool (like Zapier or Make). Build a simple, end-to-end workflow that takes one piece of raw data and automatically generates two distinct, valuable outputs (e.g., raw customer feedback $\rightarrow$ draft email sequence).
- [ ] **Master the "Why":** When evaluating expensive tech solutions, practice questioning the underlying necessity. Understand the mechanics of the problem and the solution to prevent over-engineering.
