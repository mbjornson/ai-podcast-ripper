# How Zapier’s EA built an army of AI interns to automate meeting prep, strengthen team culture, and scale internal alignment | Cortney Hickey — How I AI

## Summary
The episode features Courtney Hickey, EA to the CEO at Zapier, who details how she has leveraged AI Agents to fundamentally restructure and automate the high-context, repetitive tasks inherent in an executive assistant role. Her discussion moves beyond simple scheduling automation, demonstrating how AI can function as a "second brain" by consolidating fragmented professional context—data scattered across CRMs, email, Slack, and calendars—into actionable, digestible intelligence.

Hickey’s primary focus is on optimizing the meeting process, arguing that meetings themselves are expensive, but the preparation and follow-up are where the most value (and inefficiency) lies. She walks through a detailed workflow using Zapier Agents that automatically researches external participants, analyzes relationship history, and generates strategic prep recommendations, allowing her to shift from manual data aggregation to high-level strategic thinking.

The core message is that adopting AI is not a future possibility, but an immediate operational necessity ("a when, not an if"). She emphasizes that the power lies not just in the tools, but in the ability to build these systems iteratively, starting with basic automations and continually increasing their intelligence and scope.

## Key Points
- **The Value of Context Consolidation:** The biggest operational bottleneck is not the lack of data, but the time spent manually retrieving and synthesizing context from disparate systems (CRM, Gmail, Slack). AI Agents solve this by acting as a unified memory layer.
- **The "Second Brain" Function:** The agent doesn't just list facts; it synthesizes them. It pulls together relationship history, recent sales notes, and prior communication to provide a holistic view of the participant, allowing the user to prepare for strategic conversations rather than just logistical ones.
- **Structured Automation Output:** The system delivers two critical outputs: 1) **Actionable Tasks** (e.g., creating a task in Todoist with all necessary prep materials and scheduling dedicated prep time); and 2) **Strategic Digest** (a weekly Slack summary that includes AI-generated insights and key focus areas for the upcoming week).
- **Progress Over Perfection (Iterative Scaling):** The most crucial operational advice is to start small. Do not attempt to build a perfect, all-encompassing system immediately. Build a basic automation (e.g., just a quick digest) and then, over time, connect more systems (e.g., adding LinkedIn hyperlinks, integrating the CRM).
- **Enterprise AI Requirement:** For AI tools to be truly useful in a corporate setting, they require deep, secure access to all company systems. This necessity drives the need for specialized platforms (like WorkOS) that manage enterprise-grade security, access controls, and audit logs.

## Tools & Resources

- **HubSpot:** Used as the example CRM for checking deal status, sales notes, and relationship history.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Context Switching:** Identify the three most repetitive, context-heavy tasks in your current week (e.g., meeting prep, report compiling, follow-up emails). These are your prime automation targets.
- [ ] **Build a Minimum Viable Automation (MVA):** Do not aim for perfection. Select one repetitive task and build the simplest possible automation (e.g., a simple weekly digest or a data pull) using a no-code tool like Zapier.
- [ ] **Map Your Data Sources:** For the task you selected, list every system the information comes from (e.g., Notion, Slack, Google Drive, CRM). This map dictates the complexity and potential value of your AI agent.
- [ ] **Schedule Prep Time:** Treat the time saved by automation as a resource. Schedule dedicated, protected time (e.g., 2 hours every Friday) to review the automated outputs and elevate the insights, rather than just completing the tasks.
- [ ] **Explore AI Agent Capabilities:** Experiment with existing AI agent platforms to see if they can connect your most disparate data sources and generate a single, synthesized output (e.g., a summary of a project's status pulled from Slack, Jira, and Google Docs).
