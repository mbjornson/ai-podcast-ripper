# How Amplitude built an internal AI tool that the whole company’s obsessed with (and how you can too) | Wade Chambers — How I AI

## Summary
The episode centers on Wade Chambers, Chief Engineering Officer at Amplitude, discussing how his team built an internal AI tool called "Moda." Moda acts as a centralized enterprise search agent, unlocking and synthesizing data from nearly every corner of the company—including Confluence, Jira, Salesforce, Google Drive, and Slack. The core discussion revolves around the strategic decision to *build* this tool internally rather than relying solely on off-the-shelf AI solutions. Chambers emphasizes that the goal was not perfection, but maximizing internal leverage by creating a common, easy-to-use interface for complex data sets.

Chambers's background highlights a highly efficient approach: the tool was developed in just 3–4 weeks of engineers' spare time. The discussion provides valuable insights into modern product development, particularly how to achieve rapid AI adoption within an organization. A key takeaway is that successful internal tooling relies heavily on "social engineering"—making the product visible and letting employees see their peers using it successfully—to drive organic, viral adoption across departments like Sales, Product Management, and Engineering.

## Key Points
- **Build vs. Buy Strategy:** When implementing AI internally, prioritize building tools for maximum leverage over buying complex solutions. The key is to keep the initial investment low enough that if the tool fails or needs a major overhaul, it can be discarded and rebuilt quickly ("I will throw everything away, do it again").
- **The Power of Internal Data Unification:** Moda's primary function is unifying disparate data sources (Jira, Salesforce, Confluence, etc.) into one accessible agent. This solves the common enterprise problem where information is siloed across multiple platforms and departments.
- **Adoption via Social Engineering:** The most effective way to launch an internal tool is not through mandatory training, but by making it highly visible and letting employees see credible peers using it successfully (e.g., a sales executive seeing a colleague use the tool in Slack). This creates organic demand.
- **Speed of Development:** A sophisticated enterprise AI agent like Moda was built and pushed live within 3–4 weeks of spare engineering time, demonstrating that high-impact internal tools do not require massive, multi-year resource allocations.
- **Productizing AI:** The team is effectively "product managing" their own AI product by monitoring where employees struggle or ask questions (the friction points). This feedback loop dictates necessary improvements in data access, prompting, and output grooming.
