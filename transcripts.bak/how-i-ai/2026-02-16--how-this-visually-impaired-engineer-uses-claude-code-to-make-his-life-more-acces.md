# How this visually impaired engineer uses Claude Code to make his life more accessible | Joe McCormick — How I AI

## Summary
Joe McCormick, a principal software engineer at Babylist, shares his journey of adapting to life and work after losing most of his central vision due to a rare genetic disorder. His experience highlights how AI is not just a technological advancement, but a critical equalizer that is rapidly closing the gap between sighted and visually impaired professionals. He argues that the most powerful and underappreciated benefit of modern AI is the ability for individuals to build highly customized "personal software" solutions tailored precisely to their unique needs and workflows.

The episode details how Joe leverages multimodal AI and low-code tools (specifically Claude Code) to build micro Chrome extensions. These tools are designed to inject hyper-specific functionality into common platforms like Slack and Figma, solving real-world inefficiencies that large, generalized commercial tools often miss. His work demonstrates a powerful synthesis of accessibility technology and modern software engineering, proving that deeply personal needs can drive robust, commercially viable technical solutions.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Personal Software:** Joe emphasizes that many essential accessibility and custom software solutions are not economically viable for large companies to build, leaving a gap that individuals can fill using AI tools. This makes "personal software" a massive, untapped market and a key benefit of the current AI wave.
- **Efficiency over Feature Bloat:** He critiques the "Swiss Army knife" approach of some AI browsers, noting that while they do everything, they are often slower. He advocates for building specialized "drill" tools that execute one function with maximum speed and minimal latency.
- **Advanced Image-to-Text Workflow:** His image description tool in Slack goes far beyond simple metadata generation. It provides a rich, contextual description that allows for immediate, follow-up queries (e.g., "What are the CTAs?"), making it a highly efficient information transfer method for complex visual data.
- **Prompt Engineering for Content Integrity:** The custom spell checker is a prime example of workflow optimization. By using a specific prompt ("do not change any of the words, just fix typos"), he ensures the AI corrects only mechanical errors, preserving the original content and tone—a critical feature for professional communication.
- **The Chrome Hack:** The ability to run platforms like Slack within Chrome allows all these custom extensions to interact with the content, turning the browser into a customizable, powerful development environment for micro-apps.

## Tools & Resources
- **Claude Code:** The primary tool used for building the custom micro-apps and extensions.
- **Chrome Extensions:** The delivery mechanism for the custom tools, allowing deep integration into web platforms (e.g., Slack, Figma).
- **OpenAI API:** Used for the spell-checking functionality, demonstrating how to integrate powerful LLM capabilities into a simple, targeted workflow.
- **Figma:** Mentioned as a target platform for a future extension, demonstrating the potential for AI to extract design-specific information (e.g., CTA colors, design intent) from design files.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a "Personal Software" Thesis:** Identify a specific, repetitive, and frustrating workflow in your professional life that is currently underserved by commercial tools. This is your first project.
- [ ] **Master Micro-App Development:** Focus on building small, hyper-efficient, single-purpose tools (micro-apps) rather than attempting large, generalized solutions.
- [ ] **Experiment with API Integration:** Use low-code/no-code tools (like Zapier or direct API calls) to connect an LLM (like OpenAI or Claude) to a platform you use daily (Slack, Notion, etc.) to automate a specific, niche task.
- [ ] **Optimize for Latency:** When building or adopting new tools, prioritize speed and immediate feedback over feature breadth.
