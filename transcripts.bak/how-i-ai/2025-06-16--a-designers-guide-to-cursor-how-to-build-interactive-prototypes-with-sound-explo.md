# A designer's guide to Cursor: How to build interactive prototypes with sound, explore visual styles, and transform data visualizations | Elizabeth Lin — How I AI

## Summary
Elizabeth Lin, a designer and "Cursor Power user," demonstrates how advanced AI tools like Cursor are fundamentally changing the design workflow by empowering non-coders to create complex, interactive prototypes. The episode focuses less on coding and more on mastering prompt engineering—teaching designers to use LLMs not as calculators, but as highly creative brainstorming partners that can explore diverse visual aesthetics (e.g., Brutalist, Y2K, Cyberpunk). Her background is in design, giving her a unique perspective: she shows how AI can accelerate the conceptualization and styling phases of product development, allowing designers to rapidly test ideas they might otherwise spend weeks or months building manually.

The core message is that true mastery comes from iteration and conversation with the AI. Lin emphasizes that instead of starting with a fixed idea, the most powerful technique is prompting the AI to generate *options* for aesthetics first. This approach not only provides novel design language (e.g., "neon color schemes," "glitch effects") but also helps the user learn to speak the LLM's specific vocabulary, leading to more precise and unexpected creative outcomes.

## Key Points
- **Prompting for Concepts, Not Solutions:** Instead of prompting with a finished idea ("Make me an e-commerce site"), prompt the AI to generate possibilities: "What design aesthetics and movements are you comfortable implementing? List the styles and describe them." This shifts the process from execution to ideation.
- **The Power of Random Input:** Feeding the LLM random, unrelated sources of inspiration (like a K-pop music video or specific art pieces) forces it out of its comfort zone, generating unique visual combinations that wouldn't be obvious otherwise.
- **Iterative Prompting is Key:** Never assume the first output is final. The most valuable technique is running the *exact same prompt* multiple times to observe variable outcomes. This randomness allows for unexpected breakthroughs and prevents creative stagnation.
- **Workflow Efficiency (The Hybrid Approach):** For maximum speed, use specialized no-code/low-fidelity tools (like V0 or B0) to build the basic structural outline of a page first. Then, import that foundation into Cursor specifically for advanced styling, polish, and aesthetic application.
- **Mastering the AI's Language:** Pay attention to the descriptive language the LLM provides when defining an aesthetic (e.g., Cyberpunk = "dystopian elements," "glitch effects"). Learning these specific keywords allows you to refine your prompts with greater precision.

## Tools & Resources
- **Cursor:** The primary tool discussed; an AI-powered coding/design assistant that helps designers prototype and apply complex styling.
- **Lovable:** Mentioned as a platform for building working apps and websites via chat, ideal for marketers or product managers who need functional prototypes quickly.
- **V0 / B0 (Implied):** Tools suggested for creating the initial, foundational black and white structural outline of a webpage before applying AI styling in Cursor.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Practice Aesthetic Prompting:** When starting a new design project, resist the urge to define the outcome. Instead, prompt an LLM with: "List 5 contrasting visual design aesthetics and provide 3 descriptive keywords for each."
- [ ] **Implement the Hybrid Workflow:** For your next prototype, use a low-fidelity tool (like V0) to build the basic HTML/CSS structure first. Then, feed that structured foundation into Cursor or a similar AI tool solely for advanced styling and polish.
- [ ] **Experiment with Random Inputs:** Take an unrelated piece of media—a song lyric, a random K-pop video screenshot, or a historical painting—and use it as the sole visual inspiration prompt for your next design project to force novel results.
- [ ] **Adopt "Checkpoint Restoration":** When using any generative AI tool (Midjourney, Cursor, etc.), make it a habit to take screenshots of successful intermediate steps and remember that you can always revert or restore previous versions rather than continuing down the rabbit hole.
