# How this PM uses MCPs to automate his meeting prep, CRM updates, and customer feedback synthesis | Reid Robinson (Zapier) — How I AI

## Summary
The episode details how advanced AI automation is moving beyond simple chat interfaces into complex, integrated workflows using a concept called Model Connector Platforms (MCPs). Reid Robinson, a Product Manager at Zapier, explains that MCPs are essentially sophisticated app integrations that allow AI tools (like Claude, ChatGPT, and Cursor) to not only access knowledge stored in your company's apps but also to actively *perform actions* within them. The core discussion centers on how this capability transforms AI from a research assistant into a true, always-on operational team member.

Reid highlights that the most valuable use cases involve automating the tedious, high-friction tasks that plague customer-facing teams, such as updating CRMs or synthesizing daily research. The discussion emphasizes that for AI to be truly enterprise-ready, it must have deep, secure access to internal systems, a challenge that platforms like Zapier are solving by providing customizable, centralized tool collections.

## Key Points
- **The Two Pillars of AI Automation:** The most critical use cases for AI integration are: 1) Giving the AI access to knowledge residing in your apps (read-only access). 2) Giving the AI the ability to *execute actions* within those apps (write/update access).
- **MCPs are Custom Tool Collections:** Don't think of MCPs as a complex concept; think of them as a customizable "server" or collection of tools you build specifically for a use case. Zapier's platform allows users to create multiple, distinct tool sets for different AI clients (e.g., one set for Claude, another for ChatGPT).
- **Advanced Tool Calling via Projects:** For advanced users, the signal is not just connecting tools, but providing *detailed instructions* within the AI client (e.g., using Claude Projects). You can program the AI on the specific order and logic of tool usage (e.g., "When doing a CRM update, first run Tool A, then use Tool B to validate, then write to Tool C").
- **The CRM Pain Point:** Customer-facing teams are prime candidates for automation because updating CRMs (like Salesforce) is universally tedious and time-consuming. AI can automate the entire research and data entry process, significantly improving efficiency.
- **Enterprise Readiness:** For AI tools to pass IT scrutiny, they must offer secure authentication, access controls, and audit logs. This necessity is driving the need for platforms that provide "drop-in APIs for enterprise features."
