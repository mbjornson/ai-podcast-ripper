# How Block’s custom AI agent supercharges every team, from sales to data to engineering | Jackie Brosamer & Brad Axen — How I AI

## Summary
The episode details how Block has strategically adopted AI across its entire organization, moving beyond initial "tech pockets" to embed AI into core business functions like sales, data analysis, and engineering. The discussion centers on "Goose," an open-source AI agent designed to be highly agnostic—meaning it can solve complex problems by connecting to various specialized tools (like Python libraries or databases) rather than relying on a single model.

Jackie and Brad emphasize that the most significant hurdle in the AI future is not technological, but **organizational transformation**. They argue that successful adoption requires shifting culture and processes, noting that initial value often comes from non-technical roles—specifically sales teams—who are "begging" for tools to improve customer interaction and revenue generation.

The segment provides a deep dive into using Goose to perform sophisticated data analysis on messy, raw CSV files (using Python/Pandas). This demonstrates the agent's ability to not only process data but also identify key business insights (e.g., top-revenue items or peak sales days) and provide actionable recommendations without being explicitly prompted for them.

## Key Points
- **Bottom-Up Adoption is King:** AI adoption is most effective when it originates from operational pain points in non-technical departments (Sales, Finance). These groups are the first to identify immediate value and drive change, making technology a reactive solution to existing business needs.
- **The Core Transformation is Operational/Cultural:** The biggest challenge for companies adopting AI is not acquiring the technology; it is executing the organizational transformation required to integrate that technology into daily workflows.
- **AI Agents are Tool Orchestrators (Not Just LLMs):** A true AI agent, like Goose, is defined by its ability to use a collection of external tools and protocols (MCPs) to solve problems. Its power lies in connecting diverse capabilities rather than just generating text.
- **Open Source for Ecosystem Power:** Open-sourcing the agent framework is a strategic move that allows Block to leverage an open ecosystem, ensuring model agnosticism. This means they can integrate new models (better at coding, better at writing) as they emerge without rebuilding their core system.

## Tools & Resources
- **Goose:** The open-source AI agent developed by Block; designed to be agnostic and solve problems by connecting various tools/capabilities.
- **MCPs (Model Capabilities Protocols):** The underlying protocol that allows Goose to connect to and utilize diverse external services, enabling the system's extensibility.
- **Pandas:** A powerful Python library used in the demonstration for complex data manipulation and analysis on raw datasets.
- **ripgrep:** Mentioned as an effective tool for finding specific files (like CSVs) within a large codebase or directory structure, demonstrating simple tools can be highly efficient.
- **CodeRabbit:** An AI code review platform mentioned by the sponsor; provides instant PR comments, one-click fixes, and custom rule definitions to improve engineering velocity and quality.

## Quotable

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice treating raw, messy data (like CSVs) as an input for automated analysis. Learn to structure prompts that ask an agent/script not just for answers, but for *recommendations* and *trends*.
- [ ] **Career Move:** Identify the most inefficient or painful process in a non-tech department (Sales, Customer Success, Operations) within your current company. Frame this pain point as a proposal for an AI agent solution to demonstrate immediate ROI.
- [ ] **Tool Exploration:** Experiment with open-source agent frameworks (or similar workflow automation tools) to connect two disparate services you currently use manually (e.g., connecting a CRM data export directly into a reporting dashboard).
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** When evaluating new technology, prioritize its potential for *operational* change over its technical novelty. Ask: "What process does this make obsolete or dramatically easier?"
