# How this Yelp AI PM works backward from “golden conversations” to create high-quality prototypes using Claude Artifacts and Magic Patterns | Priya Badger — How I AI

## Summary
The episode details a novel approach to AI Product Management (AI PM), focusing on how to design and prototype conversational AI products that are non-deterministic and complex. The guest, Priya Badger, a PM at Yelp, introduces the concept of "Golden Conversations" as the starting point for any AI product design. Instead of writing traditional Product Requirement Documents (PRDs), the methodology involves defining a complete, ideal user interaction flow—the "golden conversation"—and then working backward from that example conversation to build the product requirements and features.

Badger emphasizes that managing AI products requires addressing two distinct layers: the visible user interface (the conversation flow) and the complex, non-obvious logic behind the scenes (system prompts and conversational guidance). The core challenge is ensuring the product delivers consistent, high-quality results despite the inherent variability of generative AI models.

## Key Points
- **The Golden Conversation Framework:** This is the foundational starting point for AI product design. Instead of asking "What features should we build?" the PM asks, "What is the ideal, end-to-end experience we are trying to drive?" This role-playing exercise defines the desired user journey.
- **Working Backwards from the Artifact:** The most unique and actionable take is to treat the sample conversation (the "Golden Conversation") as the first pass wireframe. You write the conversation flow first, and then use that artifact to derive all the necessary technical requirements, constraints, and capabilities.
- **Prototype the Experience, Not the Document:** Product Managers should prototype their product as close to the end-user experience as possible. This means using LLMs (like Claude) to simulate the product's interaction rather than spending time writing lengthy, abstract PRDs.
- **Leverage the AI's Thought Process:** When testing or developing with LLMs, always read the model's "thought process" or reasoning. This is invaluable for troubleshooting, understanding how the AI interprets ambiguous prompts, and identifying where your initial instructions might be misleading the model.
- **AI PM Scope:** AI PM involves managing both the user-facing interface (the chat/interaction) and the complex backend logic (the system prompts that guide the conversation flow).

## Tools & Resources
- **Claude (Anthropic):** Used in the demonstration to generate the structured, continuous sample conversation based on a prompt and an image upload.
- **ChatGPT/Foundational Models:** Any major LLM can be used for this process, but the principle remains the same: use the model to simulate the conversation flow.
- **Yelp's AI Assistant:** Used as the real-world example product (matching users with service pros based on photos/descriptions).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement the Golden Conversation:** For your next product idea, skip the initial brainstorming meeting and instead, dedicate time to role-playing the ideal user journey. Write out the full, perfect conversation flow (the "golden conversation") before writing a single requirement.
- [ ] **Adopt the "Work Backwards" PM Method:** Use the completed Golden Conversation as your primary artifact. From this single document, derive all technical requirements, edge cases, and necessary system prompts.
- [ ] **Test with Artifacts, Not Documents:** When prototyping, use an LLM (like Claude) to simulate the product's interaction. Treat the LLM output as your functional prototype, not the prompt itself.
- [ ] **Mandatory Thought Process Review:** When testing any AI feature, make it a habit to read the model's reasoning or thought process. Use this to refine your prompts and understand the model's current limitations and biases.
