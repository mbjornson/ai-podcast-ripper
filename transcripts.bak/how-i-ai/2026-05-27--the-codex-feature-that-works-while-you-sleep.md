# The Codex feature that works while you sleep — How I AI

## Summary
Clara Vo, a product leader and AI specialist, details a critical advancement in AI workflow management: the use of the `/goal` feature within Codex. She argues that traditional prompt engineering is fundamentally flawed for complex, long-running tasks because it is turn-based and requires constant human micromanagement. The `/goal` feature changes this paradigm by allowing the AI to operate autonomously, defining an overarching desired *outcome* rather than just receiving a sequence of instructions.

The core discussion centers on how defining a goal—a measurable end state—enables the AI to self-manage, verify its own work, check its progress, and iterate until the goal is met. This capability allows for multi-hour, complex, autonomous tasks, moving AI from a sophisticated autocomplete tool to a genuine, self-correcting agent.

Vo emphasizes that writing a strong goal is a specialized skill set, particularly valuable for product managers and engineers. The framework for defining these goals is highly structured, requiring the definition of not just the task, but the measurable outcome, the constraints, and the precise verification steps.

## Key Points
- **Goal vs. Prompt:** A **Prompt** is a turn-based instruction ("Do this, then wait for me to say what's next"). A **Goal** is a description of the desired *outcome* that the AI must work towards, looping and verifying its own steps until evidence of success is gathered.
- **Autonomous Task Length:** Before `/goal`, the speaker struggled with complex tasks; with it, she successfully ran a coding task for over five hours, demonstrating a massive leap in AI self-management capability.
- **The Six Components of a Strong Goal:** To design an effective goal, one must define:
    1.  **Outcome:** What must be true when the work is done?
    2.  **Verification:** How can the success be tested (e.g., a test suite, a measurable number)?
    3.  **Constraints:** What must *not* regress (e.g., "The features must stay the same")?
    4.  **Boundaries:** What tools and files are allowed?
    5.  **Iteration Policy:** How should the AI decide what to try next?
    6.  **Stopping Condition:** When should the AI stop and report failure or completion?
- **Systematic Problem Solving:** The most powerful use case demonstrated was not merely fixing a bug, but using the goal framework to systematically replay *all* historical failure cases (invalid operation traces) until the root cause was fixed across the entire system, resulting in zero remaining errors.
- **Business Application:** The concept can be applied to non-technical projects. Instead of "Rewrite this PRD," the goal becomes: "The PRD must achieve X level of clarity, verified by a successful review from three domain experts, while maintaining compliance with Y style guide."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Adopt the Goal Framework:** When tackling any complex project (coding, writing, business process), do not start with an instruction (prompt). Instead, define the ultimate, measurable *outcome* and structure your plan using the 6-point goal framework (Outcome, Verification, Constraints, Boundaries, Iteration Policy, Stopping Condition).
- [ ] **Systematic Cleanup:** When dealing with legacy systems or accumulated errors, do not apply band-aid fixes. Instead, define a goal to systematically re-run and resolve *all* historical failure cases until the error count reaches zero.
- [ ] **Optimize Financial Stack:** Review your current business financial tools. Consider modern fintech solutions like Mercury to centralize and simplify vendor payments, client receivables, and wire transfers, reducing manual oversight.
- [ ] **Practice Goal Writing:** Practice writing goals for non-technical tasks (e.g., "The quarterly report must be approved by the VP, verified by a sign-off sheet, while maintaining the brand guidelines, using only the CRM data, and if blocked, report the specific data gap").
