# 104 - Heroic Struggles in Vietnam, w/ Doyle Glass — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode uses historical accounts of military service—specifically the Vietnam War and earlier battles—to explore the enduring nature of heroism, sacrifice, and the psychological cost of glory. The discussion centers on the idea that true character is revealed not in times of peace, but during extreme struggle. Doyle Glass, the author and guest, draws heavily on these stories to argue for the urgent need to preserve the narratives of veterans, particularly those from the Vietnam generation, whose experiences are often misunderstood or forgotten by the public.

Glass's background as a former state prosecutor and accomplished sculptor informs his approach: he is interested in capturing not just the physical monument of a battle, but the intangible "image of the soul" through biography. The conversation shifts from the physical acts of bravery (like Sergeant Sullivan confronting anti-war protesters) to the internal mental fortitude required to survive trauma, exemplified by the advice given to a wounded soldier on the battlefield.

## Key Points
- **Mastering Fear is a Skill:** The most actionable insight comes from the quote regarding fear: "You can work that fear if you can get a handle on it... I knew where I was at. I knew I couldn't move. I knew they would get to me, and I knew it was just when." The lesson is that facing overwhelming fear requires accepting the current reality ("I knew where I was at") rather than dwelling on the emotional fallout.
- **The Importance of Legacy Preservation:** There is an urgent cultural mandate to record these stories. Glass notes that the veterans who hold these memories are aging, and their stories are passing away with them. This highlights a critical failure in cultural memory and historical documentation.
- **Courage as the Foundation:** Drawing on classical philosophy (like C.S. Lewis's implied point), the discussion emphasizes that courage is not merely a virtue but the foundational prerequisite that makes other virtues (justice, temperance, wisdom) possible.
- **The Disconnect Between Service and Recognition:** The anecdote of Sullivan's return home illustrates the profound struggle of reintegration. The physical danger of war is contrasted with the emotional and social danger of being reviled by one's own countrymen, suggesting that the greatest struggle is often the return to civilian life.
