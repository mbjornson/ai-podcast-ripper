# 89 - A Manual for Greatness — with Johnny Burtka — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode centers on the concept of "statesmanship"—a comprehensive skill set required for anyone pursuing large-scale ambition, particularly in business, rather than merely political maneuvering. The guest, Johnny Burtka, is the president and CEO of the Intercollegiate Studies Institute (ISI) and author of *Gateway to Statesmanship*. Burtka’s work revives the ancient tradition of the "Mirrors for Princes"—conduct manuals that provided ideal behavioral models for leaders. He argues that modern society suffers from a lack of political and personal imagination, necessitating a return to classical wisdom.

The discussion frames history not just as narrative, but as a practical educational tool. Drawing heavily on Plutarch and Machiavelli, the core thesis is that studying the lives of great figures allows one to understand both the ideal potential (aspiration) and the necessary self-assessment (warts and all). Burtka emphasizes that true leadership success hinges on mastering relationships and understanding the interplay between controllable virtue and uncontrollable fortune.

## Key Points
- **Statesmanship is a Skill Set, Not a Profession:** The ability to lead successfully is defined as a transferable skill set, applicable to any large or ambitious endeavor, including business.
- **The Revival of the "Mirror for Princes":** This ancient genre of conduct manuals (found across Greek, Roman, Chinese, and Byzantine cultures) provides structured advice on how to behave as an ideal leader. The goal is to apply this historical wisdom to modern challenges.
- **History as a Dual-Function Mirror:** Studying the past serves two purposes: 1) **Aspiration:** It shows what is possible in a person's life or nation. 2) **Self-Correction:** It allows the individual to compare their current reality against great examples, revealing personal weaknesses and areas for improvement.
- **The Primacy of Relationships:** For a leader, the primary job is managing relationships. Success is built upon navigating a complex "web of relationships," including managing up (accountability to boards, superiors, etc.).
- **The Formula for Greatness (Control vs. Chance):** Greatness is achieved through the interplay of two forces: **Virtue/Excellence** (which is controllable through discipline and imitation) and **Chance/Fortune/Providence** (external obstacles). Machiavelli notes that fortune often places obstacles (like enemies) before a prince, forcing him to conquer and thereby achieving greatness.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Curate a "Statesmanship" Learning Regimen:** Dedicate time to studying the biographies and conduct manuals of historical leaders (e.g., Cicero, Washington, etc.) to understand their decision-making frameworks, not just their achievements.
- [ ] **Analyze Your Network Web:** Identify the key relationships (the "web") that define your current professional life. Practice "managing up"—understanding the needs, expectations, and power structures of those above you—as a core leadership skill.
- [ ] **Implement the "Mirror" Exercise:** When facing a professional challenge or personal weakness, do not compare yourself to an idealized version of success. Instead, use historical or professional case studies to identify the *specific mistakes* and *failures* of others at your current stage, allowing for realistic self-correction.
- [ ] **Reframe Obstacles:** When a setback or unexpected challenge occurs (a "small obstacle"), view it not as a deterrent, but as a necessary, forced step on the ladder toward a higher level of competence or influence.
