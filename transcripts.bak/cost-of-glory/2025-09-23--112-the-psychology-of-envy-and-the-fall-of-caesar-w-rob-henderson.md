# 112 - The Psychology of Envy and the Fall of Caesar: w/ Rob Henderson — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode features Rob Henderson, a highly credentialed expert with a Cambridge PhD in psychology, a background as a former U.S. Air Force member, and a senior fellow at the Manhattan Institute. Henderson’s core argument challenges the modern academic tendency toward "presentism"—the obsession with studying only immediate, trending political or social events (like Brexit or Trump). He argues that true insights into human nature are found by studying historical patterns and classics, noting that human passions remain constant across millennia.

The discussion centers on the psychology of envy, using the assassination of Julius Caesar as a prime example of political discontent. Henderson meticulously distinguishes between envy and jealousy, defining envy as the desire for what someone else possesses. He introduces a crucial framework for understanding the motivations behind betrayal and conflict, differentiating between positive, negative, and zero-sum forms of envy.

## Key Points
- **The Critique of Presentism:** Modern academic psychology often focuses too heavily on immediate, trending topics, leading to superficial studies (e.g., surveying 300 participants on a current political event). Henderson advocates for drawing deeper, more enduring lessons from history and philosophy, where human passions are shown to recur across different epochs.
- **Envy vs. Jealousy:**
    *   **Jealousy:** Fear of losing something you already possess (e.g., a romantic partner being flirted with).
    *   **Envy:** The desire for something someone else has (e.g., wishing you had the power or success of a rival).
- **The Spectrum of Envy (Actionable Framework):**
    *   **Benevolent/Positive Envy (Upward Looking):** Seeing someone successful and using that as motivation to level yourself up and improve. (High-signal, positive action).
    *   **Malicious Envy:** Attempting to level the other person down, often through sabotage or undermining their success.
    *   **Zero-Sum Game:** The belief that if one person gains something, you must lose it (either I have it, or you have it).
    *   **Negative-Sum Game:** The belief that the resource must be destroyed so that neither party can have it, resulting in mutual loss.
- **Pattern Recognition in Betrayal:** Henderson draws a parallel between Caesar's warnings (from his wife and soothsayer) and the historical warnings given to Lee Kuan Yew (LKY) regarding political infiltration. This highlights the recurring pattern that intuition and warnings from those closest to power (often women, in this context) can signal deeper, unacknowledged threats.
