# 121 - Plutarch and Shakespeare, w/ The Base Creates — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode discusses the enduring vitality of classical culture, arguing that tradition is not preserved by scholars alone but by creators who generate new meaning from ancient sources. The central thesis is that true cultural energy requires "stakes"—a mechanism for competition and audience engagement—to drive people toward excellence.

The Base (Morgan Watkins and Ollie Bennett) founded their theater company as a form of "cultural resistance" against what they perceive as an era of censorship, fear, and intellectual shame in Western culture. Their model involves bridging the gap between deep online intellectual discourse (the modern "intellectual rock star") and physical art forms by re-engaging with key texts from the Western canon (Aeschylus, Goethe, Plutarch).

George Carter, representing Old Sovereign Publishing, provides historical context for this movement through the concept of the Great Panathenaea—a massive ancient Greek festival that served as a multi-disciplinary contest. This competition serves as the model for The Base's current project: encouraging new dramatic works based on Plutarch’s lives, thereby creating a modern cultural engine fueled by tradition and competitive spirit.

## Key Points
- **Culture Requires Stakes:** Drawing from classical history (e.g., Greek theater), the discussion emphasizes that audience participation and competition are essential drivers of culture. If there is no stake or incentive, the creative energy dissipates.
- **The Modern Cultural Crisis:** The Base explicitly frames its mission as a countermeasure to "censoriousness and fear," suggesting that modern intellectual life has become overly cautious, leading to an imposed "shame" regarding Western traditions and history.
- **Bridging Digital and Physical Intellect:** There is a recognized opportunity in linking the highly engaged online community of "intellectuals" (the Jordan Peterson model) with tangible, high-quality artistic output. The goal is to move intellectual energy from purely digital discussion into physical, shared cultural experiences.
- **The Power of the Canon:** Using classical figures like Plutarch allows creators to explore universal human conditions ("who are we?") without making definitive claims about being "the great intellects," positioning the work as a collaborative journey of discovery for both the artist and the audience.

## Tools & Resources
- **The Base (Company):** A London-based stage production company focused on cultural resistance, exploring classical texts, and creating free/accessible theatrical experiences.
- **Old Sovereign Publishing:** The publisher sponsoring the drama competition, specializing in accessible editions of classic works like Plutarch's Lives.
- **Plutarch’s Lives:** The primary source material for the current creative project; these biographies provide rich, dramatic narratives suitable for modern adaptation.
- **Great Panathenaea (Competition):** A proposed drama contest modeled after ancient Greek festivals, designed to encourage new plays based on Plutarch's lives and providing a structured platform for cultural output.
