# 105 - Breeding Future Statesmen: Interview w/ Kevin Dolan on the EXIT podcast — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode explores the historical preoccupation of classical statesmen—particularly the Greeks and Romans—with the problem of declining birth rates and population stability. The discussion centers on Sparta, using its history as a case study in extreme demographic management. The speaker highlights that Sparta's unique, highly restrictive system, established by the lawgiver Lycurgus, was designed to maintain the purity and strength of the warrior nobility (the *Homoioi*). This system was essentially an oligarchy built on strict egalitarianism and military supremacy, controlling everything from land distribution (via primogeniture) to citizenship.

The core thesis is that while these ancient societies were masters of optimization and control, their very success and rigidity ultimately led to their decline. The system, while ensuring excellence and minimizing waste, lacked the adaptability needed to survive centuries of change. The discussion draws a direct parallel between the historical failure of Sparta and the modern concern over demographic decline, framing the issue as a critical challenge for future statesmen and societies.

## Key Points
- **Ancient Concern for Demographics:** Declining populations were not merely modern anxieties; they were central concerns for classical authorities (e.g., Aristotle, who attributed Sparta's decline to *oliganthropy*—too few men).
- **The Spartan Model (Extreme Optimization):** Sparta operated as a hyper-efficient, restricted oligarchy. Citizenship was limited to the warrior nobility, who were governed by strict laws (like land redistribution and primogeniture) to maintain a legally limited supply of sovereignty.
- **The Failure of Restriction:** The system worked as long as the warrior class could sustain its numbers and maintain military supremacy. However, the very mechanisms designed to preserve excellence and resources (strict egalitarianism, limiting citizenship) eventually led to stagnation and an inability to adapt to external pressures (e.g., the Peloponnesian Wars, Alexander's campaigns).
- **The Eugenics Parallel:** The Spartan system is described as "openly nakedly eugenic" and focused solely on the strong over the weak. This extreme focus on excellence and purity, while initially successful, is presented as a failure mode when it sacrifices flexibility and breadth for narrow, defined quality.
- **The Modern Analogy:** The discussion frames the current demographic challenge as a "greatest population bust in human history," suggesting that the solution requires a strategic, state-level focus on population and societal renewal.
