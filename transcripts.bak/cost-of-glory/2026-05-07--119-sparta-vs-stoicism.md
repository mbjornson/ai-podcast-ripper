# 119 - Sparta vs. Stoicism — Cost of Glory

## Summary
This episode of Cost of Glory explores the surprising parallels between Stoicism and the Spartan ethos, challenging the conventional image of the stoic as emotionless and detached. The host, Alex Petkus, argues that a purely intellectual approach to Stoicism isn’t enough and that embracing passionate, directed emotion – particularly that of “thumos” – is crucial for achieving the desired outcomes, drawing inspiration from ancient philosophers like Plutarch and Lycurgus. The episode is largely driven by a critique of contemporary Stoic influencers, notably Ryan Holiday, who is portrayed as prioritizing ideological purity over practical application. 

The core argument revolves around the idea that true strength isn't solely about mental discipline but about harnessing and channeling powerful emotions, exemplified by the Spartans’ use of music and ritual to incite battle fervor. Petkus highlights the contrast between Stoic ideals and the pragmatic approach of figures like Lycurgus, who seized power through force to enact a radical societal transformation. The episode also delves into Plutarch’s alternative philosophy, advocating for actively manipulating emotions rather than suppressing them, offering a nuanced perspective on the role of passion in human action.

Ultimately, the episode suggests that Stoicism isn't about eliminating emotion but about directing it toward virtuous goals, a concept illustrated through the contrasting approaches of the Stoics and the Spartans. 

## Key Points
- **Stoicism isn't purely intellectual:** The episode challenges the notion that Stoicism is solely about suppressing emotions. Petkus argues for a more active engagement with passion, specifically “thumos” – spiritedness – as a vital component.
- **Spartans as a practical example:** The Spartans are presented as a surprisingly relevant model for Stoicism, demonstrating how a society can be built on a foundation of intense, directed emotion.
- **Lycurgus’s Un-Stoic Power Grab:** Lycurgus’s seizure of power through force is presented as a pragmatic, albeit unconventional, approach to achieving societal reform, contrasting with the idealized image of a virtuous, passively accepting Stoic.
- **Plutarch’s “Manipulation” of Emotion:** Plutarch’s philosophy, as presented, advocates for actively shaping emotions rather than simply controlling them, emphasizing the ability to direct “thumos” toward virtuous ends.
- **The Role of Music and Ritual:** The episode highlights the Spartans’ use of music, dance, and sacrifice to incite battle fervor, demonstrating how external stimuli can be harnessed to shape emotional responses.
- **Contrarian Take on Stoic Influencers:** Petkus critiques contemporary Stoic influencers like Ryan Holiday, suggesting their focus on ideological purity detracts from the practical application of Stoic principles.
