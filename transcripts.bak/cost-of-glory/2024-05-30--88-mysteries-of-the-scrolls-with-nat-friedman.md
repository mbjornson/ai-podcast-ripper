# 88 - Mysteries of the Scrolls — with Nat Friedman — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode uses the incredible story of the Herculaneum scrolls—a time capsule of ancient knowledge preserved by the Vesuvius eruption—as a framework to discuss the nature of deep, complex, and technologically challenging research. The guest, Nat Friedman, is a highly credible figure in the tech and startup world, known for his roles as an entrepreneur, investor, and co-founder of Xamarin, and most notably, his tenure as the CEO of GitHub. While the topic is ancient history, the discussion pivots to the modern technological effort—the Vesuvius Challenge—to digitally unroll and decode these fragile texts.

Friedman shares how his interest in such a monumental, non-tech project began during the uncertainty of the COVID-19 lockdowns. He recounts a personal shift from viewing history as "contingent and parochial" (unimportant) to realizing that this world, with all its contingent history, is the only one he would ever live in. The core signal is the transition from destructive, physical attempts to preserve knowledge (like the 18th-century excavators) to modern, sophisticated technological solutions required to unlock ancient secrets.

## Key Points
- **The Value of Contingency:** Friedman's personal shift in perspective highlights that even seemingly "contingent" events (like the specific history of Rome) are the only reality we inhabit. This is a powerful mindset shift for professionals: focusing on the unique, messy, and non-universal details of your current domain is often where the greatest value lies.
- **Knowledge Preservation is a Technological Problem:** The scrolls' survival is attributed to the volcanic ash, which acted as a perfect preservative, illustrating that the greatest threats to knowledge are often environmental (humidity, time) rather than human malice. The modern challenge is not just discovery, but advanced digital reconstruction and decoding.
- **The Danger of Selection Bias:** Historically, the best scrolls were often the ones that were easiest to access or least damaged, leading to a skewed understanding of the past. This is a cautionary tale for any field: the data you have access to (or the successes you measure) may not represent the full, unbiased truth.
- **The Power of Perspective:** Friedman notes that contemplating vast scales of time (ancient history) or space (astronomy) has a deeply calming and perspective-shifting effect, making current personal or professional problems seem "pretty small."

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *24 Hours in Ancient Rome* (Recommended for gaining historical perspective and drawing interest in classics).
- **Project:** The Vesuvius Challenge (A real-world, technologically intensive effort to scan, digitally unroll, and decode the Herculaneum scrolls).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Perspective:** When facing professional uncertainty or stress, deliberately engage with subjects far removed from your immediate work (e.g., history, astronomy, deep science) to gain emotional distance and perspective.
- [ ] **Study Classics:** Read popular history or classics (like *24 Hours in Ancient Rome*) not for factual knowledge, but for the mental exercise of appreciating the depth and complexity of human endeavor across time.
- [ ] **Focus on the "Contingent":** In business or career planning, instead of seeking universal, perfect models, focus on mastering the unique, messy, and specific details of your current market or niche, as these are the only variables you can actually control.
- [ ] **Embrace Deep Tech Challenges:** Look for complex, multi-disciplinary problems in your field that require combining historical knowledge, advanced technology, and deep human insight (analogous to the Vesuvius Challenge).
