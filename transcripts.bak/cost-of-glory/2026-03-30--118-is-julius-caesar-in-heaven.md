# 118 - Is Julius Caesar in Heaven? — Cost of Glory

## Summary
This episode of *Cost of Glory* delves into the enduring fascination with Julius Caesar, exploring his unique strengths, challenges, and relevance to our current moment. The host, Alex Petkus, grapples with the question of how to approach Caesar’s legacy, acknowledging his brilliance while also recognizing his flaws. A key discussion revolves around Caesar’s approximate net worth at the time of his death and the broader question of how we should morally evaluate figures like him. The episode incorporates a lengthy deep dive into primary source research, relying heavily on Plutarch, Suetonius, Caesar’s commentaries, and the writings of other historians like Cassius Dio and Matthias Gelser. The conversation is framed around the concept of “speed” – not just physical speed, but the ability to maintain momentum and push forward under pressure – and how this relates to Caesar’s leadership and, by extension, to success in any endeavor. The episode is largely a thoughtful, research-driven exploration of a historical figure, with a strong emphasis on understanding the context of Caesar’s actions and motivations.

## Key Points
- **Caesar's "Speed" as a Leadership Principle:** The core insight is that Caesar’s success wasn’t solely based on raw speed, but on his ability to instill that same sense of relentless forward momentum in his troops and administration. This “speed” was rooted in endurance and fortitude under fatigue and privation, a concept Napoleon famously articulated.
- **Endurance over Haste:** Napoleon’s observation highlights a critical distinction: true speed is about sustained effort and the ability to push beyond perceived limits, not simply rushing.
- **The Stonewall Brigade Analogy:** The comparison to the Stonewall Brigade in the American Civil War illustrates this point – seemingly unremarkable soldiers achieving remarkable results through relentless effort and adaptability.
- **Caesar's Genius as a Double-Edged Sword:** Matthias Gelser argues that Caesar’s exceptional ability to achieve his goals – often through manipulation and a seeming lack of genuine peer relationships – created a fundamental challenge to his leadership. His success made it difficult for others to trust him.
- **Historical Context of the Assassination:** The episode briefly touches on the conspirators’ motivations – restoring the Republic and liberty – highlighting the complex political landscape that ultimately led to Caesar’s demise.
- **The Importance of Primary Sources:** The host emphasizes the crucial role of consulting primary sources (Plutarch, Suetonius, Caesar’s commentaries) to gain a nuanced understanding of Caesar’s character and actions, rather than relying on simplified narratives.

## Tools & Resources
- **Plutarch’s Biographies:** (Specifically, the lives of Caesar, Pompey, Crassus, Cicero, Brutus, Antony) – The foundational text for understanding Caesar.
- **Ward Farnsworth’s Books:** *The Practicing Stoic* and *Farnsworth’s Classical English Rhetoric* – For developing rhetorical skills and understanding classical philosophy.
- **Matthias Gelser’s *Caesar, Politician and Statesman*:** A highly regarded scholarly biography offering a comprehensive analysis of Caesar’s life and career.
- **Adrian Goldsworthy’s *Caesar, Life of a Colossus*:** A more readable biography that closely follows Gelser’s work.
- **Anthony Froude’s *Caesar: A Sketch*:** A Victorian-era perspective on Caesar, offering a unique and engaging narrative.
- **Friedrich Gundolf’s *Mantle of Caesar*:** A thought-provoking and artistic exploration of Caesar’s legacy.
