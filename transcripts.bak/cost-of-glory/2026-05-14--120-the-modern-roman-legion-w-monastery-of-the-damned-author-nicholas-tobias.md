# 120 - The Modern Roman Legion?  w/ "Monastery of the Damned" author Nicholas Tobias — Cost of Glory

## Summary
This episode features Nicholas Tobias, a former legionnaire in the French Foreign Legion and U.S. Army infantry officer, discussing his new book, *Monastery of the Damned*. Tobias argues that modern men lack a crucial element – sustained, excruciating, and humiliating rites of passage – necessary for genuine self-cultivation and a robust sense of identity. He posits that contemporary approaches to self-improvement, particularly those offered by influencers, are insufficient, lacking the depth and transformative power of traditional experiences like military service. Tobias emphasizes the importance of confronting limitations, accepting humility, and understanding the value of deference to authority and hierarchical structures, drawing parallels to classical military traditions and cultures where these values are deeply ingrained. He offers a somewhat contrarian view, particularly regarding the experiences of women, suggesting they face more sustained challenges and require different approaches to self-improvement.

## Key Points
- **The Need for Rites of Passage:** Tobias believes modern men are lacking a fundamental need for intense, challenging, and even humiliating experiences to forge a strong sense of self. He argues that simply wanting greatness isn't enough; true growth comes from overcoming hardship.
- **Caesar as a Model:** He draws heavily from Julius Caesar’s *Gallic Wars* commentaries, viewing them as a benchmark for cultivated individuals and a source of discipline and understanding. He expresses a preference for Latin over English translations and a desire to fully comprehend Caesar’s work.
- **Critique of Modern Self-Improvement:** Tobias is skeptical of contemporary approaches to self-improvement, particularly those promoted by online influencers, arguing they often lack depth and are disconnected from real-world challenges. He contrasts this with the rigorous discipline of military service.
- **Gender Differences:** He suggests that women, particularly through motherhood and navigating hierarchical social structures, experience more sustained and challenging “rites of passage” than men, implying a different set of requirements for self-cultivation.
- **Hierarchy and Deference:** Tobias emphasizes the importance of understanding and accepting hierarchical structures, particularly within military contexts, where deference to authority is crucial. He describes this as a key element of a “manly” character.
- **Traditional Masculinity:** He contrasts contemporary “manly” behavior (e.g., Zoomer content creation) with traditional masculine values, highlighting the importance of discipline, humility, and respect for authority.
