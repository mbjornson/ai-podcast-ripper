# 82 - How to Tell a Flatterer From a Friend — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode draws heavily on Plutarch’s treatise, *How to Tell a Flatterer from a Friend*, offering timeless counsel on navigating social relationships and avoiding personal downfall through superficial praise. The core thesis is that surrounding yourself with people who are genuinely honest—even if their truth hurts in the short term—is far more critical for long-term success than being surrounded by those who merely make you feel good. This advice is framed as particularly relevant in the "internet age," where digital constraints and pressure often encourage flattery rather than frankness.

Plutarch argues that flattery is not just a social failing, but a deep psychological danger because it actively undermines self-knowledge (the maxim "Know thyself"). Flatterers are characterized by their lack of consistency; they adopt the fashion or persona required to win favor at any given moment. Conversely, true friends maintain a consistent character and possess the virtue of *paresia*—a willingness to speak difficult truths, including criticism.

The discussion provides actionable frameworks for self-assessment: distinguishing between shallow imitation (the flatterer) and genuine support (the friend). The ultimate goal is not merely identifying bad people, but cultivating the internal strength to give and receive honest feedback, recognizing that praise coming from someone willing to blame you holds far greater value than uncritical admiration.

## Key Points
- **The Danger of Flattery:** Flattery is described as an enemy to self-knowledge, leading individuals toward "an ignorance both of himself and of the good and evil." It can be dangerous to your psychic health and leads to poor life decisions (career, relationships).
- **Consistency Test (The Litmus Test):** A true friend maintains a consistent character in their dress, conduct, language, and values regardless of the social setting. Flatterers are defined by their adaptability; they change their persona—from scholarly philosopher to drunken Odysseus—to conform to the current fashion or power structure.
- **Value of Criticism (Paresia):** The most valuable praise comes from someone willing not only to commend you but also to blame you. This virtue, *paresia* (willingness to say everything), is the direct antidote to flattery and superficiality.
- **Flattery as Shallow Imitation:** Flatterers are skilled at imitation—copying the current trends or styles of those around them (e.g., copying a philosopher's stoop or an athlete’s physique). This shallow mimicry signals a lack of genuine, stable selfhood.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop *Paresia*:** Actively seek out relationships with people who are comfortable giving you difficult, critical feedback. Practice receiving criticism without becoming defensive; this builds resilience and self-awareness.
- [ ] **Audit Your Circle for Consistency:** When evaluating friends or professional contacts, look past the praise. Observe how they behave when stakes are high, when they have nothing to gain, or when they disagree with you—this reveals their true character consistency.
- [ ] **Study Classical Texts:** Explore Plutarch's writings and Xenophon’s historical accounts to understand the deep philosophical roots of integrity and self-knowledge.
- [ ] **Prioritize Depth Over Breadth in Friendships:** Instead of accumulating many superficial connections, focus on cultivating a few relationships defined by mutual honesty and accountability.
