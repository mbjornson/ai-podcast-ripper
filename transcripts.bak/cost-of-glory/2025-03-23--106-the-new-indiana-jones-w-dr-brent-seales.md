# 106 - The New Indiana Jones - w/ Dr. Brent Seales — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode details the groundbreaking effort to decipher the Herculaneum Scrolls, a massive collection of ancient texts preserved by the pyroclastic mud of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD. The discussion centers on the technological process pioneered by Dr. Brent Seales, a computer scientist, to read these scrolls without causing further damage—a feat that has been impossible for centuries. The core problem is that 95-99% of ancient philosophical and literary texts are lost, and the surviving scrolls are so fragile that traditional attempts to unroll them have been destructive.

Dr. Seales' work represents a convergence of advanced imaging technology (tomography) and computational science. The project was initially developed through general digital restoration efforts (like those on the Beowulf manuscript) and adapted from techniques used on the Dead Sea Scrolls. The breakthrough involved developing algorithms to create a non-destructive, three-dimensional view of the internal structure of the rolled papyri, allowing researchers to "unwrap" the text digitally.

Crucially, the project leveraged an open-source, crowdsourcing model, transforming what was a highly specialized academic endeavor into a global, public competition. This public engagement has accelerated the research, demonstrating that massive, distributed human effort can be a critical component in solving complex, multi-decade scientific challenges.

## Key Points
- **The Scale of Loss:** Approximately 95-99% of ancient philosophical and literary texts are lost, primarily because papyrus scrolls are not built to last in normal climate conditions.
- **The Technological Leap:** The method moves beyond simple facsimile imaging. It utilizes **tomography** (analogous to CAT scans or MRIs) to take multiple slices of a 3D image, allowing researchers to visualize the text *inside* the rolled scroll without physical intervention.
- **The Open-Source Advantage:** The decision to make the research public and run it as a global contest (crowdsourcing) was a critical, non-obvious factor. It generated massive public interest and global competition, accelerating the project far beyond what a small, specialized academic team could achieve.
- **The Iterative Process:** Seales' career shows a pattern of technological scaling: starting with simple digital editions (Beowulf manuscript, 1990s), moving to advanced digital restoration (Dead Sea Scrolls, early 2000s), and finally applying the most complex methods to the Herculaneum Scrolls.

## Tools & Resources
- **Tomography:** The core imaging technology used. It provides a 3D view through an object's internal structure by taking multiple cross-sectional "slices."
- **Open-Source Modeling:** The successful application of open-source principles to a highly specialized, historically guarded academic problem, turning it into a global competition.
- **Doge Team/Crowdsourcing:** The specific mechanism used to mobilize public interest and competitive effort in the final stages of the deciphering process.
