# 115 - Aristotle, Thiel Fellowship, and Human Greatness w/ Michael Gibson — Cost of Glory

## Summary
Michael Gibson is a prominent figure in the venture capital and early-stage funding space, serving as co-founder of the VC fund 1517 and having been instrumental in establishing the Thiel Fellowship. His background is a fascinating pivot: moving from an academic pursuit (studying ancient Greek and Latin to become a poet like T.S. Eliot) to a career focused on disruptive, real-world innovation. His narrative centers on the failure of traditional academia, which he describes as "scholarly dry as dust" and overly incremental.

The episode details his transition from a disillusioned PhD student to a journalist and eventually to a researcher at MIT, which led him to Peter Thiel. This connection culminated in the launch of the Thiel Fellowship—a program designed to bypass the traditional university system, funding young people to focus on non-academic, high-impact projects (startups, research, non-profits). Gibson uses this experience to critique the modern educational landscape, arguing that the university has become a "sacred" institution that issues diplomas functioning as modern "indulgences."

## Key Points
- **The Diploma as Indulgence:** Gibson argues that the modern university system functions like the Catholic Church's "magisterium," where diplomas are treated as "indulgences"—pieces of paper that are necessary for social and economic salvation (getting into the "heaven" of white-collar elite jobs).
- **The Failure of Academia:** He notes that academic discourse can be excessively dry, focusing on minute, incremental arguments (e.g., "arguing over the tenth decimal place") rather than inspiring, impactful work.
- **The Power of the Pivot:** His career trajectory—dropping out of a PhD program to work at a magazine (MIT Tech Review) and then into VC—demonstrates a successful pivot from theoretical knowledge to practical, market-driven application.
- **Virtue Ethics vs. Consequences:** In philosophy, he highlights the shift toward a renewed interest in Aristotle's virtue ethics, contrasting it with modern academic focus on either consequences (utilitarianism) or rights/duties (deontology).
- **The Thiel Model:** The fellowship was designed to allow young people to "cut the fat off [their] prose" by leaving school to focus on real-world problem-solving, rather than simply accumulating credentials.

## Tools & Resources
- **1517:** Venture capital fund focused on challenging established norms and funding disruptive ideas.
- **The Thiel Fellowship:** A grant-making program designed to fund ambitious, non-academic projects for young people.
- ***The Century Paper Belt on Fire***: Gibson's book detailing the critique of institutional education and the power of disruptive funding.
- **Peter Thiel:** Co-founder of Palantir and PayPal, central figure in the discussion of alternative funding models.

## Quotable

>

## Action Items
- [ ] **De-risk Your Education:** If pursuing advanced degrees, actively seek out ways to apply theoretical knowledge to real-world, revenue-generating projects (e.g., building a side business or non-profit) to validate your skills outside of coursework.
- [ ] **Adopt the "Indulgence" Mindset:** When evaluating credentials or career paths, ask: "Is this credential truly necessary, or is it merely a social/economic indulgence that others treat as sacred?"
- [ ] **Focus on Actionable Philosophy:** When studying ethics or complex systems, prioritize frameworks like Aristotle's virtue ethics (focusing on character and habit) over purely consequentialist or rights-based models.
- [ ] **Network for Pivots:** Actively seek out opportunities to work in high-signal, non-academic environments (tech startups, research labs, policy groups) to bridge the gap between theory and practice.
