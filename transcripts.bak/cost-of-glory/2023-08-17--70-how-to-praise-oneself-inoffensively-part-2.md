# 70 - How to Praise Oneself Inoffensively - Part 2 — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode analyzes Plutarch’s treatise, *How to Praise One's Self Inoffensively*, providing advanced strategies for managing one's public reputation and mitigating envy (*phonos*). The central thesis is that while self-praise is generally frowned upon, there are specific, strategic contexts where it can be deployed effectively—and even positively—to enhance one’s standing. These techniques are not about outright boasting, but rather about framing accomplishments in a way that makes the praise seem natural, deserved, or even inevitable given external forces (like luck or fate).

The discussion focuses on three main frameworks for "inoffensive" self-promotion: praising oneself during times of great adversity; strategically attributing success to outside factors (luck, gods); and adjusting compliments by giving credit away. The overarching goal is to assert one's merit without triggering the resentment that naturally accompanies visible success in a competitive environment.

## Key Points
- **Praise During Misfortune:** One of the most potent times to praise oneself is when suffering great misfortune or injustice (e.g., Phocion). Adversity removes the envy and resentment others might feel towards boasting, allowing one's resilience and character to shine through as a sign of "greatness and resolution."
- **Strategic Attribution (The Luck Shield):** When receiving success, it is strategically beneficial to attribute some portion of that achievement to chance, luck, or divine favor (e.g., Sulla). This technique diffuses the inevitable ill will attached to great success by making the accomplishment seem less purely meritocratic and more influenced by external forces ("For men would rather be bested by luck than by merit").
- **The Art of Adjusting Compliments:** When receiving praise, do not accept it wholesale. Instead, "correct" or adjust the compliment by giving credit to others (e.g., Pericles) or by confessing humble origins/faults (e.g., Agathocles). This shifts the focus from your singular genius to a collaborative effort or an impressive journey of overcoming low status.
- **The Psychology of Envy (*Phonos*):** The core mechanism driving these strategies is managing *phonos*, the Greek emotion of resentment felt by peers toward those who achieve great success. By diffusing credit, you neutralize this emotional threat and make your achievements more palatable to a skeptical audience.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** Plutarch's *How to Praise One's Self Inoffensively* (The primary source material).
- **Concept:** Understanding the difference between "merit" and "luck" in public discourse, and how framing success relative to both can be a powerful reputation management tool.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Reputation Audit:** Identify areas where your professional success is currently perceived as solely due to personal merit. Develop a strategy to publicly attribute some portion of that success to team effort, market conditions, or external resources.
- [ ] **Practice Strategic Deflection:** In public settings (meetings, networking), when praised for an achievement, immediately redirect credit by mentioning specific teammates, mentors, or historical precedents ("This was only possible because the data provided by X department...").
- [ ] **Develop a "Humility Confession":** When launching a new product or service, consider framing its success not just on technical brilliance, but on overcoming initial limitations (e.g., "We started with basic prototypes in a garage and built this...") to make the achievement seem more earned and less magically perfect.
- [ ] **Study Classical Rhetoric:** Read Plutarch's biographies of statesmen like Pericles and Themistocles to understand how they managed public perception through language, not just action.
