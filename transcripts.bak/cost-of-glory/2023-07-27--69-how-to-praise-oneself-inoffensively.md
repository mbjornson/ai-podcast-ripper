# 69 - How to Praise Oneself Inoffensively — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode analyzes Plutarch's ancient essay on "How to Praise Oneself Inoffensively," providing a strategic framework for self-promotion in professional and public life. The core thesis is that while boasting about one’s own achievements is generally offensive, it can be highly effective—and even encouraged—when deployed strategically within the context of self-defense or when defending against serious accusations.

The discussion uses historical figures like Cicero, Scipio Africanus, Epaminondas, and Demosthenes to illustrate this principle. The key takeaway is that the *context* dictates whether self-praise is viewed as arrogant boasting (Cicero's mistake) or as necessary evidence of great deeds (Scipio’s success). In modern terms, critics and rivals should not be seen merely as threats, but as strategic opportunities to elevate your reputation.

## Key Points
- **The Contextual Nature of Praise:** Self-praise is not inherently wrong; its reception depends entirely on the situation. Cicero was criticized because his boasting stemmed from a desire for "glory," whereas Scipio’s praise came out of necessity during a high-stakes judgment, which mitigated envy.
- **Self-Praise as Defense (The Golden Opportunity):** The most effective time to boast is when you are under attack or being prosecuted. Epaminondas successfully used his defense in court to highlight the magnitude of his accomplishments, turning potential condemnation into public admiration.
- **Rivals Are Strategic Assets:** View critics and rivals not just as detractors, but as sources of motivation and, more importantly, "an excuse." They provide a platform where you can tell your own story in a favorable light without incurring the resentment associated with voluntary boasting.
- **The Emotional Discipline:** When criticized or attacked, the immediate reaction should be to remain calm ("Don't let your blood boil"). Instead of reacting defensively, one must plot how to use the criticism to their advantage—a strategic pivot that turns accusation into a platform for praise.

## Tools & Resources
- **Plutarch’s Essay:** *How to Praise One's Self Inoffensively* (The foundational text for the entire discussion).
- **Oratory/Persuasion:** The principles discussed are rooted in classical rhetoric, making study of ancient orators like Demosthenes and Cicero highly valuable.

## Quotable
> "They give you an excuse. They present you with an opportunity every now and then to tell your own story in a favorable light in front of a wider audience."
>
> "When you or someone on your team gets criticized, don't tense up. Don't let your blood boil. Calm down. Crack your knuckles. And get to work plotting on how you're going to use it to your advantage."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Strategic Patience:** When facing criticism in a professional setting, practice the discipline of pausing and avoiding an immediate emotional reaction. Treat the critique as raw material for a defense narrative rather than a personal attack.
- [ ] **Reframe Criticism as Opportunity:** Actively seek to identify how current or anticipated rivals/critics can be leveraged to create a public platform (a "golden rhetorical opportunity") where your achievements must be highlighted in self-defense.
- [ ] **Study Classical Rhetoric:** Review the speeches of Demosthenes, specifically *On the Crown*, to understand the structure and timing of successful self-advocacy under duress.
- [ ] **Practice Narrative Control:** When presenting accomplishments (in a meeting, presentation, or negotiation), frame them not just as results, but as necessary defenses against potential systemic failures or external threats, giving your praise an air of unavoidable necessity.
