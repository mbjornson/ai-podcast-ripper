# 87 - Pursuing Greatness — with Pano Kanelos — Cost of Glory

## Summary
The episode centers on Pano Kanelos, the founding president of the University of Austin, discussing the immense challenge and opportunity involved in establishing a new higher education institution. Kanelos frames the act of building a university not just as an academic endeavor, but as a profound entrepreneurial act—a process of identifying and filling a critical societal need. He draws parallels between running a modern university and operating a local diner: both require taking risks, understanding community needs, and providing a core service (hospitality/knowledge).

Kaneus argues that the current American higher education system is suffering from stagnation, high costs, and a perceived disconnect between academic theory and real-world innovation. The opportunity for new institutions lies in creating a model that retains the depth of liberal arts education while explicitly preparing students to be builders, creators, and innovators—a synthesis that traditional universities often fail to provide.

## Key Points
- **The University as an Ancient City:** Kanelos posits that modern universities share the historical function of ancient cities: they are centers of intense community identity and loyalty. This sense of shared purpose is crucial for institutional success.
- **Founding Requires Identifying a Need (Market Gap):** The initial impetus for founding U of Austin was not academic theory, but an external push from journalist Bari Weiss who insisted that the current system required new players. Founding, therefore, is fundamentally about recognizing where existing structures are failing or becoming stale.
- **The American Model of Expansion:** Historically, America's dynamism allowed for a continuous flurry of university founding (e.g., 40 universities in Ohio during one era). The current slowdown—with U of Austin being the first new private university in Texas since 1963—represents a massive systemic opportunity to return to an "age of building."
- **The Crisis of Value vs. Cost:** Kanelos notes that the market is signaling a decreased value proposition for traditional degrees, evidenced by fewer students enrolling and astronomical costs (citing Vanderbilt's annual cost exceeding $100k). The solution must be creating deep liberal arts education *without* becoming merely vocational training.

## Tools & Resources
