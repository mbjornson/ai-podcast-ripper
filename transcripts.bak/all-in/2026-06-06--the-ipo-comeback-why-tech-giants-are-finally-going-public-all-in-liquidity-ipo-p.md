# The IPO Comeback: Why Tech Giants Are Finally Going Public | All-In Liquidity IPO Panel — All-In

## Summary
This panel discussion, featuring founders of newly public tech companies Andrew Feldman (Cerebris Systems) and Will Marshall (Planet Labs), alongside investor Brad Gerstner, analyzes the massive convergence of AI, space data, and the current IPO market wave. The central thesis is that technology is entering a new phase where Earth observation and satellite data are becoming foundational utilities—as essential as GPS or internet connectivity.

The discussion provided deep insight into the mechanics of going public, noting that while the process is administratively grueling ("garbage" meetings), it provides critical external validation and liquidity necessary for long-term corporate stability and credibility with major government/enterprise clients. The speakers emphasize that success in this sector requires not just capital, but a decade-long commitment to solving hard, complex problems (like global Earth imaging).

## Key Points
- **The IPO Credibility Play:** Going public is less about the immediate cash infusion and more about "legitimizing" the company's existence. For defense and civil government clients, being publicly traded signals stability ("we are here to stay")—a critical factor when dealing with national infrastructure or intelligence data.
- **AI Data Dependency:** The core insight across all sectors is that AI is only as powerful as the data it is trained upon. This elevates high-resolution, time-series Earth imaging (like Planet Labs' daily global coverage) from a niche product to an indispensable foundational utility for advanced AI models.
- **The Power of Miniaturization:** The biggest technological shift enabling space data growth isn't just cheaper rockets; it's the miniaturization of satellites. Modern small satellites can perform tasks once requiring massive, multi-billion dollar payloads, drastically lowering the barrier to entry and increasing data density.
- **IPO Timing is Anti-Hype:** Feldman offered a contrarian take on timing: "Getting it wrong for a decade... that's really the right way to get timing right." This suggests that deep, sustained focus on core technology development, rather than chasing immediate market hype cycles, builds the necessary resilience and credibility for a successful public offering.
- **Multi-Use Data Streams:** While Planet Labs notes that 60% of their revenue is currently derived from security/defense applications, they stress that the utility of their data (daily Earth imaging) spans agriculture, energy management, civil disaster relief (flooding/fires), and intelligence—making it a resilient, diversified play.

## Tools & Resources
- **Sectors to Watch:** AI Silicon Infrastructure, Geospatial Data Analytics, Small Satellite Technology (CubeSats), Time-Series Earth Imaging Platforms.
- **Companies/Models:** Cerebris Systems (AI focus), Planet Labs (Earth imaging fleet).
- **Investment Thesis:** Look for companies that solve "infrastructure" problems using data, rather than those selling abstract AI services.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Geospatial Data Skills:** Focus on learning how to process and analyze time-series satellite imagery (e.g., identifying changes in crop health, water levels, or infrastructure damage over months/years). This skill set is highly valuable and currently underserved by general AI models.
- [ ] **Build a "Credibility" Playbook:** When starting any business, model the value of external validation (certifications, government contracts, public listing) not just as revenue, but as a necessary component for enterprise adoption in regulated industries (defense, utilities).
- [ ] **Study Miniaturization Economics:** Track advancements in small satellite payload costs and capabilities. Understanding this technological inflection point is key to predicting the next wave of data infrastructure investment.
- [ ] **Adopt Long-Term Patience:** For high-stakes ventures, adopt a "decade mindset." Focus on building deep technical moats and solving foundational problems rather than chasing short-term market hype cycles or quick exits.
