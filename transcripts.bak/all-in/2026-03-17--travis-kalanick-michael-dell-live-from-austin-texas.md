# Travis Kalanick & Michael Dell Live from Austin, Texas — All-In

## Summary
The episode centers on the concept of "digitizing the physical world," proposing a paradigm shift from traditional digital computing (bits) to an "atoms-based computer" that treats physical matter like data. The discussion, featuring Michael Dell and Travis Kalanick, outlines how the three core computing resources—CPU, Storage, and Network—must be re-envisioned for the physical realm. This involves replacing digital processes with physical infrastructure: Manufacturing (for CPU), Real Estate (for Storage), and Logistics/Transportation (for Network).

The discussion uses the company "Atoms" as a case study, detailing its mission to build infrastructure for better food, automating mining, and developing specialized robot wheelbases. The speakers emphasize that while the digital world is mature, the physical world requires massive, industrial-scale automation to achieve efficiencies previously thought impossible, comparing the difficulty of automating food delivery to the relative ease of automating ride-sharing.

## Key Points
- **The Atoms-Based Computer Framework:** The core thesis is that the physical world can be viewed through a computational lens.
    *   **CPU:** Manipulates atoms (Manufacturing).
    *   **Storage:** Stores atoms (Real Estate).
    *   **Network:** Moves atoms (Logistics/Transportation).
- **Food as the First Computer:** The initial focus is on the "food computer," aiming to make prepared, delivered meals approach the cost of grocery store items. This requires industrial-scale logistics and manufacturing that current restaurant infrastructure cannot support, unlike the relatively simple app-based disruption of Uber.
- **The Physical AI Stack:** The future requires a comprehensive stack that includes not just computation and physical AI models, but also land development, chemistry, and manufacturing. This suggests that disruption must be holistic and infrastructural, not just app-based.
- **Mining Automation & Cost Curves:** Automation fundamentally changes the economics of mining. While the initial challenge is surveying, automation allows companies to overcome the geometric cost curve associated with depth. By removing human labor and safety concerns, it makes previously inhospitable or regulated locations viable.
- **Market Competition (Waymo vs. Tesla):** The conversation notes the high noise and complexity in the self-driving space. While Tesla has the fundamental science, Waymo is highlighted for its "existence proof." The key challenge for all players is achieving industrial scale, urgency, and fierceness in deployment.

## Tools & Resources
