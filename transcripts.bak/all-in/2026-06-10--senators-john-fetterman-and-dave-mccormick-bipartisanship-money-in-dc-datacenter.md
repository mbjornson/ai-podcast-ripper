# Senators John Fetterman and Dave McCormick: Bipartisanship, Money in DC, Datacenters, Graham Platner — All-In

## Summary
The discussion between Senators Fetterman and McCormick quickly moves past partisan politics, framing Pennsylvania as a crucial microcosm for national consensus building. While much time was spent discussing the necessity of bipartisan cooperation—citing shared rural backgrounds and the need to build a working-class coalition across urban/rural divides—the core signal revolves around massive infrastructural shifts driven by technology. The conversation repeatedly pivots to Artificial Intelligence (AI), establishing it not just as a technological trend, but as an economic force requiring immediate policy action, particularly concerning energy infrastructure and data center development.

The senators frame the current political climate as one of profound instability, marked by low institutional trust and high debt ($40 trillion). Their shared message is that finding common ground on issues like fentanyl crisis management, national security (Iran accountability), and AI readiness is more important than partisan victory. The discussion also touches on governance mechanics, specifically acknowledging the historical error in both parties' attempts to eliminate the filibuster, reinforcing its current role as a necessary bulwark for minority rights and stable governance.

## Key Points
- **AI & Energy Infrastructure are Intertwined:** AI growth is identified as an "enormous change" that requires massive energy capacity. The ability to win the "AI race" is directly tied to solving energy policy issues, making permitting reform a critical economic lever for the next 2-3 years.
- **Data Center Policy Risk:** McCormick strongly critiques proposed moratoriums or restrictive policies on data centers, labeling them as potentially detrimental and suggesting they are part of an overly cautious "China first" kind of policy that could stifle American opportunity.
- **The Power of Local Coalitions (Microcosm):** Pennsylvania is presented as a model where winning requires building a diverse coalition—including significant Latino and African-American turnout—across traditionally Democratic urban centers (Philly, Pittsburgh) and Republican rural areas. This suggests that localized community organizing remains the most potent political force.
- **Tangible Policy Successes:** The discussion highlighted specific, measurable policy wins: Fentanyl deaths in Pennsylvania dropped from 4,000 during the Biden administration to 1,600 in the current year, representing a **60% reduction**—a concrete example of effective bipartisan governance.
- **The Filibuster's Value:** Both senators conceded that both parties were wrong about eliminating the filibuster. It is now viewed as essential for protecting minority rights and forcing compromise, preventing the Senate from becoming an overly centralized majority body like the House.
