# If I Wanted To Scale My Consulting Business In 2025, I'd Do This — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a strategic framework for scaling a consulting business, arguing that true growth is not achieved by simply working harder or acquiring more clients through referrals. Instead, successful scaling requires shifting the business model from being founder-dependent ("a job") to being system-driven and scalable ("a real business"). The core message centers on building infrastructure and processes that allow the company to function effectively without the founder's day-to-day involvement.

The discussion outlines several high-leverage growth vectors: productizing services, expanding market reach (geography/client type), increasing client lifetime value (LTV), and most critically, raising prices based on demonstrable ROI rather than time spent. The speaker emphasizes that a successful scale requires moving away from the "value-based" model of fewer clients at high rates toward systematic repeatability while maintaining premium pricing structures.

## Key Points
- **The Definition of Scaling:** Scaling means shifting reliance *away* from you as the founder. If your business stops generating revenue when you are sick or on holiday, it is a job, not a scalable business.
- **Productization Redefined:** Productizing does not mean selling low-cost courses. For established firms, productized offers can be high-ticket items ($25,000 to $100,000). This model allows the use of standardized systems and processes, enabling mid or junior staff (after training) to execute repeatable tasks.
- **The LTV Focus:** The most important metric is increasing Client Lifetime Value (LTV). Instead of focusing solely on acquiring a new client worth $25,000, focus on expanding your offerings so that the same client is worth $45,000 through additional services or ongoing coaching.
- **Pricing Strategy Shift:** Never charge for time; charge for value and outcomes. If you can prove that working with a client will generate over a million dollars in profits within 12–18 months, charging $100,000 is justified, even if it represents only a fraction of the generated profit (a 10x+ return).
- **Growth Vectors:** To acquire more clients or revenue, consider these four paths:
    1. Productizing your offer (systematization).
    2. Expanding client geography (new countries/regions).
    3. Expanding client type (applying expertise to different industries/roles).
    4. Selling additional services to existing clients (upskilling the relationship).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Founder Dependency:** Map out your current consulting process and identify any tasks that *must* be done by you. Systematize these processes immediately to build operational redundancy.
- [ ] **Productize a Core Offering:** Take one repeatable service (e.g., initial assessment, audit) and define it as a standardized product with clear deliverables and pricing ($25k+ target).
- [ ] **Calculate LTV Gap:** For your top 3 client types, calculate your current average engagement value vs. the potential total value you could deliver over 18 months (e.g., Assessment + Coaching + Tech Implementation). Identify where the gap is.
- [ ] **Reframe Pricing Conversation:** Stop discussing hours or tasks; start quantifying the financial outcome for the client. When presenting a fee, always anchor it to the massive ROI (e.g., "This $100k investment will generate an estimated $1M in profit").
