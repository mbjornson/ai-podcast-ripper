# Ultimate Guide to Systemize Your Consulting Business — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into scaling a consulting business by implementing robust operational systems, moving the owner from reactive "grinding" to intentional growth. The core argument is that while expertise is necessary, it is insufficient for scale; systematic processes are what allow a business to run without the founder's constant involvement and generate predictable profitability. The speaker emphasizes that building these systems must happen *after* initial success has been validated, otherwise, the effort will be wasted.

The discussion outlines two critical areas for systemization: Marketing and Project Management. The guest’s credibility is established through the "Consulting Success Podcast" platform and the mention of their coaching program serving over 1,000 consultants globally who have successfully raised fees or doubled revenue. The advice is highly practical, focusing on documenting processes rather than simply adopting tools.

## Key Points
- **System Definition:** A business system is defined as a "defined set of principles, practices, and procedures that are applied to specific activities to achieve a specific result." It must be documented to allow for delegation and consistency.
- **Scaling Principle (The Order):** Do not build systems when you are just starting out. First, validate what works; then, systematize it. Systems enable the business to run without the founder, allowing growth from "moderate profitability to very positive profitability."
- **System 1: Marketing System (Priority #1):** This is called the "lifeblood" of the business because leads are non-negotiable. The system must capture *every part* of what generates results (e.g., speaking at events). Instead of relying on reactive referrals, build a structured process for asking for them, including specific scripts, timing, and follow-up protocols.
- **System 2: Project Management System (Priority #2):** This system documents the entire delivery lifecycle. The simple version involves documenting every step of an engagement; the advanced version moves into dedicated PM software to measure key performance indicators (KPIs) like utilization, capacity, and profitability at each stage.
- **Consistency vs. Genius:** Systems ensure stable and consistent results because they remove reliance on ad-hoc effort or "genius." If you follow a process, the outcome will be predictable.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Marketing Funnel:** Identify the single most effective lead source (e.g., speaking engagements, specific networking groups). Document every step required to generate a conversation from that source—from initial contact to follow-up email.
- [ ] **Systematize Referrals:** Develop a formal, repeatable process for requesting referrals. This must include specific scripts, the ideal project stage to ask at, and a defined follow-up sequence for non-responses.
- [ ] **Implement PM Software:** Select a dedicated project management tool (e.g., Asana, ClickUp) and map out every single step of your typical client engagement. Use this system not just for task tracking, but for measuring utilization and profitability at each stage.
- [ ] **Focus on Documentation:** Treat documentation as the primary deliverable. The goal is to create a process so detailed that someone else (a VA or team member) can execute it flawlessly without your input.
