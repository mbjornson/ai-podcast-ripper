# Is Your Expertise Killing Your Consulting Deals with Scott Curran — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode features Scott Curran, a consultant whose background is highly unique: he transitioned from practicing corporate law to spending a decade in major philanthropy and non-profit general counsel roles. His credibility stems from this blend of legal rigor and social impact expertise, allowing him to help multinational NGOs tackle complex challenges. Initially skeptical of the consulting industry due to negative experiences as an in-house counsel (where poor consultant outcomes were common), Scott’s own success has led him to develop clear frameworks for others to succeed. The discussion centers on moving beyond generic service provision and adopting structural practices that command premium fees, shift clients from project work to profitable retainers, and fundamentally change the mindset required to build a successful consulting practice.

## Key Points
- **The Importance of Specificity in Scope:** Scott identifies the Statement of Work (SOW) as the single most critical document. Poorly written, generic, or nebulous SOWs are immediate red flags for clients. Conversely, "exquisite" SOWs that are crystal clear about scope, deliverables, and timelines significantly reduce client concerns and build trust.
- **Avoiding Contractual Traps:** Be highly wary of agreements that are front-loaded with fees but lack the ability to terminate or recalibrate if the relationship isn't working out. A restrictive time period without an exit clause is a major warning sign.
- **The "Go Slow to Go Fast" Principle:** While understanding the need for initial ramp-up, consultants must build in flexibility and clear checkpoints rather than locking clients into rigid, long-term commitments with no revision ability.
- **Mitigating Imposter Syndrome (The Safety Net):** To overcome fear and self-doubt when starting a new venture, the most effective strategy is to assess your worst-case scenario and establish a strong "Plan B" or safety net. This reduces perceived risk and allows an individual to take the necessary leap of faith.

## Tools & Resources
- **Master's Degree in Public Service:** (University of Arkansas Clinton School of Public Service) – Indicates specialized knowledge for those blending legal/corporate skills with social impact goals.
- **Exquisite Statement of Work (SOW):** Not a tool, but a required deliverable framework that must be used to define scope, deliverables, and timelines precisely.

## Quotable
> "The ones that we love, the ones that we had really... worked out well, had exquisite statements of work or project scopes where we knew exactly what to expect along what timeline with what deliverables."
>
> "Assess what the worst case scenario is and what your safety net or your backup plan looks like."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your SOWs:** Rewrite all current Statements of Work (SOWs) to be hyper-specific, detailing every deliverable, measurable outcome, and clear timeline. Eliminate any vague language.
- [ ] **Implement Exit Clauses:** Structure contracts to ensure that while you are committed, the client (and you) retain the ability to recalibrate or exit if performance metrics are not met, avoiding restrictive front-loaded agreements.
- [ ] **Define Your Safety Net:** Before launching a new venture or pivoting career paths, formally identify your "Plan B" (e.g., consulting for a specific type of firm, returning to a previous industry) to reduce the emotional weight and perceived risk of failure.
- [ ] **Shift Focus from Time/Effort to Outcomes:** When pitching services, focus less on the hours spent or the process followed, and more on the quantifiable, high-value business outcomes you guarantee (e.g., "We will refine your niche resulting in X% fee increase within 90 days").
