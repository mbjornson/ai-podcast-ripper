# Selling Your Consulting Firm: What Buyers Want with David Mann — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
This episode features David Mann, a consultant specializing in evaluating and advising on the sale of professional services firms, particularly within the consulting industry. Mann’s credibility stems from Consulting Success’s track record of building high-performing consulting firms themselves – all coaches have built six, seven, or eight-figure businesses – and their acquisition business. The conversation centers on what buyers look for in a consulting firm beyond just revenue, emphasizing the importance of a scalable business model, repeatable sales processes, and a deep understanding of the target market. A key takeaway is that simply growing revenue isn’t enough; the firm’s structure and team dynamics are equally crucial for attracting buyers and maximizing valuation. The episode also addresses the evolving impact of AI on the consulting landscape and the need for firms to proactively adapt.

## Key Points
- **Minimum Revenue Threshold:** A professional services firm needs at least $10 million in revenue to attract serious acquirers. This level indicates sufficient scale, infrastructure, and reduced reliance on a single individual.
- **Profitability is Paramount:** An EBITDA margin of at least 20% is considered a minimum threshold for a professional services firm, with 50-80% being more desirable. Lower margins raise concerns about the business’s sustainability and value.
- **Recurring Revenue Matters:** While recurring revenue is highly sought after, the episode highlights the importance of understanding the mix of revenue streams – recurring, project-based, or hourly.  A heavy reliance on project-based work is viewed as riskier.
- **Market Understanding is Critical:** Founders often neglect a thorough understanding of their target market. Mann emphasizes the need to “own a niche” and build a concentric circle of expansion, rather than simply chasing a large, undefined market.
- **Team Dependency:** The episode stresses the importance of a diversified team, minimizing reliance on a single individual for key relationships and expertise. A founder who is the primary rainmaker is a significant red flag.
- **Client Concentration Risk:** A single client representing more than 20% of revenue is a cause for concern, as it creates vulnerability and dependence.
- **AI’s Impact:** The rise of AI is changing the landscape, requiring firms to demonstrate a forward-thinking approach and a clear understanding of how technology can enhance their offerings.
