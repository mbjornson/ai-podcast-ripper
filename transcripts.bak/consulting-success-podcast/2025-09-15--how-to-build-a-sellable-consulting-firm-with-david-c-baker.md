# How To Build A Sellable Consulting Firm with David C. Baker — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode centers on the critical challenge faced by consultants: how to build a highly valuable, defensible firm without falling into the trap of being an unspecialized commodity. David C. Baker argues that the single biggest obstacle keeping most experts from achieving true success is the fear of narrowing their focus (generalism). He reframes positioning not merely as a marketing tactic, but as an *ethical obligation* to the client—a commitment to delivering specialized, best-in-class work. The discussion deconstructs the myth that generalists are inherently superior, arguing that while large firms can appear generalist, their success often stems from massive scale or pre-existing networks of trust.

David introduces the concept of building a "sellable" firm, which requires more than just good service; it demands measurable structure and defensible positioning. He outlines a proprietary Sellability Index—a scorecard that measures true business value by focusing on key drivers like profitability and client concentration. The core takeaway is that smaller firms must leverage specialization to build credibility and predictable growth, while large firms achieve generalism through sheer size and history of capability expansion.

## Key Points
- **Positioning as an Ethical Obligation:** Positioning a firm tightly is not just for marketing ease; it's framed as the professional duty to clients to ensure they receive the highest quality, most focused work possible.
- **The Generalist Trap (For Small Firms):** For smaller consulting firms, attempting to be generalists often dilutes expertise and can lead to being perceived by clients as "ripping off" because the firm cannot credibly demonstrate deep mastery across varied fields.
- **Sellability Index:** A framework for measuring a firm's true value in the market. Key drivers that buyers (acquirers) care about include:
    *   Profitability metrics.
    *   Client concentration (the stability and diversity of client relationships).
    *   Defensibility of the niche/expertise.
- **The Scale Difference:** Large firms *can* be generalists, but they typically start as specialists. Their ability to expand capabilities over time is predicated on their initial deep expertise and the trust built with clients who then request additional services. This growth model (specialist $\rightarrow$ large generalist) cannot be replicated by a small firm simply claiming breadth of service.
- **The Confidence/Connection Dichotomy:** Successful generalists often rely heavily on either massive confidence (the "we can do anything" aura) or deep, pre-existing connections that provide initial work flow and trust—resources that are difficult for new firms to replicate.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Niche:** Identify 1-2 specific pain points that your current client base consistently brings up. This is your immediate specialization target.
- [ ] **Develop a "Sellable" Narrative:** Reframe your firm's services to articulate *why* your specialized focus makes you more valuable and defensible than a generalist competitor.
- [ ] **Map Your Network for Trust:** Instead of focusing on cold marketing, identify 3-5 existing relationships (former employers, trusted clients) that can serve as immediate sources of high-trust referrals.
- [ ] **Implement the Sellability Mindset:** When evaluating your own firm's structure, ask: "If I were selling this business today, what metrics would make it look most valuable to an acquirer?" (Focus on repeatable revenue streams and low client concentration risk).
