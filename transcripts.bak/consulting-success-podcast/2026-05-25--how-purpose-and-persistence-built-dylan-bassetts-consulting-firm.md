# How Purpose and Persistence Built Dylan Bassett's Consulting Firm — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode discusses the massive gap between being a competent consultant and successfully building a scalable, profitable consulting business. The guest, Dylan Bassett, details his journey from working in large corporate consulting firms (U.S. Bank, RSM) to founding his own values-driven practice. His story highlights that success is less about initial connections or big networks, and more about the willingness to take unconventional risks, such as cold calling, and maintaining relentless persistence.

Dylan's pivot was driven by a realization that traditional corporate consulting structures—which evaluate performance primarily on billable hours—did not align with his deep personal desire for work to be purposeful and contribute to a greater good. He transitioned from being an associate focused on meeting KPIs to becoming a founder who could control his destiny and build a structured agency, rather than remaining a mere "lifestyle freelancer."

## Key Points
- **The Billable Hour Trap:** Corporate consulting often forces associates to focus on the *number* of billable hours and completing assignments that may not be strategically beneficial or aligned with their true interests. This model can lead to burnout and a feeling of having no leverage over one's own time.
- **The Agency vs. Freelancer Mindset:** A critical distinction for growth is moving beyond the "lifestyle freelancer" model. True business growth requires the goal of becoming an *agency owner*—building a structure that can hire employees and pay them well, allowing the business to scale beyond the founder's personal time.
- **Purpose as the Primary Motivator:** For many, the shift from corporate employment is not solely about money, but about *alignment*. The most successful pivots occur when the work feels intrinsically connected to personal values (e.g., environmentalism, social good), eliminating the "friction or tension" between who you are and what you do.
- **Persistence Over Perfect Tactics:** The key to momentum is not having the perfect marketing strategy or the ideal network. Instead, it is the sustained commitment to showing up and doing the work (like cold calling) even when the results are not immediately visible.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Metrics:** Identify if your current professional success is being measured by billable hours or by genuine impact/value creation. If the former, plan a pivot toward value-based metrics.
- [ ] **Define Your "Why":** Articulate the core values (e.g., environmentalism, social justice) that drive your professional interest. Use this "why" to narrow your niche, even if it feels risky, as this alignment is a powerful magnet for clients.
