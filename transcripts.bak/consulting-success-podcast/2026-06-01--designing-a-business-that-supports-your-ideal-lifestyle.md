# Designing a Business That Supports Your Ideal Lifestyle — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode addresses the critical distinction between merely being a good consultant and intentionally designing a sustainable, high-level consulting business. The speaker argues that most entrepreneurs fall into a pattern of building a business that consumes their time, energy, and health, rather than one that supports their desired life. He uses the metaphor of a "Business and Lifestyle Triangle" to illustrate this imbalance.

The core message is a challenge to the common entrepreneurial mindset of "I'll take care of myself when I hit X revenue." The speaker shares a personal anecdote about a successful mentor whose relentless work ethic was detrimental to his health and family life, leading to a profound realization about true purpose. The goal is to shift the focus from achieving revenue goals to designing a business model that serves the founder's ideal life, thereby achieving freedom and flexibility.

## Key Points
- **The Triangle Flip:** The fundamental shift required is moving the lifestyle to the apex of the triangle, making the business the supporting structure. The business must support the life, not the reverse.
- **The Danger of Delay:** Entrepreneurs often delay self-care, family time, and well-being, believing these things will happen "when things calm down" or "after the next milestone." The speaker argues that these things can be prioritized *today*.
- **The Oxygen Mask Principle:** You must take care of yourself first (the oxygen mask) before you can effectively serve your clients or take care of your family. If you burn out, your ability to provide value collapses.
- **Building for Others:** Many consultants build their businesses to satisfy the demands of clients or external expectations, rather than building them for their own desired life. This defeats the purpose of entrepreneurship.
- **The Immediate Audit:** The most critical action is not just understanding the concept, but auditing your actual time usage. Does your calendar reflect a life supported by a business, or a business supported by a life?

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct a Time Audit:** Look at your calendar from the last week. Categorize every hour spent and determine if that time allocation reflects your desired lifestyle or your business's demands.
- [ ] **Define the Apex:** Write down your "Ideal Week" and "Ideal Day" in granular detail (including gym time, family time, and downtime). This is the non-negotiable structure that your business must support.
- [ ] **Identify "Say No" Opportunities:** List 3-5 clients or types of work that currently consume time but do not align with the ideal lifestyle defined above. Develop a strategy to politely decline or raise the fees for these services.
- [ ] **Reframe the Goal:** Shift your business goal from "Hit the next revenue milestone" to "Design a business that allows me to maintain X amount of free time/energy."
- [ ] **Explore Coaching:** If stuck in the cycle of unpredictable growth, research the "Clarity Coaching" program to learn about building predictable client pipelines.
