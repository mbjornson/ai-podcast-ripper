# How to Launch A New Consulting Offer That Sells — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a structured, seven-step framework for launching a consulting offer that is highly likely to sell. The core thesis is that successful launches today require moving away from the traditional, customized, high-touch consulting model and pivoting toward a highly *productized* offer. The speaker emphasizes that the process must be driven by market validation, not by the founder's assumptions. The entire process is designed to minimize wasted time and capital by ensuring that the offer is solving a problem that the target market is not only aware of but is also willing to pay to solve.

The framework is highly iterative, starting with a hypothesis and moving through validation, outlining, and finally, a structured launch campaign. The speaker uses personal anecdotes—such as a previous business investment of $40,000 that failed because they built too much before testing—to reinforce the critical importance of getting real, paying feedback before committing to development. The ultimate goal of the initial launch is not maximum profit, but rather generating enough data and revenue to optimize and strengthen the offering for future, larger sales.

## Key Points
- **Focus on Productization:** The advice strongly steers away from the "classic, customized, larger consulting engagement" in favor of a clear, productized offer that can be sold efficiently.
- **The 3-Part Hypothesis (Step 1):** Before doing anything else, define: 1) The specific problem you can solve, 2) Who specifically has that problem and is willing to pay for the solution, and 3) What is fundamentally *different* about your solution compared to the status quo.
- **Agitate the Problem, Don't Sell the Solution (Step 2):** Use social media (LinkedIn polls, posts) and emails to agitate the pain point. Instead of saying, "Here is my solution," say, "I've seen many people struggle with X because they try Y, but the real issue is Z."
- **The Low Engagement Signal:** Low interest or little engagement on initial polls/posts is not a failure; it is valuable data indicating that the problem is not a high-priority concern for the market.
- **Outline Before Building:** Once validation is achieved, do not spend months building the full product. Instead, create a detailed *outline* that clearly explains the scope, problems solved, and value created. This outline is what you sell first.
- **True Validation Requires Payment:** The only way to get "real, true validation" is to have people make an investment (paying money or signing a Letter of Intent).
- **The Goal of the First Launch:** The initial launch should not aim to maximize pricing or financial dollars. Its primary goal is to be profitable enough to generate feedback, strengthen, and optimize the offering for subsequent, larger sales.
- **Multi-Touchpoint Campaign:** Treat the launch as a campaign, not a single event. Use multiple channels (email, social, website, calls) and maintain consistent communication to build urgency and scarcity.

## Tools & Resources
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn (for polls and professional engagement), Email Marketing (for structured campaigns).
- **Concept:** Productized Consulting Offer (a defined, repeatable service package).
- **Method:** Hypothesis Testing / Market Validation Framework.

## Quotable
> "Don't look at it as a bad thing [low engagement], because what it means is that something about what you put out there isn't a priority. It doesn't really speak to a meaningful problem that somebody wants to solve."
> "Your goal in that first initial launch should not be to maximize pricing and capturing maximum value in terms of financial dollars. It should be to make it profitable, make money. But also you want to use that to strengthen and optimize and really improve what you've created."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Define the Hypothesis:** Draft a clear statement identifying a specific, painful problem, the ideal client who has it, and the unique mechanism of differentiation from the status quo.
- [ ] **Run Validation Polls:** Create 3-5 pieces of content (LinkedIn posts, emails) designed to *agitate* the problem, rather than presenting the solution.
- [ ] **Draft the Outline:** Once validation is positive, create a detailed, sellable outline of the service/product, avoiding the temptation to build the full product first.
- [ ] **Set Incentive Pricing:** Structure the initial launch with a clear discount or benefit (e.g., "Beta Program Pricing") to create urgency and encourage immediate investment.
- [ ] **Plan the Campaign:** Map out a multi-week launch campaign using at least three different touchpoints (e.g., Week 1: Email announcement; Week 2: LinkedIn case study; Week 3: Direct calls/webinar).
