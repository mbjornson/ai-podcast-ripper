# AI Coding Is Here To Stay (Get Ready) with David Hirschfeld — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode features David Hirschfeld, founder of Techies, a custom software development firm specializing in working with startups and scale-ups. David’s background includes significant experience in the tech sector, notably an early success that exited in 2000 after growing to 800 customers across multiple countries. He also shares lessons learned from a subsequent venture that failed, emphasizing that initial failure often stems not from lack of skill, but from poor business fundamentals—specifically, failing to charge for services immediately and relying on external funding rather than early revenue generation.

David’s core expertise lies in helping businesses scale beyond the limitations of personal referrals. He defines a "scale-up" as an entity that has achieved initial revenue, possesses a clear Ideal Customer Profile (ICP), and maintains a healthy ratio between its cost of sale versus the customer's lifetime value. The discussion pivots to practical business discipline, focusing heavily on how consultants can qualify potential clients early in the sales process to avoid spending time with non-serious buyers or those who lack defined budgets.

## Key Points
- **The Scale-Up Definition:** A scale-up is not just a startup; it must be an entity that has achieved revenue, possesses a clear Ideal Customer Profile (ICP), and generates enough profit margin to reinvest in the business (i.e., LTV/CoS ratio is healthy).
- **The Danger of Non-Charging:** David’s biggest early failure was not charging for his services right away while trying to raise money. He stresses that generating revenue first, even if it makes product-market fit harder initially, provides the necessary leverage and credibility to secure funding later.
- **Qualification by Timeline (The "When" Test):** When speaking with a potential client, if they state they expect to start in the next year or two, do not provide detailed estimates. Instead, advise them that you can only provide an estimate when they have secured both the intention and the budget commitment.
- **Qualification by Budget (The Doubling Technique):** To determine a prospect's actual spending capacity, if they are ready to start immediately but cannot state their budget, use a systematic escalation technique: Start with a low number ($10k) and double it repeatedly ($20k, $40k, $80k, etc.) until the prospect stops you. This reveals their true financial ceiling.
- **Consultative vs. Vendor Relationship:** If a potential client is unwilling to provide a clear budget number after being prompted by the doubling technique, do not proceed with the estimate or proposal. David advises walking away, as this indicates they are looking for a vendor relationship rather than a high-value consultative partnership.
