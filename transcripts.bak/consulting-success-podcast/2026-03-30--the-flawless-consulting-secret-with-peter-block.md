# The Flawless Consulting Secret with Peter Block — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
This episode features Peter Block, an expert with over 40 years of experience, who challenges the conventional understanding of consulting success. Block argues that the modern consulting industry often fails because it focuses too heavily on persuasion and proving results, positioning the consultant as a vendor rather than a true partner. His philosophy centers on shifting the focus from "winning the deal" to helping the client make the *right decision*. Block’s background is defined by building a practice around contribution and trust, rather than aggressive scale or revenue growth, a counter-narrative that is highly valuable for established business owners feeling stuck at a ceiling.

The core of his advice is a fundamental redefinition of authority. Instead of boasting or trying to convince stakeholders, the most powerful move is often one of "indifference"—a quiet confidence that allows the client to feel the truth without the consultant needing to force the outcome. He outlines specific methods for structuring sales conversations that build genuine authority and trust, even when faced with resistance or doubt.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Indifference:** The most effective consulting strategy is not persuasion, but indifference. This means being a presence available and allowing the client to draw their own conclusions, which builds deep trust and authority.
- **The Contribution Question:** To move beyond the vendor trap, consultants must ask the client: "What's your contribution to the very thing you're trying to change?" This shifts the focus from the consultant's expertise to the client's internal capacity for change.
- **Acknowledge Doubt:** When a client questions your fit or results, do not defend yourself. Instead, validate their skepticism by saying, "I understand your doubts." This builds trust and makes the sales call an example of the service you provide.
- **Prioritize Training Over Consulting (Margins):** Block noted a significant operational difference: Consulting businesses often run at 50% labor costs, while training businesses operate with better margins, around 33-35% labor costs. This suggests a structural shift toward educational offerings can improve profitability.
- **Goal is Decision-Making, Not Winning:** The goal of the initial consultation should not be to close the sale or prove your worth, but to help the client or prospective client make the best possible decision for themselves.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Flawless Consulting* (Peter Block)
- **Concept:** The "Contribution Question" framework (What's your contribution to the very thing you're trying to change?)
- **Mindset Shift:** Viewing the sales call as an *example* of the service, rather than a performance.

## Quotable
> "The real power move in consulting isn't persuasion, but indifference."
> "The goal is to help the client or prospective client to make the right decision."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Reframe your sales process. Stop focusing on "winning the business" and start focusing on helping the client achieve clarity and make the right decision, even if that decision is *not* to hire you.
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice asking the "Contribution Question" ("What's your contribution to the very thing you're trying to change?") in every client conversation to immediately elevate the discussion beyond simple problem-solving.
- [ ] **Business Strategy:** Evaluate your service mix. If possible, shift a portion of your revenue stream from high-labor-cost consulting engagements (50% labor cost) toward structured training programs (33-35% labor cost) to improve margins.
- [ ] **Communication:** When faced with client resistance or questions about your track record, do not defend your results. Instead, validate their skepticism by acknowledging their doubts ("I understand your doubts").
