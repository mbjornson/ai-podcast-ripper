# Never Work with These X Types of Clients — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode shifts focus from generic "ideal client" advice to a critical warning: consultants must learn who to actively avoid, as certain client types can drain resources and undermine business growth regardless of the consultant's expertise. The speaker draws on over 20 years of experience working with thousands of consultants globally to identify specific behavioral red flags that signal poor partnership potential.

The discussion outlines eight distinct categories of problematic clients—ranging from those who fail to take action (Action Avoiders) to those who constantly inflate project requirements (Scope Creepers). The core message is that a consultant's success depends not just on their skill, but on their ability to recognize and manage client behavior patterns that threaten profitability and referral generation.

The segment also includes a heavy promotion for the host’s coaching program, Clarity 2.0, which claims to help consultants raise fees and refine niches using an "ROI verified growth system." While promotional, the quantitative claims (e.g., over 80% of clients raising fees or refining their niche within 90 days) provide a measurable outcome hook for the audience.

## Key Points
- **Action Avoiders:** These are highly problematic because while they commit enthusiastically to projects, they consistently fail to follow through on basic agreed-upon tasks or implement recommendations. The speaker notes that without client action/implementation, even perfect consulting work cannot generate real results, leading to a lack of referrals and case studies for the consultant.
- **Scope Creepers:** These clients regularly add requirements ("Could you just add this small little thing?") without acknowledging the need for additional compensation. This behavior is described as "death by a thousand paper cuts," which gradually tanks profitability because saying "no" once trains the client to expect it repeatedly.
- **Respect Deficient Clients:** Characterized by not valuing time, frequently canceling meetings, and expecting immediate responses at all hours of the day. These clients treat the consultant's business as solely serving their needs.
- **Financially Unstable Clients:** Warning signs include discussing cash flow issues from day one or being reluctant to discuss budgets upfront. The primary risk is not that they are "bad people," but that poor financial management leads to non-payment, forcing the consultant to spend time collecting rather than consulting.
- **Perpetual Hagglers:** These clients view expertise as a commodity and focus solely on price rather than value. If this behavior appears during initial communications, it is highly likely to continue throughout the entire engagement.
- **Communication Ghosters & Decision Avoiders:** Both types undermine momentum. Ghosters disappear during critical project phases, while Decision Avoiders require excessive iterations and approvals ("let me think about it," "can we see just one more option"), slowing down progress indefinitely.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement Pre-Payment/Invoice Protocol:** Structure all consulting agreements so that payment is collected *before* or immediately concurrent with work delivery, especially when dealing with clients who show signs of financial instability.
- [ ] **Establish Clear Boundaries on Scope:** When a client requests an addition, immediately pause and frame the conversation around value: "That sounds like a valuable addition; let's scope that out in a separate phase/addendum so we can ensure proper compensation."
- [ ] **Develop Client Vetting Criteria:** Use the eight types of clients discussed (Respect Deficient, Action Avoiders, etc.) as a checklist during initial discovery calls to assess behavioral risk before signing any contract.
- [ ] **Focus on Implementation Metrics:** When pitching services, shift the conversation away from pure strategy and toward measurable implementation plans that require client action and commitment.
