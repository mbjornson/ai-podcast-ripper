# What Actually Works Right Now: Pricing in the Consulting Space — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a critical deep dive into modern pricing strategies for consultants, arguing that traditional fee structures based on effort, time, or deliverables are fundamentally flawed and limit revenue potential. The core thesis is that successful consulting businesses must shift their focus from *process* to *outcome*. The speaker, drawing on data from over 150 consultants, explains that when a client hesitates on price, they are not having a budget issue; they are signaling that the proposed solution has not been elevated to a high enough priority in their internal decision-making process.

The discussion outlines a structured, three-step methodology for implementing value-based pricing. This approach requires the consultant to first quantify the client's problem in monetary terms, then articulate a specific, measurable outcome, and finally, structure the fee so that the investment appears as a clear, high-ROI necessity rather than a cost. The speaker emphasizes that this shift in conversation—from "what we will do" to "what will be different"—is the single most powerful lever for dramatically increasing fees and profitability.

## Key Points
- **The Flaw of Effort-Based Pricing:** Pricing based on inputs (number of meetings, reports, hours) is ineffective because clients are not buying effort; they are buying the resolution of a problem. Defending fees by listing deliverables is arguing about the wrong cost structure.
- **The Priority vs. Resource Issue:** When a client says, "We don't have the budget," the true meaning is, "Your solution is not a high enough priority for us to allocate the money to." The goal is therefore to elevate the priority by articulating value.
- **The Three-Step Value Articulation Framework:**
    1. **Quantify the Problem:** Do not just describe the issue; put a dollar figure on the cost of the problem. (Example: "Your current CAC is 40% higher than the benchmark, costing you $2.3 million per year.")
    2. **Articulate the Specific Outcome:** Focus on the *result*, not the process. (Instead of: "We will conduct a comprehensive analysis," say: "We will reduce your CAC to the industry benchmark, saving you $2.3 million annually.")
    3. **Price Based on Value/ROI:** The price must be framed so that the return on investment (ROI) is immediately clear. If the client saves $2.3 million, a $200,000 fee is presented as a small, necessary investment (a 10x ROI).
- **Actionable Deliverables:** Focus on conciseness. A highly actionable, 25-page report is often more valuable and chargeable than a voluminous, 135-page report that the client will simply file away.
- **The Value Formula:** Premium positioning is achieved by combining: **Problem Cost + Specific Outcome + Value-Based Price = Premium Positioning.**

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice quantifying client problems. For any client, ask: "If we don't solve this, what is the measurable cost to your business in 12 months?"
- [ ] **Business Move:** Revamp your proposal template to lead with the quantified problem cost and the potential outcome value, rather than listing your methodology or deliverables.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Stop thinking about "time spent" and start thinking about "value created." When pricing, calculate the potential ROI for the client, not the cost to your business.
- [ ] **Process Improvement:** Audit your deliverables. Can you condense a large, complex report into a highly actionable, 10-25 page executive summary that forces immediate consumption and action?
- [ ] **Networking/Learning:** Investigate value-based consulting frameworks and consider joining a mastermind group focused on high-ticket sales and positioning.
