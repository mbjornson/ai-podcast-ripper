# How to Start a Consulting Firm From Scratch in 2025 — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a highly structured roadmap for launching or scaling a consulting firm in 2025, emphasizing that success hinges not on expertise alone, but on strategic clarity and market positioning. The speaker draws upon over 20 years of experience building multiple businesses and working with thousands of consultants to distill the process into core considerations. While the episode heavily promotes the "Clarity 2.0" coaching program (which claims high success rates like an 80% client fee increase within 90 days), the primary value lies in Michael’s actionable framework, which guides listeners through defining their niche, proving their results, and structuring a profitable offer.

The core message is that most aspiring consultants fail because they are too broad or lack demonstrable proof of concept. The speaker stresses that before attempting to charge premium rates, one must first build a verifiable track record of solving specific problems for people who not only care about the solution but also have the financial means and desire to invest in it.

## Key Points
- **Laser Focus is Non-Negotiable:** Avoid trying to be "all things to all people." The market is saturated with noise; success requires identifying one specific problem you are world-class at solving, thereby cutting through the clutter for your ideal client.
- **Experience Precedes Consulting:** If you cannot demonstrate a track record of delivering positive outcomes or solving specific problems, you are not ready to be a consultant. Your initial focus must be on building this proof (the "what have you done so far" question).
    *   **How to Gain Experience:** Take on pro bono engagements (nonprofits, board work) or gain corporate experience first before launching your own firm.
- **The Four Pillars of Opportunity:** To build a successful business, you must map out four interconnected elements:
    1.  A specific problem you are very good at solving.
    2.  One specific result/outcome people will want when that problem is solved.
    3.  Who has the problem, wants the result, and can afford to invest in the solution (the paying client).
    4.  The structured process or program (your offer) you will use to deliver the result.
- **Pricing Strategy Warning:** Do not start with advanced models like value-based pricing or ROI pricing. These are complex because early engagements have unknown variables regarding time and scope creep. Start simply with:
    *   Hourly fees.
    *   Project-based rates (with caution, as cost overruns can be dangerous).
    *   Retainer model (recommended for flexibility).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Niche Definition:** Write down 3 specific problems you are genuinely world-class at solving (not just things you *could* solve).
- [ ] **Client Profiling:** Identify the ideal client who has both the problem AND the financial ability/desire to pay for a solution. Map out their budget and pain points.
- [ ] **Experience Building:** If your track record is weak, commit to one pro bono engagement (nonprofit board or volunteer project) within the next 30 days to build verifiable results.
- [ ] **Offer Structuring:** Outline your service delivery as a structured program (e.g., "Audit $\rightarrow$ Workshop $\rightarrow$ Implementation") rather than vague advice. This is your core productized offer.
- [ ] **Initial Pricing Model:** For the first 3 clients, commit to using a retainer or hourly model to minimize financial risk and maximize flexibility while you refine your process.
