# Stop Your Consulting Business From Owning You with Jonathan Bennett — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode addresses the critical tension faced by successful consulting business owners: how to scale rapidly without allowing the business to consume your personal life, identity, or emotional well-being. The discussion centers on moving past the "tyranny of the important and urgent" of high-stakes corporate environments and finding a sustainable model of growth. Jonathan Bennett, who scaled his firm past seven figures, shares experiences of professional setbacks, including painful layoffs and hiring mistakes. Michael, the host, draws on his own experience leaving a high-level corporate role (CCO at a regional hospital) due to exhaustion and the lack of time for family, arguing that the myth of corporate security is false.

The conversation pivots from the initial struggle for work-life balance to the deeper philosophical questions of ownership and control. Both speakers challenge the notion of stability, arguing that true security comes from self-reliance and the ability to control one's destiny. They emphasize that successful entrepreneurship requires not just tools and know-how, but a deep, inherent self-motivation and expertise, warning against the "boosterism" surrounding starting a business.

## Key Points
- **The Myth of Corporate Security:** The belief that a corporate job provides inherent safety is false. Michael asserts that having a business provides a level of security because "No one can ever fire me," offering a sense of control that corporate structures cannot match.
- **The Cost of Corporate Life:** High-level corporate roles, while prestigious, often lead to extreme exhaustion (e.g., 12+ hour days) and a lack of presence in personal life, creating a "tyranny of the important and urgent."
- **The Danger of Emotional Equity:** Jonathan highlights a common business mistake: defaulting to equity (treating a partnership as an emotional or equitable arrangement) when the problem is structural. He learned that emotional attachment can derail a business more profoundly than a financial loss.
- **The Requirement for Entrepreneurship:** Success in business requires more than just tools or knowledge. It demands "deep expertise" and a foundational "risk appetite" and self-motivation that many people lack, regardless of how good the opportunity seems.
- **Scaling Strategy:** Successful scaling involves creating "productized services" that can run effectively without the founder being physically present in the room, thereby protecting the founder's time and identity.
