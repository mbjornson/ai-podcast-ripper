# How To Collab With Your Competition with Liselle Regueiro DeGrave and Rachel Dickman — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode features Liselle Regueiro DeGrave and Rachel Dickman, two successful PR professionals who run separate but highly integrated firms. Their discussion centers on a counterintuitive business model: collaborating with your competition rather than merging or competing directly. Both women detailed their journeys, transitioning from working at large international PR firms and government agencies to establishing independent practices. They found that by maintaining legally distinct corporations while sharing clients and resources, they maximized professional flexibility, minimized risk, and amplified their collective success.

The core of their strategy is built on deep trust and a shared business vision. They established clear agreements for revenue sharing (which can be flexible and not necessarily 50/50) and cost splitting. This structure allows each partner to retain the unique brand identity they built while benefiting from the combined network, expertise, and stability that comes from working with a trusted "tag team."

## Key Points
- **The Power of Separate Entities:** Running separate corporations (e.g., one being 51% owned by one partner, and the other having its own structure) is strategically superior to merging. This approach significantly lowers risk; if one partner decides to leave or pivot away from PR entirely, it does not automatically jeopardize the other's bottom line or business continuity.
- **Cost Allocation:** All costs associated with joint efforts—such as marketing, RFPs (Requests for Proposals), and general business promotion—are split regardless of whether a client is ultimately secured. This treats these expenses as a shared cost of doing business.
- **Client Acquisition Strategy (RFPs):** The majority of high-value work comes from existing relationships within public agencies. To manage the time drain of RFPs, they employ a strategic filter: before committing to a proposal, they first determine if there is an internal connection or mutual acquaintance within the client organization. This prevents chasing every opportunity and ensures focus on viable leads.
- **The Value of Shared History:** The success of this model hinges entirely on knowing your partner intimately (they knew each other for almost 10 years). A shared history, similar work ethic, and complementary strengths are non-negotiable prerequisites for the collaboration to function smoothly.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Review Partnership Agreements:** If considering collaboration with a partner/colleague, ensure all legal agreements explicitly define how costs (marketing, RFPs) are split *regardless* of whether revenue is generated from the effort.
- [ ] **Develop an RFP Vetting System:** Implement a mandatory internal filter for new RFPs. Before dedicating resources to writing a proposal, identify at least one mutual connection or known contact within the client organization to validate the lead's viability.
- [ ] **Audit Business Structure Risk:** Analyze your current business structure (e.g., sole proprietorship vs. LLC/Corp) and consult with legal counsel regarding how maintaining separate corporate entities can mitigate personal risk if a partnership dissolves.
- [ ] **Identify Complementary Strengths:** Map out the unique strengths and weaknesses of potential collaborators to ensure that the relationship is built on complementary skills, not just shared goals.
