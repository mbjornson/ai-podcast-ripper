# Beating Consulting's Feast Or Famine with Carlo Cuesta — Consulting Success Podcast

## Summary
The episode addresses the significant gap between being a skilled consultant and successfully building a scalable, resilient consulting business. The guest, Carlo Cuesta, co-founder of Creation in Common, provides a 24-year case study on how to move beyond the volatile "feast or famine" cycle common in project-based consulting. Cuesta's background is rooted in helping nonprofits and government organizations with strategic planning and community development. His success is built on a model where the majority of new work is derived from deep, long-term relationships rather than cold leads or RFPs.

The core discussion revolves around strategic market expansion and relationship building. Cuesta details how the firm achieved massive growth by consciously diversifying its market—moving from a localized nonprofit focus to include state and local government—because this new sector offered superior margins and larger project sizes. Crucially, he emphasizes that growth is not achieved through direct competition, but by identifying and partnering with complementary firms (like fundraising agencies or project managers) that can serve as entry points into new, high-value markets.

## Key Points
- **The Referral Curve:** For project-based consulting, the critical mass of relationships that generates consistent, reliable work (the "going concern" feeling) typically kicks in around year six or seven, once the initial seeds planted in the early years begin to produce.
- **Strategic Diversification:** When expanding a consulting practice, diversification should be driven by identifying markets with better margins and larger project sizes, not simply by reacting to perceived lack of demand in the existing niche.
- **The Power of Complementary Partnerships:** Instead of approaching new markets via RFPs or direct competition, the most effective method is to build relationships with strategic partners (e.g., fundraising firms, large-scale project managers). These partners need to be in adjacent fields, allowing your services to be presented as a value-add, not a replacement.
- **Building Credibility Over Transactions:** Relationships built for business must first be built for credibility and trust. The goal of initial engagement should be to transcend the transactional nature of the work, allowing both parties to sense and build upon a deeper, non-competitive value alignment.
- **High-Level Growth Metric:** The firm reported that up to 80% of its new work comes from a combined pool of referrals and past clients, indicating a highly mature, relationship-driven business model.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit your current client base:** Identify the 5-10 most valuable past clients and develop a non-sales-related touchpoint plan (e.g., sending a relevant industry report, inviting them to a thought leadership webinar) to keep the relationship warm.
- [ ] **Identify 3 complementary service providers:** List three types of businesses (e.g., legal firms, specialized tech vendors, marketing agencies) that serve your ideal client but do not compete with your core service offering.
- [ ] **Develop a "Partner Value Proposition":** Draft a brief, non-sales pitch for these partners that explains how your service *enhances* their offering, rather than just listing what you do.
- [ ] **Shift focus from "Project" to "Relationship":** When speaking with potential partners, frame the conversation around long-term mutual value and shared vision, rather than immediate revenue opportunities.
