## Key Points
- **Language Acquisition Efficiency:** Learning a language requires high-density practice (immersion) to move from "unconscious incompetence" to "conscious competence." Casual or sporadic practice is ineffective, regardless of the duration (e.g., monthly tennis lessons).
- **Adult vs. Child Language Learning:** Contrary to popular belief, adults can learn languages faster than children because they already possess a foundational layer of abstract labeling and concepts (grammar, counterfactuals) that are necessary for advanced language use. Children lack this conceptual base.
- **The Power of Immersion:** True fluency requires an environment with "no escape," forcing constant engagement with the target language—a condition often missing in structured academic settings.
- **Semantic Drift:** Linguistic concepts are not static. The word "soon" was once the Anglo-Saxon word for *now*. This semantic shift demonstrates how common usage can fundamentally alter a word's meaning and perceived urgency (e.g., the modern use of "literally").
- **Tech Superiority:** WhatsApp is repeatedly cited as an example of a superior, simpler communication system compared to the fragmented mess of multiple inboxes.
