# A Shocking Turn in the War on Men - Richard Reeves - #1087 — Modern Wisdom

## Summary
The episode analyzes the significant, structural shift in the political conversation surrounding men's health, mental wellness, and fatherhood. The guest, Richard Reeves, argues that the issue has moved from a niche, fringe topic to a mainstream policy concern, evidenced by initiatives from major state governors (e.g., Newsom, Whitmer, Wes Moore) and proposed federal legislation (like the Men Matter bill). This shift is partly attributed to the political calculus of the 2024 election, where major Democratic figures realized they were losing young men and needed to address the issue to regain electoral support.

Reeves emphasizes that while the progress is substantial—it is the first time policymakers are making serious, institutional efforts—it is not yet sufficient. He frames the ultimate goal not as winning a culture war, but as making the issue "boring" and "mainstream." This means embedding the concerns into routine, non-controversial government structures (like a permanent commission) so that the issue becomes an accepted, expected part of policy-making, regardless of the current political cycle.

## Key Points
- **The Shift from Fringe to Mainstream:** The discussion about men's issues has moved from being a topic of frustration ("banging my head against the brick wall") to one of serious policy consideration. This is evidenced by multiple state governors launching comprehensive plans for K-12 education, employment, and mental health.
- **The Political Driver:** The current momentum is heavily influenced by the political necessity of winning young men, particularly among the Democratic party. This suggests that while the policy initiatives are real, their initial impetus is strategic electoral gain.
- **The Goal of Institutionalization:** The most significant progress is not the passing of a single bill, but the institutionalization of the issue. The example of Virginia creating a dedicated commission on boys and men (alongside women and girls) is key because it ensures the issue gets a permanent line item in state policymaking, making it difficult to ignore in the future.
- **The Psychology of Activism (Contrarian Take):** Reeves offers a deep psychological insight into why social movements (on both the left and the right) struggle to accept success. Activists can be "psychologically reluctant to succeed" because their identity and purpose become tied to the narrative of failure or ongoing struggle. Accepting a win forces them to find a new, less dramatic purpose.
- **The Accountability Mechanism:** The speaker's role (and his institute's role) is to act as a persistent check on power, holding policymakers accountable for the promises they make, regardless of the political fanfare.

## Tools & Resources
- **Richard Reeves Institute:** The organization associated with the speaker, focused on tracking and advocating for systemic improvements in men's health and societal roles.
- **Men Matter Bill:** A proposed federal bill aimed at creating a national men's health strategy and supporting men's mental health, particularly after fatherhood.
- **Virginia Commission on Boys and Men:** A model for institutionalizing the issue by creating a permanent, state-level commission alongside existing commissions (e.g., women and girls).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Institutionalize Your Focus:** When tackling a major personal or professional goal, aim to embed the solution into a permanent, structural process (a "commission") rather than relying on temporary bursts of motivation or political goodwill.
- [ ] **Develop Accountability Systems:** For any major commitment (business partnership, personal goal, fitness routine), establish a clear, non-negotiable follow-up mechanism to ensure promises are kept and progress is measurable.
- [ ] **Study Policy Models:** Research state or local governments that have successfully institutionalized social issues (e.g., mandatory mental health days, permanent diversity commissions) to understand how to make a cause "boring" and therefore inevitable.
- [ ] **Analyze Your Narrative Identity:** Reflect on how your current professional or personal identity is tied to a struggle or grievance. Be aware of the psychological tendency to resist success, which can prevent you from accepting a win or a major breakthrough.
