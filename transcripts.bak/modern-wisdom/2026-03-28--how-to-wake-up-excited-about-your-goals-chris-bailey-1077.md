# How to Wake Up Excited About Your Goals - Chris Bailey - #1077 — Modern Wisdom

## Summary
The episode analyzes why some personal goals feel effortless while others become mere chores, drawing heavily from academic literature and insights gathered from Buddhist monks regarding intentionality. The core argument is that successful goal attainment is not simply about setting objectives; it requires aligning those objectives with deep-seated, fundamental human values. The discussion introduces a comprehensive framework—the "Intention Stack"—which maps out the hierarchical relationship between daily tasks, short-term goals, long-term priorities, and foundational life values.

The speaker details that motivation is complex, involving factors like procrastination, desire, and social contagion. To demystify this process, he references Shalom Schwartz's research on human values, identifying 12 fundamental motivations (e.g., Security, Self-Direction, Pleasure). The critical takeaway is that understanding the *layers* of intention—from a simple to-do list item up to one's core identity—is the "superpower" for sustained motivation and goal achievement.

## Key Points
- **The Intention Stack:** Motivation operates on a pyramid structure:
    1.  **Present Intentions (Base):** Daily tasks/to-do list items (e.g., tying shoelaces).
    2.  **Plans:** Broader, short-term steps (e.g., going for a run).
    3.  **Goals:** Medium-term objectives (e.g., running a marathon).
    4.  **Priorities/Values (Apex):** The deepest level of intent—the identity you are striving to *be* (e.g., becoming ultra-fit, valuing security).
- **Goal vs. Value Distinction:** Goals are plans for what we want to *do*; Values are intentions for who we intend to *be*. Both function as layers of intention, but linking a goal to a core value exponentially increases motivation.
- **Schwartz's 12 Fundamental Values:** Motivation is rooted in one's unique combination of these deep human values: Self-Direction, Stimulation, Pleasure (Hedonism), Achievement, Power, Curiosity, Face, Security, Tradition, Conformity, Humility, and Universalism/Benevolence.
- **Gendered Value Alignment (Fitness Example):** The value driving a goal can vary by gender. For instance, women may link fitness goals to the value of *pleasure* (feeling better in their bodies), while men might err more toward *security* or *accomplishment*.

## Tools & Resources
- **Shalom Schwartz's Theory of Basic Human Values:** The foundational academic model for understanding human motivation.
- **The Intention Stack Framework:** A conceptual tool/model used to visualize the hierarchy and alignment between daily actions, goals, and core values.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct a Value Audit:** Research Shalom Schwartz's 12 fundamental values. Identify the top 3 values (e.g., Security, Self-Direction) that currently drive your life decisions.
- [ ] **Map Your Goals to Values:** Take an existing goal (e.g., "Start running") and trace it up the Intention Stack until you hit a core value. If the connection feels weak or superficial, the goal may be misaligned.
- [ ] **Define Your Core Identity Intentions:** Instead of setting a task ("I must write 500 words"), frame your intention around who you want to *be* (e.g., "I am becoming a writer who consistently shows up," linking the action to the value of Self-Direction/Achievement).
- [ ] **Review Your To-Do List:** For every item on your daily list, ask: "How does this small task contribute directly to my highest stated value?" If the link is unclear, reconsider or eliminate the task.
