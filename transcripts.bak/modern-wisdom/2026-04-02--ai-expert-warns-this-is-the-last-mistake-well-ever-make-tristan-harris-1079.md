# AI Expert Warns: “This Is The Last Mistake We’ll Ever Make” - Tristan Harris - #1079 — Modern Wisdom

## Summary
Tristan Harris, a former design ethicist at Google and co-founder of the Center for Humane Technology (CHT), details his career journey from observing the early social media boom to warning about modern AI risks. His initial work focused on the "attention arms race," where tech companies exploited human psychological vulnerabilities—a process he argues was not inevitable but the result of specific design choices (e.g., infinite scroll, autoplay). He emphasizes that technology is not neutral; it must be intentionally designed to serve human flourishing and connection.

The discussion pivots sharply from social media ethics to AI, arguing that current large language models represent a fundamentally different technological leap. Unlike previous technologies which were built by adding layers of code (a stack), modern AI is "grown"—it's a digital brain trained on the entire internet. This growth process makes AI an opaque "black box," meaning its capabilities are not fully understood or controllable, even as development accelerates at unprecedented speeds and scales.

Harris warns that the current trajectory is moving toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The sheer scale of investment (trillions of dollars) and infrastructure (data centers the size of Manhattan) combined with the exponential speed of capability growth means humanity is building a system whose full implications—including potential for manipulation or unexpected behavior—we cannot currently predict or control.

## Key Points
- **The Shift from Code to Growth:** Previous technology was coded manually ("line by line"). AI, however, is not coded; it is *grown* (a digital brain trained on the entire internet). This makes its function inherently unpredictable and a "black box."
- **Exponential Speed of Adoption/Capability:** The speed of development is unprecedented. It took two years for Instagram to reach 100 million users, but ChatGPT reached that milestone in just two months. Capabilities are advancing rapidly (e.g., from GPT-3 writing essays to GPT-4 passing the bar exam).
- **The Nature of AGI:** The ultimate goal of major AI labs is not merely "narrow technology" (like a helpful cursor) but Artificial General Intelligence—the ability to perform everything a human mind can do.
- **Design vs. Use:** It is critical to focus attention on *design* choices, not just how people are using the technology. The ethical concern lies in the architecture itself (e.g., autoplay features).
- **The Infrastructure Scale:** AI development requires building massive data centers—some proposed at the size of Manhattan or four times the size of Manhattan Central Park—signaling a level of capital investment unmatched by any previous technological boom.
