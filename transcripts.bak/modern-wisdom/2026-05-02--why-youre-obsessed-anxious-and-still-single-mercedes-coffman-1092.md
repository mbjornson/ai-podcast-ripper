# Why You’re Obsessed, Anxious, And Still Single - Mercedes Coffman - #1092 — Modern Wisdom

## Summary
The episode analyzes the psychological impact of modern dating culture, arguing that the current environment—dominated by dating apps and the pursuit of instant gratification—is fundamentally built for "avoidance" rather than intimacy. This "avoidant culture" rewards novelty and speed (dopamine hits) while penalizing the sustained effort, consistency, and vulnerability required for deep connection. The discussion highlights the inherent conflict between the emotionally available (EA) person, who seeks substance and slow development, and the emotionally unavailable (EU) person, who seeks low-effort comfort.

The core argument is that this misalignment is detrimental to the nervous system. EA individuals are repeatedly subjected to cycles of "love bombing" followed by sudden withdrawal (ghosting or pulling away), leading to chronic stress, "micro grief," and a general erosion of trust in human connection. The speaker proposes that the solution requires a systemic shift: moving beyond surface-level compatibility (shared hobbies, location) and establishing measurable emotional accountability in dating.

## Key Points
- **Avoidant Culture Defined:** It is the tendency to avoid anything that requires too much time, effort, consistency, or causes discomfort. Modern dating apps are designed to reinforce this by prioritizing novelty and disposability.
- **The Nervous System Impact:** Being with an emotionally unavailable person is "terrible for the nervous system." The cycle of intense attachment followed by sudden withdrawal causes cortisol spikes and leads to physical symptoms like fatigue, mood disorders, and sleep disturbances.
- **The Misalignment of Needs:** EA people seek *depth* and *slow burn* development, while the dating app model caters to the *dopamine* of endless options. This creates a structural vulnerability for EA individuals.
- **Emotional Standards vs. Physical Standards:** The most crucial shift is raising standards from physical assets (which are depreciating) to emotional assets. Compatibility must be assessed based on emotional maturity, conflict repair skills, and capacity for sustained effort.
- **Assessing Capacity (The Signal):** Instead of relying on initial chemistry or "potential," one must watch for patterns. Key indicators of emotional capacity include:
    *   How the person reacts when there is no physical reward at the end of the night.
    *   Their patience and emotional regulation when minor inconveniences occur (e.g., slow service from a waiter).
    *   Their ability to discuss intentions and deal with feedback without withdrawing or avoiding.

## Tools & Resources
- **Emotional Availability Score:** The concept of implementing a measurable, public "Emotional Availability Score" (like a Trust Pilot rating) for dating profiles, requiring users to build up their score through demonstrated emotional consistency.
- **Pattern Recognition:** The primary tool for self-improvement is shifting focus from *potential* (what someone *could* be) to *patterns* (how someone *actually* behaves under low-stakes stress).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice observing "patterns over potential." When interacting with people (friends, colleagues, dates), actively observe how they handle minor inconveniences or situations where they receive no immediate reward.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Re-evaluate your dating standards. Prioritize emotional capacity, conflict repair skills, and stated intentions over physical attraction, shared hobbies, or convenience.
- [ ] **Self-Assessment:** Identify your own emotional triggers and patterns of attachment. Recognize when you are seeking the dopamine hit of novelty versus the stability of consistency.
- [ ] **Health Practice:** Be aware of the signs of "micro grief" (sudden, intense emotional dips following a period of high excitement/attachment) and recognize that these are stress responses (cortisol spikes) requiring self-regulation.
