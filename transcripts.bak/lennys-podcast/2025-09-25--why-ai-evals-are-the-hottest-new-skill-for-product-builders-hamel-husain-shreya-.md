# Why AI evals are the hottest new skill for product builders | Hamel Husain & Shreya Shankar (creators of the #1 eval course) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode establishes that mastering AI evaluations ("Evals") is currently the highest ROI skill for product builders working with LLMs, moving it from an obscure topic to a critical industry necessity. Evals are defined as systematic methods to measure and improve AI applications, providing data-driven metrics that replace unreliable "vibe checks" and guesswork in complex, stochastic systems. The discussion emphasizes that while unit tests check non-negotiable functionalities, true evals require deep data analysis to assess performance across ambiguous tasks, new user cohorts, and overall product quality over time.

The guests, Hamil Hussain and Shreya Shankar, are positioned as definitive authorities on the subject. They have played a major role in popularizing Evals, having taught thousands of PMs and engineers across major AI labs (including OpenAI and Anthropic). Their background provides credibility by offering practical frameworks to demystify the process, moving it from an intimidating technical challenge into a manageable product development cycle.

## Key Points
- **Evals are not just tests:** The common misconception is that Evals are simply writing unit tests for code. In reality, they start with *data analysis* to ground what should be tested, especially because LLMs operate in a highly stochastic (non-linear) environment.
- **The scope of Evals is vast:** Beyond checking specific functions (like booking an appointment), evals must measure ambiguous performance—such as how well the AI handles new types of user requests or identifies entirely new user cohorts that were not anticipated during development.
- **Avoid "Committee Paralysis":** When building evals, do not let a committee make the decision; this process can become prohibitively expensive and slow. Instead, appoint one person with deep domain expertise (often the Product Manager) to act as the trusted authority ("benevolent dictator").
- **The Goal is Actionable Improvement:** The purpose of running an eval is not perfection or achieving perfect scores; it is to generate a clear feedback signal that allows the team to iterate and improve the product with confidence.
- **Unit Tests vs. Evals:** Unit tests are ideal for checking non-negotiable, core functionalities. Evals encompass this but also include tracking metrics over time (e.g., user satisfaction scores like "thumbs up") and analyzing data distributions to find new opportunities.
