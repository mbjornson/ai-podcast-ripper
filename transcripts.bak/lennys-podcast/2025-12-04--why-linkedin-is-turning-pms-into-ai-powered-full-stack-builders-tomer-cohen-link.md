# Why LinkedIn is turning PMs into AI-powered "full stack builders” | Tomer Cohen (LinkedIn CPO) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode details the "Full Stack Builder" model, a radical new approach to product development being piloted at LinkedIn. The core thesis is that the traditional product development lifecycle has become unnecessarily complex, bloated by layers of sub-specialization and process steps. This complexity, while rational in its own right, slows down iteration and launch speed. The goal of the Full Stack Builder model is to collapse this bloated process back to first principles, enabling *anyone*—regardless of their formal function (PM, Engineer, Designer)—to take an idea from concept to market launch.

Tomer Cohen, LinkedIn's CPO, argues that the future of work demands a shift toward "meritocracy" enabled by AI. This model is not just about giving people new tools; it requires a fundamental change in organizational incentives and processes. He emphasizes that the challenge is not technical, but one of change management—companies must build the motivation and structure to allow individuals to operate as empowered, cross-functional builders, leveraging AI as a fluid interaction partner rather than just a tool.

## Key Points
- **The 70% Skill Shift:** By 2030, the skills required for a job will change by 70%. This is not a gradual evolution; it is a "traumatic" shift requiring individuals and organizations to fundamentally reimagine their roles and processes to stay competitive.
- **Process Bloat vs. Work Complexity:** The actual work of building a product (research, spec, design, code, launch, iterate) is simple. However, at scale, the *process* surrounding that work became exponentially complex, leading to multiple sub-steps (e.g., 10-15 sources of research, multiple review types) and subsequent organizational bloat.
- **The Full Stack Builder Model:** This model is a direct countermeasure to process bloat. It empowers generalists to operate across the entire stack, enabling rapid iteration and deployment by minimizing functional silos and maximizing the human-AI partnership.
- **Change Management is Paramount:** Simply rolling out AI agents or tools is insufficient. Successful adoption requires building the necessary incentives, motivation, and cultural examples to encourage employees to operate in this new, fluid, cross-functional manner.
- **The Builder's Mandate:** The core function of a builder remains simple: take an idea and bring it to life. The focus must return to this core principle, bypassing the bureaucratic complexity that has accumulated around it.

## Tools & Resources

- **Vanta:** A trust management platform that helps companies achieve and maintain compliance (SOC 2, ISO 27001) quickly, automating the process of completing security questionnaires.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Adopt a "First Principles" Mindset:** When tackling any project (personal or professional), strip away all established, complex processes. Ask: What is the absolute simplest, most direct path from idea to launch?
- [ ] **Practice Cross-Functional Generalism:** Actively seek out projects that force you to operate outside your defined job title. If you are a PM, learn basic prototyping/coding; if you are a designer, understand the data pipeline. Become a "T-shaped" builder.
- [ ] **Master Rapid Prototyping Tools:** Experiment with tools like Figma Make or similar low-code/no-code platforms to validate ideas *before* involving multiple teams or complex engineering cycles.
- [ ] **Audit Your Own Process:** Identify the most complex, multi-step process in your current role. Challenge every sub-step and ask if it is truly necessary, or if it is merely a historical artifact of organizational bloat.
- [ ] **Focus on Incentives, Not Just Tools:** If leading a team, remember that providing AI tools is only half the battle. You must actively build the cultural incentives and motivation for people to use those tools in new, cross-functional ways.
