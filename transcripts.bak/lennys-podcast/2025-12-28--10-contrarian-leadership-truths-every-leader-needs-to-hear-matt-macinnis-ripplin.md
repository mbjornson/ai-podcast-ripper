# 10 contrarian leadership truths every leader needs to hear | Matt MacInnis (Rippling) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
Matt MacInnis, Chief Product Officer and former long-time Chief Operating Officer at Rippling (a company valued at over $16 billion), discusses the intense, often uncomfortable realities of achieving extraordinary business outcomes. His core thesis is that exceptional results demand exceptional, relentless effort, and that complacency is the single greatest threat to a high-growth organization. MacInnis emphasizes that leadership must actively counteract the natural tendency of teams to optimize for local comfort rather than company-wide goals.

He provides a brutally honest view of management, warning that the intensity of a company naturally degrades with each layer of management beyond the founder CEO. MacInnis argues that leaders must maintain a "death march" pace—a state of constant, high-stakes pressure—because in competitive markets, any perceived break or crack will be immediately exploited by rivals. The advice is less about work-life balance and more about maintaining organizational velocity and focus.

## Key Points
- **Extraordinary Effort is Non-Negotiable:** Achieving 99th percentile outcomes is inherently difficult and must be "freaking exhausting." If a team finds itself in a comfort zone, it is a sign of failure.
- **The Danger of Complacency:** In competitive markets, there can be no relaxation. If you leave a crack for a competitor, they will fill it. The pace must be relentless.
- **The Selfishness of Withholding Feedback:** The most fundamentally selfish act a leader can commit is to withhold feedback from a team member because it would make the leader uncomfortable.
- **Overstaffing is "Poison":** Overstaffing projects leads to politics, wasted effort, and "cruft," slowing the company down.
- **Intensity Drop-Off:** The potential for intensity drops by an order of magnitude with every concentric circle of management beyond the founder CEO. Leaders must actively preserve this high level of energy.
- **The Counter-Intuitive Nature of Focus:** Giving a team too much space or time to "twiddle their thumbs" can actually cause morale to dip and focus to decline.
- **Managing the Guess:** Leaders should not aim to get decisions (budget, staffing, deadlines) perfectly right upfront. Instead, they must make their best guess and manage the process iteratively, learning and adjusting as they go.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Feedback Discipline:** Make it a non-negotiable habit to deliver difficult, constructive feedback immediately, even if it causes personal discomfort.
- [ ] **Audit Team Capacity:** Review current projects and identify areas of potential overstaffing. Challenge the necessity of every role to eliminate "cruft" and political waste.
- [ ] **Implement High-Intensity Cycles:** Structure work periods (sprints, quarters) with a "death march" mentality, ensuring the team feels the urgency and the direct link between effort and extraordinary outcome.
- [ ] **Adopt Iterative Decision-Making:** When faced with major resource allocation (budget, staffing, deadlines), treat the initial plan as a hypothesis. Focus on managing the *learning curve* rather than achieving initial perfection.
- [ ] **Utilize Product Analytics:** If in a tech role, implement or deepen the use of tools like Datadog/Epo to move beyond vanity metrics and diagnose the root cause of funnel drop-offs and UX friction.
