# The art of influence: The single most important skill that AI can’t replace | Jessica Fain (Webflow, ex-Slack) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode with Jessica Fain, a former product leader at Box, Slack, and now Webflow, focuses on the critical skill of influence, particularly when interacting with executives. Fain argues that influence is arguably the most leveraged skill for product leaders outside of AI, and that many product managers fail to understand how executives make decisions due to the “strobe light” nature of their calendars and the executive’s focus on global optimization rather than individual needs. The conversation is highly tactical, delving into specific behaviors and mindset shifts needed to effectively influence leaders. Fain’s credibility stems from her extensive experience leading product teams at major tech companies and her direct experience as a chief of staff to CPOs at Slack and Atlassian.

## Key Points
- **Executive Calendars as “Strobe Lights”:** Executives operate under immense pressure, juggling numerous urgent priorities, making it difficult for product managers to gain their attention and frame their ideas effectively.
- **Understanding Executive Incentives:** Product managers must understand the executive’s goals, metrics, and constraints to connect their ideas to the executive’s success. It’s not enough to simply present a good idea; it must align with the executive’s priorities.
- **The Importance of Curiosity & Empathy:**  Product managers need to actively cultivate curiosity and empathy for the executive’s perspective, recognizing their limited bandwidth and the context-switching demands of their role.
- **Kill or Deprioritize:**  Building trust with executives involves being willing to kill or deprioritize ideas that don’t align with their strategic goals. This demonstrates a deep understanding of the executive’s priorities.
- **Communication Chameleon:** Product managers must adapt their communication style to match the executive’s preferences – understanding what types of information (design, customer stories, dashboards) resonate most effectively.
- **It’s Your Fault If They Don’t Buy In:**  Product managers shouldn’t blame executives for rejecting ideas if they haven’t effectively communicated the value and relevance of those ideas.

## Action Items
- **Develop Executive Communication Skills:** Practice framing ideas in terms of executive goals and metrics.  Focus on how your work contributes to their success.
- **Understand Executive Priorities:**  Before pitching an idea, deeply investigate the executive’s priorities, constraints, and decision-making process.
- **Embrace Strategic Killing:**  Be willing to deprioritize or kill ideas that don’t align with the executive’s strategic goals – it demonstrates respect and understanding.
- **Build a Communication Style “Chameleon”:** Adapt your communication style to resonate with the executive’s preferences (e.g., data-driven, story-based, visual).
- **Read:** *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* by Robert Cialdini – For a foundational understanding of persuasion techniques.
