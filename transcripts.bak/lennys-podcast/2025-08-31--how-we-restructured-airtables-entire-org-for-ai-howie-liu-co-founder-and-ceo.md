# How we restructured Airtable’s entire org for AI | Howie Liu (co-founder and CEO) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode features Howie Liu, co-founder and CEO of Airtable, discussing how large, established tech companies must reinvent their core products in the face of rapid AI evolution. The discussion centers on the existential transition facing SaaS businesses: moving from a period where technical innovation was the primary value driver to one that requires constant adaptation to new technological paradigms. Liu emphasizes that true product value is rooted in intimate architectural and design decisions, not just process scaling or "blunt instruments."

A major theme is the shift in leadership roles, which he terms becoming an Individual Contributor CEO (ICCO). This involves founders getting hands-on with coding, architecture, and core product development again. Furthermore, Liu details how Airtable restructured its organization into distinct "fast-thinking" and "slow-thinking" groups to accelerate AI investments and maintain agility while scaling a decade-old platform.

## Key Points
- **The ICCO Model (Individual Contributor CEO):** In the current tech climate, CEOs must transition back into hands-on product roles—getting their hands dirty, building code, and leading initiatives themselves. This suggests that high-level strategic thinking is insufficient without deep technical involvement.
- **Product Value Proposition:** For pure software platforms like Airtable, the technology *is* the value proposition. Early success relied on intimate design decisions (both architectural and UX) that cannot be separated from the product itself; treating tech as an afterthought limits potential.
- **Organizational Structure for AI Acceleration:** To manage massive technological shifts, organizations should restructure into specialized groups (e.g., "fast-thinking" vs. "slow-thinking"). This allows for focused, rapid experimentation and investment in new areas without disrupting the core business stability.
- **Hybrid Skill Requirement:** Product leaders (PMs) must evolve beyond traditional roles to become hybrid prototypers with strong design sensibilities. The most successful individuals are those who can cross over skills between PM, engineering, and design.
- **Market Skepticism:** Be highly skeptical of viral market narratives ("X is dead"). Hype often spreads via "memes and orality" (social media virality) rather than substantiated data.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop T-Shaped Skills:** Actively seek opportunities to bridge functional gaps (e.g., if you are a PM, learn basic prototyping/design principles; if you are an engineer, understand the UX implications of your code).
- [ ] **Embrace the IC Mindset:** If in a leadership role, dedicate time weekly to hands-on product work (coding, deep architectural review, rapid prototyping) rather than solely focusing on meetings and process management.
- [ ] **Audit Your Tech Stack for AI Value:** Implement developer intelligence tools (like DX) or conduct internal audits to move beyond anecdotal claims of "AI adoption" and measure the *actual* value and impact of AI investments on engineering productivity.
