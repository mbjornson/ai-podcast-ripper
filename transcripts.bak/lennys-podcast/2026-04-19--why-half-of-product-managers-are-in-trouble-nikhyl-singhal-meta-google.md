# Why half of product managers are in trouble | Nikhyl Singhal (Meta, Google) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode with Nikkel Singhal explores the significant shifts occurring within the product management landscape, driven by technological advancements and evolving industry demands. The core argument is that the traditional role of the information mover is becoming obsolete, replaced by a need for builders who can leverage new tools, particularly AI. Singhal argues that the industry is currently experiencing a “renaissance” for builders, with increased compensation and opportunities, but this comes with considerable stress and fatigue due to rapid change and the demands of a hyper-alert, constantly evolving environment. He highlights a generational stress point, particularly for those in their 30s, juggling career ambitions with personal responsibilities. The episode paints a picture of a stressed, yet potentially rewarding, future for product managers who can adapt and embrace new approaches.

## Key Points
- **The “Information Mover” is Obsolete:** The traditional product manager role of simply moving information between teams is becoming irrelevant due to automation and increased speed of change. This is a major shift in required skills.
- **AI is a Game Changer:** The rise of AI is empowering builders to have more direct impact and control over their products, but also creating a sense of urgency to stay ahead of the curve.
- **Industry Stress & Fatigue:** The industry is experiencing a high level of stress and fatigue, driven by rapid technological advancements, constant change, and the pressure to remain relevant. This is particularly pronounced among those balancing career and personal responsibilities.
- **Renaissance for Builders:** Despite the stress, there's a “renaissance” for product builders, with increased compensation, more opportunities, and a greater sense of autonomy.
- **Generational Stress:** The pressures on those in their 30s are particularly acute, as they navigate career ambitions alongside personal commitments like family and health.
- **Shifting Responsibilities:** The industry is moving away from responsibility without authority, and towards a more direct connection between product instincts and customer impact.
