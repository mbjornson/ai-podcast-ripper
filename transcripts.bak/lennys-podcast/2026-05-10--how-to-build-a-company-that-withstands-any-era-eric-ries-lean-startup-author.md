# How to build a company that withstands any era | Eric Ries, Lean Startup author — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode features Eric Ries, author of “The Lean Startup,” discussing his new book, “Incorruptible,” which focuses on how companies can protect themselves from internal corruption and maintain long-term success. Ries argues that a company’s success can ironically become its greatest liability, leading to complacency, a loss of focus, and ultimately, decline. The core of the book is a collection of anecdotes and observations about how companies, often after achieving significant success, succumb to this “corruption.” Ries emphasizes the importance of principled decision-making and resisting the pressure to prioritize short-term profits over long-term values. He highlights the danger of assuming success guarantees continued success and the need to maintain a scientific mindset – constantly testing assumptions and iterating. The episode also touches on the relevance of Lean Startup principles in the current AI landscape, with examples of successful AI companies operating in a similar iterative, MVP-focused manner.

## Key Points
- **The “Force That No One Controls, But Everyone Obeyes”:** Ries identifies a pervasive force that corrupts organizations – a tendency to prioritize short-term gains over long-term principles, leading to mediocrity and eventual decline. This isn’t necessarily about malice, but a natural consequence of success.
- **Success as a Liability:** The episode’s central argument is that a company’s success creates a powerful incentive to do *less* – to cut corners, to prioritize immediate profits over sustainable growth, and to lose sight of its original mission.
- **Financial Gravity:** Ries uses the analogy of a collapsing bridge to illustrate this point – if you don’t understand *why* something failed, you’re doomed to repeat the mistake. It’s about recognizing the underlying forces at play and proactively addressing them.
- **Lean Startup’s Relevance to AI:** Despite the evolution of the startup landscape, Ries argues that Lean Startup principles – particularly the iterative MVP approach – are still highly relevant, as exemplified by the success of companies like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Cloud Code.
- **The Importance of Experimentation & Scientific Method:** Ries stresses the need to maintain a scientific mindset, constantly testing assumptions and learning from failures, rather than blindly following conventional wisdom.
- **Protecting Against “Ownership Structure Corruption”:** Ries illustrates how private equity and other external pressures can corrupt a company, leading to a decline in quality and customer satisfaction. He uses the example of vital eggs, owned by BlackRock, as a cautionary tale.
