# The non-technical PM’s guide to building with Cursor | Zevi Arnovitz (Meta) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode provides a highly actionable guide for non-technical Product Managers (PMs) on leveraging advanced AI coding tools to build and iterate on complex products. The guest, Zevi Arnovitz (PM at Meta, formerly at Wix), is a non-technical individual who successfully built significant products using AI, demonstrating that technical background is no longer a prerequisite for product ownership. The core thesis is that the role of the PM is shifting from merely defining requirements to becoming a "builder" who orchestrates AI agents.

Zevi details a sophisticated workflow that moves beyond simply asking AI to write code. He emphasizes that the real challenge—and the key to success—is learning how to *review* and *challenge* the code generated by AI. He introduces specific prompting techniques, such as creating a "CTO" persona, to force the AI to act as a critical technical owner, thereby mitigating the common pitfalls of AI being too agreeable or "people-pleasing."

## Key Points
- **The Collapse of Titles:** The traditional roles and titles of PMs and engineers are expected to collapse. The new core competency is being a "builder"—someone who can effectively orchestrate AI tools to execute ideas.
- **The Critical Skill is Reviewing:** The most difficult skill for non-technical PMs is not generating ideas, but reviewing the technical code written by AI. The solution is to use multiple LLMs (e.g., having Claude and Codex review the same code) to cross-validate and catch mistakes.
- **Compartmentalize Context:** Use AI "Projects" (like GPT Projects or Claude Projects) to compartmentalize work. This prevents the AI's memory from mixing unrelated contexts (e.g., mixing running goals with product strategy), which leads to irrelevant and confusing outputs.
- **The "CTO" Prompting Framework:** Instead of treating the AI as a helpful assistant, prompt it to adopt the persona of a highly critical, challenging CTO. This custom prompt forces the AI to challenge the user's assumptions and the technical feasibility of the plan, preventing the "people-pleasing" syndrome common in general-purpose LLMs.
- **The Junior Advantage:** Contrary to common fears, the current AI era is the best time to be a junior professional. It provides unprecedented opportunities to build and launch a startup independently without needing years of experience.

## Tools & Resources
- **AI Coding Tools:** Cursor, Claude, GPT-4 (for projects/custom instructions).
- **Project Management/Workflow:** GPT Projects, Claude Projects (for context compartmentalization).
- **Productivity/Integration:** Linear (for adding ideas and tracking progress), Bolt, Lovable (examples of AI-built platforms).
- **Learning/Development:** The speaker's newsletter (lennysnewsletter.com) offers access to 19 premium products (e.g., Lovable, Replit, Bold, Gamma, N8N, Linear, Devin).

## Quotable
> "It's not that you will be replaced by AI. You'll be replaced by someone who's better at using AI than you."
> "If people walk away and open their computer and start building, you've succeeded."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop the "CTO" Prompt:** Create a custom prompt for your primary LLM that forces it to act as a critical, challenging technical owner, specifically instructing it *not* to be a people-pleaser.
- [ ] **Implement Context Compartmentalization:** Start using dedicated "Projects" (GPT/Claude Projects) for distinct work streams (e.g., "Product X Strategy" vs. "Personal Fitness Goals") to keep AI memory clean and focused.
- [ ] **Adopt a Multi-LLM Review Loop:** When generating code or complex plans, do not rely on a single AI output. Force two or more different LLMs (e.g., Claude and ChatGPT) to review the same output to identify blind spots and bugs.
- [ ] **Build a Minimum Viable Workflow:** Use AI tools to connect your idea generation (e.g., brainstorming in a chat) directly to a project tracking tool like Linear, simulating a full product lifecycle from concept to plan.
