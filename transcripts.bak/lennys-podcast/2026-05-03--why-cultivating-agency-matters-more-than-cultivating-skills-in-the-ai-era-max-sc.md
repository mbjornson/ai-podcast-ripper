# Why cultivating agency matters more than cultivating skills in the AI era | Max Schoening (Head of Product, Notion) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode features Max Schoening, Head of Product at Notion, discussing the increasing importance of “agency” over traditional skills in the age of AI. Schoening argues that the ability to proactively shape and influence outcomes, rather than simply possessing technical expertise, will be a key differentiator for success. He highlights a shift in product development, with designers and PMs increasingly involved in coding and prototyping, driven by the capabilities of AI tools. The conversation centers around the idea that the static image of a "chat" interface (represented by Brett Victor’s talk) is outdated and that a more fluid, AI-integrated approach is necessary. Schoening emphasizes the need for individuals to deeply understand the “material” they’re designing with, using code as a means to achieve this. His background – spanning Google, Heroku, and GitHub – lends significant credibility to his perspective, particularly his experience with leading design and product teams.

## Key Points
- **Agency over Skills:** The core argument is that in an era of rapidly advancing AI, the ability to *act* and *influence* is more valuable than simply possessing specific technical skills. Schoening believes agency is unevenly distributed and that cultivating it is crucial for success.
- **AI-Enabled Prototyping:** Designers and PMs are increasingly involved in coding and prototyping, driven by AI tools like LLMs. This isn’t about shipping code to production, but about rapid experimentation and understanding the capabilities of these tools.
- **The “Dead Fish” Critique:** Schoening references Brett Victor’s “Stop Drawing Dead Fish” talk, arguing that static interfaces (like traditional chat windows) are obsolete and that designers need to engage with AI in a more dynamic way.
- **Notion’s Experimentation:** Notion’s internal experimentation with “playground” codebases, designed to be LLM-friendly, illustrates this shift. They’ve created a space for rapid prototyping and understanding AI capabilities.
- **Blurred Roles:** The traditional boundaries between design, product management, and engineering are blurring, with individuals taking on multiple roles and responsibilities.
- **Material Understanding:** Schoening stresses the importance of understanding the “material” – in this case, code – that designers and PMs are working with, arguing that this understanding is more valuable than simply knowing how to tweak UI elements.
- **Agency as a Competitive Advantage:** Individuals with agency – those who can proactively shape outcomes – will thrive in the new AI landscape, while those stuck in rigid roles will fall behind.
