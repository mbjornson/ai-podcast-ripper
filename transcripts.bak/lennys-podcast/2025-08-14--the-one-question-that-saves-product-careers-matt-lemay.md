# The one question that saves product careers | Matt LeMay — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode addresses a critical survival skill for product managers: shifting from executing "work around work" to driving measurable, top-line business impact. The discussion is framed by the current tech climate, characterized by layoffs and cost-cutting measures (citing Daniel Ek's 2024 Spotify message), which signal that companies are no longer funding teams simply because they exist. Matt LeMay argues that product managers must proactively understand and articulate how their work directly contributes to business-critical outcomes—a necessity for career stability and organizational relevance.

Matt LeMay, a seasoned product leader and author of *Product Management in Practice*, brings deep credibility through his consulting experience with hundreds of product teams. His new book, *Impact-First Product Teams*, formalizes the process by which individual product groups can align their efforts to measurable business goals, regardless of internal organizational chaos or lack of executive clarity. The core message is that impact must be quantifiable and understandable not just by PMs, but by the CEO and CFO.

## Key Points
- **The "Work Around Work" Trap:** Many teams fall into a low-impact death spiral, accumulating cosmetic improvements and supporting features without linking them to critical business outcomes. This work is viewed as non-essential during economic downturns.
- **Impact Must Be Top-Down (and Bottom-Up):** Product goals cannot be allowed to get "cascaded into oblivion." Every team's OKR must have a clear, direct line of sight to the company's primary revenue or growth objectives. If you can't explain the business value to a CFO, your work is at risk.
- **The Value Proposition:** In today’s cost-conscious environment, being valuable means proving that your team is a good investment. The ability to understand and articulate this direct financial contribution is now an incumbent requirement for every PM.
- **Resume Impact Quantification:** When updating professional materials (resumes), do not use passive language like "I helped" or "I was involved in." Instead, state the quantifiable outcome: "Drove X% increase in Y metric by implementing Z feature."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit OKRs:** Review all current team Objectives and Key Results (OKRs). For every goal, ask: "How does this directly move a revenue or cost metric that the CFO cares about?" If the link is vague, refine the goal.
- [ ] **Revamp Resume Language:** Rewrite your professional resume to eliminate passive language ("assisted," "helped"). Every bullet point must follow the formula: *Action Verb* + *What you did* + *Quantifiable Result (Number/Percentage)*.
- [ ] **Practice Business Articulation:** Prepare a 60-second pitch for your current product initiative that focuses exclusively on financial impact, not technical features. (e.g., "This feature will reduce churn by X%," rather than "This feature improves UX.")
- [ ] **Read/Study:** Review *Impact-First Product Teams* to formalize the steps of aligning individual work with business outcomes.
