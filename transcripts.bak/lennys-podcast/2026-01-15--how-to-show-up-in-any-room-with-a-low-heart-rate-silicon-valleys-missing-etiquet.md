# How to show up in any room with a low heart rate: Silicon Valley’s missing etiquette playbook | Sam Lessin — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode argues that proper etiquette is not a soft skill, but a critical, often overlooked, professional competency for founders and tech professionals. Sam Lessin, a partner at Slow Ventures, former VP of Product at Facebook, and two-time founder, frames etiquette as a strategic tool for building trust and making high-stakes connections. The core thesis is that as technology becomes increasingly commoditized, the ability to navigate social situations with grace, confidence, and calm is what differentiates successful founders from the rest.

Lessin challenges the prevailing Silicon Valley narrative that "only the product matters." Instead, he posits that mastering social interactions—from initial introductions to complex meetings—allows you to show up in a room with a "low heart rate." This state of calm confidence, rooted in an abundance mindset rather than scarcity, is key to projecting reliability and making people feel comfortable enough to do business with you.

## Key Points
- **The "Low Heart Rate" Mindset:** The goal of good etiquette is to show up in a room with a low heart rate. This means projecting self-confidence and the calm of abundance, rather than the high-intensity, desperate energy of someone in a "scarcity" mindset.
- **Trust is the New Differentiation:** In an era where software is commoditized and AI is rapidly changing the landscape, human trust and the ability to build relationships are the most valuable differentiators. Etiquette provides the framework for building that trust.
- **The Danger of the "Energizer Bunny" Vibe:** Over-eagerness, desperation, or high-intensity pitching (the "energizer bunny" vibe) will scare off potential partners or investors. You must project that this opportunity is valuable, but not your *only* opportunity.
- **Etiquette as a Business Tool:** The skills learned—such as proper introductions, small talk, and meeting etiquette—are not merely social niceties; they are tools that can be the difference between successfully closing a deal and missing a relationship opportunity.
- **The "Be Early" Rule:** A simple, actionable tip highlighted is the importance of being early for meetings and events, which signals professionalism and respect for others' time.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** Sam Lessin's book on proper etiquette (Recommended for deep dives into specific social categories).
- **Firm/Network:** Slow Ventures (Sam Lessin's current venture partner role).
- **Concept:** The "Low Heart Rate" framework (A mental model for approaching high-stakes social interactions).

## Quotable
> "Etiquette is a skill for how to show up in a room with a low heart rate."
> "If you show up like a little energizer bunny, you're going to scare one off."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** When entering a high-stakes meeting or networking event, consciously shift your internal narrative from "I need this deal" (scarcity) to "I have valuable things to share and learn" (abundance).
- [ ] **Practice Calmness:** Before any major meeting, take a moment to regulate your physical state. Focus on slowing your breathing and projecting calm confidence rather than frantic enthusiasm.
- [ ] **Study the Rules:** Buy and study Sam Lessin's book on etiquette to gain structured knowledge across different social categories (introductions, meals, meetings).
- [ ] **Be Early:** Make it a non-negotiable habit to arrive 5-10 minutes early for all professional commitments to signal respect and preparedness.
- [ ] **Focus on Connection, Not Collection:** When networking, shift your goal from "collecting business cards" to "building a relationship" by genuinely giving more value than you take.
