# What world-class GTM looks like in 2026 | Jeanne DeWitt Grosser (Vercel, Stripe, Google) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the evolution of Go-To-Market (GTM) strategy, arguing that GTM is no longer confined to sales and marketing, but must encompass the entire customer lifecycle—from initial awareness through to high Lifetime Value (LTV) support. Jeanne DeWitt Grosser, COO at Vercel and former CBO at Stripe, establishes her credibility by detailing her experience building world-class GTM functions at multiple unicorn companies. She frames GTM as an integrated, holistic process that must be treated like a product itself.

The core thesis is that the hyper-specialization of GTM roles is collapsing. Instead, organizations must build a unified, consultative approach that maps the customer journey end-to-end. The discussion emphasizes that modern GTM requires deep technical integration (via roles like the GTM Engineer) and must leverage AI to scale personalized, relationship-based experiences, moving far beyond simple transactional sales.

## Key Points
- **GTM is the Full Customer Lifecycle:** GTM is defined as *any function that will touch a customer or make a dollar*. This includes marketing, sales, customer success, support, and partnerships, requiring a highly integrated, "Venn diagram" approach rather than siloed functions.
- **Shift from Pain Points to Differentiation:** Instead of focusing on the "pain and problem" being solved, founders should focus on *how they will be better than their competitors*. The primary driver for enterprise customers (especially those avoiding revenue targets) is risk mitigation and avoiding competitive disadvantage.
- **The Buying Journey is the Product:** The customer buying experience itself is a powerful differentiator. If products are only different at the margin, the unique quality and feel of the sales and onboarding journey will drive buying decisions.
- **The Rise of the GTM Engineer:** This role represents the convergence of technical prowess and GTM strategy. These engineers are critical for building better tooling, utilizing data, and enabling personalized customer experiences at scale, allowing teams to operate significantly faster.
- **Consultative Selling is Mandatory:** The shift to consumption-based business models forced GTM to move from transactional selling to deeply consultative, relationship-based advising. In the AI era, this means helping customers think through complex internal workflows and defining what they *need* to change, rather than just selling a feature.
- **PLG and Initial GTM:** For early-stage companies, GTM can start with either Marketing (PLG-led) or Sales, but the goal is to layer in the other function as the company matures, ensuring the process is seamless.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your GTM Scope:** Map your current GTM functions (Marketing, Sales, CS, Support) and identify the points of overlap and friction. Treat the entire customer journey as a single, integrated product experience.
- [ ] **Shift Messaging Focus:** Re-write your core value proposition to focus less on the *pain* and more on the *competitive advantage* or the measurable difference you provide compared to the status quo or your rivals.
- [ ] **Define the GTM Engineer Role:** Identify technical roles that can bridge the gap between product engineering and customer success/sales. Train them to be consultative problem-solvers, not just technical implementers.
- [ ] **Adopt a "Product Mindset" for GTM:** When planning a new sales process or onboarding flow, ask: "If this process were a product, what would be its core features, user experience, and measurable success metrics?"
- [ ] **Experiment with AI-Driven Personalization:** Use AI tools (like those mentioned in the transcript) to scale personalized customer interactions, moving beyond generic outreach to highly tailored, consultative workflows.
