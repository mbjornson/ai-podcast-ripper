# 5 questions to ask when your product stops growing | Jason Cohen (2x unicorn founder) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode provides a highly actionable framework for founders and product teams facing stalled growth. The core discussion moves beyond generic marketing advice, focusing instead on deep product and market diagnostics. The speaker outlines three critical questions to ask when growth slows: Are customers leaving? (Indicating a failure in the user journey); Is the pricing/positioning wrong? (Suggesting prices are too low or unadjusted); and Do you actually need to grow? (Challenging the assumption that constant growth is mandatory for survival).

The guest, Jason Cohen, brings immense credibility to the conversation, having founded four companies, including two unicorns (such as WP Engine). Beyond his entrepreneurial success, Cohen is highlighted as a seasoned writer with a 20-year blogging history. His philosophy emphasizes that for content creation, quality must always supersede consistency, a lesson that applies to the disciplined, high-effort approach required for building successful products.

## Key Points
- **The "Gauntlet" Test for Retention:** When diagnosing customer churn, don't just look at the homepage bounce rate. Focus on the entire "gauntlet" the customer went through—the difficulty of discovery, the willingness to reach the pricing page, and the act of purchasing. If a customer completes this high-effort journey and then leaves, the problem is likely product/value, not initial awareness.
- **Pricing is a Hypothesis, Not a Guess:** Many founders set prices based on guesswork. The key insight is that if a company with massive revenue (e.g., $400M) views a product at $2/month, the product is fundamentally underpriced. Pricing must be treated as a strategic variable that requires testing and adjustment.
- **Growth Deceleration is Normal:** Growth stalling is rarely a sudden, catastrophic event. It is more commonly a gradual, slow deceleration—like "running through mud." Recognizing this gradual decline allows founders to proactively diagnose the root cause rather than panicking over a sudden dip.
- **Challenge the Growth Imperative:** The phrase "if you're not growing, you're dying" is often an investor-driven narrative. Founders must critically assess whether continued growth is necessary for the product's mission or if the company needs to pivot or stabilize at a sustainable size.
- **Content Quality > Consistency:** For long-form content and thought leadership, the most valuable rule is that quality is the only thing that matters. Publishing infrequently but with exceptional depth is more impactful than maintaining a rigid, low-effort publishing schedule.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Pricing:** Immediately review your product's pricing structure. Benchmark your current price against what a much larger, more established company would consider "good enough" for the value provided.
- [ ] **Map the Customer Gauntlet:** Document the entire user journey from initial awareness to purchase. Identify the single point of highest friction or highest emotional commitment, and focus retention efforts there.
- [ ] **Run a "Need to Grow" Assessment:** Schedule a meeting to critically debate the company's growth mandate. Determine if the current growth trajectory is necessary for survival or if the goal should be stability and profitability.
- [ ] **Prioritize Deep Work:** When creating content or developing features, adopt the "quality over consistency" mindset. Focus on one exceptional, high-signal piece of work rather than several mediocre, rushed outputs.
- [ ] **Monitor Deceleration:** Instead of only tracking month-over-month growth, track the *rate* of growth over time to identify the subtle, gradual deceleration pattern early.
