# He saved OpenAI, invented the “Like” button, and built Google Maps: Bret Taylor on the future of careers, coding, agents, and more — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode features Bret Taylor, a highly credible builder with an exceptional track record spanning major tech companies. His background includes serving as CTO of Meta, co-CEO of Salesforce, and holding board positions at OpenAI and Twitter, alongside co-creating foundational products like Google Maps and inventing the "Like" button (FriendFeed). The discussion centers on the future trajectory of technology, particularly focusing on AI agents and how they will fundamentally reshape business models.

A major theme is the shift in software development and sales from selling mere "productivity features" to delivering measurable "outcomes." Taylor argues that the market is moving toward outcomes-based pricing, which he considers the most logical way to build and sell modern software solutions. He also provides deep product lessons derived from his career—most notably, how Google Maps succeeded not by digitizing existing resources (like Yellow Pages), but by creating an entirely new, compelling user experience that redefined the industry.

The conversation offers actionable insights into navigating a complex tech landscape, covering everything from the changing role of coding in the age of AI to essential mindset shifts required for building successful products and careers.

## Key Points
- **Outcomes-Based Pricing:** The entire market is moving toward outcomes-based pricing because it is the most obviously correct way to build and sell software (i.e., selling results, not features).
- **The Agent Economy:** AI agents are poised to have a massive impact on business, specifically helping companies with customer service, sales, and operational tasks. The market is currently "sleeping" on this potential.
- **Product Innovation vs. Digitization:** True product breakthroughs do not come from simply digitizing what existed before (e.g., Google Local being a "Me Too version of Yahoo Yellow Pages"). Instead, success comes from creating an entirely new experience that answers the question: "Why should I use this?"
- **The Power of Failure:** Taylor emphasizes that massive failures are formative and necessary for growth. His initial failure with Google Local was critical because it led to the V2 iteration (Google Maps), which ultimately redefined his career and the industry.
- **Scaling Success Metrics:** The launch of Google Maps demonstrated rapid, viral adoption: 10 million people used it on the first day; integrating satellite imagery (Keyhole/Google Earth) resulted in 90 million users on the same day.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Adopt Outcomes-Based Thinking:** When developing or selling any product/service, shift focus from listing features (e.g., "Our app has X, Y, Z") to defining measurable business outcomes (e.g., "We guarantee a 20% reduction in customer service time").
- [ ] **Study Product Failure:** Treat major professional setbacks not as endpoints, but as mandatory data points that force the creation of V2 solutions, following the pattern seen with Google Local $\rightarrow$ Google Maps.
- [ ] **Explore AI Agents for Process Mapping:** Identify 1-2 repetitive business processes within your current role (e.g., initial customer triage, lead qualification) and research how specialized AI agents could automate or significantly improve them.
- [ ] **Review Core Skills vs. Novel Experiences:** When evaluating market opportunities, ask: Is this merely digitizing an existing process (low signal), or is it creating a fundamentally new user experience that solves a previously unaddressed problem (high signal)?
