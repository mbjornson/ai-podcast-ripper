# The coming AI security crisis (and what to do about it) | Sander Schulhoff — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode details a critical and urgent warning regarding the current state of AI security, asserting that existing defensive measures, such as AI guardrails, are fundamentally ineffective. The discussion is led by Sander Schulhoff, a leading researcher in adversarial robustness, who has pioneered the field by running the largest AI red teaming competition. Schulhoff’s background provides a unique, hands-on view of how AI models can be systematically exploited. He argues that the current reliance on guardrails—which are essentially LLMs trained to detect malicious inputs—is a "complete lie" because sophisticated attackers can bypass them.

The core thesis is that the problem is not a simple bug that can be patched, but a systemic weakness in the underlying architecture. The risk is accelerating rapidly, moving beyond simple text generation into complex, multi-step actions via AI agents. While the current low adoption rate has masked the danger, the rise of autonomous agents and AI-powered tools means that the window for catastrophic failure is closing quickly.

## Key Points
- **Guardrails are Insecure:** AI guardrails are fundamentally flawed and do not work reliably. They are insufficient because they attempt to patch a "brain," not a bug.
- **The Nature of the Threat:**
    *   **Jailbreaking:** Occurs between a malicious user and a model (e.g., tricking ChatGPT).
    *   **Prompt Injection:** Occurs when a malicious user attempts to override the developer's system prompt within an application or agent (e.g., telling a story-writing website to ignore its instructions and output bomb instructions).
- **The Escalating Risk (The "Why Now"):** The danger is not AGI, but the current state of AI being integrated into functional systems. The risk increases exponentially with the adoption of:
    1.  **AI Agents:** Systems that can take actions on behalf of a user.
    2.  **AI-powered Browsers/Robots:** Tools that interact with the real-world digital environment.
- **High-Impact Attack Vectors:** Attacks are moving beyond simple text output and can now facilitate real-world damage, including:
    *   Performing **C-R-U-D actions** (Create, Read, Update, Delete) on databases.
    *   Sending **external emails** using internal company data (e.g., the ServiceNow Assist AI example).
- **The Mitigation Fallacy:** Experts suggest that the belief that the model "doesn't just do a good enough job" is fundamentally insufficient. The only reason we haven't seen a massive attack is the low level of adoption and power, not inherent security.

## Tools & Resources
- **Adversarial Robustness:** The field of study focused on making AI systems resistant to being tricked into performing unintended actions.
- **AI Red Teaming:** The process of actively testing and attacking AI systems to find vulnerabilities (Sander Schulhoff is a leading practitioner in this field).
- **Open Source Dataset:** The speaker mentioned that the data set collected from the first generative AI red teaming competition is open-sourced and used by frontier labs and Fortune 500 companies for benchmarking.

## Quotable
> "AI guardrails do not work. If someone is determined enough to trick GPT-5, they're going to deal with that guardrail. No problem."
>
> "The only reason there hasn't been a massive attack yet is how early the adoption is, not because it's secured."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Expertise in Adversarial Robustness:** Focus on understanding the technical vulnerabilities of LLMs, moving beyond simple prompt engineering to system-level security testing.
- [ ] **Prioritize Agent Security Audits:** If building or managing AI agents, assume the system prompt will be compromised. Implement strict, multi-layered authorization and capability controls (e.g., limiting what a "benign" agent can ask a "powerful" agent to do).
- [ ] **Study Prompt Injection vs. Jailbreaking:** Understand the difference between a model-only attack (jailbreak) and an application-level attack that targets the developer's system instructions (prompt injection), as the latter is the more dangerous business risk.
- [ ] **Follow Red Teaming Research:** Keep track of research from leading AI security conferences and labs that focus on real-world exploitation vectors, rather than just model performance benchmarks.
