# How 80,000 companies build with AI: products as organisms, the death of org charts, and why agents will outnumber employees by 2026 | Asha Sharma (CVP of AI Platform at Microsoft) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode details a massive paradigm shift in product development, moving from static "products as artifacts" (software you ship) to dynamic "products as organisms." The core thesis is that modern AI products are no longer fixed deliverables; instead, they are living systems that learn and improve continuously with every user interaction. This continuous feedback loop—where proprietary data fuels model refinement—is presented as the new source of intellectual property (IP).

The discussion emphasizes the rise of "agentic society," where sophisticated AI agents will handle complex tasks autonomously. This shift fundamentally changes organizational structure, suggesting that traditional org charts and layers of management may become obsolete due to exponential productivity gains. The guest, Asha Sharma, brings deep credibility, having held roles at Microsoft (AI Platform CVP), Instacart (COO), and Meta (VP Product), giving her a unique vantage point on how large-scale companies are building and failing with AI.

## Key Points
- **Product as Organism:** Products must be designed to ingest data, digest rewards models, and create continuous outcomes rather than simply solving a single problem and being shipped. The product's metabolism (its ability to learn from usage) is the new KPI.
- **The Agentic Shift:** We are moving toward an agentic society where the marginal cost of good output approaches zero. This requires building systems that can take actions autonomously, leading to fewer necessary organizational layers ("the org chart starts to become the org chart").
- **Technical Evolution (Post-Training):** The economic viability of training massive models is shifting. Once a model hits approximately 30 billion parameters, it becomes more efficient and cost-effective to optimize using proprietary data loops (post-training) rather than relying solely on expensive pre-training runs.
- **The New Interface:** GUIs are giving way to code-native interfaces. The product's UX will evolve automatically based on how the user interacts with it, requiring less manual intervention from product teams.
- **Successful AI Adoption Framework (3 Stages):** Companies that succeed in the AI era follow a clear progression: 1) Achieving universal "AI fluency" across all employees (using Copilots); 2) Applying AI to map and improve existing processes (e.g., reducing fraud detection time from 15 days to 10); and 3) Using proven impact to actively *inflect* growth, improving LTV or co-creating entirely new product categories.
- **Failure Point:** The primary failure mode is "doing AI for AI's sake"—kicking off multiple projects without a unified blueprint or understanding how the AI stack connects to core business value.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop AI Fluency:** Commit to using at least one Copilot or AI assistant in your daily workflow (coding, writing, research) until it becomes second nature, ensuring you understand how AI can raise the ceiling on existing skills.
- [ ] **Map a Process for Optimization:** Select an existing, painful business process within your current role (e.g., reporting, client onboarding, internal approvals). Map out every step and identify three specific points where an agent or automated workflow could reduce manual effort by 30%+.
- [ ] **Focus on the Data Loop:** When evaluating new tech investments, do not ask "What does this product do?" Instead, ask: "How will using this product generate proprietary data that can be used to retrain and improve the core model over time?" (This is the signal for true IP.)
- [ ] **Read/Research:** Investigate concepts of 'agentic workflows' and 'product metabolism' to understand how continuous learning systems are being built.
