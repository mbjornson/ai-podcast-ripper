# The internal AI tool that’s transforming how Stripe designs products | Owen Williams — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This Lenny’s Podcast episode features Owen Williams, a design manager at Stripe, discussing “proto dash,” an internal prototyping tool built to address the pain points of designing complex data dashboards within Figma. The core issue is the difficulty of prototyping interactive data experiences – particularly with zero data – in Figma, leading to “uncanny valley” prototypes that break immersion. Williams’ credibility stems from his engineering background and his role as a design manager at Stripe, a company known for its rigorous design standards. The episode highlights a pragmatic approach to design problem-solving, leveraging technical skills to lower barriers to entry for designers. The shift in usage – from designers to PMs – is a surprising and significant trend.

## Key Points
- **The Figma Prototype Pain:** Designers struggle to realistically prototype data dashboards in Figma due to the complexity of interactions, filters, and states, especially when starting with zero data. This leads to “uncanny valley” prototypes that feel jarring and unnatural.
- **PM Adoption:** A surprising trend is the increased use of proto dash by PMs, not just designers. This suggests a broader need for rapid prototyping capabilities across teams.
- **Technical Designer Role:** Williams’ transition into a “technical designer” role, leveraging his engineering background, was key to developing proto dash. He recognized the difficulty designers face with technical tools and sought to simplify the process.
- **Lowering the Barrier to Entry:** Proto dash aims to reduce the technical barrier to entry for designers by providing a pre-configured environment within Cursor, minimizing the need for deep coding knowledge.
- **Dev Box Infrastructure:** Stripe’s use of Dev Box infrastructure allows designers to access a server environment instantly, further streamlining the prototyping process and eliminating the need for local setup.
- **Demos, Not Memos:** Williams references Dan Nelson’s concept of “demos, not memos,” emphasizing the value of interactive prototypes over static presentations.
- **Data Dashboard Challenges:** The episode specifically addresses the difficulty of prototyping data-heavy experiences, highlighting the need for realistic data integration and interaction design.

## Tools & Resources
- **Cursor:** A low-code development environment used by Stripe for building prototypes and applications.
- **Figma:** The design tool used as a starting point for many of the prototypes.
- **Sale:** Stripe’s design system.
- **npm:** Node Package Manager, a JavaScript package manager.
- **Dev Box:** Stripe’s infrastructure for providing server environments.
- **Dan Nelson:** Design leader at Stripe, referenced for his “demos, not memos” concept.
