# "Sell the alpha, not the feature": The enterprise sales playbook for $1M to $10M ARR | Jen Abel — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
This episode provides a highly tactical deep dive into the transition of a startup's sales playbook, specifically moving from founder-led sales (Zero to $1M ARR) to structured enterprise sales ($1M to $10M ARR). Jen Abel, co-founder of Jellyfish, argues that enterprise sales is less about product features and more about the art of relationship building and strategic deal crafting. The core thesis is that successful enterprise selling requires shifting the focus from solving an immediate "problem" to selling a massive, compelling "opportunity" or "gap" that the client hasn't even realized they need yet.

Abel challenges common assumptions about market segmentation, arguing that the "mid-market" does not exist. Instead, companies must operate in two distinct silos: SMB (powered by marketing) and Enterprise (powered by sales). Furthermore, she provides counterintuitive advice on targeting: instead of going after smaller, easier logos, founders should prioritize "Tier 1 logos" (the industry giants like Walmart or McDonald's). These companies, because they are the leaders, are the most desperate to stay ahead and are therefore the most willing to take risks and adopt disruptive technology.

## Key Points
- **The Mid-Market Myth:** The "mid-market" is a non-existent category. Companies must define their play based on whether they are operating in the SMB silo (marketing-powered) or the Enterprise silo (sales-led). Attempting to serve both results in losing ground in both.
- **Sell the Gap, Not the Problem:** The fundamental shift in enterprise selling is moving from "problem selling" (fixing a known pain point) to "gap selling" (selling a future opportunity or "alpha"). When speaking to a leader, you must sell an opportunity that will define their next phase of growth.
- **Targeting Strategy (Counterintuitive):** Do not start by targeting smaller, easier-to-close logos. Instead, focus on "Tier 1 logos" (the market leaders). These companies are under the most pressure to innovate and stay number one, making them highly receptive to disruptive solutions.
- **The Importance of the Founder:** To land a major enterprise deal, the founder must be involved as early as possible. The founder's voice is the most powerful tool for turning a $100k deal into a $1M deal.
- **Deal Qualification:** Be wary of clients who "nickel and dime" you or who are willing to discount excessively. These behaviors suggest they are not fully bought into the value proposition and may be giving a false sense of Product Market Fit.
- **Sales is an Art:** Enterprise sales is described as a creative art form built on deep relationships. The goal is to build trust so that when you need a favor, the client is willing to "turn over rocks" for you.

## Quotable
> "You need to vision cast. You need to sell to a gap, don't sell to a problem. When you're selling to a leader, you need to be selling an opportunity."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Vision Casting Skills:** Practice framing your product's value not as a solution to a current problem, but as the inevitable next step in the client's industry evolution.
- [ ] **Re-segment Your Target Market:** Stop thinking of a "mid-market." Clearly define if your ideal customer is a marketing-powered SMB or a sales-led Enterprise client, and tailor your entire GTM strategy to that single silo.
- [ ] **Prioritize Tier 1 Logos:** When building your sales outreach list, focus disproportionately on the largest, most established companies in your industry. Their need to maintain market leadership makes them the most receptive to disruptive technology.
- [ ] **Involve the Founder Early:** Structure your sales process to get the founder/CEO involved in the discovery phase, as their personal endorsement is the most powerful accelerator for large deals.
- [ ] **Study Deal Crafting:** Treat sales as a relationship-building exercise, focusing on how to "craft" the deal and build enough trust that the client is willing to advocate for you internally.
