# Brian Chesky's secret mentor who died 9 times, started the Burning Man board, and built the world's first midlife wisdom school | Chip Conley (founder of MEA) — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode features Chip Conley, a highly accomplished figure who has served as a founding board member for Burning Man and established the Modern Elder Academy (MEA), the world's first midlife wisdom school. His background includes building Juada Vive, the second largest boutique hotel chain in the U.S., before joining Airbnb to help transform it into a global hospitality brand. The discussion centers on navigating professional life and product development while aging—specifically, how to be successful in tech when you are decades older than your average colleague or user base.

Conley recounts his experience joining Airbnb at age 52, where the average employee was 26. He highlights that success requires a delicate balance of being "wise and curious," often forcing him into the role of the least technically knowledgeable person in the room while simultaneously mentoring leadership figures like Brian Chesky. The core thesis is that older experiences (wisdom) must be effectively combined with younger energy (speed/focus) to create superior innovation, particularly in product definition and user empathy.

## Key Points
- **The Value of "Older Brains":** When developing products or strategies, the combination of experienced, thoughtful perspectives ("older brains") connecting dots, paired with fast, focused execution from younger team members, is a powerful formula for breakthrough ideas.
- **Redefining the Product:** In tech discussions, there is often a conflict between traditional product thinking (e.g., "the homes and apartments are the product") and modern tech definition ("product is something different"). The value of diverse experience lies in challenging these assumptions by asking: *What is the actual core offering?*
- **Midlife as Chrysalis, Not Crisis:** Conley reframes midlife not as a period of decline or crisis, but as a "chrysalis"—a necessary stage of transformation. He notes that personal fulfillment and happiness can increase significantly with age (e.g., being happier at 64 than at 47).
- **The Humility Paradox:** To succeed in fast-moving tech environments while bringing decades of experience, one must maintain extreme humility. The key is to "not pretend to know things I didn't know," but rather to show respect and curiosity, which earns professional credibility regardless of age or title.

## Tools & Resources
- **Modern Elder Academy (MEA):** World's first midlife wisdom school; focuses on helping individuals navigate the transition and potential of middle age.
- **Juada Vive:** Boutique hotel chain founded by Chip Conley at a young age, demonstrating early entrepreneurial success.
- **Burning Man / Esalen Institute:** Boards served, indicating deep involvement in counter-culture, community building, and experiential learning.

## Quotable
> "Midlife is not crisis."
>

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Professional Humility:** When entering a new field or working with younger colleagues, adopt the mindset of being intensely curious. Focus on asking *why* rather than immediately asserting what is known.
- [ ] **Seek Cross-Generational Input:** Actively structure product testing and brainstorming sessions that include diverse age groups (e.g., Boomers, Gen X, Millennials) to ensure a holistic understanding of user needs and pain points.
- [ ] **Reframe Midlife Transitions:** View personal or professional plateaus not as crises, but as necessary periods of transformation (the "chrysalis") that precede significant growth.
- [ ] **Practice the Mentor/Mentee Balance:** If you are in a leadership role, remember that being a mentor does not preclude you from being the primary learner; maintain intellectual curiosity regardless of your seniority.
