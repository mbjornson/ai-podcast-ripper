# A rational conversation on where AI is actually going | Benedict Evans — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
Benedict Evans argues that AI represents a technological shift on par with the advent of the Internet or mobile computing, fundamentally changing how value is created across all industries. However, he cautions against oversimplifying this magnitude, noting that the current state of AI adoption is analogous to the early days of the Internet in 1997—highly exciting, immensely powerful, but still confusing and lacking clear, universally adopted use cases. The core message is that while the technology is transformative, the path to widespread, profitable application is not yet clear.

Evans debunks the "doomer" narrative, dismissing attempts to predict exact job losses or percentage automation rates. Instead, he focuses on the structural shift: AI is forcing a re-evaluation of professional services. He points out a significant trend where major AI labs (like OpenAI and Anthropic) are heavily investing in professional services and consulting firms. This suggests that the value accrual point is shifting from pure model development to integrating AI capabilities into complex, enterprise-level business processes that traditionally required human consulting expertise.

## Key Points
- **AI Magnitude is Fundamental, Not Incremental:** AI is a fundamental, non-linear change, not just a marginal improvement over existing tech. Comparing it to the Internet/Mobile is appropriate for scale, but the current implementation phase is highly immature, making specific predictions difficult.
- **The 1997 Analogy:** The current moment is characterized by wide, uneven adoption. Like the early web, the technology is available to early adopters (the "tech cluster"), but the general public and many professional sectors are still figuring out how to use it, leading to a "spread of maturity."
- **The Professional Services Convergence:** The most significant signal is the investment trend. AI labs are buying into professional services and consulting firms. This indicates that the immediate value capture will occur by automating or augmenting the complex, high-touch problem-solving currently handled by McKinsey, BCG, and Accenture—the "how-to" layer, not just the core model.
- **The VisiCalc Parallel:** The adoption curve mirrors the introduction of spreadsheets (VisiCalc) in the 1970s. Software developers are the "accountants" in this new economy—the group best positioned to understand and implement the core computational power, while other sectors (like law or journalism) are still in the "puzzled" phase of integration.
- **Focus on the "Jagged Frontier":** The true challenge is not *if* AI will work, but *where* it will work, *where* it won't, and whether the application is intuitive. This frontier of applicability is the current source of both opportunity and confusion.

- [ ] **Study the Professional Services Value Chain:** Instead of viewing AI as a pure tech play, analyze how AI can automate or augment the core deliverables of consulting firms (e.g., process mapping, market analysis, operational redesign). This is where the immediate, high-value money is moving.
- [ ] **Adopt a "VisiCalc Mindset":** Approach new technologies not as end-user tools, but as foundational computational engines. Ask: "What complex, manual, spreadsheet-like process can this automate or radically simplify?"
- [ ] **Follow the "Jagged Frontier":** Dedicate time to researching specific, non-obvious industry verticals (e.g., specialized legal tech, niche manufacturing processes) to identify the precise points where AI integration is currently difficult or non-intuitive—these are the biggest opportunities.
