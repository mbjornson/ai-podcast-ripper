# Slack founder: Mental models for building products people love ft. Stewart Butterfield — Lenny's Podcast

## Summary
The episode features Stuart Butterfield, co-founder of Flickr and Slack, who provides deep insights into product development, company scaling, and the psychology of user adoption. Butterfield emphasizes that true success is measured solely by the value created for the customer, not the effort expended by the company. He argues that product strategy must shift its focus from simply "removing friction" to ensuring deep "comprehension"—designing experiences so intuitive that the user never has to think about how to use the software.

The core of the discussion revolves around two major frameworks: the Utility Curve and the concept of Divine Discontent. The Utility Curve provides a scientific lens for evaluating product investments, helping founders determine if they are still on the initial, low-value part of the adoption cycle or if they are approaching the "I can't live without this" moment. Furthermore, Butterfield introduces the idea that user standards constantly rise (Divine Discontent), meaning continuous, systemic investment in core usability (search, login, checkout) is as critical as building new features.

## Key Points
- **The Value Metric:** The ultimate measure of success is the amount of value created for customers, not the effort put into the product.
- **Comprehension over Friction:** Instead of aiming to reduce friction (which is often a superficial fix), the goal should be to improve comprehension. The ideal product is one that requires zero cognitive effort for the user.
- **The Utility Curve Framework:** This S-curve model maps effort (X-axis) against value/convenience (Y-axis).
    - **Phase 1 (Junk):** Initial effort yields minimal value (e.g., adding a database table without a use case).
    - **Phase 2 (Magic Threshold):** A critical investment point where value explodes. This is the goal for product development.
    - **Phase 3 (Plateau):** Continued investment yields diminishing returns.
    - *Actionable Insight:* Use this curve to rationally decide whether to continue investing in a feature or pivot away from it.
- **Divine Discontent:** As users become familiar with a product, their standards for usability, speed, and quality rise. This means that even after a product is "finished," continuous, systemic investment in core experiences (search, login, checkout) is mandatory to prevent stagnation.
- **Rational Pivoting:** When considering a pivot or major change, the decision must be made through cold, intellectual rationality, not emotional attachment to the original idea.

## Tools & Resources
- **Utility Curve:** A mental model for product managers and founders to assess the ROI of feature development and identify the point of diminishing returns.
- **Divine Discontent:** A strategic concept for maintaining product relevance by continuously raising the baseline standard of usability and experience.
- **Product Pass (Newsletter):** Mentioned as a resource for various tools (Devon, Lovable, Replit, Bolt, Innate, Linear, Superhuman, etc.).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a Utility Curve Assessment:** For any major product feature or business initiative, map out the expected effort vs. potential value. Determine if the current investment is still on the steep, high-return part of the curve, or if it's entering the plateau of diminishing returns.
- [ ] **Audit Core User Flows:** Systematically review the top 3 most used features (e.g., search, login, checkout). Identify where the user has to "think" or make a decision. The goal is to eliminate cognitive load, not just physical friction.
- [ ] **Practice Rational Decision-Making:** When faced with a business pivot or major change, force yourself to create "distance" from the emotional attachment to the current idea. Treat the decision as a purely intellectual exercise.
- [ ] **Focus on Systemic Improvement:** Dedicate resources not just to "new features," but to improving the underlying infrastructure and core usability of the product (e.g., improving the search algorithm, streamlining the onboarding flow) to combat Divine Discontent.
