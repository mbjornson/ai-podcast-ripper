# 735: Meet the Master Reseller Who Gets Literally Tons of Inventory for Free — The Side Hustle Show

## Summary
The episode details the journey of Angie Nelson, a highly successful reselling entrepreneur who runs a major eBay store, having sold nearly 200,000 items, most of which were acquired for free. Her business model, Eways Direct, is built on advanced inventory sourcing and arbitrage, moving far beyond simple flea market sales. She started accidentally in 2008 by bidding on a massive liquidation auction, which provided her with an overwhelming amount of surplus goods for a minimal investment.

Angie’s core methodology involves three pillars: sourcing from large-scale liquidations (abandonment auctions), securing free inventory by offering to remove junk from businesses (B2B pickups), and monetizing scrap materials (e-waste recycling). She leveraged the global nature of e-commerce, specifically eBay, to sell obsolete or vintage US technology to international markets, turning seemingly worthless items into significant revenue streams.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Liquidation Sourcing:** The business began with a low-cost entry point ($300 investment) into a massive liquidation auction, resulting in a tenfold return. This highlights that high-volume, low-cost inventory acquisition is the foundation of the model.
- **Three-Pillar Inventory Strategy:** Inventory is sourced not just through buying, but through: 1) Auctions/Liquidations; 2) Free pickups (offering to remove junk from businesses); and 3) Scrap/Recycling (turning waste into revenue).
- **Global Arbitrage:** The most significant insight is the global demand for obsolete US tech. A single $8,000 flip involved selling an Apple RAID server to Amsterdam, proving that local "junk" has high international value.
- **Building Trust for Free Inventory:** The initial strategy of offering to pick up junk for free (B2B) was key. Companies, seeing the professional service and the profit potential, began recommending the service, creating a scalable, low-cost pipeline of inventory.
- **E-commerce Platform Necessity:** Using platforms like eBay is critical because they provide the necessary tools (global shipping, international customs handling) that allow sellers to operate without needing expertise in international law or logistics.

## Tools & Resources
- **eBay:** Essential platform for e-commerce, particularly for unique, vintage, and high-value tech items.
- **Whatnot:** Mentioned as a rapidly growing live shopping platform, suggesting a modern alternative to traditional marketplaces for sellers.
- **Local/Online Liquidation Auctions:** Primary source of bulk, low-cost inventory (e.g., mortgage company auctions, corporate liquidations).
- **eBay Global Shipping Program:** Crucial tool for sellers who need to sell obsolete items internationally without handling complex customs or shipping logistics.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a "Free Pickup" Service:** Identify local businesses (offices, small shops) that accumulate obsolete electronics or junk. Offer to remove this material for free, positioning yourself as a professional removal service.
- [ ] **Master Liquidation Sourcing:** Research local auction houses, mortgage liquidators, and corporate surplus outlets. Focus on bulk, high-volume, low-cost acquisition.
- [ ] **Research Global Demand:** When sourcing tech or vintage items, immediately check international pricing and demand (e.g., using global search tools) to identify arbitrage opportunities beyond the local market.
- [ ] **Establish a Scrap Value Baseline:** Learn the local market rates for scrap materials (metals, aluminum, copper) to ensure that even if an item can't be resold, it still generates immediate cash flow.
- [ ] **Build a Professional Presence:** Invest in professional branding (e.g., a branded truck/vehicle) and service reliability to build trust with corporate clients, enabling repeat, high-volume pickups.
