# 721: $1k a Month with your First Co-Living Property — The Side Hustle Show

## Summary
The episode details "co-living," a high-leverage real estate strategy where large single-family homes are converted into multiple private rooms rented out individually. The guest argues that this model is significantly more impactful and faster for generating wealth than traditional single-family home hacking, which typically aims for modest cash flow per unit. By maximizing density, co-living can generate 4x to 5x the monthly cash flow of conventional rentals, meaning investors need fewer properties to meet aggressive income goals.

The core challenge discussed is navigating restrictive local zoning laws that limit the number of unrelated people allowed in a single dwelling. The guest provides a sophisticated legal workaround: instead of operating under standard lease agreements, the property should be managed by forming a private "club" or entity structure (like a country club). This structural hack allows for high occupancy while circumventing outdated municipal regulations.

Finally, success requires meticulous market analysis focused on two factors: the local co-living friendliness score (avoiding hostile cities) and the price point/cash flow potential. While large metropolitan areas are viable, focusing on mid-sized markets with solid demand but more affordable real estate is key to maximizing returns.

## Key Points
- **Cash Flow Multiplier:** Co-living aims for 4x–5x the monthly cash flow of traditional single-family rentals (e.g., moving from $250/month goal to $1,000-$2,000+ per property).
- **Scaling Metric (Size):** The primary factor in co-living is square footage, not number of bedrooms. A rough guideline provided is that every 250 sq ft added to a home can yield an extra private room/unit.
- **The Legal Workaround:** To bypass restrictive zoning laws limiting unrelated occupants, the strategy involves forming a legal "club" or membership entity structure rather than using standard residential leases.
- **Regulatory Progress:** Colorado has recently passed legislation making it illegal for any local jurisdiction to regulate the number of unrelated people in a home, potentially setting a national precedent for co-living viability.
- **Market Selection Criteria:** When choosing an area, assess two things: 1) The "co-living friendly score" (avoiding cities known for aggressive enforcement); and 2) Price point/Cash Flow potential (favoring mid-sized markets like Dallas, Charlotte, or Tampa over high-cost areas like San Francisco).

## Tools & Resources

- **Co-Living Model:** The core framework/strategy itself (converting large single-family homes into multiple private units).
- **Club Entity Structure:** A legal mechanism used to operate the property, replacing standard lease agreements.
