# How Anthropic, Every, & Ramp design with AI — Dive Club

## Summary
This episode provides a deep dive into how AI is fundamentally reshaping professional design workflows, moving designers from isolated creative roles toward integrated engineering partners. The discussion features experts from Anthropic (Megan Choi), Every (Dan Shipper), and Ramp (Bradley Ziffer), who collectively outline the necessary shifts in mindset and process for modern product teams. The core thesis is that the future of design requires abandoning "design-only" thinking and embracing direct, iterative interaction with production codebases.

The panel outlines two major milestones: first, giving designers access to the live production codebase (a shift from sandboxes); and second, requiring designers to become comfortable letting go of perfectionism. The focus shifts from achieving polished final features to prioritizing deep thought, research, and functional output (V1, V2, V3) that can be rapidly tested against real-world data endpoints.

## Key Points
- **The Production Code Milestone:** The most critical shift is granting designers access to the live production codebase, moving beyond segregated "playground" or sandbox environments. This integration provides necessary context and data visibility (logging/data queries) that cannot be replicated in isolated tools.
- **Embracing Iterative Output over Polish:** Designers must become comfortable shipping V1, V2, and even V3 features without the expectation of initial perfection. The goal is to build automations and checks that maintain quality while accelerating deployment speed.
- **The "Care" Mindset Shift (Intention > Perfection):** As tools make basic functionality accessible to many, the value proposition shifts from execution ability to *intention* and *care*. Design engineers must focus on deeply considering performance implications (e.g., animations) and user flow boundaries, rather than just visual polish.
- **Future-Proofing Prioritization:** When working at the frontier of AI, teams should prioritize deep research and "future looking" work over perfecting features that may be obsolete in six months. Time allocation must favor exploration on a large canvas over optimizing small, contained tickets.

## Tools & Resources
- **Claude Code / Cowork (Anthropic):** Platform focused on integrating design workflows with production codebases, enabling designers to build and prototype directly within real product flows.
- **Destin:** A specialized design tool that allows users to design and prototype directly on top of existing live pages and flows pulled from a production environment, facilitating immediate testing against reality. (Check: dive.club/Destin)
- **Paper:** A design tool utilizing a Chrome extension feature that allows copying any component or element from a live website and pasting it into Paper as editable layers, maintaining real HTML/CSS integrity for seamless iteration. (Check: dive.club/Paper)

## Action Items
- [ ] **Shift Focus from Polish to Intent:** When executing a feature, consciously shift your focus from achieving 10/10 polish to ensuring the *intention* and *care* behind the design are flawless.
- [ ] **Audit Your Workflow for Code Access:** Identify areas in your current product workflow where you rely on isolated sandboxes or mockups; advocate for direct access to production data endpoints or code branches.
- [ ] **Adopt an Iterative Mindset:** When launching new features, prioritize getting a functional V1 out quickly (even if imperfect) over spending excessive time perfecting the initial design. Use this early output as a basis for rapid user feedback and subsequent iterations.
- [ ] **Experiment with Production Design Tools:** Test tools like Destin or Paper to understand what it feels like to prototype directly on top of live, existing product flows rather than starting from scratch in an isolated canvas.
