# Brian Lovin - How to level up with AI as a designer — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode centers on the fundamental shift required for designers working with Artificial Intelligence, arguing that traditional UI/UX design methods (like mocking up in Figma) are obsolete when dealing with generative AI. Brian Lovin, who works at Notion and has been deeply involved in building Notion's AI agent infrastructure, details how the focus must move from designing static interfaces to designing the "harness" or the underlying logic that allows an agent to perform complex, multi-step tasks and verify its own work.

Lovin emphasizes that the current state of AI requires designers to operate at the boundary of what is possible, embracing imperfection and the limitations of current models. He advises designers to abandon the comfort of established design systems and instead prototype in live code environments (like Medium) to understand the real-world friction points—such as model slowness, errors, and the need for constant clarification.

The discussion also touches on the changing nature of professional roles, warning that traditional titles (PM, Designer, Engineer) are becoming irrelevant. Success now belongs to the highly fluid individual who can move seamlessly between these disciplines, making the ability to adapt and learn on the fly the most valuable skill.

## Key Points
- **Shift from Interface Design to Process Design:** The core design task is no longer designing the visual layout, but designing the "harness for the agent to do longer things and verify its own work."
- **The Early Adopter Advantage:** Those who are truly pushing AI to automate knowledge work or remove entire company processes are "very early adopters." This group has a high tolerance for subpar experiences and is willing to "crawl over glass" to achieve a functional outcome.
- **The Irrelevance Cycle:** Assume that everything you build today will be obsolete in six months. This requires adopting a mindset of constant iteration and being prepared to strip away old assumptions, as demonstrated by Notion rewriting its agent harness multiple times.
- **Discipline Fluidity is Mandatory:** The obsession with rigid job titles (Designer vs. PM vs. Engineer) is a career trap. The most valuable professional is one who can move between these disciplines fluidly.
- **Prototyping Must Be Code-Based:** Figma mocks are insufficient for AI. Designers must prototype in live code environments (like Medium) to accurately simulate the real-world performance, speed, and failure modes of AI models.

## Tools & Resources
- **Notion:** The platform used for building and deploying the AI agent infrastructure (App Builder, Notion Agent, Notion Workers).
- **Medium/Code Sandbox:** Recommended environment for designers to prototype AI interactions and test model responses in a live, isolated code base, rather than relying on static mocks.
- **Jitter:** Tool mentioned for generating short video clips from uploaded assets using AI, adding motion and depth to static visuals.
- **Desen:** A tool that allows users to prototype directly within their existing codebase without opening an IDE, extracting the design language for seamless sharing.

## Quotable
> "As soon as you realize you can't design half of this stuff in Figma, what you're really designing is the harness for the agent to do longer things and verify its own work."

> "The people who are really engaging with these tools are, I think, by definition, early adopters. ... The tolerance for those people for subpar experiences is actually quite high."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice thinking in terms of *process flow* and *system logic* rather than visual components. Focus on defining the inputs, steps, and verification points for an automated task.
- [ ] **Career Strategy:** Actively seek roles or projects that require cross-functional fluency. Do not get boxed in by a single title; aim to be the person who can bridge the gap between design, engineering, and product management.
- [ ] **Tool Mastery:** Move beyond Figma for complex prototypes. Learn to use code sandboxes or low-code environments (like Notion's internal tools) to test the *behavior* of AI, not just its appearance.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Adopt the "six-month obsolescence" mindset. When starting a new project, assume the core assumptions will fail and build the system with maximum flexibility to pivot.
- [ ] **Networking:** Engage with engineers and model builders. The most valuable insights come from understanding the technical limitations and capabilities of the underlying AI models.
