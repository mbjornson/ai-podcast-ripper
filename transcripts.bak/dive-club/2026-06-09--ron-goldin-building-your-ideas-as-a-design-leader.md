# Ron Goldin - Building your ideas as a design leader — Dive Club

## Summary
Ron Goldin is presented as an exemplary design leader who has built high-impact products across major tech companies including Google, Shopify, and Uber Eats. The episode details his journey from being a hands-on designer/researcher rather than just a conceptual strategist. His core philosophy centers on the necessity of extreme empathy—going beyond standard user interviews to immerse oneself in the user's actual environment and pain points.

The main discussion revolves around how Goldin tackled systemic product failures at Uber Eats, specifically concerning courier efficiency. Instead of relying on internal assumptions (that demand was only concentrated at major restaurants), he conducted deep ethnographic research across multiple international cities (Tokyo, Mexico City, Bangkok). This fieldwork revealed that the true bottleneck wasn't service availability, but a lack of visualization tools for localized demand, forcing couriers to waste time and resources.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Ethnographic Documentation:** Goldin’s breakthrough was recording his actual delivery trips using an Insta 360 camera mounted on a bike. This raw footage—capturing the anxiety, difficulty navigating security gates in Tokyo, or simply waiting for hours with no gig—provided undeniable evidence that internal assumptions were fundamentally flawed.
- **Shifting the Problem Scope (The "Hot Spot" Insight):** The initial assumption was that service needed to be directed toward large, established restaurants. The research proved this wrong: couriers were wasting time and resources because they lacked tools to visualize localized demand ("hot spots"). This insight shifted the focus from *service capacity* to *demand visualization*.
- **The Value of Failure Documentation:** He emphasizes that capturing moments of failure (like sitting in a car for three hours with no gig) is often more valuable than documenting success. These painful, mundane moments are what drive deep product investigation and systemic change.
- **Scaling Empathy into Programs:** The personal research effort led to the creation of an "Uber dogfooding program," which scaled the practice of having researchers actively plan and operate the system they were designing for, ensuring insights remained grounded in real human experience.

## Tools & Resources
- **Insta 360 Camera (or similar action camera):** Used as a tool for capturing raw, first-person ethnographic data (mounted on bikes/vehicles) rather than just taking static photos or recording voice notes.
- **Jitter:** A design tool mentioned that allows designers to create animated components and reuse them across files with single edits, improving efficiency in complex UI systems.
- **Dessin:** A design tool that enables prototyping directly on top of existing production interfaces (pages/flows pulled from prod), allowing for rapid iteration and testing against live system constraints.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct Deep Ethnographic Field Studies:** When tackling a product problem, move beyond interviews. Physically immerse yourself in the user's environment (e.g., riding bikes, working shifts) and use recording equipment (action cameras, dash cams) to capture raw pain points over time.
- [ ] **Challenge Core Assumptions:** Always question the initial scope of demand. If your product assumes users will go to "A" because it’s visible, investigate if they are actually going to "B" or "C" and why (e.g., investigating local alternatives instead of major hubs).
- [ ] **Build a 'Pain Point' Narrative:** When presenting findings to leadership, don't just present data points; create a narrative around the raw emotional pain or inefficiency experienced by the user. This is more motivating than a spreadsheet.
- [ ] **Master Design Workflow Tools:** Explore modern design tools like Jitter (for component animation) and Dessin (for designing directly on production flows) to increase efficiency in complex, interconnected product systems.
