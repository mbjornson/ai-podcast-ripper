# Kris Puckett - Becoming an AI-native designer — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode explores the concept of the "AI-native designer"—a professional who leverages generative AI not just for efficiency, but to realize ambitious, previously impossible design visions. The discussion centers on how modern AI tools are fundamentally changing the design workflow, moving beyond traditional "craft" to incorporate a deeper, more personal "soul." The guest, Chris Puckett, is a design leader who has worked at major tech companies including Dropbox, Mercury, and Stripe. He details his personal journey of building a complex book-reading app (Epilog), demonstrating how AI tools allow individuals without deep coding expertise to tackle highly technical challenges like integrating APIs and implementing advanced visual effects (Metal shaders).

Puckett's narrative emphasizes that the biggest hurdle in modern development is often not technical skill, but the mental block of needing a perfect plan. He argues that AI acts as a "co-conspirator," allowing designers to move from vague ideas and questions ("I don't know") directly into functional, complex prototypes, thereby accelerating the path from concept to viable product.

## Key Points
- **The Shift from Craft to Soul:** The future of design requires incorporating the "human side" or "soul" into technology, moving beyond mere functionality.
- **AI as a Co-Conspirator, Not Just a Tool:** AI tools (like Cloud Code) are best utilized as collaborative partners that generate initial concepts and solve technical roadblocks, rather than just executing commands.
- **Anti-Spec Doc Philosophy:** The most significant learning curve is realizing that you do not need perfectly flushed-out plans, detailed PRDs, or spec documents to get AI development started. Momentum is gained by asking questions and iterating immediately.
- **The Power of the "I Don't Know":** The phrase "I don't know" is reframed as an invitation for deeper conversation and technical problem-solving. It is the starting point for complex development (e.g., asking how to connect a service like Google Books API).
- **Accelerated Technical Complexity:** AI has made highly complex, previously inaccessible techniques—such as integrating APIs (e.g., Google Books API) and implementing advanced visual effects like Metal shaders—much more attainable for non-expert developers.

## Tools & Resources
- **Cloud Code:** A development environment that allows users to build ideas and prototypes with AI guidance, significantly lowering the barrier to entry for complex iOS app development.
- **Paper:** Described as a "fully connected canvas," this tool is presented as a shared whiteboard that integrates with coding environments, allowing designers to rapidly iterate and refine concepts before passing them to code.
- **Perplexity:** Used by the guest to create "spaces" for research and questions related to physical books, demonstrating its utility for linking disparate knowledge sources.
- **Jitter:** A tool mentioned for generating short video clips from static assets using AI, useful for adding motion and depth to brand visuals.
- **Xcode:** The native Apple development environment, highlighted as a complex tool that AI assistance makes manageable for first-time users.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice using AI tools (like Cloud Code/Cursor) to build prototypes based on vague concepts, specifically focusing on integrating external APIs (e.g., using a public API like Google Books) without a detailed plan.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Adopt the "I don't know" mindset: Treat technical unknowns not as roadblocks, but as prompts for AI-assisted problem-solving.
- [ ] **Tool Exploration:** Experiment with "canvas-based" AI design tools (like Paper) to rapidly visualize and iterate on ideas before committing to code.
- [ ] **Business Move:** Identify a personal passion project (like the book app) and commit to building a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) using AI assistance, bypassing the need for a formal PRD.
- [ ] **Learning Resource:** Explore the functionality of Perplexity for linking research and knowledge from physical sources (books) to digital, actionable questions.
