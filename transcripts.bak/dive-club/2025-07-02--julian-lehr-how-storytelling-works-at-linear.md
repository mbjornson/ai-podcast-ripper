# Julian Lehr - How storytelling works at Linear — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode delves into the art of storytelling for intangible digital products, using Linear's growth as a case study. Julian Lehr, Storytelling Lead at Linear, discusses how effective narrative is critical for building excitement and adoption around software that cannot be physically touched. His background highlights an organic career path: starting as a heavy beta user who was eventually brought into the company after sharing insights on another product (Superhuman). He initially joined with an undefined role, leading to a focus on highly creative, non-traditional B2B SaaS marketing assets.

The core discussion revolves around moving beyond generic marketing copy and embracing "packaging design" for your brand narrative. Julian emphasizes that great storytelling is not just about writing; it's about creating shared beliefs—a network effect—that makes the product feel inevitable. He argues that modern copywriting must be viewed as a visual, structural design discipline, requiring extreme polish and simplicity to resonate with technical audiences (like developers).

## Key Points
- **Storytelling Mechanics:** Stories function like "shared beliefs" or a "network effect." The more people believe in a shared narrative around your product, the stronger that belief becomes, creating a flywheel effect.
- **The Value of Polish:** Initial marketing efforts focused on highly designed, polished websites and landing pages (like the initial Linear manifesto). These assets succeeded not because they were different, but because their unique design elements (e.g., CSS animations) encouraged users to recreate and share them on platforms like CodePen, generating organic buzz.
- **Copywriting is a Design Job:** The visual presentation of copy is as important as the words themselves. Copy must be tested for functional consistency across various environments (e.g., ensuring it looks good in both light mode and dark mode).
- **Specificity Over Opinionation:** When defining your value proposition, focus on being "purpose-built" rather than merely "opinionated." General purpose tools are infinitely flexible but lack specificity; a product that is narrowly built for a specific use case (like Linear) has a clearer, more compelling narrative.
- **The Simplicity Mandate:** The greatest trap in marketing copy is trying to make it look overly creative or complex. If the audience doesn't immediately understand what you are building or selling, the most beautiful copy will fail. Always prioritize clarity and simplicity.

## Tools & Resources
- **Mobbin:** A design inspiration tool used by over 1 million designers (Airbnb, Uber, Headspace) to view real-world examples of how different products handle specific UI patterns and flows. Recommended for understanding best practices in product design.
- **Framer:** Mentioned for its AI features:
    *   **Wire Framer:** Allows users to chat with AI to skip the blank canvas and generate responsive pages with structure and starter content.
    *   **Workshop:** Enables creating advanced components using natural language, eliminating the need for coding in visual effects.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit your value proposition:** Re-examine your core messaging and ensure you are describing *what* you do (purpose-built) rather than just stating an abstract belief about what you do.
- [ ] **Treat copy as design:** When writing marketing material, don't stop at the words. Consider how the typography, spacing, animations, and visual hierarchy will make those words feel on a live landing page.
- [ ] **Implement "Simplicity Testing":** Before publishing any major piece of content or messaging, ask: "If someone who knows nothing about our industry reads this, what is the single sentence that describes what we do?" If the answer isn't immediately clear, simplify the copy further.
- [ ] **Study UI Patterns:** Use Mobbin daily to analyze how top products handle specific user flows and patterns (e.g., onboarding, error states) for design inspiration.
