# Nad Chishtie - How to get hired as a designer at Lovable — Dive Club

## Summary
Nad Chishtie, Head of Design at Lovable, outlines a modern, high-signal thesis on what top-tier design talent must possess in today's rapidly scaling tech environment. The core message is a rejection of rigid specialization and the traditional "Individual Contributor (IC)" track. Nad argues that the most successful designers are now "generalists"—multidimensional individuals who possess incredible cross-domain intuition and the ability to run projects end-to-end.

He critiques outdated corporate structures, citing the over-engineered cross-functional squads (like the historical Spotify model) and the creation of siloed career tracks. These systems, while well-intentioned, normalize boundaries and discourage the kind of fluid, holistic thinking needed for true innovation. Instead, he emphasizes that designers must operate with the scope of a Product Manager, taking ownership not just of building something, but also of validating it and, critically, knowing when to delete it.

## Key Points
- **The Generalist Imperative:** The most successful employees are "incredibly cross domain." This means having deep intuition across multiple areas and being able to pivot quickly, rather than being limited by a specific title or role.
- **End-to-End Ownership (The PM Role):** Designers are expected to operate far outside the traditional design box. This includes defining *what* should be built, determining *when* a feature is working, and being fully empowered to *delete* features that fail. This process of running things end-to-end is the new standard.
- **Critique of Siloed Structures:** Traditional corporate models (like the "IC track") are criticized for drawing artificial boxes around talent. This leads to a "race to the common denominator" and stifles the multidimensional thinking required for scaling.
- **AI as an Accelerator, Not a Replacement:** AI is viewed as a core part of the modern workflow. It is excellent at filling knowledge gaps, allowing designers to achieve "zero to one" in almost any domain, provided they maintain overall project ownership.
- **The Power of the "Spicy Take":** A key mindset shift is adopting the confidence to challenge existing processes. Nad suggests that doing something and getting immediate feedback is vastly more effective than spending weeks of collective energy in consensus-building.

## Tools & Resources
- **Framer:** Positioned as a comprehensive design tool for the entire workflow. It allows users to sketch, iterate, structure, and publish to the web without jumping between tools. (Code RID for a free month of Pro).
- **Mobbin:** A resource for viewing how world-class apps use motion and animation. It is presented as a "cheat code" for improving design quality and user delight.

## Quotable
> "You're doing your job correctly when someone else tells you that you're stepping on their toes."
> "The most successful people internally are incredibly cross domain."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop End-to-End Ownership:** Practice taking ownership of projects from ideation through execution, validation, and eventual sunsetting. Don't just design the solution; own the outcome.
- [ ] **Acquire PM Skills:** Actively seek opportunities to define *what* should be built and *why*, rather than just *how* it should look. Treat design decisions as strategic business calls.
- [ ] **Build Cross-Domain Knowledge:** Intentionally learn about adjacent fields (e.g., basic engineering concepts, data infrastructure, business metrics) to broaden your intuition and make yourself less reliant on a single specialty.
- [ ] **Explore Framer:** Use Framer to build out portfolio pieces that demonstrate full web publishing capability, proving you can manage the entire design-to-live workflow.
- [ ] **Study Motion Design:** Regularly use Mobbin to analyze world-class app animations, integrating motion thinking into your design process to improve user delight and guidance.
