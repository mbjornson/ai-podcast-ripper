# Flora Guo - How to accelerate your design career with AI — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into modern design engineering, arguing that the practice of design is undergoing a fundamental shift driven by AI. The discussion centers on moving beyond static "pretty images" to designing and showcasing complex, interactive user experiences. Flora Guo, a founding design engineer at Paradigm, and the host, Ridd, explore how AI is not just a tool, but a mechanism for revealing the "map of the territory"—showing what is technically and creatively possible for designers.

A major theme is the strategic evolution of the designer's role. The conversation emphasizes that the modern designer must act as a "very good host," actively creating artifacts, hosting events, and intentionally building a personal network. Success is framed not as waiting for opportunity, but as proactively creating the conditions (or "geometry") for serendipity and collaboration.

## Key Points
- **AI Shifts Design Focus from Aesthetics to Interaction:** The industry is moving away from creating visual artifacts for show and toward designing and demonstrating complex interactions. Tools like Notion’s Prototype Playground allow designers to showcase the "nitty-gritty" of how things will actually function and be experienced by the end-user.
- **The Designer as a Host:** The core role of the designer is shifting from merely executing ideas to being the facilitator of ideas. The most successful designers are those who "show up" by creating artifacts, hosting events, or gathering people around a common, intentional interest.
- **Luck Has a Geometry:** Drawing from Soleil’s talk, the concept of "luck" is not random. It has a specific, learnable shape. To invite more luck, one must be constantly "liminally aware" of possibilities and proactively identify opportunities, much like studying historical maps of resource flow.
- **The Power of Initiation:** Career growth is heavily dependent on intentional networking. The most valuable actions are not waiting for connections, but proactively initiating contact (e.g., sending a specific DM to someone whose work you admire) and hosting specific, focused events to gather like-minded people.
- **Feedback Must Be Optimized for Speed:** Traditional feedback loops are insufficient in the age of AI. Specialized tools are needed to share prototypes, provide context (video walkthroughs), and gather precise, actionable feedback at the speed of modern development.

## Tools & Resources
- **InFlight:** A beta feedback tool designed for the speed of AI, allowing users to share prototypes, provide context, and gather specific feedback (voting on directions, green light to ship) in a single link.
- **Raycast:** A highly recommended productivity tool praised for keeping users "in flow" and deepening the power of using a computer.
- **Notion (Prototype Playground):** An example of how AI is empowering teams to move beyond static images and showcase functional interactions.

## Quotable

>

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit your "Surface Area for Luck":** Identify 3-5 areas of your professional interest and commit to creating one piece of content (an article, a short video, a mini-prototype) about it this week. The goal is to put something out online to become a magnet for people who care.
- [ ] **Shift from Asking to Offering:** When networking or proposing a collaboration, flip the script. Instead of asking, "What can I get from this person?" ask, "What specific value or space can I offer them?"
- [ ] **Implement Proactive Networking:** Identify 3 people in your industry whose work you admire. Instead of a generic connection request, send a highly specific DM detailing *exactly* what resonated with you in their work (e.g., "I loved the way you handled the state change on the checkout page").
- [ ] **Test a Productivity Tool:** Download and test a tool like Raycast to streamline your daily workflow and reduce friction in your digital environment.
- [ ] **Focus on Interaction, Not Imagery:** When building a portfolio or presenting a concept, prioritize demonstrating the *user flow* and *interaction* (the "how") over just showing the final, polished visual (the "what").
