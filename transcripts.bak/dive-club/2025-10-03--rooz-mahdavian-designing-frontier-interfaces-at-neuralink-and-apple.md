# Rooz Mahdavian - Designing frontier interfaces at Neuralink and Apple — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode explores the concept of "frontier interfaces"—design spaces where the interaction model is fundamentally unknown and undefined by current technology. The discussion centers on how Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs), exemplified by Neuralink, are moving human-computer interaction beyond physical constraints like hands or cursors, allowing intent to be captured directly from cognitive processes. Ruz Mahdavian, a Design Engineer at Neuralink, details the evolution of this field, tracing his own journey from early research in fMRI data prediction (reconstructing thoughts/dreams) to working on the Apple Watch Faces team and ultimately transitioning to neural interfaces.

Mahdavian emphasizes that historically, technology's capabilities were defined by its input mechanism (e.g., cursor clicks, multi-touch gestures). Frontier design requires thinking from first principles because the device itself can potentially change the interaction model entirely. He discusses how current systems decompose mental intent into thousands of motor movements, whereas a BCI aims to read that core intent much higher up the cognitive stack.

## Key Points
- **The Shift from Motor Intent to Cognitive Intent:** Traditional HCI requires decomposing abstract thought (e.g., "I want this image") into physical actions (moving hands to color a pixel). BCIs bypass this by reading the underlying, pure *intent* directly, which is much further up the cognitive stack.
- **Defining Frontier Interfaces:** A frontier interface is characterized by having no known or fixed interaction model. The device itself dictates the potential for entirely new user experiences that defy current design paradigms.
- **Proactive Computing (Apple Watch Model):** Early work on the Apple Watch demonstrated a shift toward "proactive computing." Instead of requiring active use, the computer surfaces the most relevant information (e.g., a workout prompt while walking) while fading into the background, making it useful without being actively engaged with.
- **Design Constraint Thinking:** When designing for truly novel interfaces (like BCIs), constraints are often too few to be meaningful. Designers must operate by first principles and focus on the *human experience* rather than existing technological limitations.
