# Brandon Jacoby - Seeing Taste vs. Creating Taste as a designer — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode centers on the concept of "taste"—the ability to create, rather than just see, good design. Brandon Jacoby, a designer with high-profile experience at companies like Early, Cash App, and X, argues that true creativity lies in knowing when to push boundaries and break rules, and when to maintain a steady, flowing approach. This nuanced balance is identified as a core human ability that current AI models struggle to replicate. Jacoby's discussion draws heavily on his experience at X, where he learned the value of radical decisiveness, first-principles thinking, and operating without the bureaucratic drag of traditional large organizations.

A significant portion of the conversation focuses on the modern design and product development process, particularly in growth areas like onboarding. Jacoby posits that while established patterns exist (working for 90% of products), the industry often suffers from two extremes: either rigidly adhering to these patterns (reverting to the mean) or attempting to reinvent the wheel unnecessarily. His background also provides insights into the value of deep, exploratory work, noting that the most fulfilling professional experiences often come from periods of uncertainty and incubation.

## Key Points
- **The Taste Framework:** True taste is not just about generating ideas; it is the ability to balance deep, focused exploration (zooming in) with the wisdom to let things be and not force novelty (zooming out).
- **Decisiveness over Process:** In high-velocity environments (like X), decision-making is instant ("Yeses, nos, there's no committees"). The biggest hurdle in large organizations is not necessarily the inability to get things done, but the time required to even *determine* if they can be done.
- **First Principles Thinking:** The most valuable skill is the ability to question every requirement and assumption. Instead of accepting a metric or process due to historical precedent, one must ask: "Why are we even tracking this metric?"
- **The Onboarding Paradox (Growth):** For product flows like onboarding, there are established patterns that work for 90% of products. The critical skill for a designer is knowing when to *follow* these proven patterns and when to *deviate* or radically innovate.
- **The Designer's Constraint:** Designers often develop a "calloused way of working" that makes them overly focused on micro-decisions and constraints. A key professional breakthrough is learning to turn this hyper-vigilance off to design without thinking.

## Tools & Resources

## Action Items
- [ ] **Practice First Principles:** When faced with an established process, metric, or requirement, immediately ask "Why?" until you reach the fundamental, undeniable truth of the need.
- [ ] **Embrace the Exploratory Phase:** When transitioning between roles or facing career uncertainty, dedicate time (e.g., 3 months) to non-committal, exploratory side projects to rediscover genuine professional interest outside of immediate pressure.
- [ ] **Master the Pattern/Novelty Balance:** When designing or solving a business problem, first identify the 90% proven patterns. Then, deliberately brainstorm 1-2 radical, counter-intuitive deviations that challenge those patterns.
- [ ] **Utilize AI for Structure:** Experiment with Framer's AI features (Wireframer/Workshop) to bypass the initial blank canvas paralysis and rapidly prototype structured layouts using natural language prompts.
- [ ] **Develop Decisiveness:** In group settings, practice making rapid, high-conviction decisions, even if imperfect, rather than getting stuck in committee-style consensus building.
