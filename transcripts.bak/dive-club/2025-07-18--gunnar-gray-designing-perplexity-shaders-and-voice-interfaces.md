# Gunnar Gray - Designing Perplexity, shaders, and voice interfaces — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the mindset shifts required for designing products in the age of Artificial Intelligence, featuring Gunnar Gray, who leads mobile design at Perplexity. Gunnar emphasizes that modern AI products should be viewed not as static applications but as new mediums defined by dynamic, outcome-driven interactions. His career trajectory—from founding designer at Artifact (founded by Instagram's Kevin Systrom and Mike Krieger) to leading high-velocity design at Perplexity—illustrates the value of being a generalist who can rapidly adapt across different technical domains, including shaders, voice interfaces, and complex AI features.

The core thesis is that traditional product development cycles (spending long periods in static tools like Figma) are becoming obsolete. The new paradigm demands designers operate in an environment where design and engineering blur, requiring continuous co-creation with working software to achieve a tighter feedback loop. This shift allows for unprecedented speed of iteration, enabling teams to test more hypotheses and reach conclusions much faster than ever before.

## Key Points
- **The Generalist Advantage:** The value of the generalist designer is "unreal" in the AI era because they can contribute across multiple technical domains (e.g., shaders, voice, mobile UI) rather than being siloed into one specific function.
- **Outcome-Driven Design:** AI native products shift from fixed user journeys to outcome-driven interactions. The user states what they want, and the system dynamically handles it in a non-deterministic way, making context awareness paramount.
- **Multimodality is Foundational:** Voice interfaces are critical components of modern AI design. Designers must understand the foundational mechanics of voice conversations and how prompt engineering affects output quality and performance.
- **The "Fire Hose" Mindset (Rapid Contribution):** High-growth tech environments require designers to bypass traditional, slow onboarding processes. The goal is immediate contribution—auditing existing products for inconsistencies and identifying new paradigms (like redesigning a settings view or building a zero-to-one feed) rather than waiting for perfect instructions.
- **Tighter Feedback Loops:** The most effective modern teams operate with the line between design and engineering blurred, constantly working on "working software." This proximity allows designers to immediately see outputs, understand technical limitations/possibilities, and iterate faster, maximizing the number of problem reps gained.

## Tools & Resources
- **Genway AI:** A tool described as "vibe researching" for validating product ideas. It runs AI agent interviews with a panel of global participants based on defined Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs), providing actionable insights quickly.
- **Raycast:** Presented as a comprehensive portal to AI, functioning as an advanced keyboard shortcut utility. Key features include supporting over 30 models (OpenAI, Claude, Perplexity) and allowing users to build presets and add attachments without needing a subscription or account.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Generalist Proficiency:** Actively seek out projects that force you to work across multiple technical domains (e.g., combining UI design with basic coding/shader concepts) rather than staying within a single discipline.
- [ ] **Master Outcome-Driven Thinking:** When designing, shift focus from mapping fixed screens and clicks to defining the desired *outcome* for the user, allowing the system's AI logic to determine the path (e.g., "The goal is X," not "Click A then B").
- [ ] **Practice Rapid Prototyping/Co-Creation:** When starting a project, prioritize getting working software or functional prototypes over perfecting static mockups in design tools. Aim for the fastest possible feedback loop with engineering partners.
- [ ] **Explore Voice UI Fundamentals:** Dedicate time to understanding how voice assistants function beyond simple Q&A. Study prompt engineering and conversational flow mapping as core design skills.
- [ ] **Utilize AI Research Tools:** Experiment with platforms like Genway AI or similar market validation tools to replace traditional, slow user interviews when validating initial product hypotheses.
