# Catt Small - How staff designers build influence and lead as ICs — Dive Club

## Summary
This episode provides a deep dive into how individual contributors (ICs), particularly designers, can build significant influence and strategically advance their careers without relying on management titles. The discussion centers on shifting from merely executing tasks to becoming a strategic partner who casts vision for the product. Kat Small, a staff designer at major tech companies like Asana and Etsy, shares that true influence is less about being the best hands-on worker and more about understanding organizational power structures and mastering communication.

The core thesis is that career growth requires building "social capital"—the currency of your professional relationships. Influence is built by strategically investing energy into high-leverage connections (stakeholders and leaders) and ensuring your ideas are memorable and easily repeatable by others ("memeable"). The advice moves beyond general design best practices, focusing instead on the business skills required to elevate one's impact from a team level to an organizational scale.

## Key Points
- **Social Capital is Influence:** Social capital is defined as measuring the currency of your professional relationships. Building it (e.g., by having leaders consult you or taking your ideas seriously) increases your vulnerability and ability to share bold, high-risk ideas.
- **The "Ideas Traveling" Signal:** A major sign of influence is when your ideas are mentioned in rooms where you were not present. This indicates that others have adopted and championed your concepts on your behalf.
- **Storytelling Over Dashboards:** To bridge the gap from senior to staff, focus less on presenting data (dashboards) and more on telling a compelling story that overlaps a *customer pain* with a *business problem*.
- **The Relationship Audit:** When charting a path to influence, first survey your current workplace relationships. Analyze if you are connecting primarily with peers or with high-level stakeholders (e.g., Director of Product Design).
- **Strategic Connection Building:** To build stakeholder allies, schedule regular (monthly/quarterly) meetings with leaders above your direct chain. Crucially, do not bring status updates; instead, ask open-ended questions like: "What is top of mind for you?" or "What are things the team wishes were doing/stopping doing?"
- **The Power of Being Useful:** The most effective way to build trust and influence with managers is to become a person who consistently gets things done for them. This increases your perceived value, making you a short-list candidate when they need help.
- **Energy Auditing (Contrarian Take):** Many ICs waste energy on valuable but non-core tasks (e.g., improving JIRA tickets, running ERGs). To advance, audit where you spend your time and ruthlessly cut activities that are not directly aligned with the title/scope of the next role you want. Focus only on problems you want to be known for solving.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct an Energy Audit:** Map your current workload and identify 2-3 activities that are valuable but *not* core to the next professional level (e.g., non-design process improvements). Commit to cutting or delegating these tasks immediately to free up focus time.
- [ ] **Initiate Stakeholder Mapping:** Identify 3-5 leaders/stakeholders who are one or two levels above your direct manager. Schedule a recurring, low-stakes meeting (monthly) with each person.
- [ ] **Shift Meeting Focus:** When meeting with stakeholders, do not present status updates. Instead, prepare 3 high-level questions focused on their strategic goals and pain points ("What is top of mind for you?" or "Where are the biggest blockers right now?").
- [ ] **Develop Memeable Frameworks:** Practice distilling complex ideas into simple frameworks, diagrams, or analogies that can be easily repeated by others. This improves your ability to communicate influence through others.
