# Hannah Hearth - Design Careers in the Age of AI — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode features Hannah Harf, Head of Product Design at Vercel, discussing the profound shifts occurring in the design industry due to AI and changing market dynamics. Harf's background provides a high-level view of modern design leadership, particularly within a fast-moving, technical company like Vercel. She frames the current era as a transition from an over-reliance on funding and exponential growth to a more sustainable, "course correction" phase.

The core discussion revolves around how design leaders must adapt their focus. The industry is shifting away from merely producing "beautiful things" to proving the ability to successfully "ship them to production and land them with customers." Harf argues that design leaders are now judged on outcomes and business impact, not just aesthetic outputs. Furthermore, she addresses the challenge of balancing increased team responsibility and the need for efficiency in a post-growth market, positioning AI tools as a necessary, if challenging, catalyst for change.

## Key Points
- **Shift from Aesthetics to Outcomes:** Design leaders are now primarily judged on the ability to ship products and achieve customer outcomes, rather than just producing beautiful designs.
- **The New Role of Senior Designers:** Contrary to older advice, senior staff and principal designers require significant support. They need design leaders who are "extremely present" to help them navigate organizational complexity and ambiguity, ensuring they move from generating mere outputs to achieving tangible outcomes.
- **Market Correction vs. Correction:** The current market slowdown is not a temporary "one-time mistake" correction, but a permanent, sustained "course correction." Companies must operate more like early-stage startups, maximizing output with leaner teams.
- **AI as a Necessity, Not a Feature:** The pressure to adopt AI tools is unavoidable. Harf suggests that while some companies use AI usage for performance tracking (a "ruthless" environment), the most effective approach is for teams to proactively use the tools to disrupt themselves and solve future business problems.
- **Leadership Challenge:** Design leaders must manage the tension between the need for efficiency (doing more with less) and the risk of team burnout, requiring careful planning in hiring and growth.

## Tools & Resources
- **Granola MCP:** An AI tool that allows users to connect their AI tools directly to meeting notes, enabling the ability to find specific topics, pull out action items, and answer questions based on meeting history.
- **Lovable:** An AI software development tool that is 71% better at solving complex tasks. Key features include intelligent planning, prompt queuing (stacking requests), automated testing (opening a browser, navigating flows, and investigating edge cases), and self-correction (fixing bugs on the spot).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop "Outcome-Oriented" Case Studies:** When building a portfolio, focus less on the design process and more on quantifiable business results (e.g., "Increased conversion rate by X%" or "Reduced onboarding time by Y minutes").
- [ ] **Master Ambiguity Management:** Practice breaking down highly ambiguous, complex problems into manageable, actionable steps, demonstrating how you guide senior team members through political or organizational complexity.
- [ ] **Adopt AI for Contextual Knowledge:** Experiment with tools like Granola MCP to connect AI to your professional context (meeting notes, project documentation) to rapidly synthesize information and identify action items.
- [ ] **Evaluate Team Efficiency:** When considering a new role or team structure, assess the ratio of required output to team size, looking for signs of sustainable growth rather than exponential, unsustainable scaling.
