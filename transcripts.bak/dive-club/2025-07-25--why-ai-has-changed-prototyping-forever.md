# Why AI has changed prototyping forever — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode details how Artificial Intelligence and advanced code integration are fundamentally transforming the prototyping process, moving design workflows away from linear, incremental improvements toward rapid, non-linear exploration. Nicholas Klein and Holly Lee, both from the Figma Make team, discuss how tools like Figma Make allow designers to build complex, functional prototypes—such as 3D modeling environments or interactive games—without writing a single line of code. This capability vastly accelerates the ability to test ideas and validate concepts early in the product lifecycle.

The core message is that while AI dramatically lowers the barrier to building a prototype, it does not solve the hardest problems. The designer's role remains critical: they must still define the problem space, articulate vague hunches into concrete requirements, and drive multiple cycles of iteration until the right solution emerges. The focus shifts from mastering design tools to mastering the process of deep user empathy and rapid hypothesis testing.

## Key Points
- **The Non-Linear Workflow:** AI makes prototyping progress feel "miniature" compared to previous years' incremental improvements (e.g., smart animate, intact components). It allows designers to build complex systems (like 3D environments) that were previously out of reach for non-developers.
- **User-Centric Interaction Design:** The goal is now "meeting users where their head is at instead of getting them to meet where the computer is at." This means focusing on the quality and feel of interaction—testing variables like speed or bounce rate—rather than just visual fidelity.
- **The Persistence of Hard Problems:** AI will help express the first idea, but solving truly complex problems (where you "just have a hunch" and can't put words to it) still requires deep human iteration. The tool is an accelerator for *expression*, not a replacement for *insight*.
- **Structural Flexibility:** Figma Make’s integration of code layers into a canvas environment allows designers to extend existing designs with interactive elements (like React components) without losing the intuitive, visual nature of design tools. This addresses the historical friction between production code and design exploration.

## Tools & Resources
- **Figma Make:** The primary tool discussed; an AI-powered platform that extends Figma's capabilities, allowing users to build complex prototypes from simple ideas or existing designs.
- **Lovable:** An alternative prototyping tool highlighted by Nicholas Klein for its ability to create real software without touching code, encouraging designers not to get "stuck in a rectangle land."
- **Granola:** A specialized AI meeting transcription and note-taking tool designed specifically for design critique sessions ("design crit"), allowing users to capture structured feedback and action items while remaining present in the conversation.

## Quotable
> "Hard problems are going to stay hard... AI will help you express your first idea, of course. And then Figma Make will help you build a first prototype faster. But as a designer, you'll still have to do that [iteration] a lot of times until you find the right solution."

> "We're meeting users where their head is at instead of getting them to meet where the computer is at."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Focus on improving *problem definition* skills. Practice articulating vague, complex business or design problems into testable hypotheses before jumping to solutions.
- [ ] **Tool Exploration (Prototyping):** Experiment with Figma Make and Lovable to bypass traditional prototyping constraints and build functional prototypes using minimal code knowledge.
- [ ] **Process Improvement:** Implement an AI meeting transcription tool like Granola during all design critiques or stakeholder meetings to ensure structured capture of feedback, questions, and actionable items, rather than relying on manual note-taking.
