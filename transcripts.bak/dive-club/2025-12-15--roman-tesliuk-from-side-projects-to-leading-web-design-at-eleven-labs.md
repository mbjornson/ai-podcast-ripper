# Roman Tesliuk - From side projects to leading web design at Eleven Labs — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the process of scaling web design for a rapidly evolving, complex AI company (Eleven Labs). Roman Tesliuk details how they developed a cohesive brand identity—moving beyond simple aesthetics—by linking the visual design system to the core function of the product: voice and sound. The process involved extensive collaboration between internal teams and external agencies, culminating in the use of abstract concepts like "Cluttony patterns" to create a unique, resonant visual language.

The discussion moves beyond mere branding to focus on advanced UX architecture. Roman outlines how to design for highly complex, multi-faceted AI products (like conversational agents), requiring a multi-layered approach. This involves structuring the user experience to guide the user through three distinct levels of interaction: passive visualization, explanatory video content, and finally, active, hands-on product experience. The overall message is that modern web design for AI must be highly interactive, telling a story through its structure, not just its visuals.

## Key Points
- **Connecting Abstract Concepts to Design:** The brand identity was anchored not just in mood, but in the product's core function (voice). They used "Cluttony patterns"—patterns formed by specific frequencies of music—to create a visual system that was inherently connected to the concept of sound, giving the design depth and meaning.
- **The Multi-Layered UX Strategy:** For complex AI products, the landing page must guide the user through three distinct interaction layers:
    1.  **Layer 1 (Immediate):** Visualization of the core function (e.g., chat/tool calling) to provide instant understanding.
    2.  **Layer 2 (Passive):** Explanatory content (videos, use cases) to build understanding and scope.
    3.  **Layer 3 (Active):** A dedicated, integrated widget or component that forces the user to *interact* with the product (e.g., talking to the agent) to achieve conviction.
- **The Value of Semi-Technical Design Skills:** Roman emphasizes that being "semi-technical" (able to go into the code and play with it) is crucial for modern designers. This capability allows for faster iteration, deeper collaboration with developers, and the ability to prototype complex interactions quickly, which is vital in fast-paced startup environments.
- **Design for Scale and Sub-Products:** When a company has many diverse sub-products (like Eleven Labs), the design system must be flexible enough to maintain a unified brand while allowing different product lines to have unique, story-driven applications.
- **UX Safety Net:** When designing high-stakes, conversion-focused pages, always reserve space (e.g., the bottom 5-10% of the viewport) to ensure critical CTAs or information are never cut off or obscured by scrolling or other elements.

## Tools & Resources
- **Granola:** A product used for organizing and synthesizing meeting notes, user research, and key themes. (Mentioned as a highly effective productivity tool for designers/researchers).
- **Lovable:** A platform that allows users to build and customize online stores powered by Shopify quickly, reducing the intimidation factor of custom e-commerce development.
- **Figma/Design Tools:** The core platform used for the brand experimentation and pattern generation.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Semi-Technical Proficiency:** Dedicate time to learning basic front-end code (HTML/CSS/JavaScript) to move beyond pure visual design and understand implementation constraints.
- [ ] **Adopt the 3-Layer UX Framework:** When designing for any complex product (AI, SaaS, etc.), structure the experience to move the user from *Visualization* $\rightarrow$ *Education* $\rightarrow$ *Active Interaction*.
- [ ] **Practice Concept-to-Visual Mapping:** Instead of starting with aesthetics, start with the product's core function or scientific principle (e.g., sound, data flow, physics) and let that concept dictate the visual patterns and color palette.
- [ ] **Explore Productivity Tools:** Investigate tools like Granola for synthesizing complex research and meeting notes, improving the efficiency of the design/research workflow.
- [ ] **Focus on Iterative Prototyping:** Given the speed of tech, prioritize rapid, functional prototypes over polished, static mockups to test interaction flow early.
