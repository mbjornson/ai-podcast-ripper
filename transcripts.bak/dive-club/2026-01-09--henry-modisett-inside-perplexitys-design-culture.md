# Henry Modisett - Inside Perplexity's design culture — Dive Club

## Summary
Henry Modisett, VP of Design at Perplexity, discusses how design must be viewed not merely as an aesthetic function, but as a core strategic "weapon" that can be used to beat competition through marketing and product experience. The central theme of the episode is the profound challenge of scaling a design culture: moving from the chaotic, high-velocity "move fast, break things" mindset of a startup to the mature, process-driven state of a major industry player. This transition requires revisiting foundational values and processes that were necessary for early growth but become liabilities when the goal shifts from gaining users to retaining them.

The discussion details Perplexity's organizational structure, which is designed to handle massive parallel growth without internal collision. The design team is segmented into three non-overlapping sub-organizations: the Creative Studio (handling all brand/marketing efforts), Operations (managing internal function and merch), and Product Design (managing the consumer, enterprise, and browser products). This structure allows for deep specialization and rapid execution, enabling the company to maintain agility while scaling to a large, high-quality team of 31 people.

## Key Points
- **Design as a Strategic Weapon:** Design cannot be treated as a simple requirement; you must articulate a clear theory of *why* design matters to the business (e.g., "We will beat the competition because of our design commitment").
- **The Scaling Value Shift:** Early-stage startups prioritize **Velocity** (shipping and learning). Mature companies must shift focus to **Retention** (keeping the user) while still maintaining growth. This requires fundamentally different processes and values.
- **The Power of Non-Collision:** Perplexity benefits from a product structure where features do not inherently conflict (unlike a typical social feed). This allows the design team to ship in parallel without competing metrics or conflicting goals, which is a massive organizational advantage.
- **Specialized Organizational Structure:** The design team is split into three distinct, non-colliding sub-orgs:
    1. **Creative Studio:** Handles all external brand needs (motion, video, global advertising, brand marketing).
    2. **Operations:** Manages internal function, processes, and supporting revenue streams (e.g., Perplexity Supply merch store).
    3. **Product Design:** Manages the core product surface areas (Consumer, Enterprise, Browser).
- **Hiring for Complexity:** To scale in the AI/tech space, hiring must prioritize individuals who are highly technical, comfortable with non-deterministic product experiences, and capable of working across complex, undefined surface areas.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a Strategic Design Thesis:** Stop saying "design is important." Instead, write a clear, actionable theory (a "weapon") explaining how design will directly solve a market problem or beat a competitor.
- [ ] **Audit Your Scaling Values:** Identify the core values and processes that got you to your current success. Determine which of those values are now hindering retention or maturity, and plan for a formal process shift.
- [ ] **Implement Parallelization:** If managing a product team, structure the work into distinct, non-colliding sub-teams (like the three sub-orgs mentioned) to prevent competing metrics and conflicting goals.
- [ ] **Focus on T-Shaped Technical Talent:** When hiring, prioritize candidates who are not only skilled in design but also possess deep technical knowledge, are comfortable with ambiguity, and can contribute code or highly technical problem-solving.
- [ ] **Explore Automation/Synthesis Tools:** Investigate tools like Granola or similar knowledge management systems to synthesize vast amounts of qualitative data (user research, meeting notes) into actionable, high-level synopses.
