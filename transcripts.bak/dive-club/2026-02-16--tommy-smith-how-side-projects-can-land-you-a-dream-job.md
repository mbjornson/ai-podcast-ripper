# Tommy Smith - How side projects can land you a dream job — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode centers on the strategic use of side projects as a powerful professional asset, arguing that a personal, passion-driven project can reveal a designer or engineer's true capabilities far better than limited client work. Tommy Smith, the guest, details how he overhauled his portfolio to reflect this philosophy, moving away from "over-engineered" visual flair toward a clean, highly digestible, and content-focused layout. His goal was to strip away unnecessary design details to let the quality and depth of his work speak for itself.

Smith’s core strategy involves making his side projects the most visible elements of his portfolio. He built a complex calendar application ("Crono") not just to showcase design skills, but specifically to challenge himself with difficult front-end logic (like handling time zones and complex drag-and-drop interactions). This demonstrated a deep commitment to mastering technical complexity, positioning him as a high-potential design engineer rather than just a visual designer.

## Key Points
- **Side Projects > Client Work:** In the modern job market, side projects are often more revealing than professional client work because they showcase what you *want* to build and what you are genuinely proud of, rather than what you were contracted to do and "boxed into."
- **The "Less is More" Portfolio Principle:** Avoid over-designing or making your portfolio feel like a product itself. The goal is maximum digestibility and scanability. The best portfolios are clean, restrained, but still possess unique, subtle elements (like the floating avatar) to convey personality.
- **Showcase Technical Depth, Not Just Aesthetics:** When building a side project, choose a domain that forces you to learn complex, non-visual logic (e.g., a calendar app dealing with time zones, drag-to-create events). This demonstrates engineering thinking alongside design skill.
- **Strategic Placement is Crucial:** Do not bury your best work. If a side project is your strongest piece, it must be front and center—in the sidebar, the biography, and listed prominently in the body of work—to ensure immediate visibility.
- **The Power of Animation Tools:** Tools like Rive are game-changers because they allow non-motion designers to create highly polished, lightweight, and interactive animations (like cursor-following mascots), elevating the perceived quality of the entire portfolio.

## Tools & Resources
- **Rive:** A powerful, lightweight tool for creating complex, interactive animations (described as "Flash 2.0"). Excellent for adding polish and personality to a portfolio.
- **Mobbin:** A resource that allows designers to analyze how world-class apps use motion to guide, delight, and create seamless user experiences.
- **Paper:** A design tool that uses real CSS, meaning designs are inherently code. This allows designers to one-click copy and paste layouts directly into a coding agent (like React).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a "Logic-First" Side Project:** Instead of building a visually appealing app, build one that forces you to grapple with a complex, non-visual problem (e.g., time zone management, state machine logic, complex data filtering).
- [ ] **Audit Your Portfolio for Digestibility:** Review your current portfolio and ruthlessly strip away unnecessary visual flair (drop shadows, excessive gradients, busy layouts). Prioritize clean, scannable content.
- [ ] **Master a Modern Animation Tool:** Dedicate time to learning Rive or similar tools to add professional, interactive polish to your personal brand/portfolio.
- [ ] **Elevate Your Technical Storytelling:** When presenting a project, spend 70% of the time explaining the *technical challenge* and *process* (the "how" and "why") and only 30% on the final visual output.
- [ ] **Explore Code-Native Design Tools:** Experiment with tools like Paper to understand how design directly translates into production code, bridging the gap between design and engineering.
