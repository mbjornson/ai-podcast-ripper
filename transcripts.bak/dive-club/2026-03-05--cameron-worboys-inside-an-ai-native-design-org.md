# Cameron Worboys - Inside an AI-native design org — Dive Club

## Summary
The episode features Cam Worboys, Head of Product Design at Cash App, discussing how modern design organizations must adapt to the exponential pace of technological change, particularly driven by AI. Worboys argues that the biggest bottleneck in the tech industry is no longer the speed of building, but the operational capability to move a product from concept to live customer experience. He details how his team has successfully eliminated historical trade-offs, proving that high quality and high velocity are not mutually exclusive.

Worboys' core thesis revolves around the necessity of structural and process overhaul. He describes moving away from the traditional, multi-layered "pizza team" model—which often requires excessive management overhead—to a hyper-lean, flattened structure. This organizational shift, combined with the ability of AI tools to increase output by 20x to 100x, has allowed his team to drastically reduce bureaucracy and focus purely on building and iterating.

## Key Points
- **The Operational Bottleneck:** The primary industry blocker in the next two years will be operational execution—the ability to move a built product through the "operational cogs" to the customer—not the speed of development itself.
- **The Death of Trade-offs:** The historical trade-off between high quality (slow) and high speed (low quality) no longer exists. Achieving both requires running extremely lean teams and leveraging tools for 10X output.
- **Structural Flattening:** Traditional product development structures often require multiple management layers. Worboys notes that by flattening the organization (e.g., limiting management to three layers), teams become significantly more nimble, allowing them to react instantly to market changes without navigating bureaucratic inertia.
- **Process Deletion is Key:** Many "professional" design processes, such as mandatory design critiques, are often inefficient time sinks. Worboys advocates for systematically identifying and deleting processes that do not demonstrably improve output, allowing the team to run on minimal, highly focused rituals.
- **Quality through Iteration, Not Theory:** Quality does not come from a designer "pontificating about the future of software" in isolation. It emerges from rapid iteration, the speed of failure, and the ability to quickly experiment and course-correct.

## Tools & Resources
- **Lovable:** An AI tool described as the easiest way to build software today. Key features include solving complex tasks (71% better), intelligent planning, prompt queuing, and automated testing (opening browsers, navigating flows, and fixing bugs).
- **Granola MCP:** A tool that connects AI capabilities directly to meeting notes. It allows users to find specific topics, pull out action items, and answer questions based on historical meeting context.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Challenge Organizational Hierarchy:** Advocate for structural flattening within your team or company. Identify and eliminate unnecessary management layers or reporting structures that slow down decision-making.
- [ ] **Audit Rituals:** Systematically review all mandatory team rituals (e.g., weekly meetings, mandatory design critiques, status reports). Ask: "Does this process demonstrably improve output, or is it just a habit?"
- [ ] **Prioritize Output over Process:** Shift the team mindset from "following the process" to "achieving the outcome." Focus on rapid, low-fidelity experimentation and iteration speed as the primary measure of quality.
- [ ] **Explore AI Automation:** Investigate AI tools like Lovable or similar automation platforms to handle repetitive, complex tasks (like automated testing or data synthesis) to free up human bandwidth for high-level problem-solving.
- [ ] **Develop "Operational Thinking":** When planning a project, focus less on the initial build phase and more on the operational pipeline—how the product will move through the organization to reach the customer.
