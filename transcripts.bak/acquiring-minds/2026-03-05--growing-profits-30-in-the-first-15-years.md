# Growing Profits 30% in the First 1.5 Years — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode focuses on the process of acquiring an existing small business—specifically a distribution company—and navigating the associated risks and financial strategies required for growth. The guest, Jonathan Taylor, successfully transitioned from a 15-year tech career at companies like Google and DoorDash into owning AEK Technology, a distribution business. He reports significant early success, achieving 40% revenue growth and 30% EBITDA growth in just the first year and a half of ownership.

A core discussion revolves around mitigating common risks inherent in distribution businesses, such as supplier concentration (where many products come from a single source). Furthermore, the conversation provides crucial financial advice regarding capital structure, warning against "over-equitization"—the temptation to give away excessive ownership equity simply to fund an acquisition or project, which can weaken both the business's and the founder's personal balance sheet.

The episode also addresses the career pivot itself, detailing how Taylor leveraged his professional experience (finance, operations) and a sense of "now or never" urgency—particularly as he approached 40—to execute a self-funded search for an established business rather than starting from scratch.

## Key Points
- **Growth Metrics:** Jonathan achieved impressive early growth in AEK Technology: 40% revenue increase and 30% EBITDA increase within the first 18 months of ownership.
- **Risk Mitigation (Supplier Concentration):** A major risk for distribution businesses is reliance on a single supplier; this must be proactively identified and mitigated during due diligence.
- **Financial Caution (Over-Equitization):** Be wary of giving up too much equity just to raise capital. The optimal strategy favors *less leverage* and *more cash reserves*, which provides greater stability and flexibility post-acquisition.
- **The Value of Part-Time Searching:** Contrary to conventional wisdom that requires full-time dedication, conducting a self-funded search while working full-time can be highly effective. This method helps maintain a high standard for deal quality because the founder is extending their runway (and thus, maintaining financial discipline and judgment).
- **The "Now or Never" Pivot:** For career pivots, the sense of urgency—whether driven by age, life stage, or professional questioning—can be a powerful catalyst. This psychological deadline can force action when comfort might otherwise dictate staying in a stable role.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Financial Discipline:** When evaluating potential investments or business deals, prioritize maintaining strong cash reserves and minimizing excessive debt/equity dilution over maximizing immediate ownership percentage.
- [ ] **Conduct a Risk Audit:** If involved in supply chain or distribution businesses, immediately audit the supplier base to identify single points of failure (supplier concentration) and develop alternative sourcing strategies.
- [ ] **Test Part-Time Search Viability:** If considering an entrepreneurial pivot, experiment with dedicating structured, limited time (e.g., 10 hours a week) to researching acquisitions before quitting your current job. This maintains financial stability while testing commitment.
