# From $1m to $25m in 6 Years — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode details the entrepreneurial journey of Jeff Flannery, who successfully transitioned from a high-earning, but ultimately non-scalable, service provider to a successful business acquirer. Flannery spent 18 years running an executive search firm, earning up to $980,000 at its peak. However, he realized his business was merely a "practice"—highly dependent on his personal service delivery and time—and therefore lacked true equity value or scalability. This realization led him to pivot into the field of Entrepreneurial Transition and Acquisition (ETA) franchising.

In his second chapter, Flannery acquired his first Hand & Stone franchise in 2019, building an empire of 17 locations over just six years. His current metrics demonstrate significant success: over $25 million in revenue and over $2.5 million in EBITDA. The discussion provides a powerful framework for entrepreneurs: the difference between building a high-income "practice" (a job) and building a scalable, asset-backed "business" (equity).

## Key Points
- **The "Practice" vs. "Business" Distinction:** The most critical insight is that a "practice" (like a service-based recruiting firm or law office) is not a saleable asset because its revenue is tied directly to the founder's time and effort (Key Man Risk). A true business, by contrast, builds scalable systems and assets.
- **The Power of the Pivot:** Flannery started his successful acquisition journey at age 49, demonstrating that pivoting into a new, scalable model is possible regardless of age or previous high earnings.
- **Scaling Metrics:** Flannery's rapid growth provides concrete targets: building 17 locations and achieving over $25M in revenue and $2.5M in EBITDA within a six-year window.
- **Advanced Deal Mechanics (Sponsor Insights):** The sponsors highlighted crucial, non-obvious areas where buyers often overpay or miscalculate value:
    *   **Networking Capital & Net Debt:** These are common areas where buyers overpay during due diligence.
    *   **Revenue Normalization:** Specific focus on normalizing accounts payable and defining project-based revenue streams.
    *   **Financing Special Cases:** Understanding how banks underwrite loans for "add-ons" (tuck-ins) and "carve-outs" (businesses without their own tax return) is essential for inorganic growth.

## Tools & Resources

- **Aspen HR:** A Professional Employer Organization (PEO) that provides HR compliance, payroll, and benefits, specifically offering a "complimentary HR diligence checklist" for new CEOs/owners.
- **Hand & Stone:** The franchise system used in the example, representing a scalable, asset-based business model.

## Quotable

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Business for Key Man Risk:** Identify if your current income is dependent on your personal time/service delivery. If so, develop systems and processes to decouple your income from your direct effort.
- [ ] **Research PEO Services:** If you plan to acquire a business, immediately investigate Professional Employer Organizations (PEOs) like Aspen HR to ensure compliance and robust HR infrastructure from day one.
- [ ] **Study Advanced Deal Mechanics:** Focus on understanding how to properly normalize accounts payable, define project-based revenue, and correctly calculate net debt to avoid overpaying for an acquisition.
- [ ] **Target Scalable Assets:** When considering a pivot, prioritize acquiring businesses that are asset-heavy and system-dependent, rather than service-heavy and person-dependent.
- [ ] **Set Aggressive Growth Goals:** Use the 6-year, $25M revenue/17 location model as a benchmark for achievable, aggressive growth in your own industry.
