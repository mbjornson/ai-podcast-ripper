# Taking a Single-City Acquisition Nationwide — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode features Edward McDonald, who successfully navigated the full cycle of an acquisition and exit using the traditional search fund model. He started by acquiring Botanical Designs—an indoor plant business with 80 employees in Seattle—and grew it through multiple stages: expanding to three states, continuing as CEO under Private Equity (PE) ownership, and ultimately growing it further to eight branches and 200 employees.

The core discussion revolves around the advantages of traditional search funds compared to self-funded models. While self-funding offers greater autonomy from a cap table perspective, Edward argues that the resources provided by traditional search—specifically capital for due diligence and operational cash flow—are crucial "oxygen" in small business acquisitions. These funds provide necessary latitude to execute the search properly without constantly having to nickel-and-dime every decision.

## Key Points
- **Cash Flow as Operational Oxygen:** The primary critique of traditional search is its perceived lack of independence, but Edward counters that the capital and cash flow provided by the fund are essential resources. Being perpetually tight on cash constrains freedom of movement and limits due diligence scope.
- **The Value of Search Capital:** Having dedicated budget for deal fees and proper due diligence (e.g., Quality of Earnings reports) allows searchers to make better, more objective decisions that would be impossible if relying solely on personal funds or debt.
- **Traditional Model Benefits (Beyond Money):** Beyond capital, traditional search offers invaluable mentorship and networking access from experienced investors and former company operators—a critical support system for less experienced acquirers.
- **Search Scope & Diligence:** Successful searching requires being broad initially (e.g., B2B recurring revenue services) but disciplined in narrowing the focus. Due diligence must be thorough, especially regarding financial metrics; Edward advises caution against high valuation multiples (e.g., avoiding deals at 15x EBITDA if it's unrealistic).
- **Cybersecurity Risk:** SMBs often have "unseen cyber gaps" that become immediate operational problems upon acquisition. These vulnerabilities hide in people, processes, technology, and data, requiring a professional risk assessment before closing the deal.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Financial Planning:** When considering an acquisition, evaluate the available capital not just for the purchase price, but specifically for robust due diligence (QoE reports, legal fees) and operational runway post-close.
- [ ] **Risk Mitigation:** Prioritize a comprehensive cybersecurity risk assessment on any target business *before* signing an LOI to identify hidden vulnerabilities in people, processes, or data.
- [ ] **Networking/Mentorship:** Actively seek out experienced investors or former searchers who can provide guidance and referrals, recognizing that this network support is as valuable as the capital itself.
- [ ] **Due Diligence Focus:** When evaluating potential acquisitions, maintain a broad initial search scope (e.g., B2B recurring revenue) but be disciplined in narrowing it down based on realistic valuation metrics and operational feasibility.
