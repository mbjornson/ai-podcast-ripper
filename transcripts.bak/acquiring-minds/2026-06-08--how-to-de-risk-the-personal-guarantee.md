# How to De-Risk the Personal Guarantee — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
This episode focuses on a major systemic barrier in the small business acquisition market: the personal guarantee (PG) requirement associated with SBA loans. The PG forces potential buyers—even highly qualified, seasoned entrepreneurs—to risk their entire personal assets, including their homes, making many otherwise viable deals too risky to pursue. Brendan Burdett and Ryan Conner, founders of Braddock Road Insurance Corporation (BRIC), introduce Personal Guarantee Insurance (PGI) as a product designed specifically to mitigate this catastrophic financial exposure.

The discussion details how PGI works by stepping in when corporate assets are liquidated following a business default. Instead of the lender having to pursue the owner's personal wealth entirely, PGI provides a direct payout to the lender, significantly reducing the remaining debt burden on the entrepreneur. The founders emphasize that while PGI does not eliminate all risk, it fundamentally changes the financial calculus of failure, making acquisition opportunities accessible to more people and improving capital flow within the SBA ecosystem.

## Key Points
- **The Personal Guarantee Barrier:** The PG is identified as a primary deterrent in the searcher community, preventing many qualified buyers from entering deals due to the overwhelming personal risk involved (losing all assets).
- **PGI Mechanism & Coverage:** PGI provides coverage that pays out directly to the lender. Specifically, BRIC covers an estimated 50% of the remaining loan balance after corporate assets have been liquidated by the lender.
- **Risk Mitigation Scale:** The product's value is framed as reducing personal financial risk from a "10 to a 5," making failure less devastating and more manageable for the entrepreneur.
- **Systemic Benefit:** Beyond individual protection, PGI aims to improve recovery rates for banks and the SBA while empowering entrepreneurs who might otherwise be forced off the sidelines due to fear of total loss.
- **Deal Evaluation Framework (Secondary Signal):** The episode also promoted a high-signal framework by Daniel Duran from Acquisition Lab Capital: using a proprietary 22-dimension rubric to quickly evaluate SMB deals, allowing searchers to separate viable opportunities from time sinks ("Score It or Skip It").
