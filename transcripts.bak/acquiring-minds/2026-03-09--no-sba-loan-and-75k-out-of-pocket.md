# No SBA Loan and $75k Out of Pocket — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode discusses the process of acquiring existing small businesses, using the journey of guest Megan McGee as a primary case study. Megan, who has a background in entrepreneurship and business management (MBA from Darden/UVA), navigated a multi-year search to acquire a 50-year-old short-term rental business. Her initial search followed the formalized "Search Fund" model, which she found valuable for its structured mentorship and access to diverse investor capital.

The discussion highlights the significant operational complexities involved in acquisitions, ranging from transferring utilities and accounts to setting up payroll and navigating systems. Furthermore, the segment provides a crucial lesson in due diligence: even highly promising deals can fail if their revenue streams are dependent on temporary or unstable funding sources, such as government grants.

## Key Points
- **Acquisition Opportunity:** Acquiring existing businesses (like the B&B mentioned) offers a proven model and longevity, often providing a more stable entry point than starting from scratch.
- **Search Fund Models:** There are multiple paths to acquiring a business. Megan compared the single-sponsor/accelerator model to the traditional search fund. She ultimately favored the traditional path for its financial security and the benefit of a diversified group of mentors and investors.
- **The Pitfall of Temporary Funding:** A major lesson learned during Megan’s search was the risk of over-relying on non-sustainable revenue. An LOI for an education services company ($4M+ EBITDA) fell through because a significant portion of its revenue was derived from expiring government funds (ESSER funds), demonstrating the need to validate core, sustainable payer sources.
- **Operational Transition Planning:** The post-closing phase is highly complex and time-consuming. Critical tasks include opening new bank/payment processor accounts, moving registrations/utilities, accessing systems/passwords, and setting up payroll/benefits—all of which must be managed immediately after closing.
- **Financial Outsourcing:** For new owners, managing core financial tasks (payroll, billing, bookkeeping, financials) is critical but distracting. Outsourcing these functions (e.g., via System 6) allows the owner to focus on high-leverage leadership and growth work.
