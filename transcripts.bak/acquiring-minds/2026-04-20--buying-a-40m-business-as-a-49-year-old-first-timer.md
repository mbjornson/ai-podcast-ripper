# Buying a $40m Business as a 49-Year-Old First-Timer — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode challenges the common narrative that successful acquisition entrepreneurship is limited to younger individuals. The guest, Greg Hirsch, provides a detailed account of his journey, demonstrating that years of corporate experience and operational maturity can be significant assets. After a long career in corporate sales (Cisco Foods), Greg realized his "independent, strong, fierce spirit" was stifled by corporate structures. He initially struggled with the "biz buy sell" startup model, learning that his true skillset was not building from zero, but rather taking existing businesses and implementing robust sales cultures and processes to drive growth.

His successful pivot came when he became an operating partner for a specialized trucking company. He and his partners identified a critical niche—specialized flatbed trucking—that had never been successfully rolled up. Crucially, he capitalized on the post-recession market environment, where second and third-generation owners were seeking stability and growth through consolidation. By focusing on building infrastructure and processes, Greg was able to scale the platform company significantly, proving that structured growth and market timing are more critical than age or initial capital.

## Key Points
- **Age is an Asset, Not a Barrier:** The episode directly refutes the notion that acquisition entrepreneurship is only for the young. Greg's success at 49 demonstrates that accumulated experience and professional maturity are valuable assets.
- **The Value of Operational Skillset:** Greg learned that his strength was not in being a "startup guy" (zero-to-one), but in improving and scaling existing operations. His core skill was implementing "sales culture and drive processes."
- **Market Timing and Niche Identification:** The acquisition strategy was highly dependent on identifying an underserved niche (specialized flatbed trucking) and capitalizing on a specific economic moment (the post-recession desire for "resiliency" and consolidation).
- **The Growth Formula:** Sustained growth (e.g., the target of 20% annual growth) is not achieved by luck, but by building out the necessary infrastructure and processes to support that growth.
- **The Importance of the Personal Angle:** The segment featuring his wife, Barbara, highlights that buying a business is an "all-in proposition" that requires considering the impact on the entire household and partnership.
