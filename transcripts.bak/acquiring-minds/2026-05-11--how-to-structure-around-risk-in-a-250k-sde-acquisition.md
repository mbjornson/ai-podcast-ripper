# How to Structure Around Risk in a $250k SDE Acquisition — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
This episode features Alicia Powers, owner of Price Builders, discussing her acquisition of a design-build general contractor in San Diego. The core message revolves around the importance of deep operational experience, a genuine passion for the business, and a structured approach to risk mitigation, particularly when acquiring a business outside the typical searcher profile. The episode highlights that experience in related fields – air traffic control, elevator management, and even corporate governance – can provide a unique advantage. Andrew Hipper and Daniel Duran from Acquisition Lab Capital are introduced as resources for acquirers seeking to identify early warning signs of potential problems in SMB deals. Finally, the podcast announces the upcoming launch of a searchable archive of past “Acquiring Minds” episodes, called the ETA database.

## Key Points
- **Operational Experience is Paramount:** Alicia’s background in air traffic control, elevator management, and corporate governance provided a critical advantage in understanding and running a construction business, particularly one involving tradespeople. Her ability to lead and learn from experienced individuals was a key differentiator.
- **Structured Deal Terms for Risk Mitigation:** Alicia’s ability to negotiate deal terms that mitigated risk stemmed from her experience with previous ADU projects. She recognized the potential pitfalls of a standard searcher approach and proactively structured the acquisition to protect her investment.
- **Judgment Calls Over Due Diligence:** The episode emphasizes that experienced acquirers don’t rely solely on extensive due diligence. Instead, they focus on making astute judgment calls based on factors like seller motivation, growth trends, and advertising spend – often overlooked by less experienced buyers.
- **Understanding the “Dirty Work”:** Alicia’s experience in the “grease and pants dirty” world of construction, contrasted with the corporate environment of Schindler, highlights the value of hands-on experience and a connection to the realities of the business.
- **Service Business Caveats:** The conversation touches on the challenges of acquiring elevator servicing businesses, specifically the pressure to reduce service levels due to rising costs, a common trap for inexperienced buyers.
