# Year 1 in a $300k SDE Services Business — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode features Scott Crosby, owner of American Services St. Louis, who details his journey acquiring a small service business with $300k in SDE and four employees. Scott's background is highly varied—spanning politics, media, construction CEO roles, and software sales—giving him a unique perspective on career pivots and self-determination. His core message for aspiring "searchers" (business buyers) is that the transition to ownership is transformative, often leading to professional fulfillment that far outweighs previous employment experiences.

Scott emphasizes the critical importance of reinvestment in the early stages of ownership. He advises against taking personal income out immediately, advocating instead for plowing all capital back into the business's growth and stability. The discussion also highlights the necessity of comprehensive due diligence, covering not only legal issues but also often overlooked vulnerabilities like cybersecurity gaps within people, processes, and technology.

## Key Points
- **The Reinvestment Principle:** In the early years post-acquisition, do not take money out for personal use. Scott advises that all capital should be "plowed back in" to stabilize and grow the business.
- **The Trigger Point of Ownership:** A major defining moment for Scott was when his previous employer withheld commission payments despite a record quarter. This financial control loss served as the catalyst, shifting his mindset from employee dependency to the desire for self-directed destiny—a key signal for those considering buying a business.
- **Leverage Local Resources:** Searchers are strongly advised to utilize local economic resource centers, specifically Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs). These are presented as free and powerful tools often overlooked by entrepreneurs.
- **The Value of Network over Paycheck:** Scott's first defining career choice was prioritizing networking opportunities and long-term professional development in politics over a higher immediate salary offered in the wine industry, illustrating that strategic connections are more valuable than short-term income.
- **Cybersecurity Risk Assessment is Critical:** Small businesses often harbor unseen cyber gaps (in people, processes, technology) that become operational problems immediately upon acquisition. A formal risk assessment must be conducted *before* day one to mitigate these expensive risks.
