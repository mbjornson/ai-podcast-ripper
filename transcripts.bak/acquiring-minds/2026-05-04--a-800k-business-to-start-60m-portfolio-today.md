# A $800k Business to Start, $60m Portfolio Today — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
Andrew Blazenko, founder of Eterna Equity, is an acquisition entrepreneur who has successfully built a portfolio generating $60 million in aggregate revenue and $12.4 million in EBITDA. His journey exemplifies the "stair-stepping" model of wealth creation, starting with a small, under-$1 million business (a building exterior cleaning company) and scaling up to major acquisitions, including a $19 million manufacturing business. His credibility lies in his hands-on experience: he did not come from a traditional Private Equity background, but rather built his expertise through small business brokerage, corporate development, and repeated deal execution.

The core discussion revolves around the critical skill gap in business acquisition. Most people mistake the process for simply "finding, valuing, and closing" a deal. Blazenko emphasizes that the real challenge—and the source of value—is the transition from deal-making skills to operational management. He highlights that successful buyers must be prepared to become the operator, not just the financier, to ensure the acquired business thrives post-close.

## Key Points
- **The Operator Mindset is Non-Negotiable:** While many assume they can simply install operators, Blazenko stresses that the buyer must be prepared to function as the operator from day one. This is the key differentiator between a successful acquisition entrepreneur and a passive investor.
- **Focus on EBITDA, Not Just Revenue:** When assessing a portfolio, the total EBITDA ($12.4M) is a more critical metric for understanding true operational cash flow than the aggregate revenue ($60M).
- **The "Stair-Stepping" Model:** The most effective path is starting small. Blazenko's trajectory—from a sub-$1M side investment to a multi-million dollar portfolio—demonstrates that early, small wins build the necessary skills and confidence to tackle larger, more complex deals.
- **The Importance of Options in Deals:** When pursuing an acquisition, never rely on a single prospect. Having multiple potential sellers (options) is crucial because business owners often have a specific idea of who they want to sell to.
- **The Value of the Ecosystem:** The process requires more than just financial modeling. The existence of dedicated accelerators and communities (like Acquisition Lab) that provide deal reviews, active networking, and post-close support is vital for minimizing risk and maximizing success rates.

## Tools & Resources
- **Acquisition Lab:** An ecosystem and accelerator designed for acquisition entrepreneurs, offering deal reviews, community support, and dedicated funding to help members acquire larger businesses.
- **CBV (Canadian Valuation Designation):** A specific valuation designation mentioned, indicating specialized expertise in valuing private companies.
- **CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst):** A standard professional designation in finance, foundational to the discussion of financial modeling and valuation.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Operational Skills:** Focus on learning the day-to-day management and operational infrastructure of a business, rather than solely focusing on deal sourcing and valuation.
- [ ] **Model the "Stair-Step" Approach:** Start by identifying and acquiring a very small, manageable business (under $1M revenue) to build initial operational experience and cash flow, before attempting larger deals.
- [ ] **Build a Deal Pipeline of Options:** When considering an acquisition, always identify and engage with multiple potential sellers to increase the chances of a successful transaction.
- [ ] **Engage with Acquisition Communities:** Investigate local or virtual accelerators/labs (like Acquisition Lab) that provide structured deal reviews and post-close support to minimize the learning curve.
- [ ] **Prioritize EBITDA:** When analyzing any business opportunity, focus your due diligence on the EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) to gauge true operational cash flow.
