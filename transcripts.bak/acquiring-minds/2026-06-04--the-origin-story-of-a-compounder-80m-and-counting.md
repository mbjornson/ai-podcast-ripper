# The Origin Story of a Compounder ($80m and Counting) — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode centers on Hickory, an HVAC roll-up company founded by Greg Shapiro and Zach Cooper Vistola, which has grown to $80 million in top line revenue with 300 employees. The discussion details their "anti-Private Equity" (PE) approach to acquiring and scaling trades businesses. Instead of following the traditional PE model—which often involves large funds buying up assets for quick flips—Hickory leverages a unique combination of advanced data sourcing, originally developed for political campaigns, applied to finding small, non-public service businesses.

The core narrative highlights a strategic pivot: realizing that generating value was better achieved by *owning and operating* the acquired business rather than building a tech platform merely designed to sell leads or data to large PE groups. This focus on operational control allows them to build generational wealth in trades services, positioning themselves as an alternative model for industrial consolidation.

## Key Points
- **The Anti-PE Model:** Hickory aims to be a "home for trades businesses," contrasting their approach with traditional Private Equity (PE) firms that are aggressively acquiring the sector. Their strength lies in controlling the entire process from sourcing to modernization and growth, rather than just providing capital or leads.
- **Targeting Underserved Assets:** The founders identified a significant market gap: finding great small businesses with EBITDA below $2 million was traditionally difficult, requiring old-school networking (country clubs, brokers). They utilized data and tech approaches to source these "non-radar" companies efficiently.
- **The Value Pivot (Ownership vs. Data):** Initially considering building a software platform to sell business leads/data to PE shops, the founders pivoted. They realized that the greatest value came from *owning* the business and running it well, allowing them to build something large without needing massive external fund backing or being tied to short-term exit timelines.
- **Cybersecurity as Deal De-risking:** For any buyer considering an acquired small business, the immediate post-acquisition step must be addressing cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Many small businesses have glaring security gaps that represent a major operational risk and should be addressed first (Step One).
- **SBA Financing Updates:** The SBA loan limit has expanded to $10 million for entrepreneurs combining 7A and 504 loans. Furthermore, the policy regarding defaulted deals was reversed: non-controlling, non-guarantor investors who lost money on a default can now access future SBA financing.
