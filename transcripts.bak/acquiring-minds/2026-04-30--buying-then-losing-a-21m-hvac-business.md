# Buying Then Losing a $2.1m HVAC Business — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode details the cautionary tale of Dan Burnside, who attempted to acquire Parker Mechanical, an HVAC business in rural Colorado, for $2.1 million. Despite appearing to be a bargain—generating $800,000 in SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings), which represented a low multiple of under 3X—the acquisition quickly unraveled. Dan's failure was due to a confluence of severe operational and market challenges, including the business operating without proper licensing, unaccounted employee bonuses, toxic personnel issues, a historically poor summer for the HVAC industry, and local competition poaching staff. Ultimately, the weight of these issues overwhelmed him, leading him to shut down the business and file for personal bankruptcy.

Dan's background highlights a shift from being semi-entrepreneurial (running side ventures like Turo) to seeking a path to build generational equity, rather than simply trading time for money. He was initially drawn to the promise of buying a business with support, a service he later noted was an "over promise." The discussion serves as a stark warning to prospective buyers, particularly those drawn to seemingly low-competition "tertiary markets," emphasizing that surface-level analysis is insufficient and deep due diligence is paramount.

## Key Points
- **The Danger of Tertiary Markets:** While small, rural markets may be less competitive for searchers, they possess unique "idiosyncrasies" that can derail an acquisition. Buyers must look beyond the perceived low competition and conduct exhaustive operational due diligence.
- **The Pitfalls of Superficial Valuation:** The initial valuation ($2.1M for $800k SDE, or <3X) was misleading. The true cost of the business included unlisted liabilities (e.g., unlicensed work, unaccounted bonuses, toxic employee retention).
- **The Equity vs. Time Trap:** A core motivation for buying a business is the desire to build equity that can be passed down, moving away from the trap of "trading time for money." This mindset shift is critical for successful acquisition entrepreneurs.
- **Financial Structuring for Growth:** When considering scaling, understanding how banks underwrite loans for **add-ons** (expanding an existing business) versus **carve-outs** (acquiring a business without its own tax return) is essential.
- **The Importance of Operational Due Diligence:** The failure was not just financial; it was operational. Key red flags included: lack of proper licensing, payroll irregularities, and poor employee management (keeping toxic staff).
