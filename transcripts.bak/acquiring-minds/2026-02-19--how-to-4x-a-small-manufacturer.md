# How to 4x a Small Manufacturer — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode features Tato Corcoran, owner of Brandt Molded Marble, detailing her journey scaling a small manufacturing business from an initial baseline revenue of $400,000 to $1.6 million in 2025—a quadrupling of its original size. The discussion provides a raw look at the operational chaos and steep learning curve involved in transitioning into owning a physical manufacturing asset, particularly for someone without industry experience (Tato came from tech). Corcoran emphasizes that while success is possible, it requires massive investment in infrastructure, people, and process improvement.

Beyond her personal story of growth, the episode incorporates expert advice regarding business acquisition due diligence. Max Lummis and his team at LCS provide critical warnings about common "red flags" found during M&A deals, focusing on financial red tape that can kill or fundamentally reshape an acquisition deal. The overall theme is that scaling a small manufacturer requires not just sales growth, but deep operational de-risking across finance, technology, and physical assets.

## Key Points
- **The Scaling Curve (Data):** Corcoran's revenue trajectory demonstrates exponential growth:
    *   *Baseline:* ~$400k annual revenue (normalized).
    *   *2023 Top Line:* $985k. Bottom Line: Dropped $80k.
    *   *2024 Top Line:* $1.16 million (achieving 20% growth). Net: $225k.
    *   *2025 Top Line:* $1.6 million (beating her goal of $1.3M). Net: ~$300k.
- **The Nature of Manufacturing Ownership:** The biggest challenge is not the initial acquisition, but the continuous operational complexity. Corcoran notes that "It doesn't get easier. The challenges just change." This suggests scaling requires adapting to new, evolving systemic problems (e.g., labor shortages, supply chain shifts) rather than simply hitting a revenue target.
- **Critical Due Diligence Red Flags:** LCS highlights specific financial risks buyers must treat as critical:
    *   Revenue quality issues (Is the revenue sustainable or tied to temporary spikes?).
    *   Unsupported EBITDA adjustments (Are profitability claims based on questionable accounting methods?).
    *   Expense timing issues (Are expenses being artificially shifted between years?).
- **Post-Acquisition Priority:** For acquired small businesses, immediate cybersecurity implementation is paramount. This should be treated as the "step one to de-risk" the deal closed, preventing downtime and surface vulnerabilities before scaling efforts begin.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Cybersecurity:** Immediately after any business acquisition (or even significant operational change), conduct a full cybersecurity assessment and implement best practices to de-risk the operation.
- [ ] **Focus on Financial Quality over Raw Numbers:** When evaluating potential acquisitions or business performance, prioritize understanding the *sustainability* of revenue and EBITDA adjustments rather than just looking at top-line growth figures.
- [ ] **Develop Operational Playbooks for Chaos:** Recognize that scaling a physical/manufacturing business means accepting that challenges are cyclical and evolving; build systems to manage continuous change (e.g., labor shifts, supply chain volatility).
- [ ] **Review Due Diligence Checklist:** If considering an acquisition, specifically vet the seller's books for unsupported EBITDA adjustments and expense timing issues before closing any deal.
