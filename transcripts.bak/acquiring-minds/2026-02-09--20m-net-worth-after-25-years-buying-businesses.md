# $20m Net Worth After 25 Years Buying Businesses — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
Paul LaJoy details a 25-year entrepreneurial journey centered on acquiring existing businesses, culminating in an estimated $20 million net worth and annual income of $1 million. His narrative serves as a powerful case study advocating for "Acquiring Existing Businesses" (ETA) over the high risk and uncertainty of starting from scratch. Paul emphasizes that while the path to wealth through acquisitions is highly rewarding—leading to generational wealth—it demands intense sacrifice, including long hours and financial austerity in the early years.

His credibility is built on a remarkable track record: he has successfully acquired 12 businesses with only one failure, compared to starting four businesses of his own, three of which failed. This stark contrast establishes him as an expert who understands both the potential rewards and the brutal difficulty inherent in the process. The discussion also shifts focus from deal sourcing (finding deals) to psychological readiness, suggesting that mindset is often the true bottleneck for successful buyers.

## Key Points
- **Acquisition Success Rate:** Paul’s track record of 12 acquisitions with only one failure demonstrates a significantly higher success rate compared to his attempts at starting businesses (3/4 failures). This quantifies the power and reliability of the acquisition model.
- **The Mindset Barrier is Greater than Deal Flow:** The most critical hurdle for buyers is not finding deals, but overcoming internal psychological barriers—specifically fear, overthinking, and mental shortcuts that cause buyers to second-guess or dismiss good opportunities.
- **Initial Investment Metrics:** Paul's first deal was a $300,000 SDE (Seller’s Discretionary Earnings) business acquired for an offer price of $900,000, representing a 3x multiple. This provides a concrete benchmark for early acquisition valuation.
- **Focus on Repeat Business:** When evaluating deals, the focus should be on businesses with strong repeat and referral revenue streams (like local services/home services) rather than chasing large, unpredictable new construction bids.
- **The Value of Ignorance:** Paul offers a contrarian view that initial ignorance about the complexity of the field is beneficial; knowing too much can be daunting and prevent one from starting ("if we knew how little we knew, we'd never begin").
