# Second Time's the Charm as Owner of a $4m Business — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode features Joe Springsteen, owner of Mallard Systems, discussing his journey into small business ownership. His background is highly varied, spanning corporate law, international counsel roles at General Mills, and extensive experience in commercial real estate (CRE) and property management. He spent years managing massive portfolios—including 18,000 units across 70 properties for Cushman & Wakefield—which gave him deep operational insight into running a profitable service business.

Joe's path to ownership was marked by initial failure; his first attempt at owning a biohazard cleaning company was "harrowing." However, this experience fueled a strong desire for agency and independence. He successfully transitioned through professional networking (meeting broker Jackie Hirsch) to acquire Mallard Systems, an outdoor cleaning and maintenance provider. The discussion highlights the strategic importance of acquiring a business that aligns with one's existing operational expertise while demonstrating significant growth.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Operational Expertise:** Joe’s experience running property management businesses taught him how to scale services based on revenue percentage (e.g., charging 2.5%–3% fee on $300M in rental revenue, generating a $7M–$10M business). This operational knowledge is transferable and highly valuable when acquiring service-based companies like Mallard Systems.
- **High Margin Potential:** In his property management roles, Joe reported achieving net profit margins of 40%, significantly above the industry average of 10%–20%. Identifying and replicating high-margin models is critical for profitable acquisitions.
- **The "Why" Drives the Pivot:** For Joe, the decision to leave stable corporate careers (lawyer/property management) and pursue ownership was directly linked to a major life event—a divorce in 2015. This suggests that for many professionals, the pursuit of business agency is often rooted in personal identity reconstruction and control over one's own destiny.
- **Acquisition Metrics:** Mallard Systems achieved $4 million in revenue in 2025, representing a strong 25% increase from its 2023 revenue ($3.2 million), indicating successful scaling and market fit post-acquisition.
