# 40% Growth in Year 1 of a Paving Business — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode features Eric Donahue, a former member of SEAL Team 6, who successfully transitioned from a highly structured military career into the small business ownership space. After retiring, he acquired a paving business with $4.1 million in revenue. His primary discussion centers on how he achieved a 40% revenue growth in the first year, reaching nearly $6 million. The core thesis is that success in blue-collar services is not about complex technology or massive scale, but about executing basic operational tasks at an exceptionally high level.

Donahue emphasizes that the skills developed in elite special operations—such as efficiency, rapid problem-solving, and high-functioning teamwork—are directly transferable to running a profitable service business. The discussion also provides actionable advice on the acquisition process itself, detailing specific due diligence factors and financing tips for aspiring business buyers.

## Key Points
- **The "Basic Things at a High Level" Principle:** True success in service industries comes from mastering simple fundamentals (e.g., quick customer response, prepping job sites, consistent punctuality) rather than relying on complex, expensive technology or processes.
- **Strategic Growth Tactics:** Instead of simply referring out work, the business focused on *subbing* related work internally, keeping revenue within the company and controlling the client relationship.
- **Financing and Scaling:** When acquiring a business, it is possible to spread out large Capital Expenditure (CapEx) and hiring costs over time, rather than needing to make massive, immediate investments to hit capacity.
- **Acquisition Due Diligence Factors:** When assessing a potential business deal, forensic accounting experts recommend looking for five key indicators: low entry multiple, business buyer fit, quality of revenue, seller alignment, and industry tailwinds.
- **Loan Strategy:** When securing a loan for an acquisition, it is beneficial to use the same bank where the acquired business currently does its operations, as the bank will have a vested interest in maintaining the client relationship.
