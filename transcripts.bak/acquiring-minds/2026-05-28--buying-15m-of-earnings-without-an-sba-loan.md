# Buying $1.5m of Earnings Without an SBA Loan — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode details the acquisition of a $1.5 million earnings business without relying on traditional Small Business Administration (SBA) financing. The guest, Justice Ludig, provides a deep dive into the complex deal structure, explaining how he successfully closed the deal using a combination of seller rollover, seller notes, investor equity, and a convertible note—a financing instrument he notes is uncommon in the current market.

Ludig's background is highly unique, transitioning from a blue-collar upbringing on a ranch in South Africa to high-finance roles in banking, venture capital, and private equity in the U.S. His core professional realization was that the hands-on, operational problem-solving skills learned in ranching were far more valuable and applicable to running a service business than the theoretical knowledge gained in the VC bubble.

The discussion serves as a cautionary tale and a guide for aspiring acquirers, emphasizing that while the process of buying a business seems simple on paper, the reality requires wearing multiple hats and developing deep operational expertise.

## Key Points
- **Alternative Financing Structures:** When traditional debt (like SBA loans) is unavailable due to personal status (e.g., not having a U.S. green card), successful acquisitions can be financed through creative structures: seller rollover, seller notes, investor equity, and convertible notes.
- **The Value of Operational Experience:** Ludig argues that the most valuable skills are often those learned in "unsexy" blue-collar environments (like ranching), which require detailed, process-driven, and hands-on problem-solving (e.g., tracking missing cattle) rather than abstract, theoretical modeling.
- **The "Sexy" vs. "Unsexy" Career Trap:** There is a pattern where individuals in white-collar environments (VC, Wall Street) are exposed to small business owners and realize that the passion and purpose found in trades and local services often outweigh the perceived status or financial glamour of corporate life.
- **The Acquisition Model Pitfall:** While the model of working with an owner-seller to learn the business before acquisition is appealing, the speaker cautions that many stars must align for it to succeed in reality, and even in Justice's case, he did not end up buying that specific business.
