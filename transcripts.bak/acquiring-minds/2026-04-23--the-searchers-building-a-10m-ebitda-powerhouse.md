# The Searchers Building a $10m EBITDA Powerhouse — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode details the journey of Nicolás Luli, founder of Colca Capital, and his partners in building Poli Sistemas, a category-leading records management firm in the Andean region. The discussion centers on the extreme operational challenges and strategic pivots required to scale a business, particularly within the search fund model. Luli acquired the target business in Peru in December 2020, right at the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. The initial $1 million EBITDA quickly dropped to zero due to draconian government restrictions, forcing a complete operational overhaul.

The core narrative highlights the necessity of the founder being deeply embedded in the business—the "operator" phase—for years. Luli spent five years on the ground in Lima, leading the turnaround. Instead of collapsing, the company survived by aggressively shedding non-core, pass-through services and doubling down exclusively on records management, which proved to be the most resilient, recurring revenue stream. This intense, hands-on period of rebuilding was the foundation that allowed the company to eventually grow to a $10 million EBITDA powerhouse.

## Key Points
- **The "In to On" Principle:** True growth requires the founder to move from being a strategic advisor ("on") to being the hands-on operator ("in") for years. The battle scars and expertise gained during the operational phase are the necessary foundation for future scaling.
- **The Power of the Pivot:** When the initial $1M EBITDA business faced a near-total collapse (to zero) due to external shocks (COVID), the critical move was not to save every revenue stream, but to ruthlessly eliminate non-core, pass-through services and double down on the most stable, recurring revenue line (records storage).
- **Focus on Run Rate:** In the records management industry, financial discussions must use "run rate" rather than standard GAAP metrics. This is because large contracts are multi-year, recurring, and the customer base is highly sticky, minimizing churn risk.
- **Financial Trajectory:** The company demonstrated extreme resilience, moving from $1M EBITDA (2020) to near zero (2021), then recovering to $5M (2022), and ultimately achieving $10M EBITDA. This growth was driven by winning multiple large, anchor contracts (e.g., the largest pension fund in Peru, a major bank in Bolivia, and the Ministry of Education).
- **Advanced Deal Mechanics:** The discussion highlighted the complexity of valuation, noting that purchase price can be manipulated by factors like networking capital and net debt. Buyers must understand how to normalize inventory and correctly define debt for accurate valuation.
