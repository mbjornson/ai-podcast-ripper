# Buying Small to Build for the Long Term ($300k SDE) — Acquiring Minds

## Summary
The episode centers on the philosophy of "buying small" as a deliberate strategy for long-term business building, contrasting the pursuit of massive, rapid growth with the value of deep impact and cohesion. The guest, Brendan Dubner, is an experienced entrepreneur with a background in military service, government consulting, and multiple side hustles, demonstrating a consistent pattern of self-directed learning and business building. His vision is not to maximize EBITDA or IRR, but rather to build a stable, impactful company of a modest size (20-40 employees).

Dubner emphasizes that the true gratification in ownership often comes from the impact on employees and the ability to build a cohesive "tribe," rather than the sheer scale of the revenue. He details his journey from a government consulting role—which he found too slow and political—to executing a self-funded search for a small Managed Service Provider (MSP). The discussion highlights that the most effective search tactics, while difficult, often involve direct, persistent outreach like cold calling.

## Key Points
- **The Sweet Spot of Size:** Aiming for 20-40 employees is presented as an ideal size, likened to "tribes of prehistory or army platoons." This size facilitates mutual dependence, shared purpose, and cohesion, properties that are difficult to maintain in larger, more corporate structures.
- **The "Sailboat vs. Cruise Liner" Metaphor:** Buying a very small business (like Dubner's $300k SDE MSP) is recommended as a learning tool. It allows the owner to deeply and intimately understand the business mechanics, unlike the superficial experience of managing a much larger enterprise.
- **The Power of Self-Funding:** Dubner's success was built on a self-funded search, which provided confidence and practical knowledge that traditional financing paths often obscure.
- **Cold Calling Efficacy:** While initial search methods like high-volume, low-touch cold emailing were decent, Dubner found that cold calling was "by far the most effective thing" for sourcing deals. The persistence and directness of the call proved superior to automated outreach.
- **Financing Structure:** For larger acquisitions, the discussion highlights the availability of "peri-pursuit debt," which allows buyers to unlock up to $3 million in conventional debt on top of the $5 million limit of SBA 7A loans, enabling more complex deal structures.

## Tools & Resources
- **Pioneer Capital Advisory:** A firm specializing in peri-pursuit debt for SBA business buyers, helping structure larger, more complex acquisitions.
- **SBA 7A Loans:** A specific government loan program mentioned, providing a foundational layer of financing for small business acquisitions.
- **Josh Meadow:** Referenced as a successful self-funded searcher and resource for understanding the day-to-day mechanics of running a small business.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Adopt an Anti-Growth Mindset:** When considering a business, define a modest, sustainable target size (e.g., 20-40 employees) and prioritize cohesion and impact over maximizing revenue or EBITDA.
- [ ] **Practice Small-Scale Acquisition:** Seek out opportunities to acquire a very small business (low SDE) specifically for the purpose of deep, intimate operational learning, rather than immediate scaling.
- [ ] **Master Cold Calling:** Treat cold calling as a core, non-negotiable skill for business development and deal sourcing. Recognize that direct, persistent human outreach is often more effective than automated, high-volume digital outreach.
- [ ] **Research SBA Financing:** Investigate SBA 7A loans and related debt structures (like peri-pursuit debt) to understand how to mitigate risk and structure financing for small business acquisitions.
