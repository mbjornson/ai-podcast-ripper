# David Rutherford Show: #1 Economist Warns - The Fed Turned 66% of Gen Z Socialist — The David Rutherford Show

## Summary
The episode provides a deep critique of modern monetary policy, arguing that the Federal Reserve's actions—specifically the use of Quantitative Easing (QE)—are the primary drivers of current economic instability, inflation, and social polarization. The discussion centers on how the Fed has fundamentally altered the banking system by flooding it with money, a process that has tripled the money supply in the United States over the last eighteen years.

The core argument is that this massive injection of money, while ostensibly saving the financial system, has created profound inequality. The policy disproportionately benefits those who already own assets (like real estate or stocks), allowing them to profit from asset inflation. Conversely, those without significant assets, particularly younger generations (Gen Z), are losing relative ground, even if their nominal wages increase with inflation.

The discussion connects this economic failure to political unrest. The resulting economic divide is blamed for the rise of socialist sentiment among young voters, leading to poor policy decisions at the local level. The speakers conclude that the Fed's policy is not merely an economic issue, but a systemic failure that has exacerbated social and political divisions.

## Key Points
- **Money Supply Inflation:** The current money supply is heavily inflated; approximately 68% of a $100 bill was printed in the last 18 years, compared to only 32% printed over the previous 200 years. This massive printing is cited as the direct cause of inflation.
- **QE Mechanism:** QE involves the Fed buying bonds from private banks, which removes bonds from the banks' books and replaces them with "excess reserves." This process eliminates the historical constraint of "scarce reserves," leading to an explosion in the Federal Reserve's balance sheet and the money supply.
- **The Class Divide:** The policy creates a winner-take-all scenario: asset owners (like Baby Boomers who saved for decades) benefit immensely from asset inflation, while non-asset owners (like Gen Z) lose ground because they lack the accumulated wealth to hedge against inflation.
- **Political Fallout:** The economic instability and perceived systemic rigging have fueled political discontent, leading to a significant shift toward socialist sentiment among young Americans, which the speakers argue is a direct consequence of bad monetary policy, not inherent generational differences.
- **Zero-Rate Critique:** The use of zero-interest rates is questioned, with the speakers arguing that it does not solve fundamental economic problems and merely masks the underlying monetary devaluation.
