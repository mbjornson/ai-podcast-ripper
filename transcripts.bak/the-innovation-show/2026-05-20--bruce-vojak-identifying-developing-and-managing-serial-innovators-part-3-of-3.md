# Bruce Vojak — Identifying, Developing and Managing Serial Innovators (Part 3 of 3) — The Innovation Show

## Summary
The episode discusses the critical, yet often overlooked, process of identifying and developing "serial innovators"—individuals who possess wide-ranging interests and the capacity to drive growth through multiple domains. The core premise, supported by research cited by David Epstein, is that traditional corporate HR policies are designed for specialized, linear roles, causing them to screen out these "pie-shaped people" whose breadth of interest does not fit a neat rubric.

Bruce Vojak outlines a systematic process for assessing these high-potential individuals, arguing that most companies are excellent at optimizing existing innovators but fail to identify the nascent talent required for future growth. The framework moves beyond simple skill checklists, focusing instead on observing *how* an individual engages with the core components of a business: problems, projects, business goals, and people.

The discussion emphasizes that true innovation is not merely technical brilliance, but a blend of deep curiosity, tenacity, and a constant focus on generating financial impact. The research methodology itself—relying on in-depth interviews ("Just tell us your story") rather than theoretical philosophy—highlights the importance of observing real-world behavior patterns to uncover these rare talents.

## Key Points
- **The "Pie-Shaped" Profile:** Serial innovators are defined by their wide-ranging interests and ability to connect disparate fields. Their value lies in their ability to operate at the boundaries and interfaces between different systems.
- **The Four Pillars of Identification:** To assess a potential serial innovator, focus on their engagement in these four areas:
    1.  **Problems:** They are inherently systems thinkers who don't just solve problems, but *reframe* or *redefine* the underlying question itself.
    2.  **Projects:** Look for tenacity and follow-through. The ability to see a complex initiative through to completion is a rare and critical indicator.
    3.  **Business Acumen:** The most crucial factor is the ability to view work through a lens of financial impact. Innovators must be focused on generating new products or processes that directly contribute to revenue.
    4.  **People:** They are natural team builders who value and draw upon the right people (coworkers, management, customers) to solve complex problems.
- **Process Over Theory:** The research successfully identified these patterns by conducting extensive, qualitative interviews, asking people simply to "tell their story," rather than asking them to articulate a theory of innovation.
- **The Executive-Innovator Relationship:** The relationship must be a collaborative partnership, not a "command and control" structure. Overmanagement or under-attention will cause the innovator to shut down or leave.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Serial Innovators* (Bruce Vojak)
- **Resource:** The book's appendix, which provides a mechanical list of observable traits for HR managers or leaders looking to develop future skills.
- **Concept:** The "Pie-Shaped Person" model (David Epstein/Range)
- **Methodology:** Using qualitative data analysis (like the "war room" of 3x5 cards) to find emergent patterns rather than relying on pre-defined checklists.

## Quotable
> "A mechanistic approach to hiring while yielding highly reproducible results, in fact, reduces the number of high potentials for innovation candidates."
>
> "They are systems thinkers. They look not only at the dots, but how the dots form patterns."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Systems Thinking:** When faced with a problem, resist the urge to find a single solution. Instead, practice redefining the core question or identifying the underlying patterns connecting seemingly unrelated data points.
- [ ] **Practice Tenacity (Project Completion):** Commit to seeing complex, multi-stage projects through to completion, even when initial momentum fades. Focus on the discipline of follow-through.
- [ ] **Adopt a Profit Lens:** When evaluating any idea or project, immediately ask: "How will this generate a new product or process that has a measurable financial impact?" This shifts focus from technical curiosity to business value.
- [ ] **Build Your Network Intentionally:** Instead of waiting for a team to be assigned, proactively identify and build relationships with people who fill specific, complementary skill gaps you need to solve a problem.
- [ ] **Read:** *Range* by David Epstein (for understanding the "pie-shaped" talent profile).
