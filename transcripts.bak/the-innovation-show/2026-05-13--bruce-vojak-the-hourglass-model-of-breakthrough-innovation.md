# Bruce Vojak — The Hourglass Model of Breakthrough Innovation — The Innovation Show

## Summary
This episode with Bruce Vojak, author of *Serial Innovators*, introduces the Hourglass Model, a descriptive framework for understanding the stages of innovation rather than a rigid, linear “phase gate” approach. The model outlines five distinct states – upper left (problem definition), upper right (deep understanding), middle (prototyping & iteration), lower left (implementation), and lower right (market influence) – and emphasizes that innovators don’t necessarily progress through these stages in a strict order. Vojak’s credibility stems from extensive research into successful innovators and their behaviors. The conversation highlights the importance of deeply understanding customer problems and the role of pragmatism alongside idealism in innovation. The episode also touches on the influence of organizational culture and maturity on the innovation process, suggesting that a supportive environment is crucial for success.

## Key Points
- **The Hourglass Model:** A descriptive model of innovation, contrasting with linear “phase gate” approaches. It emphasizes a fluid, iterative process across five states: problem definition, deep understanding, prototyping/iteration, implementation, and market influence.
- **Serial Innovators' Behavior:** Successful innovators often begin by being motivated to solve customer problems and advance the company, frequently redefining the problem itself.
- **Tom Osborne Example:** The story of Tom Osborne and the Always Ultra product illustrates the importance of redefining the problem – shifting from a diaper-like design to a garment-like one – as a key innovation step.
- **Peter Schmeichel Analogy:** The reference to Peter Schmeichel and the quote about “ideas, we don’t ship ideas here” underscores the need to avoid prematurely committing to solutions and to remain open to new insights.
- **Deep Understanding is Crucial:** The upper right state emphasizes the need for a deep, holistic understanding of the customer, technology, market, and financial aspects, rather than simply pursuing “crazy” ideas.
- **The “Cusp” (Middle State):** This is the critical zone where innovators experiment, iterate, and learn from their efforts, often moving between states.
- **Organizational Context Matters:** The maturity of the organization, the skill set of its people, and the overall S-curve of the company influence the innovation process. Organizations reliant on data alone can be more challenging to navigate.

## Action Items
- **Assess Your Problem Definition:** Critically evaluate the problem you’re trying to solve – is it truly the right one?
- **Embrace Iteration:**  Don't be afraid to redefine the problem and experiment with different approaches.
- **Deepen Your Understanding:** Invest time in truly understanding your customer’s needs, the market landscape, and the technical aspects of your project.
- **Seek Diverse Perspectives:**  Talk to people from different backgrounds and disciplines to broaden your understanding.
- **Observe Organizational Dynamics:**  Recognize how your organization’s culture and maturity might impact your ability to innovate.
