# Expected Value xV - Revolutionizing Innovation Metrics with Simon Hill — The Innovation Show

## Summary
The episode introduces Expected Value (xV), a new systemic framework designed to solve the critical problem facing corporate innovation teams: the inability to translate creative activity into quantifiable financial metrics that senior leadership and CFOs can sign off on. Simon Hill argues that while innovation itself is not broken, the *measurement* of it is fundamentally flawed, leading many organizations into "innovation theatre"—a state where buzzwords and activity reports replace concrete value proof.

Hill emphasizes that for an initiative to survive corporate scrutiny, it must speak the language of finance (P&L and Balance Sheets). The book provides a structured methodology to move beyond abstract concepts like "momentum" or "velocity." Instead, it offers a way to formally recognize and track the expected value of ideas from their conceptual stage through to realized financial assets.

The core message is a shift in accountability: innovators must stop blaming the CFO when funding hits headwinds. The onus is on the innovation team to build robust metrics that bridge the gap between creative potential and measurable, future-state corporate value.

## Key Points
- **The Problem of "Innovation Theatre":** Most organizations lack a system to prove financial ROI for new ideas. This results in Chief Innovation Officer roles having short tenures because the business cannot justify funding without clear, finance-friendly metrics.
- **Speaking the Language of Finance:** To gain buy-in, innovation must translate its activities into terms that impact the P&L and Balance Sheet. The focus must shift from *activity* (pilots, engagements) to *future realized value*.
- **The Onus is on the Innovator:** When funding fails or projects stall, innovators often blame senior leaders or the CFO. Hill reframes this: it is the responsibility of the innovation team to develop and present the metrics that justify the investment.
- **Bridging the "Fuzzy Front End":** Traditional financial models (ROI, NPV, DCF) are excellent for mature investments but fail when applied to early-stage ideas. xV provides a mechanism to formalize value creation at this "fuzzy front end," linking initial concepts directly to potential future assets and revenue streams.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Expected Value: The System to Measure, Prove, and Scale Value* by Simon Hill. (The primary resource for the methodology.)
- **Framework:** Expected Value (xV). A systematic approach designed to quantify the value of nascent ideas, allowing organizations to track potential revenue/asset creation from concept through realization in the P&L.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Financial Storytelling Skills:** When presenting an innovative idea, do not lead with activity metrics (e.g., "We ran 10 pilots"). Lead with the financial impact: "This initiative is expected to generate $X in revenue by Q3 of next year, which will translate into Y assets on the balance sheet."
- [ ] **Map Value Streams:** For any new project, create a clear path showing how the initial investment (cost) directly translates through measurable milestones into a specific P&L line item or Balance Sheet asset.
- [ ] **Adopt an xV Mindset:** When evaluating ideas, stop asking "Is this cool?" and start asking: "What is the quantifiable expected value of this idea, and what are the three key metrics that prove it will move our bottom line?"
