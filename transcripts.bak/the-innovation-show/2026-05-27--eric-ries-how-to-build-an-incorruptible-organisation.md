# Eric Ries — How to Build an Incorruptible Organisation — The Innovation Show

## Summary
The episode addresses a systemic crisis in modern business, which the guest, Eric Ries, terms "corruption." Ries argues that the current dominant "best practices" for leadership and corporate structure are often value-destroying, leading to a profound moral compromise within companies. This corruption is not merely "mission drift" or "bureaucracy," but a gut-punch betrayal where the original, valuable mission is corroded beyond recognition due to external financial pressures.

Ries's work, detailed in *Incorruptible*, provides a blueprint for building organizations that can resist these corrosive forces. He contrasts the unsustainable model of "value extraction" (making money by any means necessary) with the "builder's intuition"—the belief that the best way to make money is to create more value than you capture. The core challenge is that modern corporate structures are structurally weak to the very forces that lead to moral and ethical failure, requiring a rejection of deeply ingrained, yet flawed, business orthodoxies.

## Key Points
- **The Problem is "Corruption," not "Mission Drift":** The failure of great companies is a deep moral compromise, where the original, precious mission is corroded by short-term financial demands.
- **The Danger of Value Extraction:** The current economic model teaches people to make money via "value extraction" and "value destruction." This method is inherently dangerous because it is short-term, unsustainable, and ultimately leads to moral compromise and systemic failure.
- **The Builder's Intuition:** The most sustainable and ethical way to build wealth is to "create more value than you capture." This principle must guide the company's core purpose, making the mission the primary driver, not the quarterly earnings.
- **The Structural Weakness:** Most companies are built using best practices that are structurally weak to the forces of financial gravity. To build an "incorruptible" organization, one must actively identify and reject these flawed, ingrained practices.
- **The Compromise Trap:** Many founders and leaders are pressured to abandon their "gut feeling" or ethical purpose and become "business-like" to satisfy investors and the financial system, leading to undifferentiated and mediocre companies.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop the "Builder's Intuition":** Re-evaluate your personal or professional mission statement. Ensure your primary goal is defined by the value you *create* for others, not the value you *capture* (money).
- [ ] **Audit Your "Best Practices":** Identify 3-5 deeply ingrained industry or career "best practices" you follow (e.g., quarterly growth targets, aggressive M&A, maximizing shareholder returns). Challenge them by asking: "Does this practice inherently compromise our core mission or ethical integrity?"
- [ ] **Build Structural Resistance:** When designing a business or team, build structural safeguards (governance, ethical checkpoints, non-negotiable values) that actively resist the pressure of short-term financial gains.
- [ ] **Read:** *Incorruptible: Why Good Companies Go Bad and How Great Companies Stay Great* by Eric Ries.
- [ ] **Practice:** When faced with a high-stakes business decision, pause and ask: "Is this decision driven by the need for immediate financial gain, or by the long-term, sustainable value we are committed to creating?"
