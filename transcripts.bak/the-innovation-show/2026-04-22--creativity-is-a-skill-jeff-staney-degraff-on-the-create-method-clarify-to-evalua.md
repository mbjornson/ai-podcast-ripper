# Creativity Is a Skill: Jeff & Staney DeGraff on the C.R.E.A.T.E. Method (Clarify to Evaluate) — The Innovation Show

## Summary
The episode argues that creativity is not a mystical gift reserved for geniuses, but a learnable, observable skill that can be systematically developed. The DeGraffs introduce the C.R.E.A.T.E. method (Clarify, Replicate, Elaborate, Associate, Translate, and Evaluate) as a practical, step-by-step curriculum for applied creativity, positioning it as essential for modern innovation. The discussion grounds this concept in academic history, tracing the study of creativity from Gestalt psychology (early 1900s) through the work of Rudolph Arnheim, lending significant credibility to the framework.

The core message is that true innovation stems from small, compounding, and deliberate creative moves, rather than sudden "lightning bolt" moments. The DeGraffs emphasize that the process requires understanding one's personal creative rhythm ("your instrument"), actively seeking out constraints, and embracing failure. The overall tone is highly practical, treating creativity like a business process that can be taught and implemented in a corporate setting.

## Key Points
- **Creativity is a Skill, Not a Gift:** The most transformative innovation comes from incremental, observable moves, not genius-level breakthroughs.
- **The C.R.E.A.T.E. Method:** This is the central framework (Clarify, Replicate, Elaborate, Associate, Translate, Evaluate) designed to structure the creative process.
- **"Creative-izing to Creative Eyes":** This coined term refers to the practice of adding creative thought to ordinary, standard things, shifting the focus from grand, revolutionary ideas to everyday improvements.
- **The Power of Constraints:** Contrary to popular belief, boundaries and constraints do not stifle creativity; they actually *create* it. The analogy of children playing in a fenced playground demonstrates that limits give the mind a place to push its boundaries.
- **The Failure Cycle:** Learning and creativity are fundamentally driven by failure. The discussion uses the analogy of the "hurt, didn't it?" parent, suggesting that embracing failure is necessary for growth and adaptation.
- **Anomaly Detection:** A key technique is to actively look for incongruities and anomalies—things that "don't make sense." These unexplained gaps are considered "golden" because they represent untapped potential or overlooked assumptions.
- **Mindset over Mood:** Positivity is defined not as constant happiness, but as having a "sense of destiny"—a focused, directional effort toward a specific goal.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *A Practical No Nonsense Guide to Everyday Creativity* (The DeGraffs' book).
- **Framework:** The C.R.E.A.T.E. Method (Clarify, Replicate, Elaborate, Associate, Translate, Evaluate).
- **Academic Lineage:** The work is rooted in the theories of Gestalt psychology and Rudolph Arnheim, providing a deep academic foundation for the concepts.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Identify Your Creative Rhythm:** Track when and what activities give you energy and when they drain it. Schedule deep work sessions during your peak "creative time" (e.g., early morning or late night).
- [ ] **Practice "Creative-izing":** Take three routine, mundane processes in your work or life and deliberately apply a novel, non-obvious improvement to them.
- [ ] **Embrace Constraints:** When tackling a problem, do not start with infinite resources. Instead, impose artificial, challenging constraints (e.g., "Solve this using only $100 and a 2-hour deadline") to force novel solutions.
- [ ] **Seek Anomalies:** When analyzing data, reports, or processes, dedicate time specifically to looking for incongruities, outliers, or things that defy the established pattern.
- [ ] **Reframe Failure:** View every failed attempt or negative piece of feedback not as a personal failing, but as necessary "disconfirming feedback" required for the next iteration.
