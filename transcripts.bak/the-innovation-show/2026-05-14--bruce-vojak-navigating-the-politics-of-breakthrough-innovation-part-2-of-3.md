# Bruce Vojak — Navigating the Politics of Breakthrough Innovation (Part 2 of 3) — The Innovation Show

## Summary
This podcast episode, Part 2 of a 3-part series with Bruce Vojak, delves into the political challenges inherent in bringing breakthrough innovations to established organizations. Vojak, a serial innovator and author of “Serial Innovators,” argues that most companies are overwhelmingly focused on exploiting existing products and processes, leading to resistance towards radical new ideas. The core concept, dubbed the “Galileo scenario,” highlights the disconnect between an innovator’s perspective and the organization’s, where the innovator sees a clear path forward while the company remains tethered to the status quo. The episode emphasizes that innovation isn’t just about generating ideas; it’s about navigating the complex political landscape within an organization to get those ideas adopted. Vojak’s credibility stems from his own experience as an innovator and his extensive research interviewing successful innovators like Nancy Dawes at Procter & Gamble. The discussion centers around the need for strategic and tactical coherence within an organization, and the importance of understanding the organizational mindset to effectively drive change.

## Key Points
- **The Galileo Scenario:** Companies are often resistant to breakthrough innovations because they are focused on exploiting existing products and processes, leading to a disconnect between the innovator’s vision and the organization’s.
- **Political Resistance:**  Over 90% of organizational resources are dedicated to improving existing products, creating a natural bias against new, disruptive ideas.
- **Strategic & Tactical Coherence:**  Organizations need both strategic (is the company open to innovation?) and tactical (will it invest in this specific idea?) coherence to successfully adopt breakthrough innovations.
- **Seeing Differently:** Innovators need to be able to "see things differently" – to identify trends and patterns that others miss – and then effectively communicate those insights to the organization. This requires a shift in perspective, moving beyond process-oriented thinking.
- **The Burden of Explanation:** Innovators have a responsibility to not just generate ideas, but to help others understand and see those ideas, translating complex concepts into actionable insights.
- **Organizational Dysfunctions:** The episode suggests that resistance to innovation often stems from a lack of strategic thinking, a focus on short-term gains, and a reluctance to challenge established norms.
