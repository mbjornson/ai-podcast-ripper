# Corporate Innovation Strategy: Return Maps, Managing Up & Forecasting with Chuck House — The Innovation Show

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the mechanics of corporate innovation, framing it as the complex, often frustrating, process of intrapreneurship. The core thesis is that successful innovation is not merely about having a brilliant idea, but about understanding and managing the structural connections between a specific **Project** (the deliverable), the **Program** (the group of related projects), and the overarching **Strategy** (the company's long-term direction). The guest, Chuck House, emphasizes that these three levels must be clearly linked for any work to have long-term impact.

House outlines four fundamental traits necessary for an intrapreneur: **Curiosity** (asking "Why can't we get free music?"), **Perspective** (seeing the big picture beyond the immediate model), **Resilience** (treating setbacks as new opportunities), and **Comfort with Data** (using data-driven decision-making). Crucially, the discussion pivots to the often-overlooked soft skills: navigating corporate politics and the ability to articulate complex, long-term vision to skeptical senior management.

## Key Points
- **The Innovation Hierarchy:** Projects must be nested within Programs, and Programs must directly support the corporate Strategy. Running any element in isolation guarantees failure.
- **The Intrapreneurial Mindset:** True innovation requires more than just talent; it demands a combination of intellectual curiosity, the ability to shift perspective, and extreme resilience (the "lemonade" mentality).
- **The Political Skill Gap:** Intrapreneurs often fail not because of lack of ideas, but because they neglect corporate politics. It is vital to "honor your sponsors" (people who can help you) and learn how to persuade superiors, rather than challenging them directly.
- **The Illusion of Speed:** Breakthrough innovation is not a six-month sprint. House details a multi-phase, decade-long cycle: Vision Forming $\rightarrow$ Prototyping $\rightarrow$ Validation $\rightarrow$ Building. Expecting immediate results is a common failure point.
- **The Power of Documentation:** When presenting progress, do not simply say you are "busy." You must provide a structured diagram or record of your achievements to validate your efforts and manage expectations with senior leadership.
- **Data-Driven Strategy:** The best way to approach innovation is through **Return Maps**, which provide a structured method for gathering, validating, and utilizing data to guide decision-making.

## Tools & Resources
- **Return Maps:** A specific framework/toolkit for using data-driven decision-making in innovation.
- **Intrapreneurial Manager's Playbook:** A concept/guide focusing on building team trust and managing internal stakeholders.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Strategic Mapping:** Practice mapping your current work initiatives (projects) to their immediate program, and then up to the company's stated 3-5 year strategy. Identify any disconnects.
- [ ] **Master "Managing Up":** Identify 3 key sponsors/stakeholders in your current or desired field. Develop a plan to "honor" them by anticipating their needs and presenting solutions that align with their career goals, rather than just your own.
- [ ] **Build a Progress Portfolio:** Stop describing your work as "busy." Start creating a visual, phased diagram (like the one House used) that shows the *process* of your work, including phases like "Vision Forming" and "Validation," and estimate the time required for each.
- [ ] **Practice Perspective Shifting:** When solving a problem, force yourself to adopt the viewpoint of a completely different industry or role (e.g., how would a logistics company solve a software problem?).
- [ ] **Read/Research:** Look into the concept of "Return Maps" to formalize your approach to data-driven decision-making.
