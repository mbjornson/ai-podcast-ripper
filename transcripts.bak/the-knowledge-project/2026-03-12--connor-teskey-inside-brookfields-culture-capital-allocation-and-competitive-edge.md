# Connor Teskey: Inside Brookfield’s Culture, Capital Allocation, and Competitive Edge — The Knowledge Project

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into Brookfield's operational scale, discussing how the firm manages and deploys capital across global markets, handling assets valued at approximately a trillion dollars. The core business strategy revolves around identifying and investing in the "backbone of the global economy"—critical assets and services that drive productivity and growth. While the investment focus remains consistent (infrastructure, real estate, private equity), the assets themselves are constantly evolving, shifting from traditional sources like hydro dams to modern necessities like solar, nuclear, batteries, data centers, and fiber optic networks.

Beyond the financial mechanics, the discussion highlights the critical role of culture and mentorship. The guest emphasizes that the firm's longevity and success are underpinned by a culture of measured thinking, forward-looking strategy, and a unique commitment to helping others succeed. On a personal level, the conversation provides actionable career advice, stressing that professional success is less about achieving perfect analytical models and more about developing judgment, taking initiative, and maintaining high availability to colleagues.

## Key Points
- **The "90% Rule" for Deals:** Do not wait for absolute certainty. The advice is to execute a transaction or deal when it feels "90% right." The probability of success increases dramatically by acting on high-confidence opportunities rather than waiting for perfect de-risking.
- **The Shift in Investment Assets:** While the focus remains on the global economic backbone, the asset class has modernized. The investment portfolio has evolved from traditional infrastructure (hydro dams, ports, railroads) to modern digital and energy assets (solar, nuclear, batteries, data centers, and telecom towers).
- **Productization of Strategy:** To service a wider and more diverse spectrum of Limited Partner (LP) clients, the firm has dramatically increased its product offerings. The number of distinct products grew from four to 60, allowing the same core investment approach to be packaged for various risk appetites and client needs (e.g., flagship, main debt, super core, retail wealth).
- **The Value of Availability:** In professional settings, "working hard" is often misinterpreted. The most valuable, underappreciated skill is *availability*—being willing to make time for colleagues (both junior and senior) to bounce ideas, ask questions, or receive advice, regardless of the time or location.
- **Initiative as a Force Multiplier:** Taking proactive initiative, especially when forced by geographical or organizational separation (e.g., working in a different time zone), significantly increases one's "shooting percentage" and momentum in a career.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Mindset/Career:** Adopt the "90% Rule" when making decisions. Instead of waiting for perfect data or certainty, identify high-probability opportunities and execute them to build a track record of success.
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice "judgment overlay." When analyzing complex business problems, consciously step back from the data model and ask: "What external factors or human elements are not accounted for in this spreadsheet?"
- [ ] **Networking/Professional Life:** Actively increase your *availability* to colleagues. Make time for informational interviews, quick advice calls, or mentorship sessions, even if it requires adjusting your schedule or location.
- [ ] **Career Strategy:** Seek out opportunities that force you to work with people in different time zones or physical locations. This simulates a "forcing function" that demands self-reliance and initiative, accelerating professional growth.
