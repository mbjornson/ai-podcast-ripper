# #236 Harley Finkelstein: Why You Must Requalify for Your Role—Every Year — The Knowledge Project

## Summary
This episode features Harley Finkelstein, President of Shopify, who discusses the necessity of "requalifying" for your role—a radical concept suggesting that continuous self-assessment and re-commitment are required to maintain professional excellence. The conversation delves into his high personal standards, which apply both professionally (in achieving goals) and personally (in maintaining deep friendships). Finkelstein frames ambition not just as hard work, but as a disciplined process of confronting perceived gaps between current ability and potential.

A significant portion of the discussion explores how deeply personal history—specifically multi-generational trauma and family financial struggles—shapes professional standards. He recounts his father's immigrant journey (from Holocaust survivor roots to establishing businesses) and the profound impact of major life disruptions, such as his father’s arrest at age 17. These experiences forced him to confront the fragility of perceived security, leading him to adopt an intensely high standard for self-improvement that drives his career success.

## Key Points
- **The Requalification Imperative:** The core thesis is that commitment requires annual "re-qualifying." This suggests professional roles are not static; maintaining excellence demands constant re-evaluation and renewed dedication to the mission, rather than relying on past achievements or title alone.
- **Abundance vs. Scarcity Mindset:** Finkelstein identifies a struggle with abundance mindset, attributing it partly to multi-generational trauma (the weight of Holocaust survivors and financial hardship). The realization that one "is enough" is presented as the primary area needing high standards.
- **The Power of Early Validation:** He highlights his father's habit of physically manifesting ideas into business cards for him. This simple, consistent act served as a physical equivalent of validation: "You absolutely can do this," establishing an early belief in his capacity and entitlement.
- **Adopting Environmental Standards:** The discussion points out that people often adopt the standards and mindsets of their environment (parents, community). If one is not raised with high personal standards, they may struggle to develop them independently, leading to a persistent feeling of "I can be doing more."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Standards:** Identify 2-3 areas (career, relationships) where your personal standards are unreasonably high. Use this self-imposed pressure as a metric for growth rather than a source of anxiety.
- [ ] **Challenge Scarcity Beliefs:** When feeling the internal pressure that "I am not enough," trace the origin of that belief—is it based on current reality or inherited/historical trauma?
- [ ] **Seek Foundational Validation:** Identify one area in your life where you need consistent, external validation (like the father's business cards). Implement a ritual (e.g., journaling, having a mentor review ideas) to physically manifest and acknowledge your own potential.
- [ ] **Recommit Annually:** Treat every professional role as if it requires annual requalification—don't assume past success guarantees future performance; actively seek new skills or knowledge yearly.
