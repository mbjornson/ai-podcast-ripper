# Would You Buy This 6.9M Revenue Sign Manufacturer? — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode is a deep dive into the process of acquiring small businesses, featuring Brian Kavisa, a searcher backed by a family office. Brian's background is rooted in military service, distribution, and project management, giving him an operational focus rather than a financial one. He emphasizes the value of partnering with a single, dedicated sponsor for consistent communication and support, which is crucial for operators entering the M&A space. The discussion centers on a specific target: a high-end architectural sign manufacturer in Miami. The deal presents a $6.3M asking price for a business with $6.9M in gross revenue, but also includes a significant, separate real estate component ($4.7M), which raises questions about the owner's potential value extraction.

The conversation provides significant insight into modern M&A financing, particularly for businesses with higher EBITDA levels. Brian outlines a preferred acquisition structure that bypasses traditional SBA limitations, favoring conventional commercial debt and seller notes. This approach allows for a higher equity contribution (30-40%) and relies on the business's cash flow rather than personal guarantees, provided the EBITDA is sufficiently large (above $2M-$3M). The target business itself is presented as a stable, specialized operation with strong word-of-mouth growth and minimal local competition.

## Key Points
- **Target EBITDA Range:** Ideal acquisition targets range from $1.5 million to $3.5–$4 million in true adjusted EBITDA.
- **Financing Structure:** The preferred structure is 30-40% equity contribution, supplemented by commercial debt and a seller note. This avoids the limitations and personal guarantees often required for smaller, SBA-sized deals.
- **Operational Focus:** The best acquisitions are those that are highly specialized, have strong word-of-mouth growth, and operate without needing a dedicated sales force or advertising budget.
- **Real Estate Risk:** Be highly cautious when the owner owns the real estate. The separation of the real estate value (e.g., the $4.7M property in the deal) from the business value suggests the owner may be extracting more value from the property than from the operational business itself.
- **Sponsor Value:** Having a single, dedicated financial sponsor is highly beneficial for operators, as it ensures constant communication and focused support throughout the deal process.
- **Franchising Alternative:** For those unfamiliar with M&A, franchising is presented as a viable alternative, offering established brands, documented systems, and unit-level data, which mitigates the risk of buying a business with fragile systems or unclear demand.
