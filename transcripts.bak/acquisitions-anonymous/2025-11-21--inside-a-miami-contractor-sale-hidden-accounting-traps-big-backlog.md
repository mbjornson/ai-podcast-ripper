# Inside a Miami Contractor Sale: Hidden Accounting Traps & Big Backlog — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers around an in-depth analysis of a potential acquisition: a specialty general contractor in Miami-Dade County with significant revenue and a robust $52 million backlog. The discussion, led by M&A experts Bill and Heather, utilizes Josh Tonneson's expertise in Quality of Earnings (QoE) reporting to dissect the deal’s financial structure and underlying value proposition.

The core thesis is that the business's true value lies not in its physical assets or labor capacity, but in its specialized *process*, regulatory competence, and ability to secure long-term contracts within highly regulated sectors like healthcare and aviation. The analysis highlights how recurring revenue streams derived from government/institutional clients provide significant downside protection, making the company a potentially valuable platform for strategic growth or consolidation.

## Key Points
- **Valuation Multiple:** The seller is asking $23 million for a business with $4.7 million EBITDA, resulting in an implied valuation multiple of approximately 4.9x EBITDA. This multiple requires deep due diligence to justify the premium.
- **The Value of Process Over Assets:** The extremely low amount of Furniture, Fixtures, and Equipment (FF&E) ($100,000) compared to the high revenue suggests the business may function less as a traditional labor contractor and more as a highly specialized *business services* provider—meaning its value is in paperwork, certifications, project management, and regulatory compliance.
- **Recurring Revenue Stability:** The majority (80–85%) of revenue comes from renewable 3–5 year contracts with government/institutional clients who have maintained relationships for eight or more years. This structure provides predictable cash flow and minimizes reliance on volatile open bids.
- **Regulatory Moats:** Operating in aviation and healthcare requires navigating complex, strict regulatory environments (e.g., FAA, TSA). The company's ability to maintain certifications and manage compliance is a significant competitive advantage ("moat") that general contractors often lack.
- **SBA Scrutiny & Status:** There is increased federal scrutiny on minority/disadvantaged business enterprise (DBE) status programs. Contractors must prove they are genuinely performing the work (e.g., 51% self-performance), making compliance and legal process management a critical, high-value skill set.
