# Sports Bar Franchise for Sale (Florida) – $9.4M Revenue Deal Breakdown — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on a detailed breakdown of a multi-unit sports bar franchise opportunity in Sarasota County, Florida. The deal is highly structured, featuring three locations with a total revenue of $9.4 million and a Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE) of $1.5 million, with an asking price of $6.499 million. The discussion quickly moves beyond the financials to analyze the inherent risks and opportunities within the restaurant industry, particularly the volatility of revenue streams and the critical need for an experienced restaurateur.

A major strategic discussion point revolves around the future consumer base: Gen Z. The hosts analyze the decline in alcohol consumption among younger demographics, prompting a speculative discussion about what alternative business model could fill the void left by traditional sports bars. The conversation emphasizes that successful acquisition requires deep, specialized industry knowledge, as the deal structure—which includes a significant real estate component ($2.9M of the asking price)—requires careful due diligence to ensure the valuation is sound.

## Key Points
- **Financial Structure & Valuation:** The deal presents a strong cash flow profile ($1.5M SDE on $9.4M revenue), but the valuation is heavily hedged by real estate. Of the $6.499M asking price, nearly half ($2.9M) is attributed to real estate, which mitigates some of the risk associated with the operational side of the sports bar business.
- **Revenue Volatility Risk:** The revenue stream is highly susceptible to day-to-day fluctuations (game days vs. slow nights). The hosts suggest that a more stable, attractive business model would be one with a frequent, consistent customer base throughout the week, rather than one dependent on single, high-volume events.
- **Industry Expertise is Non-Negotiable:** The seller specifically seeks an "experienced franchisor/restaurateur." This highlights a critical signal: the business's profitability may rely on the owner's "secret sauce" or hands-on operational involvement, making a lack of industry experience a major red flag for a potential buyer.
- **The Gen Z Void:** The decline in alcohol consumption among Gen Z is identified as a significant long-term market risk for traditional sports bars. The episode frames this as a strategic challenge, suggesting that the next generation's social gathering spot must be fundamentally different from a traditional bar setting.
- **Profit Margin Analysis:** The reported margins (mid-teens) are noted as strong for the restaurant industry, suggesting the current owner is highly involved in optimizing operations, which is a key area for a new buyer to investigate.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Industry Specialization:** Focus on acquiring deep, niche knowledge within a target industry (e.g., specialized restaurant operations, tech infrastructure, etc.) rather than general business acumen.
- [ ] **Master Due Diligence on Revenue Streams:** When evaluating any business, do not accept surface-level financials. Investigate the source of profit (e.g., are margins driven by high-cost inventory, or are they optimized through proprietary processes/supplier relationships?).
- [ ] **Analyze Asset Composition:** When evaluating a deal, separate the operational cash flow (SDE) from the asset value (Real Estate/FF&E). Understand the percentage of the asking price dedicated to non-cash, non-operational assets.
- [ ] **Explore Alternative Social Venues:** If targeting a market facing demographic shifts (like the sports bar market), research non-alcohol-dependent social activities (e.g., experiential retail, niche entertainment, fitness/social hybrid models) to identify future market voids.
