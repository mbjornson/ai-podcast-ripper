# The “Best Business Ever”? A $6.5M Day Spa Franchise in Dallas — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on a detailed analysis of a potential business acquisition: a two-unit luxury franchise day spa in Dallas, Texas, listed for $6.5 million. The discussion highlights the financial metrics and operational structure of the business, noting its strong revenue and cash flow generation from two established locations. The opportunity is presented as a highly tenured, stable franchise (20 years) operating under an absentee ownership model, which allows the new owner to focus on strategic oversight rather than daily management.

The conversation also delves into the mechanics of business acquisitions, specifically the importance of proper financing. The podcast features a sponsor who specializes in securing SBA loans, emphasizing that financing is not a one-size-fits-all process and requires working with multiple lenders to find the optimal structure. The overall discussion provides a deep dive into evaluating the quality of a business listing, particularly when the seller's motivation or the broker's claims of "best deal ever" are questionable.

## Key Points
- **High Cash Flow Density:** The business generates $1.4 million in EBITDA/SDE from two locations. The hosts note that this level of cash flow is typically associated with five or more locations, suggesting exceptional efficiency and revenue density for a two-unit operation.
- **Acquisition Metrics:** The deal features a gross revenue of $6.4 million and an EBITDA/SDE of $1.4 million, with an asking price of $6.5 million. The low CapEx requirement (service business) is noted as a positive factor.
- **Operational Model:** The spa operates under an absentee ownership model, which is highly desirable for buyers as it means the owner is focused on high-impact strategy (pricing, marketing, brand launches) rather than daily tasks (bookkeeping, payroll).
- **Market Question:** The hosts question why a successful, long-tenured franchise owner would list the business, suggesting potential reasons include the owner needing capital or the market being saturated with local competitors.
- **Financing Strategy:** The best financing for a business acquisition is not a single rate or speed, but the specific loan structure tailored to the buyer's needs, requiring coordination across multiple lenders.
