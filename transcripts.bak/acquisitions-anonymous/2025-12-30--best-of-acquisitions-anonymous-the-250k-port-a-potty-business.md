# Best of Acquisitions Anonymous - The $250K Port-A-Potty Business?! — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on a listener-requested analysis of acquiring a local service business—specifically, a Port-a-Potty and septic cleaning operation in San Antonio, Texas. While the deal itself is presented as a potential $250K acquisition with gross revenue near $253K, the discussion quickly pivots away from the specific numbers to focus on the brutal reality of small business acquisitions (M&A). The hosts use an intense personal anecdote about a previous e-commerce closing that was nearly derailed by lender compliance issues and last-minute legal roadblocks.

The core takeaway is not about the industry (though they note it's "shitty," implying low barrier to entry) but about the process itself. The episode serves as a warning to prospective buyers: the timeline for deal closure is highly unpredictable, often extending far beyond initial estimates due to institutional inertia and bureaucratic delays from lenders and legal counsel.

## Key Points
- **M&A Timeline Reality:** Do not plan for quick closings. Expect deals to take 90 days from an LOI (Letter of Intent), with a high probability of stretching to 120+ days. The hosts emphasize that *no single party* is in control of the timing.
- **Lender/Bank Counsel Pitfalls:** Bank counsel often operates on fixed costs and shared firms, leading them to review deals only at the last minute (e.g., a week before closing). This can result in manufactured or exaggerated compliance problems designed merely to buy time due to high deal volume.
- **Valuation Red Flags:** When reviewing a listing like the $250K Port-a-Potty business, be wary of missing data points. The lack of listed EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) or detailed FFE (Furniture, Fixtures, and Equipment) means critical financial health metrics must be aggressively sought out.
- **Investment Professionalization:** Capital Pad is highlighted as a marketplace that standardizes the often "hairy" details of small business acquisitions by handling standardized terms for governance, distributions, and investment structures.

## Tools & Resources
- **Capital Pad:** A specialized online marketplace designed for acquisition entrepreneurs. It allows individuals needing capital to list deals and provides investors with a platform to solicit funds for buying small businesses. (Website: CapitalPad.com)

## Quotable
> "I tell people they should plan for 90 days from signed LOI and that it's very easy for a deal to go 120. And that they're not really in control. No one party is in control of the timing, and that's part of the problem."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Deal Due Diligence:** When evaluating any small business acquisition, immediately request historical P&L statements to calculate actual EBITDA (if not provided) and scrutinize asset listings for comprehensive FFE details.
- [ ] **Manage Expectations on Timeline:** Build a minimum 120-day buffer into your deal timeline when factoring in bank/lender compliance reviews; do not rely on quick closings.
- [ ] **Vet Legal Counsel:** Be aware that lender counsel may be shared or fixed-cost, increasing the risk of last-minute, procedural roadblocks. Build contingency plans for delays caused by external legal review processes.
- [ ] **Explore Structured Capital:** Utilize specialized platforms like Capital Pad to professionalize your investment process and standardize governance/distribution terms across multiple investors.
