# Inside a Senior Care Franchise Doing $21M in Revenue — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on the acquisition of small businesses, specifically highlighting the advantages and due diligence required when pursuing franchise ownership versus traditional acquisitions. The discussion is led by Alex Merezniak, a serial entrepreneur and founder of Franzi, a platform designed to democratize access to franchise opportunities. Merezniak's background includes scaling a tech company (laundry delivery) to an $18 million annual revenue and $100 million valuation, giving him deep operational and investment credibility.

The core thesis is that franchising mitigates the major risks associated with buying a single-owner business (fragile systems, unclear demand). Instead, buyers purchase into established brands with documented, repeatable operating playbooks and unit-level data. The conversation culminates in a deep dive into a specific, high-growth sector: non-medical, in-home senior care, exemplified by the Comfort Keepers franchise, which has the potential to generate $21 million in annual revenue.

## Key Points
- **Franchising vs. Single-Owner Risk:** Franchising provides a systematic approach (documented playbooks, unit data) that significantly reduces the risk inherent in acquiring a business reliant on one person's knowledge or fragile systems.
- **The Brokerage Problem:** Traditional business brokers often take excessive commissions (up to 60%) and restrict access to quality data. Franzi addresses this by offering a flat success fee, maintaining objectivity, and providing access to 4,000+ brands.
- **Due Diligence via FDD:** When evaluating a franchise, do not rely on general listings. The Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) is the legal source. Focus specifically on three items to cut through the legal jargon:
    *   **Item 7 (Total Investment Cost):** Determines the true working capital requirement (startup costs, technology, etc.).
    *   **Item 19 (Audited Financials):** Provides unit economics and historical performance data for individual territories.
    *   **Item 20 (Unit History):** Tracks the number of locations opened and shut down, indicating the system's growth health.
- **Senior Care Market Opportunity:** The non-medical, in-home senior care sector is identified as a high-demand, low-overhead opportunity. The startup cost for this model is low ($100,000 to $160,000 working capital), while the revenue upside is massive (up to $21 million).
- **Operator Dependency:** The success of a franchise location is heavily dependent on the operator's effort and strategy—specifically, whether they are "empire building" (bolting on multiple territories) versus simply "income replacing."
