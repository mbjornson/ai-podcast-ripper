# 8M Aerospace Deal… 95% Customer Concentration. Buy or Run? — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on a deep dive into a potential acquisition—a specialized U.S.-based export distributor of military and aerospace components—and provides a masterclass in the modern M&A process. The guest, Brian, is a searcher with an aerospace background, detailing his partnership structure with a financial sponsor. He outlines the rigorous process, which involves ongoing discussions, financial modeling, and structured checkpoints leading up to an LOI.

The discussion provides valuable insight into deal structuring, particularly the financing components. The financial sponsor group utilizes commercial debt and mandates a seller note, while the valuation and structure are negotiated deal-by-deal. The hosts analyze the target company's financials, noting stable revenue and EBITDA (around $1.9M–$2.1M) and a high profit margin (near 25%).

The core of the analysis focuses on the deal's inherent risks and strengths. While the company benefits from a highly repeatable, mission-critical industry (aerospace maintenance) and a consistent backlog, the analysis highlights significant structural weaknesses, most notably the extreme customer concentration and the potential for a long cash conversion cycle due to inventory management.

## Key Points
- **M&A Deal Structure:** The financing model is structured around commercial debt and a seller note, rather than relying solely on SBA financing.
- **Equity Vesting:** Ownership stakes are not fixed; they are structured with multiple hurdles (e.g., 8 1/3% at close, 8 1/3% after four years of operation, and another tranche based on hitting a 30%-35% IRR hurdle).
- **Valuation Multiple:** The seller is asking for a valuation of 4.2 times the adjusted EBITDA ($8M for $1.9M EBITDA).
- **Customer Concentration Risk:** The target company has an extreme concentration risk, with approximately **95% of its revenue generated from only five customers**.
- **Cash Conversion Cycle Warning:** Due to the nature of distributing small, specialized parts, the hosts estimate a long cash conversion cycle, likely **well over 90 days**, which suggests the business may be financing a large amount of inventory rather than pure receivables.
- **Industry Strength:** The business model benefits from the highly repeatable and mission-critical nature of aerospace maintenance (pre-flight, mid-flight, after-flight inspections), ensuring consistent, recurring demand.
