# Alabama NEMT Company for Sale: $3M Revenue, $376K SDE Breakdown — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on a deep-dive analysis of a specific business opportunity: an Alabama-based Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) company. The discussion moves beyond simply presenting the financials, instead focusing on a rigorous due diligence process, highlighting both the durable nature of the healthcare sector and the significant operational and financial risks inherent in the deal. The business is positioned as stable, benefiting from aging populations and federal contracts, boasting $3 million in revenue and $376,000 in Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE).

The core of the analysis is a critical examination of the provided financial data, which reveals several potential red flags, including discrepancies in gross profit and unusually high interest expenses relative to the asking price. Furthermore, the conversation emphasizes that while the industry is stable, the business's reliance on government payers (Medicare/Medicaid) introduces substantial political and regulatory risk. The overall takeaway is that while the market is durable, the deal's viability hinges on navigating complex billing, licensure, and political landscapes.

## Key Points
- **NEMT Industry Durability:** The NEMT sector is considered a durable space due to aging populations and the continuous need for transport services (e.g., doctor appointments, assisted living). The model is highly stable and contract-driven.
- **Financial Due Diligence Red Flags:** The analysis flagged immediate financial inconsistencies:
    *   **Gross Profit Error:** The listing showed gross profit higher than total revenue, indicating a likely data entry error or misrepresentation.
    *   **Interest Expense Concern:** A $146,000 interest expense on a $3M revenue business is questioned, suggesting potential overpayment or a complex debt structure that needs immediate clarification.
- **High Concentration Risk:** The business's revenue stability is tied to "high-volume federal agreements." The risk is that the company may be overly dependent on only two or three major federal contracts, making it vulnerable to contract expiration or political shifts.
- **Systemic Political Risk:** The primary risk is not operational, but systemic. The reliance on Medicare/Medicaid means the business is exposed to political volatility regarding federal aid cuts and changes in government policy, which can instantly impact revenue streams.
- **Operational Challenge (Asset Efficiency):** The model requires balancing capacity utilization (fleet size) with fluctuating demand. It is difficult to "right-size" specialized vehicles and staff, meaning over-capacity is a significant risk.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Master Financial Scrutiny:** When reviewing any business listing, immediately check for basic accounting consistency (e.g., Gross Profit cannot exceed Total Revenue). Assume data gaps and self-reporting errors are common.
- [ ] **Analyze Payor Risk:** For any service business relying on government contracts (Medicare/Medicaid), research the political and legislative risk associated with those funding sources.
- [ ] **De-risk Revenue Streams:** When evaluating a target business, determine the degree of customer concentration. If revenue is tied to only one or two large contracts, model a scenario where those contracts are lost.
- [ ] **Study Niche Industries:** Deep dive into the operational mechanics of highly regulated, durable service industries (like NEMT) to understand the specific regulatory hurdles (licensure, credentialing) and required asset efficiency.
- [ ] **Follow SBA Financing:** If considering an acquisition, research multiple lending options and understand the nuances of SBA loan structures to ensure the financing supports the deal, not the other way around.
