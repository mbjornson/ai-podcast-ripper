# $37M for Jet Skis?! Inside a Lake Powell Rental Empire — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on the potential acquisition of a massive, highly asset-intensive boat and watercraft rental business operating across Lake Powell, Utah/Colorado. The asking price is $36.985 million, covering a diverse portfolio of assets including multiple commercial facilities, dozens of jet skis and ATVs, and a large fleet of Mastercraft boats. The discussion, led by co-host Josh Tonneson, quickly pivots from the sheer size of the deal to the complex financial mechanics of valuing asset-heavy, operational businesses.

The core discussion revolves around the difference between valuing a business based on traditional multiples (like EBITDA) versus valuing it based on its physical assets and the yield they generate (Cap Rate/IRR). The hosts highlight that the true value proposition lies not just in the revenue, but in the operational efficiencies (e.g., 1% downtime, dealer pricing) and the ability to generate a high annual return on the capital tied up in the physical fleet.

The analysis concludes that any potential buyer must perform deep due diligence, not only on the financial statements but also on the physical assets, the operational contracts (especially regarding marina leases), and the true, sustainable cash flow to determine if the asking price is justified by the asset yield.

## Key Points
- **Valuation Shift: From EBITDA Multiple to Asset Yield:** For asset-intensive businesses (like rentals, logistics, or fleets), the valuation should shift focus from a standard EBITDA multiple to the **Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)** or **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**. The key question is: What is the annual return generated by the assets relative to the capital permanently tied up in them?
- **The "Asset-Heavy" Due Diligence Checklist:** When evaluating a deal with significant physical assets (boats, equipment, real estate), the buyer must calculate three metrics: 1) Total Capital Tied Up (the cost of the fleet/assets); 2) Annual EBITDA; and 3) The Annual Return on Assets. The yield must be attractive enough to justify the operational complexity.
- **Operational Efficiency as a Value Driver:** The business boasts a reported **less than 1% downtime per year** for its watercraft services. This metric is critical, as it suggests superior maintenance, inventory management, and operational reliability, which significantly boosts revenue potential.
- **Structural Risk (The Marina Lease):** A major non-obvious risk is the physical location. The business operates out of marinas that the buyer may not own. The deal requires absolute assurance that the operating agreement/lease for the physical dock space survives the sale of the primary marina property.
- **The Power of Vertical Integration:** The ability to secure "dealer and manufacturer pricing" on supplies and services, combined with proprietary tracking and reservation systems, suggests a high degree of operational control and cost advantage that must be quantified.
