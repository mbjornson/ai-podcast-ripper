# Recruiting Business Valuation: Is 3x SDE a Good Deal? — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on the mechanics of acquiring and operating small businesses, specifically through the lens of executive recruitment. The guest, Ty, runs Search, a boutique executive search firm, giving him deep insight into the lifecycle of small businesses—from the founder deciding to exit to the investor acquiring the assets. Ty's expertise lies in identifying and placing "operators" (CEOs, GMs, etc.) who have P&L responsibility, making him a key resource for both founders looking to sell and investors looking to buy.

The discussion moves beyond simple revenue multiples to focus heavily on the structure of compensation and the valuation process. The episode culminates in a detailed analysis of a specific acquisition deal—an executive recruiting firm—allowing listeners to see how valuation multiples (like 3x SDE) are applied in a real-world context, while also highlighting the critical importance of systematization and mitigating key-person risk.

## Key Points
- **Compensation Structure Over Total Comp:** When structuring compensation for a high-level executive, the *structure* is far more important than the total dollar amount. Compensation should be designed to align with the business model, the candidate's risk profile, and the desired time horizon.
- **The Four Compensation Buckets:** Effective executive compensation packages should be designed across four main buckets: Base Cash Comp, Performance-Based Cash Comp, Equity, and Performance-Based Long-Term Comp.
- **Valuation Multiple Analysis (3x SDE):** The deal presented is for a business with $365,000 in SDE, asking for $1,135,000, equating to approximately 3x SDE. This multiple is typical for niche, established, and systematized service businesses.
- **Key-Person Risk:** The deal analysis revealed a significant vulnerability: the current owner generates approximately 60% of the client engagements and 36% of total revenue. This high dependency is a major risk factor that must be addressed in the acquisition plan.
- **Systematization is Value:** The business's strength lies in its systematized internal operations, strong CRM (200,000 candidate profiles), and established processes, which mitigates the risk associated with the owner's high involvement.
- **Recruiting Model:** The guest's firm operates primarily on a retained search model, requiring an upfront retainer. This commitment signals a higher level of dedication and vetting compared to a simple success fee.

## Tools & Resources
- **Capital Pad:** A marketplace designed for acquisition entrepreneurs. It allows individuals to list deals and solicit capital from investors, professionalizing the process of investing in small businesses.

## Quotable

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Compensation Structure:** If you are in a leadership role, review your current compensation package (or the compensation packages you design) to ensure you are utilizing a mix of base cash, performance cash, equity, and long-term incentives to maximize alignment and retention.
- [ ] **Identify Key-Person Risk:** If you own a business, quantify what percentage of your revenue and client relationships are tied directly to you. Develop a concrete plan and process to delegate or systematize these functions immediately.
- [ ] **Explore Niche Markets:** Focus your acquisition search on niche, reputable service firms (like executive search) that have strong industry reputations and predictable client retention, as these tend to command stable multiples.
- [ ] **Research Capital Pad:** If you are considering buying or investing in a small business, utilize Capital Pad to access a standardized, professionalized marketplace for deals and capital.
