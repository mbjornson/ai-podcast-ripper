# Would You Bet $1.6M of Equity on This California Courier? — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode reviews a specific, established courier company located in Ventura County, California, which is listed for sale. The business has a strong operational history, having served the central California coast for 49 years, and boasts significant financial metrics, including $2.6 million in gross revenue and $770,000 in Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE). The hosts conduct a deep dive into the business model, praising its stability and its asset-light structure. The discussion is highly focused on due diligence, particularly scrutinizing the definition of "recurring revenue" and the potential for geographic and client concentration risk, while simultaneously identifying clear opportunities for modernizing the operations and maximizing value.

The discussion highlights that while the business is profitable and has a strong reputation built on word-of-mouth referrals, the valuation (asking 5x cashflow) is noted as being on the high end. The core takeaway is that the business is a solid foundation, but its current "lo-fi" operational model presents a significant opportunity for a strategic buyer to implement modern technology and management strategies to elevate performance and create greater value.

## Key Points
- **Asset-Light Model Advantage:** The business operates with zero vehicle ownership requirements, utilizing 1099 contractors (similar to Uber). This minimizes capital expenditure, depreciation, and liability, which is a major financial strength.
- **Revenue Structure Scrutiny:** The claim of "80% recurring monthly revenue" requires careful diligence. The hosts suggest this might refer to *reoccurring* revenue (repeat customers) rather than strictly *contractually* recurring revenue, necessitating a deep dive into customer contracts.
- **Valuation Critique:** The asking price is 5x cashflow ($3.85M asking price on $770K SDE), which the hosts acknowledge is on the high end of the market.
- **Operational Technology Gap:** The current dispatch system is described as "lo-fi" (relying on calls and texts). The most immediate and valuable opportunity is the implementation of modern dispatch and marketing strategies, suggesting a clear path to tech-driven efficiency gains.
- **Financial Flow Diligence:** A critical point raised is the need to understand the Profit and Loss (P&L) structure. The hosts question whether the revenue recorded is the full fee, while the cost of goods sold (COGS) is merely the payment to the 1099 driver, suggesting the true margin lies in the administrative overhead and fee structure.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Diligence Contracts:** When evaluating any service business, do not accept "recurring revenue" claims at face value. Demand to see contracts or evidence of contractual obligations to confirm revenue stability.
- [ ] **Analyze P&L Flow:** Always scrutinize the P&L to determine if revenue is the gross fee, and if the cost of services (COGS) is merely the operational expense, allowing for a higher margin on administrative overhead.
- [ ] **Tech Modernization:** Identify "lo-fi" operational processes (e.g., calling/texting dispatch) and budget for implementing modern, scalable dispatch software and CRM systems to maximize efficiency.
- [ ] **Evaluate Concentration Risk:** For service businesses, map out the customer base to identify if revenue is overly reliant on a few key clients (e.g., a handful of pharmacies) or a small geographic area.
