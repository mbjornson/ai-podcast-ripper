# $3.2M for a Dog Grooming Business?! — Acquisitions Anonymous

## Summary
The episode centers on the potential acquisition of a profitable, established mobile dog grooming franchise located in Long Island, NY. The deal is valued at $3.2 million, generating $2.1 million in gross revenue and $744K in EBITDA. The discussion, featuring franchise expert Connor Gross, moves beyond simply evaluating the financials to critically dissecting the underlying business model, the franchise structure, and the market dynamics. The consensus among the experts is that while the business is profitable and asset-light (no real estate), several key metrics raise flags regarding its scalability and valuation.

The primary critique focuses on the low revenue per vehicle and the structural issues inherent in the franchisor owning corporate locations. The conversation highlights that a successful franchise system should prioritize empowering franchisees, rather than having corporate units divert resources to maximize their own enterprise value. Ultimately, the discussion suggests that the sale might be motivated by the franchisor needing to offload a flagship territory that has reached its operational ceiling, rather than signaling a robust, exponential growth opportunity for a new buyer.

## Key Points
- **Low Revenue Per Asset:** With $2.1M in revenue across 13 vans, the revenue per vehicle averages only $161,000 annually. This is significantly lower than comparable service industries like commercial roofing, where experts estimate a service crew can generate $350,000 to $450,000 per year.
- **Asset Efficiency vs. Cash Flow:** While the fact that the vans are "bought and paid for" suggests strong free cash flow, it is a poor signal for asset efficiency. The discussion notes that leasing the fleet would be a more capital-efficient approach for a buyer.
- **Franchisor Conflict of Interest:** A key structural risk is the franchisor owning corporate locations. While this can serve as a "guinea pig" test kitchen, it often leads to resource diversion, where corporate units prioritize maximizing the enterprise over helping individual franchisees build value.
- **Market Saturation Risk:** The experts question whether the founding location is "tapped out." The sale could be a sign that the franchisor has maximized the potential of its flagship territory and is offloading the asset rather than signaling a new, untapped growth market.
- **Customer Ownership:** The discussion raises the critical point of "who owns the customer." The anecdote of a former employee poaching clients demonstrates that customer loyalty is not guaranteed and is a major operational risk in service-based franchises.

- [ ] **Analyze Revenue Per Asset:** When evaluating any service-based acquisition, calculate and benchmark the revenue per physical asset (e.g., van, crew, machine) against industry averages (e.g., HVAC, roofing) to determine if the current revenue stream is maximized or if the asset is underutilized.
- [ ] **Prioritize Franchisee Focus:** When vetting a franchise opportunity, investigate the franchisor's operational focus.
