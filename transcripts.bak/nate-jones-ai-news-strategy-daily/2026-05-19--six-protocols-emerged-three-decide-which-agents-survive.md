# Six protocols emerged. Three decide which agents survive. — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
This episode of Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily focuses on the evolving landscape of AI agent protocols, particularly those developed by Google. The core discussion centers around six protocols – MCP, A2A, AGUI, A2UI, AP2X402 – and how they underpin the “agentic revolution.” The guest, Nate, argues that these protocols aren’t just technical specifications but shape the entire customer experience. He emphasizes a critical distinction: the first three protocols (MCP, A2A, AGUI) are becoming the core standard stack, while the remaining three (A2UI, AP2, X402) are still contested and represent more specialized areas. The episode highlights the importance of understanding these protocols for anyone building AI agent products, particularly regarding security and control.

## Key Points
- **Six Core Agent Protocols:** The six protocols discussed are MCP (tool access), A2A (agent coordination), AGUI (human control layer), A2UI (structured UI rendering), AP2 (agent-led purchases), and X402 (machine-to-machine payment).
- **Standard Stack:** MCP, A2A, and AGUI are identified as the foundational protocols, representing the most immediate needs of AI agents.
- **Contested Layers:** A2UI, AP2, and X402 are considered “contested layers,” meaning their definitions and implementations are still evolving and subject to debate.
- **Agentic Protocols’ Core Questions:** The episode frames the development of these protocols around three key questions: 1) What can the agent use? 2) Who else can the agent work with? 3) How does the human stay in control?
- **MCP’s Significance:** MCP is highlighted as a critical breakthrough, standardizing access to tools for agents, previously handled through custom “glue” code. It now supports 14,000 servers and presents security challenges requiring careful scoping and approval flows.
- **A2A’s Delegation Layer:** A2A facilitates delegation of tasks between agents, using “agent cards” to define terms and interfaces. However, coordination introduces latency and potential failure points.
- **AGUI – The Human Control Layer:** AGUI is presented as a crucial layer for ensuring trust in agentic workflows, enabling humans to observe, approve, and inspect agent activity, addressing the "supervision debt" issue.
- **A2UI – Secure Rendering:** A2UI focuses on rendering structured UIs, preventing agents from executing arbitrary code and mitigating security risks.
- **AP2 – Agent-Led Purchases:** AP2 is a protocol for authorizing agent-led purchases, streamlining the transaction process.
- **X402 – Machine-to-Machine Payments:** X402 facilitates machine-to-machine payments at the HTTP layer, enabling automated transactions.
