# How to verify AI-generated Office files before they ship — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
The episode details a critical evolution in using AI for knowledge work, moving beyond simple prompting to complex, multi-stage workflow engineering. The speaker argues that while AI models (like those from Hyperscalers) can now generate sophisticated office artifacts (Excel, PowerPoint) at an unprecedented scale—potentially leading to an "order of magnitude increase in productivity"—the output is often unreliable, lacking verifiable foundations. The core problem is that AI models optimize for the *artifact* (the polished look) rather than the *accuracy* (the verifiable data).

The solution is a fundamental mental shift: treating knowledge work not as a single prompt, but as a structured, multi-stage pipeline. This process requires the user to act as the system architect, defining the stages of trust and verification. The speaker outlines a four-stage workflow—Source Preparation, Specification, Artifact Creation, and Verification—to ensure that every claim and calculation is traceable, verifiable, and robust enough for serious business use.

## Key Points
- **The Paradigm Shift:** The focus must move from the "prompt world" (asking for an output) to the "workflow world" (defining the stages the output must pass through to be trusted).
- **Productivity Potential:** By building agentic workflows, users can achieve massive gains, such as drafting 8 to 10 complex documents simultaneously.
- **The Reliability Gap:** Current AI outputs are often "financial models in a costume"—they look correct and professional, but the underlying formulas or data sources are flawed, making them massive "trust breakers."
- **The 4-Stage Workflow for Office Files:**
    1.  **Source Preparation:** Do not ask for the document. First, ask the AI to inventory the sources. Organize the data into a "controlled work packet" by assigning metadata (owner, date, file type, status: current/superseded/estimate/raw).
    2.  **Structure/Specification:** Before any file is built, the AI must produce a detailed blueprint.
        *   *For PowerPoint:* A narrative spine, slide list with claim headlines, supporting source IDs, assumptions, and open questions. Insist on plain English.
        *   *For Excel:* A defined tab architecture detailing where raw data, assumptions, calculations, checks, and the final summary live.
    3.  **Artifact Creation:** Build the document, strictly constrained by the prepared sources and the defined specification. (Suggested method: Two passes—first, the argument/storyboard; second, the visual rendering).
    4.  **Verification:** The file is not done when it opens. It must survive an aggressive review. Use a second, specialized model (e.g., Claude Opus 4.7) to review the artifact and generate "edit loops" until the quality is high.
- **Source Discipline is Paramount:** The speaker emphasizes that source discipline is not optional; it is the foundation of serious knowledge work.

## Tools & Resources
- **Models:** Codex (for building artifacts), Claude Opus 4.7 (for aggressive review and edit loops).
- **Templates:** A full source packet template available on the speaker's Substack, including an ID schema, a status taxonomy, and a conflict log format.
- **Content:** The speaker's previous Substack post on organizing sources before asking AI to write anything (linked in the description).

## Quotable

> "The goal is to make every important claim and calculation something that can be checked and that invites that scrutiny."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement a Source Taxonomy:** Before starting any major project, create a controlled work packet by indexing all sources with mandatory metadata (owner, date, file type, status: current/superseded/estimate/raw).
- [ ] **Mandate a File Specification:** For any high-stakes document (PowerPoint or Excel), force the AI to first generate a detailed blueprint (spec) before building the artifact.
- [ ] **Adopt the Two-Pass Method (PPT):** When building a presentation, separate the process into two passes: 1) Storyboard (argument and evidence trail only), and 2) Render (visual polish, constrained by the storyboard).
- [ ] **Build a Review Loop:** Integrate a secondary, specialized AI model into your workflow specifically for aggressive review and generating "edit loops" to test the document's accuracy and completeness.
- [ ] **Treat Knowledge Work like Code:** Adopt the mindset that every claim and calculation must be traceable back to a specific, verifiable source ID.
