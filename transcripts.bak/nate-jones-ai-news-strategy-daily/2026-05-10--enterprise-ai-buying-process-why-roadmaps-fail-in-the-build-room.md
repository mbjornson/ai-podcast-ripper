# Enterprise AI Buying Process: Why Roadmaps Fail in the Build Room — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
The episode analyzes the recent security breach involving McKinsey's AI platform, "Lily," where an autonomous agent was compromised via a simple SQL injection, allowing an attacker to rewrite the AI's core reasoning prompts. While the immediate industry reaction focused on "technical hygiene"—patching endpoints and sanitizing inputs—the speaker argues this is missing the point. The failure was not a technical oversight by the engineers, but a systemic failure in the *process* of building and buying AI software.

The core thesis is that the traditional enterprise software procurement sequence (Strategy $\rightarrow$ Procurement $\rightarrow$ Security $\rightarrow$ IT $\rightarrow$ Development) worked for bounded SaaS applications (like Salesforce or Workday). However, this sequence is fundamentally broken by the nature of AI agents. Agents are "unbounded," capable of crossing multiple, disparate systems (CRM, support tickets, internal wikis) and assembling business context from scratch. This complexity requires a level of auditable, coded permissions and workflow orchestration that cannot be treated as an afterthought.

For any company building or buying AI, the speaker warns that the implementation question is no longer downstream of the strategic decision; it *is* the strategic decision. If the platform cannot guarantee secure, auditable, and cost-effective cross-system access for an agent, the entire roadmap is non-viable.

## Key Points
- **The Failure is Process, Not Patching:** The root cause of the Lily breach is not a lack of technical skill (McKinsey has great engineers) but a structural assumption that the purchased software could handle the complexity of autonomous, cross-system agentic access.
- **The Broken Procurement Sequence:** The traditional sequence (Strategy $\rightarrow$ Procurement $\rightarrow$ Security $\rightarrow$ IT $\rightarrow$ Dev) is obsolete for AI. For agents, the technical feasibility (can the agent access, read, and write across all necessary systems?) must be defined and tested *before* the strategy is finalized.
- **Agentic Unboundedness:** Unlike human users who rely on the visible screen (the UI is the permissions model), agents operate by querying systems in code. This requires that every single system involved (CRM, ticketing, wiki, etc.) must have clear, auditable, and coded permissions that compose together—a massive engineering lift that is rarely accounted for in early roadmaps.
- **The Signal of the Market:** The simultaneous announcements from major vendors (Anthropic, OpenAI, SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow) all focused on exposing their platforms via APIs, tools, and command lines (e.g., Salesforce Headless 360, ServiceNow Action Fabric) confirms that the bottleneck is no longer the foundational LLM model; it is the ability to connect, govern, and audit the underlying enterprise data and workflows.
- **The Strategic Question:** The most critical question to ask when evaluating an AI investment is: "Can we guarantee that the agent can reach the right data, use the right permissions, trigger the right workflow, and leave the right audit trail, all at a cost the company can live with?"

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Technical Foresight:** When participating in strategic planning (business or career), actively push for the inclusion of technical architects and engineers in the initial decision-making room, ensuring the discussion moves beyond "what can AI do" to "what can AI *reliably and securely* do."
- [ ] **Challenge the Status Quo Process:** When evaluating a new enterprise software purchase, do not accept the standard procurement sequence. Demand that the technical team model the agent's workflow across *all* required systems (CRM, ticketing, wiki, etc.) and verify the auditable permissions model *before* the contract is signed.
- [ ] **Focus on the "Unbounded" Test:** When assessing a platform, ask: "If an agent needs to pull data from three different systems, how is the cross-system permission and audit trail guaranteed?" If the answer is vague, the platform is a liability, not an asset.
- [ ] **Follow API/Headless Trends:** Keep monitoring vendors like Salesforce and ServiceNow for their API-first, "headless" offerings, as these represent the necessary shift toward building AI workflows around exposed data layers rather than relying on graphical user interfaces.
