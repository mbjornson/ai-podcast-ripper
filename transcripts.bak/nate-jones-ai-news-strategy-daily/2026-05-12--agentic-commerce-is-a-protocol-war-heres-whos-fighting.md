# Agentic Commerce Is A Protocol War. Here's Who's Fighting. — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
This episode, hosted by Nate Jones, dissects the emerging “agentic commerce” landscape – the shift towards software agents autonomously handling purchases – and frames it as a multi-faceted protocol war. The core argument is that the traditional online shopping model, with its clear lines of responsibility, is crumbling under the weight of autonomous agents. Six key layers of contention are identified, ranging from where the agent shops to the ultimate responsibility for errors. The guest advocates for a shift in focus from simply completing transactions to establishing verifiable proof of authorization for each agent action. He highlights two primary competing approaches: ACP (Assistant Commerce Protocol), focused on streamlined checkout flows, and UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol), designed for broader merchant system interoperability. Ultimately, the episode argues that trust – specifically, the ability to verify an agent’s permission to act – is the critical missing piece in this new commerce paradigm.

## Key Points
- **Six Protocol Layers:** The core of the discussion revolves around six layers of contention in agentic commerce: 1) Where the agent shops, 2) Proof of allowed action, 3) Ownership of the credential, 4) Rails for software-to-software payments, 5) Enterprise-scale governance, and 6) Responsibility for errors.
- **Breaking the Traditional Model:** The current online shopping model relies on a shared understanding of evidence, but agentic commerce disrupts this, requiring a new system for verifying agent actions.
- **ACP vs. UCP:** Two competing protocols are emerging: ACP focuses on a clean, streamlined checkout experience, while UCP prioritizes merchant system interoperability and preserving merchant control.
- **Authorization is Key:** The most critical battle isn’t about completing transactions, but about establishing proof that an agent was authorized to take a specific action – a “mandate” as described.
- **Agent Use Cases:** The discussion highlights practical use cases, including conference bookings, software vendor comparisons, and data requests, illustrating the potential for widespread agentic commerce.
- **Emerging Authorization Solutions:** Stripe’s Approved Payment Link and Google’s Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) are presented as early attempts to address the authorization challenge, providing a verifiable record of agent actions.
- **Stable Coin Relevance:** Stablecoins are positioned as a crucial element for agentic commerce, particularly for small, frequent, software-to-software transactions, offering faster settlement and bypassing traditional card networks.
