# Codex Plugins: Why the AI Bottleneck Moved to Workflow — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
The episode argues that the current bottleneck in AI adoption is not the raw intelligence of Large Language Models (LLMs) like Codex or GPT-5.5, but rather the ability to structure and execute complex, multi-step workflows—the "scaffolding" or "mech suit" around the model. The speaker provides a comprehensive map to demystify the various components that make up these advanced AI agents, moving beyond the abstract concepts of "plugins" and "connectors." The core message is that true "frontier work" requires understanding how to package, automate, and connect disparate tools and data sources.

The discussion systematically breaks down the hierarchy of AI components, contrasting simple text inputs (Prompts) with reusable processes (Skills), packaged workflows (Plugins), and the critical layer of live data access (MCPs/Connectors). By clarifying these relationships, the listener gains the ability to confidently build, customize, and audit sophisticated agentic systems without needing to be a deep-stack engineer.

## Key Points
- **The AI Bottleneck Shift:** The limitation has moved from the LLM's intelligence to the *workflow* (the scaffolding). Agents are becoming capable enough to perform "very rich work," but the structure is what enables it.
- **Prompt vs. Skill:**
    *   **Prompt:** Used for one-off, temporary, or highly specific, single-instance tasks. It is just a piece of text and does not carry tools or permissions.
    *   **Skill:** Used for repeatable, consistent processes (e.g., a team's specific way of writing marketing documents or reviewing PRs). A skill is defined by a clear Markdown document, making it universal across different LLM tools.
- **Plugins are the Workflow Package:** A plugin is a large package that wraps an entire workflow, including app integrations, MCPs, hooks, and assets, making the whole process installable and shareable. Plugins are necessary when the workflow is too complex or requires too many external components to be contained in a simple skill.
- **MCPs/Connectors are the Data Layer:** These act as the "universal plug" or "internet plug" for the agent. They are the mechanism that allows the agent to access *live, real data* from external systems (e.g., Salesforce, Figma, Slack). Without this, the AI cannot perform real-world, data-dependent work.
- **The "Human Plugin" Concept (Contrarian Take):** The speaker notes that the listener is *already* performing plugin work simply by copying data from one app, pasting it into the chat, asking the model to reason, and then going to another app to check the result. This highlights that the skill is already present; the goal is to automate the process.
- **The 80/20 Rule for Skills:** When developing skills, focus on identifying the 20% of processes that are repeated most often and have the highest sensitivity to accuracy, as these will deliver 80% of the value.

## Tools & Resources
- **Substack Workbook:** The speaker is providing a "super practical do it now workbook" on Substack. This resource includes a workflow audit, a decision tree (Prompt vs. Skill vs. Plugin vs. MCP), examples of skill markdown files, a starter plugin structure, and a testing checklist.
- **Core Components:** Prompts, Skills (Markdown files), Plugins, MCP Servers, Connectors, Hooks, and Scripts.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Audit your team's most frequently repeated, high-value processes (e.g., client onboarding, content review). Document these processes in detailed Markdown files to formalize them as "Skills."
- [ ] **Workflow Mapping:** Use the decision tree framework (available in the Substack workbook) to categorize your current business processes: Is it a one-off task (Prompt)? Is it a repeatable process (Skill)? Does it require multiple app integrations (Plugin)?
- [ ] **Data Connection Audit:** Identify the critical external data sources (CRM, project management tools, etc.) that your team relies on. Treat connecting to these sources as the highest priority, as this is where the "real work" lives.
- [ ] **Adopt the "Human Plugin" Mindset:** When solving a complex problem, stop viewing it as a single prompt. Instead, map out the sequence of actions: [App A $\rightarrow$ Data Extraction $\rightarrow$ LLM Reasoning $\rightarrow$ App B $\rightarrow$ Verification]. This structured approach is the foundation of building a true plugin.
