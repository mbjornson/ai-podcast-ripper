# Prove Your Value at Work in the AI Era: Judgment Artifacts — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
The episode argues that the rise of AI has fundamentally shifted the definition of professional value, moving it away from the mere production of polished artifacts (memos, prototypes, polished resumes) toward the demonstrable quality of human judgment and critical thinking. While AI makes it easier to look productive, the resulting output often fails to signal *how* the work was achieved or the depth of understanding involved. The core problem is that the old evidence—the finished product—no longer carries the same signal.

The solution proposed is to make the *process* of thinking visible. The speaker advocates for the "whiteboard conversation" as the ideal setting, where a person must articulate their understanding of a complex problem, defend their choices, and adapt under pressure from a knowledgeable peer. This live reasoning—showing what was noticed, what was rejected, and where the system is fragile—is the new scarce commodity. The ultimate goal is to capture this evidence of thinking, not just the final result.

## Key Points
- **The Evidence Problem:** AI makes it easier to generate high-quality output, but this masks the underlying judgment. The value is now in "processing"—what you question, what you keep, and what you reject—rather than just generating.
- **The Whiteboard Conversation:** This is the optimal setting to prove judgment. It requires the individual to think in the room, hold the situation in their head, and respond to pressure, making private judgment visible.
- **The 4 Pillars of Judgment (The Framework):** To demonstrate deep thinking, the conversation must cover four key elements:
    1.  **Situation:** Defining the context, constraints, missing facts, and source of pressure.
    2.  **Decision:** Identifying plausible paths and, crucially, explaining *why* certain options were rejected.
    3.  **Risk:** Explicitly naming what could go wrong, what risk is being accepted, and what loss was prevented. (Prevented losses count).
    4.  **Change:** Articulating what will be different or clearer *because* of the decision, preventing the exercise from becoming a mere diary entry.
- **Career Application:** Standard advice to "build a portfolio" is incomplete. The focus must shift to creating "judgment artifacts"—records that show the reasoning and the struggle, not just the polished outcome.
- **Onboarding Strategy:** Instead of just listening and learning, a strong first month move is to proactively ask for a "whiteboard session" with a domain expert to test your early point of view and show how it improves under challenge.

## Tools & Resources
- **Talent Board:** A conceptual project/framework designed to capture and preserve the evidence of complex thinking and judgment (the "whiteboard session") for use in hiring packets or promotion notes.
- **Codex / Claude Code:** Mentioned as AI tools that can be used to develop prompts to help structure and elicit the complex, nuanced thinking required for these judgment artifacts.

## Quotable
> "The deeper problem is that AI makes more people look productive, and the old evidence does not carry the same signal."
> "The value we need to demonstrate is the value in processing, in compiling all of this in sensemaking. What do we question? What do we keep? What do we reject?"

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a "Judgment Artifact" Framework:** When documenting work, structure your thinking around the four pillars: Situation, Decision, Risk, and Change.
- [ ] **Practice "Thinking in Public":** In your next professional setting (meeting, presentation), proactively ask for a challenge or a deep dive with a domain expert, forcing yourself to defend your initial assumptions.
- [ ] **Schedule a "Whiteboard Session" (Onboarding):** When starting a new role, do not wait for guidance. Schedule time with a senior stakeholder and present your initial, high-level point of view on a core problem, explicitly asking them to challenge your assumptions and point out missing constraints.
- [ ] **Use AI for Prompt Engineering:** Utilize tools like Codex or Claude Code to generate structured prompts that force you to articulate the rejected options and the identified risks in a project summary.
