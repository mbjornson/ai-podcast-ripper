# MIT Says Half Your AI Gains Come From How You Ask. Not the Model. — Nate Jones AI News & Strategy Daily

## Summary
The core thesis of the episode is that the concept of "prompt engineering" is obsolete and represents a "table stakes" skill, not a differentiator. The evolution of AI, particularly with advanced agentic models (like Opus 4.7 and OpenAI 5.5), has fundamentally changed how humans must interact with AI. The speaker argues that we must shift our mental model from treating AI like a junior partner—who requires highly specific, task-based prompts—to treating it like a senior partner on a team.

This shift requires moving away from the "prompt world" (asking for a defined answer) and into the "question world" (partnering with AI to open up and define the scope of a complex problem). The speaker introduces the "AI Question Method," a framework designed to help the user structure their thinking and questions to enable the AI to perform heavy, deep knowledge work. This method is not just about better inputs; it's about fundamentally changing the user's approach to problem-solving using AI.

## Key Points
- **The End of Prompting:** Prompt engineering is no longer the primary skill; it is now basic competency. The focus must be on the *questions* asked, not the prompts written.
- **The Senior Partner Model:** The biggest mental model shift is viewing AI as a senior partner, not a junior one. This means the user's job is to guide the thinking process and define the problem space, rather than simply assigning a task.
- **The AI Question Method:** This method involves structuring interactions as a series of strategic questions that enable the AI to do its best work. It is designed for "heavy knowledge work" (e.g., synthesizing complex data, developing narratives, or coding projects).
- **Defining the Scope (The Flashlight Analogy):** Effective questioning requires conveying a "center of the flashlight"—your own thesis, angle, or perspective. You must tell the AI where your focus is (e.g., "I have a thesis that our marketing attribution is broken because...") while giving it room to explore.
- **Setting Boundaries (The Edges):** A critical part of the method is explicitly telling the AI what *not* to include. This prevents the AI from getting lost in irrelevant details and maintains focus on the core problem.
- **Distinction Between Agents:** The speaker clarifies that the "agent" discussed here is not a predictable, buttoned-up *agentic pipeline* (like an invoice processor). It refers to deep, custom, co-work with frontier models on complex, novel knowledge work.

## Tools & Resources

## Action Items
- [ ] **Shift Mental Model:** When engaging with AI, stop thinking of it as a task executor and start thinking of it as a strategic co-worker or senior partner.
- [ ] **Develop a Thesis Statement:** Before asking the AI to analyze any data or solve a business problem, write a clear, directional thesis statement that defines your initial perspective or hypothesis.
- [ ] **Practice Boundary Setting:** When using AI, explicitly include instructions that define the scope and what must be *excluded* from the final output (e.g., "Do not include any discussion of X," or "Focus only on the data from Q3").
- [ ] **Adopt the "Question Set" Approach:** Structure complex AI interactions not as a single prompt, but as a series of guiding questions designed to open up the problem and force the AI to consider multiple angles.
- [ ] **Read the Substack:** Check out the speaker's Substack for quick-start guides on adopting the question-based approach.
