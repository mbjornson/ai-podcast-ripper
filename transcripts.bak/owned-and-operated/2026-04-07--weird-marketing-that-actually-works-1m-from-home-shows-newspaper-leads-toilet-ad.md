# Weird Marketing That Actually Works ($1M from Home Shows, Newspaper Leads, & Toilet Ads) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode centers on "weird local marketing channels" that generate significant, measurable ROI, particularly for businesses in the home service industry (plumbing, HVAC, electrical). The discussion is led by John Wilson, CEO of Wilson Plumbing, heating, cooling and Electric, who details his journey of scaling his business from $3 million to $40 million in revenue. His credibility is established through his long-term commitment to the industry and his deep knowledge of local, often overlooked, marketing opportunities. The guest, Sam Preston, CEO of Service Scalers, reinforces the focus on operational efficiency and growth strategies. The core message is that the most effective marketing is often the most unconventional and physically integrated into the customer's daily life.

## Key Points
- **High-Impact, Low-Cost Visibility:** The most effective marketing is often found in public, high-traffic, low-cost physical locations. Examples include sponsoring urinals or bathroom stalls in public facilities (like stadiums), which provides constant, unavoidable brand exposure.
- **The Power of the "Weird":** Marketing that is unconventional (e.g., advertising in church bulletins, local newspapers, or on gas pumps) stands out because it breaks through the noise of standard digital advertising.
- **Event Frequency Matters:** When choosing a sponsorship, prioritize venues with a high frequency of events (e.g., a baseball stadium with 80 games/year) over those with fewer events, even if the initial cost is higher.
- **Operational Efficiency is Critical:** For field service businesses, managing fuel fraud and administrative time is a major revenue leak. Implementing GPS-verified fuel card systems can save tens of thousands of dollars annually (one client saved over $130,000/year).
- **Quantifying ROI:** While some weird sponsorships are purely "brand plays" (i.e., for fun or PR), the underlying principle is that the visibility creates a mental association: *When the problem happens (e.g., a leak), the brand is top-of-mind.*
