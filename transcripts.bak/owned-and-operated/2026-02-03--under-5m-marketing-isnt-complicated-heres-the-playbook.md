# Under $5M? Marketing Isn’t Complicated (Here’s the Playbook) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode provides a highly contrarian playbook for home service business owners regarding marketing spend and internal staffing. The core thesis is that if your company's revenue is under $5 million, effective marketing is not complicated; it simply requires discipline and structure. The discussion strongly cautions against relying on the owner to handle all marketing tasks or expecting an agency to solve every problem with minimal budget.

The conversation focuses heavily on the transition from external agency management to building internal capability. While many operators feel frustrated with agencies, the expert advises that the optimal model is rarely "all in-house" or "all outsourced." Instead, successful scaling requires a hybrid approach: pairing an internal marketing manager (for control and focus) with specialized external agencies for specific disciplines like PPC or SEO.

## Key Points
- **The $5M Marketing Threshold:** The host asserts that if your business is under $5 million in revenue, the necessary marketing strategies are manageable and not inherently complex. This suggests that complexity increases disproportionately as revenue scales past this point.
- **Marketing Discipline Over Magic:** Do not expect "sprinkling Jesus juice" to generate leads. Marketing requires paid channels (PPC, LSA) because you must actively pay for high-quality leads; it is a transactional process, not an organic one.
- **The First Hire Rule:** If scaling beyond basic efforts, the first dedicated internal hire should be a Marketing Manager. This role provides structure and control that the owner cannot afford to neglect while focusing on core operations.
- **Three Drivers for Going In-House:** The decision to bring marketing in-house is driven by specific operational needs:
    1.  **Control:** Needing direct oversight of time, budget allocation, and execution quality.
    2.  **Speed:** Requiring rapid change implementation (e.g., updating a website coupon or executing an SEO idea) that agencies often slow down.
    3.  **Focus:** Preventing the owner/leadership from spending excessive time on marketing tasks that should be delegated.
- **The Optimal Model is Hybrid:** The most successful, significantly sized operators do not choose between in-house and agency; they use a pairing model (e.g., internal team handles strategy/oversight while specialized agencies handle execution for PPC or SEO).

## Tools & Resources

- **Marketing Channels to Master:** Local Service Ads (LSA), Pay-Per-Click (PPC), Search Engine Optimization (SEO), and optimizing the Google Business Profile.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Marketing Spend:** Review current agency contracts and spending to identify where ad dollars are being wasted on low-quality leads. Focus on channels that drive *measurable* conversions, not just "attractive reporting."
- [ ] **Define the Scaling Trigger:** Determine if your primary reason for hiring an internal marketing role is Control, Speed, or Focus. This will guide whether you need a full-time hire or simply better process documentation.
- [ ] **Implement Hybrid Marketing:** Instead of choosing between in-house and agency, map out which specialized function (e.g., PPC management) requires deep external expertise, and which functions (e.g., overall strategy/board coordination) require internal control.
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Deepen understanding of the difference between LSA and PPC to better allocate budget and understand lead quality differences.
