# The Marketing Stack for Home Service Businesses: $1M to $10M — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the evolution of the marketing stack for home service businesses, detailing the necessary shifts in strategy, technology, and operations as a company scales from $1 million to $10 million in revenue. The discussion is highly practical, moving beyond generic marketing advice to focus on the mechanics of rapid, profitable growth.

The host, John Wilson, is the CEO of Wilson Plumbing, heating, cooling and Electric, providing real-world credibility by discussing his own journey of scaling and acquiring multiple businesses. The guest, Ethan Wright, works with service scalers, reinforcing the focus on repeatable, systemic growth. The core message is that scaling is not simply about spending more money; it requires a fundamental overhaul of the technology and operational playbook to handle increased volume without chaos.

## Key Points
- **The $1M Stage Focus:** At the initial level, the primary marketing spend must be dedicated to immediate lead generation—specifically, filling the job board and hiring the next technician. The focus is on immediate cash flow and filling capacity, not brand awareness through expensive, slow-burn methods like billboards or radio.
- **The Tech Stack is the Bottleneck:** The marketing stack and the tech stack are equally critical. Operating without the right technology (e.g., speed-to-lead software) forces the business to run "on hard mode," relying on manual input and severely limiting growth potential, regardless of how many leads are generated.
- **Scaling Requires Standardization:** When acquiring or expanding into new markets, the key is implementing a standardized, proven playbook. The goal is to quickly replicate success, even in new areas, rather than reinventing the wheel.
- **Critique of Delayed Lead Sources:** Be cautious of allocating significant budget to delayed lead sources (like SEO agencies paying $3,000/month) when cash flow is tight. The money must come back within a relatively appropriate timeframe to sustain operations.
- **AI for Operational Efficiency:** Modern AI solutions must be integrated into the entire customer journey (inbound calls, outbound follow-ups, texts, dispatching) and must maintain a seamless human handoff (24/7 live transfer) to prevent customer frustration and lost revenue.

## Tools & Resources
- **Avoca:** Recommended AI partner specifically designed for contracting businesses. It handles inbound calls, outbound follow-ups, texts, web leads, dispatching, and CSR coaching, all within one system.
- **ServiceTitan:** Mentioned as a key industry platform; the ideal tech stack should integrate deeply with systems like this, avoiding the need to "duct tape" multiple disparate tools together.
- **Local Service Ads (LSA):** A powerful lead source, but the speaker cautions that success is highly dependent on having a strong local reputation (e.g., having many reviews) to compete with established, high-review competitors.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct a Tech Stack Audit:** Map out every piece of software used in the customer journey (from initial call to job completion). Identify manual inputs or processes that slow down the team, as these are immediate bottlenecks.
- [ ] **Reallocate Marketing Spend:** If revenue is under $5M, shift budget away from expensive, delayed brand awareness campaigns (billboards, radio, SEO retainer fees) and toward immediate, high-intent lead sources that fill the job board and hire techs.
- [ ] **Evaluate AI Integration:** Research AI partners that offer comprehensive, multi-channel communication (calls, texts, web) and, crucially, guarantee a seamless human handoff for complex customer interactions.
- [ ] **Develop a Scalable Playbook:** Document the exact processes, sales training, and marketing steps that led to the most recent successful acquisition or expansion. This playbook must be standardized for rapid deployment in new markets.
