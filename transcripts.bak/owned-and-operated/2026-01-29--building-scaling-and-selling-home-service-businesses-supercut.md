# Building, Scaling, and Selling Home Service Businesses (Supercut) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
John Wilson is a highly successful entrepreneur and serial acquirer specializing in building, scaling, and exiting local home service businesses (primarily HVAC, but now focusing on standby generators). His background provides an invaluable roadmap for anyone looking to professionalize their trade business into a scalable asset. He details his journey from running a small company that faced payroll tax issues to becoming a self-proclaimed "private equity shop" in the trades.

His core philosophy is that success requires moving beyond simply fixing operational problems and instead building repeatable, documented systems (which he calls EMOS). Wilson emphasizes that the goal of these businesses is not just steady revenue, but creating an asset with enough structure, management depth, and predictable profit margins to command a massive exit valuation—often multiples far exceeding initial goals.

## Key Points
- **The Scaling Bottleneck:** Growth inevitably breaks when your internal team cannot keep up with demand or complexity. This necessitates building reliable virtual teams for support roles (HR, accounting, marketing, lead follow-up) that are pre-trained on industry software and SOPs.
- **Targeting Distress Assets:** The most lucrative deals often involve acquiring "broken," bankrupt, or struggling businesses—especially those in declining industries or with retiring owners. This allows the acquirer to implement systematic improvements rather than starting from scratch.
- **The Exit Strategy is Paramount:** Every business built must have a clear exit plan. Wilson structured his entire career around this goal, treating himself like a private equity firm that sprints for 3–5 years of intense growth and optimization, only to sell the asset for 10–15 years worth of profits.
- **The Value Multiplier:** The value is not just in current revenue; it's in the documented processes, management team depth, and predictable profitability (steady revenues and profits) that make the business attractive to larger buyers or PE firms.
- **Industry Expansion/Diversification:** Once a niche market (like HVAC) is mastered, the model can be replicated across entirely different verticals (e.g., moving from HVAC to standby generators).

## Tools & Resources
- **Quick Staffers:** A recommended resource for building reliable virtual teams in home services. They provide vetted, remote staff pre-trained on industry platforms like ServiceTitan and familiar with best-in-industry SOPs.
- **ServiceTitan:** The primary software platform mentioned that the acquired businesses utilize, indicating its importance in standardizing operations across multiple locations.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Systematize Everything:** Document every process (sales, lead follow-up, scheduling, fulfillment) into a repeatable Standard Operating Procedure (SOP). This is the single most valuable asset for an exit.
- [ ] **Identify Distress Opportunities:** Actively look for local service businesses that are struggling, owned by retiring individuals, or in decline—these represent undervalued acquisition targets.
- [ ] **Build Virtual Support Infrastructure:** Do not rely solely on internal staff; immediately implement outsourced virtual teams (e.g., using services like Quick Staffers) to handle administrative and support functions while you focus on core operations and growth.
- [ ] **Focus on the Exit Metric:** When managing the business, always ask: "What does a buyer need to see in 3 years to justify X valuation?" (Answer: Documented systems, stable profits, scalable management).
