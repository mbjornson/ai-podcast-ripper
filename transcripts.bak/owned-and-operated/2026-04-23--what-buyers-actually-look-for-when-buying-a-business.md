# What Buyers Actually Look for When Buying a Business — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the mechanics of selling a business, shifting the focus from what owners *think* matters (like maximizing cash flow or minimizing taxes) to what buyers *actually* care about. The discussion, featuring David Barnett, a seasoned business advisor who has helped sell hundreds of companies, emphasizes that a business must be structured for sale, not just for operation. Buyers are primarily concerned with stability, systems, and the quality of cash flow, not just headline revenue figures.

The core message is that most business owners operate with a blind spot regarding valuation. They focus on operational KPIs, while buyers and their bankers are looking at the underlying financial structure and the potential for growth and reinvestment. The conversation highlights that valuation is not a single formula, but a complex negotiation that requires understanding the difference between various financial metrics and the context of past deals.

## Key Points
- **The Buyer's Two Questions:** When evaluating any business, buyers focus on two things: 1) What is the reliable cash flow? and 2) Are there documented systems in place that allow a new owner to easily take over and run the business?
- **The Valuation Trap (EBITDA vs. SDE):** Never assume a simple multiple applies. There is a massive difference between EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) and SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings). SDE includes the value of the owner's full-time effort, while EBITDA does not. Using the wrong metric can lead to a valuation gap of hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- **The Multiplier Context:** When hearing a deal sold for "X times EBITDA," the immediate follow-up question must be: "Multiple of what?" (e.g., Proforma, last year, average of five years). The context of the baseline year is critical.
- **Operational Blind Spots:** For home service businesses, the discussion highlighted that Yelp is a far more critical lead generation source than often realized. Over 125 million home service leads were generated on Yelp last year, and its data powers major search engines (Google, Apple Maps, etc.), making it a key discovery point for consumers.
- **The Goal of Preparation:** The ultimate goal of preparing a business for sale is not just to present good numbers, but to make the numbers "speak for themselves" to a third-party banker, minimizing the need for the owner to tell a story.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Systems:** Document every core business process (sales, fulfillment, accounting) to ensure they are repeatable by a new owner, reducing reliance on your personal effort.
- [ ] **Calculate SDE vs. EBITDA:** Determine which metric is most accurate for your business and understand the financial implications of using the wrong one in valuation discussions.
- [ ] **Review Your Yelp Presence:** Treat Yelp as a primary lead generation channel, not just a review platform. Optimize your profile and consider paid advertising there.
- [ ] **Research Valuation Multiples:** When researching potential sales multiples, always establish the baseline period (e.g., "3x EBITDA based on 2023 Proforma").
- [ ] **Focus on Upside Potential:** When preparing for a sale, don't just maximize current cash flow; identify and document areas for reinvestment or efficiency improvements that a buyer could capitalize on.
