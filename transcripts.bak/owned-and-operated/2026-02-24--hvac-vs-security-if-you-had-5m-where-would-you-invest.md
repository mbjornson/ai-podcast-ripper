# HVAC vs. Security: If You Had $5M, Where Would You Invest? — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode centers on a high-stakes business investment debate: choosing between acquiring an HVAC company or a security system company with $5 million. The discussion features John Wilson, who runs a large plumbing and HVAC operation, alongside Steven and Colin from the security industry. Their analysis quickly pivots away from general industry pros/cons toward financial metrics, specifically focusing on recurring revenue streams and client switching costs.

The core argument presented is that the security sector offers superior structural advantages for long-term investment compared to traditional home services like HVAC. While both industries benefit from a mix of residential and commercial work, the speakers emphasize that maximizing profit requires prioritizing commercial contracts—specifically new builds and retrofits—because these segments involve higher complexity, greater required licensing, and significantly increase customer stickiness.

## Key Points
- **Recurring Revenue is the Decisive Factor:** The primary differentiator between the two industries is the quality of recurring revenue. For HVAC, the membership component was cited as generating only about 1% of total gross revenue. In contrast, the security industry provides a much "stickier" and more substantial stream of recurring income from service contracts and monitoring subscriptions.
- **Commercial Focus Drives Value:** Commercial installations are strongly preferred over residential ones because they involve larger devices, complex operations, and higher stakes for the client. This complexity translates into significantly higher switching costs for the customer (making it difficult for them to leave your service).
- **The Ideal Model is Diversification with a Commercial Tilt:** While both segments complement each other (residential can be high when commercial is low), the strategic focus must remain on growing the commercial segment, as this provides the most robust foundation and highest average contract value.
- **Market Barrier to Entry:** The security industry has built-in barriers—specifically mandatory licensing for installation and marketing in states like Texas. This makes it harder for competitors (especially those operating with a DIY model) to enter the market compared to residential services.
