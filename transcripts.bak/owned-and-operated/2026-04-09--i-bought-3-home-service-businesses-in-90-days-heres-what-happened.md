# I Bought 3 Home Service Businesses in 90 Days — Here’s What Happened — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode details the strategy of scaling a home service business through a combination of organic growth and aggressive mergers and acquisitions (M&A). The host, John Wilson, is the CEO of Wilson Plumbing, heating, cooling and Electric, and has built a multi-location, third-generation company. His background demonstrates significant growth, having achieved high revenue in the high twenties while maintaining a strong organic component (70% of revenue last year).

The core thesis presented is that best-in-class companies do not rely solely on one growth method. Instead, they build robust internal processes (marketing, sales, operations) and then use these processes as a "supercharger" when acquiring functioning businesses. This approach allows them to rapidly scale market presence and revenue while simultaneously improving efficiency and profitability across newly acquired assets.

The discussion emphasizes that the most powerful growth levers are acquiring into new trades (vertical expansion) or new geographies (horizontal expansion). Crucially, Wilson details how building centralized, scalable infrastructure (accounting, HR, marketing) allows the company to outgrow its overhead, making subsequent acquisitions far more profitable than they would be for a smaller, less systematized company.

## Key Points
- **The Best-in-Class Growth Model:** Successful scaling requires a balanced approach: **Organic Growth** (refining internal processes, marketing, and sales) combined with **Inorganic Growth** (M&A). Companies that are too lopsided on either side are deemed "weak."
- **Outgrowing Overhead:** The primary goal of scaling is to increase revenue while decreasing the proportional overhead. Wilson notes that by building centralized systems (e.g., accounting, HR), the company can handle massive revenue increases without adding proportional staff or fixed costs.
- **The Overhead Reduction Play:** A key financial strategy is acquiring businesses with high overhead (e.g., 35-40% overhead) and applying centralized corporate resources (internal teams, better vendor contracts) to bring that overhead down dramatically, potentially to 15-20%.
- **Growth Levers:** The easiest and most effective ways to expand are:
    1. **Vertical Expansion:** Acquiring a business in a new trade (e.g., buying electrical when you are already plumbing). This bypasses the extreme difficulty of starting a new trade from scratch.
    2. **Horizontal Expansion:** Acquiring a business in a new, adjacent geography.
- **The Importance of Process Centralization:** The ability to scale is directly tied to having a "locked and loaded" back office. The speaker notes that having centralized accounting and HR teams means adding $10 million in revenue does not require adding proportional staff, which is the definition of scalable infrastructure.
- **The Operational Pain Point:** Many businesses lose jobs not because they are bad, but because they are *busy*. Missed calls, forgotten follow-ups, or context loss leads to lost leads. Centralized communication platforms are critical for maintaining a consistent customer experience.

## Tools & Resources

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Overhead:** Calculate your current overhead percentage. Identify specific areas (e.g., vendor contracts, software subscriptions, staffing ratios) where you can centralize purchasing or processes to reduce fixed costs and improve gross margin.
- [ ] **Develop a Centralized Process Playbook:** Document your most successful marketing and sales processes. Treat these processes as intellectual property that can be packaged and sold/applied to any acquired business, making your company the "system provider," not just the service provider.
- [ ] **Prioritize Infrastructure Investment:** Before attempting a major acquisition, ensure your back office (accounting, CRM, HR) can handle a 2x or 3x increase in revenue without proportionally increasing headcount.
- [ ] **Evaluate Communication Stack:** Implement a centralized communication platform (like Quo) to ensure every customer interaction is logged and visible to the entire team, eliminating context loss and improving lead capture consistency.
- [ ] **Target Strategic Acquisitions:** When looking for M&A opportunities, prioritize businesses that offer either a new, high-margin service vertical or a desirable new geography, rather than simply acquiring revenue for its own sake.
