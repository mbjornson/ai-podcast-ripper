# What Home Service Business Should You Start in 2026? (And How to Win Early) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode provides a highly critical and updated playbook for starting or expanding a home service business in 2026, arguing that all previous strategies (pre-2020) are obsolete due to market maturity and consolidation. The core thesis is that success hinges entirely on deep, current research to identify unmet needs—the "gap" where leads will flow organically without aggressive marketing spend.

The discussion emphasizes that the industry landscape is shifting from a fragmented, high-growth environment (like 2018) toward one of heavy consolidation. This means traditional 'gold mine' sectors like HVAC, plumbing, and electrical are nearing the end of their profitable growth cycle, leading to depressed multiples for potential buyers. The guest’s credibility stems from his deep involvement in these industries and his ability to observe market cycles, warning listeners that assuming a business will be sold at high multiples is dangerous if they don't enter an unfragmented niche.

## Key Points
- **Research Over Skillset:** The single most important factor for starting a home service business is rigorous research into the local job/business market. A guaranteed win occurs when you identify a specific, unmet need in a region.
- **The Consolidation Warning (Multiples Drop):** Avoid industries that are currently undergoing heavy consolidation (e.g., HVAC, plumbing, electrical). As big firms buy up smaller players, the available "big meaningful targets" diminish, and multiples for acquisitions drop significantly.
- **Niche Selection:** Instead of aiming for massive, saturated markets, look for areas with low competition or those that are not yet consolidated. The goal is to find a niche where leads flow organically from necessity, rather than requiring paid lead generation.
- **The Irrelevance of Old Playbooks:** Any business playbook created between 2016 and 2020 is outdated. Today's competitive environment requires current data and localized market analysis.
- **High Exit Multiples (Pest Control Example):** The discussion highlighted that certain niche, route-based services (like pest control) can command extremely high exit multiples (e.g., an 18x multiple on $1M EBITDA), suggesting these areas may be more lucrative for building a sellable asset than highly consolidated trades.
- **Cash Flow vs. Net Worth:** When advising on business value, the guest noted that focusing solely on maximizing net worth is misleading if the goal is immediate cash flow. A stable income stream (e.g., $1M/year) can be incredibly wealthy regardless of the company's total valuation.
