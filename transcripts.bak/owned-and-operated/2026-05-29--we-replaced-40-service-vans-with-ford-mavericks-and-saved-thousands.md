# We Replaced 40 Service Vans with Ford Mavericks and Saved Thousands — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode details a significant operational overhaul where the company replaced a large portion of its service fleet (40 out of 130 vehicles) with Ford Mavericks, a smaller, more affordable truck model. The core discussion centers on challenging the industry's outdated assumption that large, expensive vans are necessary for professional service work. The guest, who manages a large service fleet, demonstrates that by implementing a highly efficient "sales to install system" and optimizing internal storage, the operational capacity of a smaller vehicle can match or exceed that of a much larger, more costly van.

The main takeaway is that the vehicle itself is less important than the business process and the efficiency of the stock system. By adopting a smaller, more fuel-efficient, and significantly cheaper vehicle, the company achieved massive capital savings while maintaining service capability. The success of this transition is attributed not just to the truck, but to the ability to redesign the workflow and inventory management process.

## Key Points
- **Capital Efficiency through Vehicle Choice:** The cost difference is massive: Vans typically cost $50,000 to $60,000, while the Mavericks are priced around $27,000. This represents a huge capital savings per vehicle.
- **The System is the Solution, Not the Vehicle:** The most critical insight is that the perceived need for a van is based on a flawed assumption of storage capacity. The actual bottleneck is the lack of an efficient stock system. The solution is integrating inventory into standardized, portable "packout kits."
- **Optimizing Space:** Storage capacity can be maximized by removing non-essential elements, such as back seats, to install custom shelving. The use of tonneau covers and caps can add significant, usable vertical space to the truck bed.
- **The "Sales to Install" Model:** The operational success hinges on a structured sales process that leads directly to an installation follow-up. This system ensures that inventory is sold and immediately utilized, preventing the accumulation of unused "shelf space" inventory that plagues traditional van setups.
- **Operational Advantages of Smaller Vehicles:** Beyond cost, the Mavericks offer physical advantages: they are easier to drive, less prone to accidents, and better suited for navigating tight urban service areas.

## Tools & Resources
- **Ford Maverick:** The specific vehicle used for the fleet replacement.
- **Packout Kits:** Essential, standardized kits used to organize and transport tools and consumables, allowing for maximum inventory density in a smaller vehicle.
- **Tonneau Covers/Caps:** Accessories that add usable, protected storage space to the top of the truck bed and cab.

## Quotable
> "The reality is I can get two of these [Mavericks] for the price of one of these [vans]."
> "The industry got used to one model 15, 20 years ago where we have to do a van. But the reality is... we just need to learn how to run a better and tighter system."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct a Capital Efficiency Audit:** Analyze your current fleet/equipment spending. Identify if the cost of the asset is disproportionate to its actual utility (e.g., are you paying for "shelf space" that is never used?).
- [ ] **Implement a "Sales-to-Service" Workflow:** Redesign your business process so that every sale immediately triggers a follow-up service or installation. This ensures inventory is utilized and prevents capital from being tied up in stagnant stock.
- [ ] **Standardize Inventory with Kits:** Stop treating inventory as bulk storage. Invest in standardized, modular, and portable storage systems (like packout kits) to maximize density and mobility.
- [ ] **Challenge Industry Assumptions:** When faced with a perceived need for a large, expensive solution, force yourself to first design a hyper-efficient, smaller-scale system before committing to the large asset purchase.
