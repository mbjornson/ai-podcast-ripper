# How to Budget Marketing in 2026 (Without Wasting Cash) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into advanced financial strategies for allocating marketing budgets, particularly for service-based businesses. The central thesis challenges conventional wisdom by arguing that basing marketing spend as a percentage of total revenue is fundamentally flawed. Instead, the speaker advocates for calculating all overhead costs, including marketing, based on the company's **Gross Profit**. This approach provides a much more accurate picture of financial health because it accounts for varying gross margins (e.g., comparing a 40% margin business to a 60% margin business).

The discussion also emphasizes that modern budgeting must treat marketing as a flexible, demand-driven expense rather than a fixed cost. Furthermore, the speaker stresses that lead generation is paramount; having multiple diverse channels driving leads increases overall business resilience and safety. The guest's background in running an agency focused on local service businesses lends credibility to these practical financial recommendations.

## Key Points
- **Budgeting Metric Shift:** Marketing budget should be calculated as a percentage of **Gross Profit**, not total Revenue. This is crucial because Gross Profit accounts for the direct cost of goods sold (labor, materials, permits), providing a truer picture of available funds before operating expenses are considered.
- **P&L Structure Overhaul:** For accurate budgeting, Marketing Expense should be separated from general Overhead in the Profit & Loss statement. Treating marketing as a flexible expense allows businesses to scale spending up or down based on market demand and performance, rather than being locked into fixed monthly amounts.
- **Lead Volume > Budget Adherence:** The primary goal of marketing spend is lead volume and quality. Businesses should prioritize building multiple, diverse channels for lead generation (e.g., PPC, SEO, Google Business Profile) because the more sources available, the safer the business is from market fluctuations.
- **The Danger of Fixed Costs in Marketing:** Treating marketing as a fixed cost ignores its dynamic nature. If a channel (like Google Ads) proves highly effective, the budget should be flexible enough to rapidly increase investment without being constrained by an arbitrary "2026 budget."

## Tools & Resources
- **Gross Profit Margin Calculation:** Understanding this metric is foundational. Formula: Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold = Gross Profit.
- **PPC (Pay-Per-Click) Advertising:** A core channel mentioned for targeted lead generation.
- **Local Service Ads / Google Business Profile:** Essential, high-intent local search channels that should be prioritized over general advertising to capture actively searching customers.

## Quotable
> "I think that marketing budget should not be a percentage of revenue. I think that it should be a percentage of gross profit."
> "The more channels you have driving leads, the safer your business is."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Recalculate Your Margins:** Stop using total revenue as your benchmark for overhead spending. Calculate and track your Gross Profit Margin (Revenue minus COGS) to determine a financially sound percentage for marketing spend.
- [ ] **Audit Your P&L:** Work with an accountant to separate Marketing Expense from general Overhead in your financial reporting, treating it as a flexible cost center rather than a fixed one.
- [ ] **Diversify Lead Channels:** Do not rely on a single lead source (e.g., only paid ads). Implement and optimize at least 3-4 diverse channels (PPC, SEO, Local Ads, etc.) to ensure consistent lead flow regardless of market changes.
- [ ] **Focus on High-Intent Channels:** Prioritize optimizing your Google Business Profile and local service ads over broad, general advertising campaigns, as these capture customers who are actively searching for your services right now.
