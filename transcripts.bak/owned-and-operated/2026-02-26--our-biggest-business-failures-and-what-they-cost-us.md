# Our Biggest Business Failures (And What They Cost Us) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode features John Wilson, CEO of the $40 million Wilson Companies (a plumbing, HVAC, and electric service provider in Ohio and Indiana), discussing major business failures with fellow industry leader Jack Caram. The discussion centers less on specific services and more on high-level financial discipline, noting that almost all their biggest mistakes were tied directly to poor cashflow management rather than apparent Profit & Loss (P&L) wins. Wilson emphasizes that true growth requires locking down processes and understanding the actual cash impact of every investment or acquisition.

The hosts also provided a macro view of the home service industry, noting that while the sector is currently experiencing a strong rebound in early 2026, this recovery follows a difficult period throughout the back half of 2025. This downturn was cyclical and affected all parts of the supply chain—manufacturers, distributors, and service providers alike—giving listeners insight into industry-wide resilience and timing.

## Key Points
- **Cashflow is the ultimate metric:** The speaker asserts that nearly every major business failure they experienced stemmed from poor cashflow management, not just a seemingly profitable P&L statement. The focus must be on understanding the actual investment required and the time to payback.
- **Acquisition Metrics are Critical:** When acquiring new businesses, success can be rapid. One example cited involved taking over a company that was losing money, which subsequently achieved an EBITDA of $40,000–$50,000 per month within 30 days. Another deal reportedly doubled the business size.
- **Operational Leakage is Constant:** Even seemingly small operational mistakes can be material. Examples include overspending $10,000 monthly on software licenses or spending $13,000 on ad spend that only generated $7,000 in revenue. This highlights the need for continuous process auditing.
- **Industry Cycle Awareness:** The current strong performance (early 2026) is noted as a rebound from a difficult period throughout the back half of 2025. Understanding these cyclical dips can help anticipate when and how to position capital during industry slowdowns.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Operational Spending:** Conduct a deep dive into recurring monthly expenses (software licenses, ad spend) to identify "leakage" and ensure every dollar spent is directly tied to verifiable revenue generation.
- [ ] **Cashflow First Due Diligence:** When evaluating any acquisition or major CapEx decision, prioritize calculating the immediate cash flow impact and time-to-payback over simply looking at projected P&L growth.
- [ ] **Implement GPS/Vehicle Tracking for Expenses:** If running a fleet, adopt specialized fuel cards (like Coast) that use real-time vehicle data to verify transactions, thereby preventing fraud and improving financial controls.
- [ ] **Study Industry Cycles:** Monitor macro trends in the sector (e.g., home services, construction) to anticipate cyclical dips (like the back half of 2025 mentioned) and prepare capital strategies for slow periods.
