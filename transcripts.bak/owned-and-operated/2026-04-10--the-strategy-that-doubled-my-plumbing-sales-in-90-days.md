# The Strategy That Doubled My Plumbing Sales in 90 Days — Owned and Operated

## Summary
John Wilson, CEO of a $40 million home service company, details how his team doubled the sales of a functional, profitable plumbing business in just 90 days. His core thesis is that massive growth does not require revolutionary overhauls, but rather the disciplined application of fundamental business "basics." The initial business was profitable but limited by inefficient processes, poor lead capture, and underpriced services. Wilson emphasizes that the strategy was not about being a "mastermind," but about optimizing the entire operational funnel—from lead generation to technician compensation—to maximize efficiency and revenue.

The discussion provides a highly tactical playbook for service-based businesses. The approach involves systematically addressing bottlenecks in marketing (shifting focus from brand awareness to immediate lead volume), technology (closing the gap between lead acquisition and contact), and human capital (optimizing pricing, compensation, and training). The overall message is that consistent, fundamental improvements across multiple departments yield exponential, rapid growth.

## Key Points
- **Focus on Lead Volume over Brand Building (Short-Term):** For a growing service business, the immediate priority must be filling the "board" (the schedule) daily. The focus should be on high-contact, lead-driven marketing (Google, Meta, direct calling/texting) rather than expensive, long-term brand advertising (billboards, TV).
- **Speed to Lead is Critical:** The biggest loss point is the time gap between acquiring a lead and contacting the customer. Implementing "Speed to Lead" technology (placed between the marketing source and the CRM) automates immediate contact, preventing leads from going to competitors.
- **Pricing is the Fastest Lever:** A 15% pricing adjustment was implemented, raising rates from 20-30% below market to a more competitive level. This provided an immediate boost to net profit with zero additional cost and no reported drop in closing rate.
- **The Leader Investment Principle:** Investing in the manager or leader ("the leader that is leading that team") is paramount. The speaker describes this as an "endless bucket"—the more you give to your leader in terms of resources and education, the more they will pour into their team, which then serves the customer.
- **Operational Consolidation:** Removing administrative burden from the field team (taking over accounting, marketing, dispatch, and call center management) allows technicians to focus 100% of their energy on the core revenue-generating activities: presenting options, closing sales, and customer satisfaction.

## Tools & Resources
- **Speed to Lead Technology:** A middleware solution placed between lead sources (Angie, Google, etc.) and the CRM to ensure immediate, automated contact with incoming leads.
- **CRM (Customer Relationship Management):** Essential for tracking leads and customer interactions.
- **AI Call Center Partners (e.g., Voca):** Used to provide 24/7 coverage for missed calls and voicemails, ensuring no lead is lost due to time constraints.
- **Book:** *Only the Paranoid Survive* (Discusses strategic reinvention, using Intel's transformation as a case study).

## Quotable
> "We didn't overhaul everything. We didn't break the business and rebuild it from the ground floor. We took a functioning business that was operating profitably and we made it better."
>
> "The more you invest into the leader that is leading that team... the more that leader is going to pour into his team. It is an endless bucket."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Lead Flow:** Immediately map the journey of a lead from acquisition (Google/Meta) to contact. Identify the time gap and implement a "Speed to Lead" solution to close it.
- [ ] **Conduct Pricing Analysis:** Perform secret shopping and market research to determine if your service pricing is significantly below the local market average. Test a modest, strategic price increase (e.g., 10-15%).
- [ ] **Optimize Compensation:** Review the commission structure for field professionals. Ensure that incentives are competitive and motivating enough to drive top performance.
- [ ] **Delegate Admin Tasks:** Identify non-revenue generating administrative tasks (e.g., basic accounting, initial dispatch, basic marketing) and either automate them or hire dedicated staff to remove them from the hands of technicians/sales staff.
- [ ] **Invest in Leadership Training:** Dedicate resources to training the immediate managers/supervisors, focusing on advanced skills like objection handling, pricing presentation, and team motivation, rather than only training the front-line staff.
- [ ] **Read:** *Only the Paranoid Survive* for a framework on navigating major business reinvention and disruption.
