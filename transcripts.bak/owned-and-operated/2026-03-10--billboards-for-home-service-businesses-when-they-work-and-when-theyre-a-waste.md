# Billboards for Home Service Businesses: When They Work (and When They’re a Waste) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into Out-of-Home (OOH) advertising, specifically billboards, arguing that they are only effective when implemented as a highly integrated component of a larger, multi-channel marketing strategy. The discussion, featuring host John Wilson (CEO of Wilson Plumbing) and Sam Preston (CEO of Service Scalers), establishes that billboards are not a standalone solution; they are a visual extension of a brand that must be consistent across TV, radio, and digital platforms. The core message is that spending money on isolated, poorly targeted billboards is a sign of desperation, not strategy.

The experts emphasize that successful billboard campaigns build pervasive brand recognition—the kind where the brand or a unique visual element (like an eyebrow) is so omnipresent that it becomes instantly recognizable, regardless of the specific ad copy. Furthermore, the discussion provides a critical view of marketing spend, noting that a healthy, growing business should be allocating a significant percentage of revenue to marketing, and that the *allocation* of that budget is more important than the sheer amount spent.

## Key Points
- **Integration is Mandatory:** Billboards must be part of a "larger strategy" (e.g., matching the message on a billboard to an ad running on Roku, YouTube, or TV). They should not exist in a vacuum.
- **The Danger of Isolation:** Spending money on a single, isolated billboard on a road with low traffic, or without corresponding efforts in other channels, is deemed a waste of money and a sign of desperation.
- **The Power of Pervasiveness:** The most successful campaigns achieve saturation and recognition (e.g., Tim Ney's repeated branding or Jeff Cook's local visibility). This builds trust and recall, making the brand synonymous with the service.
- **Budget Sweet Spots:** Billboards are most effective at two extremes: either a very small budget (e.g., $500/month) for initial testing, or a very large budget (e.g., $50,000/month) for core branding. The middle ground is often inefficient.
- **Marketing Spend Benchmark:** A key indicator of business health is the marketing spend. The discussion highlighted a successful business spending a "perfect 10%" of its revenue on marketing, suggesting that a low percentage indicates a lack of reinvestment and growth focus.
