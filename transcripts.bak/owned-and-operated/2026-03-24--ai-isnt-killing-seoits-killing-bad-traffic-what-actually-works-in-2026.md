# AI Isn’t Killing SEO—It’s Killing Bad Traffic (What Actually Works in 2026) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode tackles the prevailing anxiety surrounding AI's impact on Search Engine Optimization (SEO). The core thesis presented is that AI is not killing SEO; rather, it is eliminating "bad traffic"—low-intent, high-volume traffic that previously inflated vanity metrics. The focus must shift from simply ranking for keywords to providing the most trustworthy, reliable, and contextually accurate answer.

The discussion emphasizes that modern SEO success, particularly in local markets, hinges on understanding the user's *intent* and *context* ("the why, the where"), not just the search query. The speakers critique traditional SEO metrics, arguing that raw traffic numbers and even established metrics like Domain Authority (DA) are often misleading or irrelevant compared to actual conversion data.

The host, John Wilson, is the CEO of Wilson Plumbing, heating, cooling and Electric, providing a strong background in the home service industry. The guest, Lisa from Service Scalers, reinforces the strategic shift toward measurable, high-quality leads over sheer volume.

## Key Points
- **The Shift from Keywords to Context:** In 2026, SEO success requires building for a deep understanding of user context and intent, moving far beyond simply optimizing for keywords.
- **The "Bad Traffic" Filter:** The perceived drop in traffic is often not a failure, but a positive indicator that low-intent, informational traffic (e.g., "how to fix a dripping faucet") is being filtered out by AI overviews and search engines.
- **The Conversion Metric is King:** The most critical metric is conversions. The speakers argue that focusing on traffic volume is a "false sun," as many visitors (especially those sent to blogs) are not potential customers.
- **Domain Authority (DA) is Flawed:** DA (and similar metrics like DR) are criticized as non-measurable, tool-specific metrics. They are not reliable indicators of true site performance or ranking power.
- **High-Intent Local Searches Persist:** While AI is changing search, high-intent, local searches (e.g., "best Thai food in STO Ohio") are still highly likely to be conducted directly on Google, making local SEO relevance paramount.

## Tools & Resources
- **CFO Made Easy:** A service specializing in financial clarity for home service owners, helping improve profit by tightening up pricing, labor, and overhead.
- **Ahrefs:** Mentioned as a tool, but the speakers provide a strong critique of its metrics (DA/DR), advising users to focus on conversions instead.

## Quotable
> "Bad traffic went down. Not that it's necessarily bad traffic, but I think it was low intent."
> "I could ignore every single other metric if conversions going in the right direction."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit your reporting:** Stop obsessing over raw traffic volume. Shift all reporting to focus on **conversion rates** and **lead quality** (i.e., how many visitors are actual potential customers).
- [ ] **Optimize for Context:** When creating content, don't just answer a question; build the page to be the most trustworthy, reliable, and accurate *solution* to a problem, incorporating local context ("the where").
- [ ] **Financial Deep Dive:** If running a service business, treat financial clarity as a core business function. Seek out a financial partner (like CFO Made Easy) to analyze pricing, labor, and overhead to maximize profit margins.
- [ ] **Re-evaluate SEO Metrics:** When reviewing SEO performance, ignore Domain Authority scores. Focus instead on the correlation between content changes and measurable increases in high-intent, local conversions.
