# Why We Stopped Buying Service Vans — And You Should Too — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode details a significant operational overhaul within a commercial service business (HVAC, plumbing, electrical), centered around replacing traditional, expensive service vans and box trucks with more cost-effective pickup vehicles, specifically the Ford Maverick. The core argument is that large vans are prohibitively expensive ($55k–$65k) to purchase and maintain, suffer from poor fuel economy, and are operationally overkill for modern service requirements. By shifting to Mavericks (priced around $26k–$27k), the company achieves massive cost savings while maintaining or improving operational reliability, even during severe weather events like ice storms.

The discussion goes beyond mere vehicle replacement; it outlines a complete rethinking of workflow and inventory management. The business is moving away from bulky, expensive "wrap packs" for tool storage toward modular, highly efficient systems like Milwaukee Packouts. This shift emphasizes keeping specialized tools secure within the cab area while relegating bulk materials (like copper or tanks) to the bed, transforming the vehicle into a streamlined, high-efficiency mobile workshop.

## Key Points
- **Cost Efficiency is Paramount:** The primary driver for the fleet change was cost. Switching from $60,000 vans to Mavericks cut capital expenditure by roughly half, making it "the most cash efficient decision."
- **Operational Shift: From Inventory Storage to Tool Systems:** The company is moving away from large, costly inventory storage solutions (like wrap packs) because they are inefficient and expensive. They are adopting modular tool systems (Milwaukee Packouts) which provide superior organization and scalability for specialized tools.
- **The "Hair-Brained" Idea:** The ultimate goal is to move away from vans and box trucks entirely, suggesting that a smaller vehicle combined with optimized workflow can handle nearly all service calls previously requiring massive utility vehicles.
- **Reliability in Adverse Conditions:** The Mavericks proved superior during an ice storm, remaining mobile and functional where the larger, high-top vans frequently got stuck.
- **Strategic Purchasing:** To manage costs further, the company leverages large group purchases (e.g., 30 units) to acquire surplus vehicles (28 units), maximizing savings through strategic purchasing relationships with dealerships.

## Tools & Resources
- **Ford Maverick:** The recommended vehicle for service fleets due to its lower cost, better fuel economy, and sufficient utility for HVAC/plumbing work.
- **Milwaukee Packouts:** A modular tool storage system that replaced expensive "wrap packs," allowing for highly organized, customizable, and scalable tool management within the truck bed or cab area.
- **Tono Covers / Custom Racks:** Specialized roof rack systems used to mount necessary equipment (like water heaters) while maintaining vehicle profile and utility.

## Quotable
> "We started just for like, our salespeople who are running around in, in like F1 fifties... we ended up doing the Chevy Trailblazers... [and] Mavericks because they're so much cheaper."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct a Fleet/Equipment Cost Audit:** Analyze your current service vehicle costs (purchase price, fuel efficiency, maintenance) against smaller, more agile alternatives to identify immediate capital expenditure savings.
- [ ] **Implement Modular Tooling Systems:** Replace large, fixed storage units with modular systems (like Packouts or similar interlocking containers) to maximize space utilization and improve tool organization on job sites.
- [ ] **Redefine Inventory Storage:** Challenge the necessity of storing bulk inventory in your service vehicles. Instead, focus on keeping only specialized tools and critical parts within the vehicle, while managing larger stock offsite or through optimized supply chains.
- [ ] **Prioritize Fuel Economy/Agility:** When selecting commercial vehicles, prioritize fuel efficiency and ground clearance/maneuverability over sheer volume capacity to improve operational reliability in varied conditions.
