# We Merged Our Home Service Companies—Now We’re Building to $100M — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode details the aggressive scaling strategy of John Wilson and Jack Carr, who are merging their respective home service companies (HVAC, plumbing, electrical) into a massive, super-regional platform. John’s existing platform is already valued at $40 million across three Midwest states, and the goal is to expand this into a multi-state behemoth, eventually aiming for $100 million. The core discussion revolves around the mechanics of scaling beyond organic growth by acquiring and integrating adjacent local businesses.

The central thesis is that true scale and profitability in the home service industry are achieved through operational synergy and economies of scale, not just increased revenue. The speakers emphasize that by merging multiple local operations, they can solve critical industry pain points, such as limited installer capacity and inflated material costs, allowing them to compete in a fundamentally different, more profitable market space.

## Key Points
- **Operational Leverage is the New Scale:** The most significant insight is that the bottleneck in growth is often not sales, but physical capacity. The hosts noted they were previously "purely handicapped" by having limited install crews, even when sales were strong (e.g., selling $180,000 more than they could install). The solution is acquiring adjacent businesses to share installer capacity, eliminating the concept of a "slow day."
- **The Power of Group Buying/Consolidation:** The discussion highlighted massive cost discrepancies when operating independently versus being part of a large group. They cited instances where equipment costs were 50% less through group purchasing (e.g., through Lennox at the corporate level) compared to what individual contractors were paying.
- **Marketing Focus: Calls, Not Clicks:** The hosts criticized generic marketing metrics (clicks, impressions, traffic). They stressed that for home services, the only metric that matters is driving "real high quality calls and book jobs."
- **Targeting the Super-Regional Gap:** The strategy involves connecting major metropolitan areas across state lines (e.g., connecting the West side of Pennsylvania to Chicago, or expanding through Kentucky). This allows them to capture market share and resources in previously isolated regions.
- **Profit Margin Discrepancies:** They pointed out that large groups can achieve significantly higher gross margins (e.g., 65%) compared to smaller, independent operators (e.g., 45%), primarily due to superior purchasing power and operational efficiency.

## Tools & Resources
- **Service Scalers:** Mentioned as a marketing partner built specifically for home service companies. They focus on driving high-quality calls and booked jobs and offer a 60-day money-back guarantee on LSA management and Google Business Profile optimization.
- **ServiceTitan:** Mentioned in the context of pricing concerns, suggesting that while it is a major industry tool, its pricing structure can be a point of friction for smaller operators.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Operational Audit:** Analyze your current business's capacity constraints. Determine if your growth is limited by sales, marketing, or physical installation/service capacity.
- [ ] **Acquisition Strategy:** Identify 2-3 adjacent, non-competing service businesses in a contiguous geographic area. Develop a plan to acquire them specifically for the purpose of sharing labor/install capacity.
- [ ] **Vendor Negotiation:** Benchmark your material and equipment costs against industry groups. Investigate how much cost savings (e.g., 30-50%) could be achieved by consolidating purchasing power with other local businesses.
- [ ] **Marketing Shift:** Re-evaluate your marketing spend to focus exclusively on lead generation metrics (booked jobs/high-quality calls) rather than vanity metrics (impressions/clicks).
- [ ] **Networking:** Actively connect with peers in the industry (the "building with friends" model) to identify potential partners for joint ventures or acquisitions.
