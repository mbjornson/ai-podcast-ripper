# The MOST Overlooked Growth Lever in Home Services — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode focuses on "lifecycle marketing"—the systematic process of communicating with a customer base *after* the initial service job is complete. The discussion, led by John Wilson (CEO of Wilson, a multi-state contracting business) and Sam Preston, argues that most home service companies treat every job as a one-off transaction, failing to capitalize on the immense value of their existing customer database.

The core thesis is that the most overlooked growth lever is not speed to lead (though that is critical), but rather the disciplined "retouching" of the customer file. This involves using multi-channel communication (email, SMS, outbound calls, postcards) to keep the brand top-of-mind for future needs, cross-sell services, and generate referrals. The speakers emphasize that maximizing the ROI on paid leads requires treating the customer database as a continuous, revenue-generating asset.

## Key Points
- **The Lifecycle Gap:** Most home service companies stop marketing after the job is done. The true opportunity lies in the entire customer lifecycle: pre-appointment prep, post-job review generation, maintenance reminders, and years-later upselling.
- **Cost of Leads vs. Value of Database:** Leads cost significant money ($30 to $80 per contact). The goal of lifecycle marketing is to maximize the return on this investment by ensuring the customer file is continuously engaged, preventing the spend from being "wasted."
- **Speed to Lead Benchmark:** While speed is crucial, the ideal response time when a lead requests information must be **under 15 seconds**.
- **The Power of Reacquisition:** Reacquiring a customer is significantly cheaper than acquiring a new one. The speakers cite a case where implementing this discipline led to a **48-50% business growth** in the first year.
- **Multi-Channel Approach:** The most effective strategies involve a mix of channels: SMS, outbound calling, postcards, and targeted digital ads (Google Ads/Meta) aimed at past customers.
- **Reputation as an Asset:** Maintaining a strong Google Business Profile (GBP) is not just marketing; it's a background system that compounds trust, automatically generating leads and improving local search ranking.

## Tools & Resources
- **Big Reputation:** A tool recommended for managing and scaling Google Business Profile reputation across multiple locations, providing automated posting, review generation, and location health monitoring.
- **SMS/Email Marketing Platforms:** Any system capable of executing automated, multi-stage communication funnels (e.g., follow-up sequences, maintenance reminders).
- **Outbound Calling/CRM Systems:** Tools necessary to scale high-volume outbound calling campaigns (e.g., 1,000 calls/day).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Customer Journey:** Map out every touchpoint a customer has with your company, from initial inquiry to 5 years post-service. Identify where communication currently drops off.
- [ ] **Implement the 15-Second Rule:** Mandate that all lead inquiries (phone, web form, etc.) are acknowledged and addressed within 15 seconds.
- [ ] **Systematize Post-Job Follow-Up:** Create an automated sequence (SMS/Email) that triggers immediately after a job is completed. This sequence must include: 1) Review request, 2) Cross-sell opportunity (e.g., "Since we were in your HVAC, did you know we also handle electrical?"), and 3) Maintenance reminder for the next year.
- [ ] **Establish a "Reactivation" Campaign:** Dedicate a specific budget and channel (postcards, targeted ads) to re-engage customers who haven't purchased in 12-24 months.
- [ ] **Optimize GBP:** If multi-location, implement a system (like Big Reputation) to automate review generation and consistent posting across all Google Business Profiles to maximize local SEO authority.
