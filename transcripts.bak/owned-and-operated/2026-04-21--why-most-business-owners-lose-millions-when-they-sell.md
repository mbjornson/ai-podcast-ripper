# Why Most Business Owners Lose Millions When They Sell — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode focuses on the critical, often overlooked financial and operational pitfalls that cause business owners to lose significant wealth when selling their company. The discussion, featuring McCall Wealth Advisors, highlights that the perceived sale price is rarely the actual net value. The advisors emphasize that most business owners' wealth is disproportionately tied up in the business itself (estimated at 80%), making proper exit planning paramount.

The core message is that preparing for an exit is not a sacrifice; it is a necessary process of cleaning up the business's financials and structure. The guests stress that timing is everything, advocating for involvement 5-7 years before an anticipated sale, rather than waiting until an offer lands. They provide a detailed cautionary tale illustrating how common pitfalls—such as excessive debt, required post-sale employment (earnouts), and tax liabilities—can drastically reduce the final payout.

## Key Points
- **Wealth Concentration Risk:** Most business owners have 80% of their wealth tied up in their business, leaving only 20% outside. This high concentration requires strategic planning to protect the primary asset ("the golden goose").
- **Optimal Exit Timeline:** Advisors recommend getting involved 5-7 years before an anticipated exit. Waiting until an offer is on the table (8 months out) severely limits optionality and negotiating power.
- **Tax Planning Tools (C-Corp):** Converting the business to a C-Corp can be a powerful tool for tax mitigation. Historically, capital gains up to $5 million could be written off, and more recently, qualified small business legislation has allowed for write-offs up to $75 million (though caveats apply).
- **The "Hidden Costs" of Sale:** A sale price is often misleading. The true net value must account for:
    1.  **Debt:** Significant portions of the sale price may be immediately liquidated to pay off existing debt.
    2.  **Earnouts:** Requiring the owner to stay on for years with compensation tied to performance (earnouts) compromises the asset's value and the owner's freedom.
    3.  **Taxes & Fees:** Taxes, attorney fees, and broker commissions must be factored out of the headline number.
- **Involuntary Exits:** Statistically, 50% of all exits are involuntary, making proactive preparation essential regardless of current plans.
