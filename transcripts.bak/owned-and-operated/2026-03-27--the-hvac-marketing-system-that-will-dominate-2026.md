# The HVAC Marketing System That Will Dominate 2026 — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode argues that for any home service business (HVAC, plumbing, etc.), relying on unpredictable sources like word-of-mouth, referrals, or seasonal weather patterns creates a "feast or famine" cycle, which is a sign of a structurally weak business. The speaker, John Wilson, CEO of a local HVAC company, asserts that marketing must be treated as a systematic, controllable process designed to generate consistent, ever-growing lead volume. He emphasizes that the goal is not just to spend money on marketing, but to build a predictable lead machine that can be scaled up or down as needed.

Wilson outlines that a healthy business must allocate a significant portion of its revenue (around 7-10%) to marketing. He strongly advises shifting marketing focus from "brand-heavy" efforts to "lead-focused" campaigns. The most critical operational insight is that even the best lead sources (like paid ads) fail if the business lacks the internal systems to handle them. Therefore, the ultimate focus must be on "Speed to Lead"—the ability to respond to a paid lead within minutes, if not seconds, to secure the job.

## Key Points
- **The Weakness Indicator:** A business that experiences "feast or famine" lead flow is fundamentally weak because it is over-reliant on uncontrollable variables (weather, organic referrals).
- **The Three Pillars of Marketing Success:** Consistent scaling requires three elements: 1) Generating enough demand, 2) Having controllable demand levers, and 3) Responding to leads fast enough.
- **Underinvestment Thresholds:** If a business spends less than 5% of its revenue on marketing, or if the phone rings only once or twice a day, it is likely underinvesting and cannot scale consistently.
- **The Power of Controllable Levers:** The most effective marketing sources are those the business can actively control and scale, such as Local Service Ads (LSA), Google Ads, paid listings (Angie's List, Modernize), and even canvassing/outbounding. These are superior to relying solely on SEO or referrals.
- **The Speed-to-Lead Imperative:** The biggest mistake contractors make is slow response time. Leads are lost because the response is not immediate. The goal must be to establish systems that allow the team to respond within 5 minutes, if not 5 seconds, because the first company to pick up the phone often wins the job.
- **Budget Allocation Shift:** Successful scaling involves shifting budget from brand-building activities (which are slow and passive) to immediate, high-intent lead generation (which is fast and measurable).

## Tools & Resources
- **Local Service Ads (LSA):** A key Google tool for generating high-intent leads based on rankings and reviews.
- **Google Ads (PPC):** The core pay-per-click mechanism, allowing for immediate, adjustable spend based on specific keywords (e.g., "Plumber Akron Ohio").
- **Google Business Profile:** Essential for local visibility; treated as a "mini-website hosted by Google" that requires consistent energy input.
- **Canvassing/Outbounding:** Physical or direct outreach methods highlighted as highly controllable lead sources.

## Quotable
> "If your business feels like it's feast or famine... that business is actually really weak."
>
> "The problem is never that those sources don't work. The problem is always that the contractor does not have the systems inside their business to allow those sources to work."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Marketing Spend:** Calculate the current percentage of revenue dedicated to marketing. If it is below 7-10%, develop a plan to increase investment.
- [ ] **Implement Lead-First Budgeting:** Reallocate marketing budget away from general brand awareness and toward paid, high-intent lead sources (LSA, Google Ads).
- [ ] **Establish a 5-Minute Response Protocol:** Design and train the sales team to ensure that every incoming lead (from any source) is acknowledged, contacted, and qualified within five minutes of arrival.
- [ ] **Optimize Google Business Profile:** Treat the GBP as a critical, active asset. Ensure all services, hours, and review responses are consistently managed to maximize map pack visibility.
- [ ] **Test Outbounding/Outreach:** Dedicate resources to proactive lead generation (e.g., calling past customers, canvassing specific neighborhoods) to create controllable demand levers.
