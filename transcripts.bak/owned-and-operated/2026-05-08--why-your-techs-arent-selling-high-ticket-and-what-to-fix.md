# Why Your Techs Aren’t Selling High Ticket (And What to Fix) — Owned and Operated

## Summary
The episode argues that low average ticket sales are rarely a sales team problem; they are almost always an organizational failure rooted in solving low-value problems. The speaker, John, CEO of Wilson Plumbing Heating Cooling, runs a $40 million HVAC/plumbing/electric business and has acquired 14 companies, giving him deep insight into common operational pitfalls in the service industry. His core thesis is that businesses are fixated on the easiest, lowest-cost solution (the "band-aid") rather than the most valuable, long-term solution.

The discussion pivots on the concept of "high-ticket problems." True high-value sales require identifying a massive, painful, and expensive problem (e.g., property damage from sewage backup) and presenting a comprehensive, high-cost solution (e.g., full system replacement), rather than just a small, immediate fix. To scale revenue, businesses must restructure their offerings using a "Value Ladder" and overhaul their internal processes by prioritizing input metrics (training, ride-alongs) over mere output metrics (closing rate).

## Key Points
- **The Problem is the Offering, Not the Salesperson:** Most businesses are solving for a low-value pain point. The goal is to shift the focus from "fixing the immediate symptom" to "solving the underlying, expensive problem."
- **High-Ticket Sales = High-Ticket Problems:** The value of the solution must match the value of the problem. Low-cost tools (like ChatGPT drafting a contract) are insufficient for high-stakes, multi-million dollar deals, requiring expensive, expert human intervention (like an attorney).
- **The Value Ladder Framework:** This is the mechanism for upselling. Instead of offering a single solution, the company must structure its services so that the initial, low-cost entry point leads the customer up to the highest-value, most profitable "big job."
- **Inputs vs. Outputs (Operational Focus):** Do not solely measure success by output metrics (average ticket, closing rate). Focus equally on input metrics, which are the systems that drive success: consistent training, structured curriculum, mandatory role-playing, and field ride-alongs.
- **Define Core Services:** To build a high average ticket, the entire company (marketing, sales, training, and operations) must be fully aligned around 1-3 "Core Services"—the most profitable jobs the company can perform.

## Tools & Resources
- **Value Ladder:** A strategic framework for structuring service offerings, ensuring that every initial interaction leads the customer up to a higher-ticket, more comprehensive solution.
- **Input/Output Metrics Model:** A system for measuring business performance that mandates tracking operational inputs (training frequency, ride-along hours, curriculum quality) alongside traditional outputs (revenue, close rate).
- **Core Service Definition:** A strategic exercise requiring the business owner to identify the 1-3 most profitable services and build all marketing and sales efforts around them.

## Quotable
> "High ticket sales is solving high-ticket problems."
>
> "If your average ticket is low, if your average sale is low, it's probably not just your text. What's the offering? Are you solving a valuable enough pain point? How often are you training? How often are you riding along?"

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Offering:** For your top 3 services, identify the "band-aid" solution (low cost) and the "replacement" solution (high value). Re-package your service to always present the high-value option first.
- [ ] **Implement the Value Ladder:** Map out a mandatory 3-step sales process for every lead, ensuring the initial consultation introduces the low-cost fix, but the final recommendation is the comprehensive, high-ticket solution.
- [ ] **Mandate Input Tracking:** Start tracking and scoring the following operational inputs weekly: 1) Training hours per rep, 2) Number of ride-alongs per rep, and 3) Completion of role-play scenarios.
- [ ] **Define Core Services:** Hold a meeting with your leadership team to formally select the 1-3 most profitable services and mandate that all marketing materials, sales scripts, and training modules revolve around these services.
- [ ] **Establish Cadence:** Institute a minimum weekly cadence of training (ideally 2-3 sessions) and schedule monthly or quarterly ride-alongs to coach reps in real-time.
