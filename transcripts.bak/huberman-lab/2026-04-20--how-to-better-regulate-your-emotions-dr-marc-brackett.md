# How to Better Regulate Your Emotions | Dr. Marc Brackett — Huberman Lab

## Summary
Dr. Mark Brackett, a professor of psychology at Yale University and director of Yale's Center for Emotional Intelligence, reframes emotional regulation (ER) not as the act of suppressing or eliminating feelings, but as a strategic, goal-oriented process. For the performance-focused listener, the core takeaway is that ER is about using your emotions *wisely* to achieve specific life goals. Brackett argues that the biggest misunderstanding is the idea that one must constantly "check and regulate" emotions; instead, emotions are background noise that only become critical when there is a shift in environment or relationships.

The discussion emphasizes that the most powerful tool in emotional intelligence is the ability to shift one's *mindset* about emotions. Brackett introduces the concept that there are no inherently "bad" emotions; rather, the difficulty lies in the *expression* of those emotions, which must be context-specific. By adopting a mindset that all feelings are acceptable, the listener can harness emotions like anxiety—which is simply a signal of perceived uncertainty—as a motivator rather than a source of dysregulation.

## Key Points
- **ER is Strategic, Not Suppressive:** Emotional regulation is defined as a goal-oriented process. It is not about getting rid of a feeling, but establishing a healthy, functional relationship with it (e.g., "saying hello" to anxiety until it passes).
- **The PRIME Framework:** Brackett provides a structured acronym for managing emotions:
    *   **P**revent unwanted emotions.
    *   **R**educe difficult ones.
    *   **I**nitiate emotions (e.g., creating an upregulating emotion in a room).
    *   **M**aintain an emotion (e.g., savoring a good moment).
    *   **E**nhance an emotion (boosting positive feelings).
- **The Mindset Shift:** The most critical step is changing your assumption about emotions. Instead of viewing anxiety as a negative state, recognize it as a useful signal of perceived uncertainty, which can motivate preparation and action.
- **Context Matters:** Emotional strategy selection is not universal. It is a function of the emotion, the individual's personality (e.g., neurotic, introverted), and the immediate context (e.g., you cannot use a cognitive strategy if you are physically stuck in a crowded room).

## Tools & Resources
- **PRIME Framework:** A structured model for intentionally managing emotional states (Prevent, Reduce, Initiate, Maintain, Enhance).
- **Emotional Relationship Skill:** The practice of acknowledging a feeling ("Hey, how are you doing today?") without getting swept away by it, allowing it to dissipate naturally.
- **Red Light Therapy (Juve):** Mentioned as a science-based tool for improving cellular and organ health, including faster muscle recovery, reduced inflammation, and improved mitochondrial function.

## Quotable

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement the PRIME Framework:** When facing a goal that requires emotional management (e.g., a difficult negotiation, a presentation), identify which PRIME strategy (Prevent, Reduce, Initiate, Maintain, Enhance) is most appropriate.
- [ ] **Practice "Helloing":** When a difficult emotion arises (anxiety, frustration), pause and simply acknowledge it ("I notice I feel anxious right now"). This non-judgmental observation reduces the emotion's power and allows it to pass.
- [ ] **Reframe Anxiety:** When feeling anxious about a future event (a pitch, a meeting), do not label it as "bad." Instead, identify the specific uncertainty it signals (e.g., "I am anxious because I care deeply about the outcome of this pitch").
- [ ] **Mindset Check:** Before engaging in a high-stakes social or professional situation, ask: "What is my current assumption about this emotion? Is it a signal or a threat?"
- [ ] **Consider Biofeedback Tools:** Research the use of near-infrared or red light therapy for physical recovery and inflammation reduction, as mentioned in the sponsor segment.
