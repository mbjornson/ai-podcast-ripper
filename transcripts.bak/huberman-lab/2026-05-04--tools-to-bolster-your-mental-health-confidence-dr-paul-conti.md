# Tools to Bolster Your Mental Health & Confidence | Dr. Paul Conti — Huberman Lab

## Summary
This episode provides a deep dive into the practical, actionable aspects of building mental health and confidence, moving beyond generic self-help platitudes. Dr. Paul Conti, a psychiatrist and expert in trauma recovery, emphasizes that true mental wellness is not merely an introspective process; it is a dynamic balance between thoughtful reflection and concrete action. The discussion centers on developing a greater sense of personal agency by shifting how one relates to their own self.

The core thesis revolves around adopting a "position of strength" when examining oneself. Instead of dwelling on what is "going wrong"—which often leads to negative labels and feelings of helplessness—the listener is encouraged to start by identifying "What's Going Right?" This approach is presented not just as a feel-good mechanism, but as a scientifically consistent way to build momentum for real change.

Furthermore, the conversation introduces advanced concepts regarding the self, including the difference between state-dependent behavior and the "Observing Ego." The goal is to provide listeners with specific, repeatable frameworks and questions to challenge limiting self-narratives, thereby increasing the malleability and flexibility of one's self-view.

## Key Points
- **Start from Strength:** When addressing mental health, always begin by identifying what is working well in your life ("What's Going Right?"). This is a position of strength that facilitates change, unlike focusing on deficits, which can lead to hopelessness and labeling.
- **Action > Introspection:** Mental health improvement requires a balance of thinking and doing. The process often involves "more doing than thinking," meaning that specific, external actions are required to solidify internal shifts.
- **The Two Pillars of Self-Exploration:** To understand yourself better, focus on two areas:
    1. **Self-Talk:** What messages are you giving yourself in quiet, private moments? Identifying negative or critical self-talk is a primary strategy for change.
    2. **Life Narrative:** What story do you tell yourself, or others, about who you are? Examine if this narrative accurately matches the reality of your life.
- **The Observing Ego:** Most people are "state dependent," meaning their thoughts and feelings change drastically based on their current environment or who they are with. The goal is to develop a higher, constant self—the "Observing Ego"—that remains true across all different states and situations.
- **Self-View is Malleable:** The relationship to the self is highly flexible. By approaching self-examination with "compassionate curiosity" and a belief that change is possible, one can overcome the feeling of being "stuck."

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *What's Going Right? A Powerful New Method for Optimizing Your Mental Health* (By Dr. Paul Conti).
- **Therapy:** Professional therapy is highly recommended for its ability to provide specific, actionable tools and insights that can be applied outside of the session.
- **Framework:** The "Structure of Self" and "Function of Self" (foundational pillars for understanding self-improvement).

## Quotable
> "There's far more going right in any of us, in all of us, than there is going wrong if we're here."
> "To be able to see, OK, I'm here and this is what I'm doing. The people I'm with and how I'm feeling and how I'm behaving, to be able to observe ourselves is how we knit together one self across situations."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Daily Self-Audit:** Dedicate time to identifying 3-5 things that are currently going well in your life (professional, physical, relational) to establish a "position of strength."
- [ ] **Journal Self-Talk:** For one week, pay close attention to the negative or critical messages you send yourself in private moments. Write them down to become aware of the pattern.
- [ ] **Challenge Your Narrative:** When describing yourself to others, ask: "Does this story I am telling match what is real and true about my life?" Identify discrepancies.
- [ ] **Practice Observation:** When in a social setting, consciously try to observe your internal state (thoughts, feelings) without judgment, recognizing that your current state is not your permanent self.
- [ ] **Seek Professional Guidance:** If motivation or mood challenges persist, engage in effective therapy to gain specific, actionable tools for change.
