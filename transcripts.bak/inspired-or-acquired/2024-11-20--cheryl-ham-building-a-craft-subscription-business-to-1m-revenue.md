# Cheryl Ham - Building A Craft & Subscription Business To +$1M Revenue — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Cheryl Ham detailed the journey of Hypnotic Yarn, a successful e-commerce business that started as a side hustle in her kitchen in 2017. She deliberately built the business with the intention of making it a full-fledged enterprise, rather than a hobby. The company successfully bootstrapped its growth, reinvesting profits until she quit her corporate job in 2020. The pivot to a closed subscription box model proved to be the major growth catalyst, transforming the business into a highly predictable, recurring revenue model.

The discussion shifted to the business's current valuation and health. The company is on track for $1 million in revenue this year, with 80% derived from subscriptions. The conversation highlighted that for modern, recurring revenue businesses, valuation is not simply a multiple of earnings. Instead, it relies heavily on key metrics like Customer Lifetime Value (LCV), Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), low churn, and a high Net Promoter Score (NPS).

## Key Points
- **The Power of Recurring Revenue:** The business model is heavily weighted toward subscriptions (80% of revenue), which significantly increases valuation stability and predictability compared to one-off e-commerce sales.
- **Bootstrapping Success:** The entire business was built without external debt or loans, relying solely on reinvested profits.
- **Key Health Metrics:** The business boasts a low churn rate (4-6%) and a high retention rate (95-96%), with an average customer lifetime of over 400 days (more than a year).
- **Valuation Drivers:** For high-tech, online, or subscription-based businesses, acquirers place high value on metrics beyond simple earnings, specifically: LCV, CAC, low churn, and high NPS.
- **Scaling Operations:** To manage rapid growth (from a small team to nine employees), the company is implementing the Entrepreneurial Operating System (EOS) to professionalize processes and allow the founder to shift focus from generalist tasks to high-level CEO duties (strategy, marketing, revenue goals).

## Tools & Resources
- **The Automatic Customer** by John Warlow: A recommended book for business owners with recurring revenue models, detailing simple ways to apply advanced metrics to their operations.
- **EOS (Entrepreneurial Operating System):** A systematic framework used to organize and grow the business, helping the founder transition from operational generalist to strategic CEO.
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS):** A critical metric for assessing customer loyalty and growth potential. It is calculated by asking customers (on a 1-10 scale) "How likely are you to refer our business to a friend or family member?" (Promoters: 9-10; Detractors: 0-6).

- [ ] **Implement NPS Tracking:** Immediately begin tracking the Net Promoter Score (NPS) using a simple 1-10 scale survey to quantify customer loyalty and provide a key data point for valuation.
- [ ] **Study EOS:** Invest time in learning the Entrepreneurial Operating System (EOS) framework to professionalize business processes and structure the transition from operational generalist to strategic CEO.
- [ ] **Focus on LCV/CAC:** Develop systems to rigorously track Customer Lifetime Value (LCV) and Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) to ensure marketing spend is maximizing long-term customer value.
- [ ] **Read *The Automatic Customer*:** Consult John Warlow's book for practical methods on applying advanced metrics to recurring revenue models.
- [ ] **Prioritize Recurring Revenue:** Structure the business model to maximize subscription or membership components, as this is the primary driver of valuation in modern e-commerce.
