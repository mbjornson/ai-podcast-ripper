# Jordan Evans - Buying The Family Business and Scaling to $12M — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Jordan Evans details his journey scaling a family-run language services business, Language Network, into a multi-million dollar enterprise through an aggressive roll-up and acquisition strategy. The company initially started in the 1980s as a local interpreter service for immigrants in Los Angeles, operating as a "lifestyle business" with no clear exit plan. Jordan entered the picture around 2017 after being fired from his previous career (software startups), recognizing an opportunity to apply modern entrepreneurial scaling techniques to this nascent service industry.

His core strategy has been acquiring smaller, local businesses across the US West Coast and integrating them into a cohesive platform. He built three primary vertical pillars: healthcare/hospitals, state/local government services, and global business support (manufacturing/marketing). This process allowed him to scale the company from an initial revenue of $700K in 2016 to projected annual revenues of $11-12 million by 2024.

The discussion provides deep insight into the mechanics of M&A for service businesses, particularly focusing on creative financing and operational standardization. Jordan emphasizes that success wasn't driven by a single massive loan or capital injection, but rather by developing repeatable systems—a "cookbook"—to buy, integrate, and grow lookalike companies in new geographies and verticals.

## Key Points
- **The Power of the Niche Market:** The domestic language services industry in the US is described as being larger than the global music industry, touching critical sectors like healthcare, government, education, and social services.
- **Scaling through Roll-Up Acquisitions:** Jordan executed four acquisitions to build Language Network into a platform. This strategy involved finding "lookalike businesses" and implementing standardized systems across each acquisition, creating operational efficiency (the "cookbook").
- **Financial Growth Metrics:** The company scaled from $700K in revenue (2016) to nearly $9.4M last year, projecting $11-12M this year—a factor of over 15x growth in seven years.
- **High Profit Margins:** Despite scaling significantly, the business maintains a robust margin structure, reporting an 18–19% bottom line (based on SDE/EBITDA).
- **Creative Deal Structuring (Non-Bankable Deals):** When traditional SBA loans were not viable, Jordan utilized advanced financing methods including:
    *   Equity investment.
    *   Seller Notes.
    *   Royalty Agreements (structuring payments as a commission for X number of years based on client retention). This is a highly non-obvious and actionable deal structure.
- **The "Sell vs. Keep" Dilemma:** While the financial outcome ($10M+ net pre-tax asset value) was massive, Jordan stated that money was never the primary motivation; staying engaged because of enjoyment/passion remains his core driver.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop an Acquisition Playbook:** Formalize a repeatable process (a "cookbook") for integrating acquired businesses into a central platform, focusing on standardizing operations across different verticals.
- [ ] **Explore Creative Financing Models:** When considering business acquisitions or exits, investigate structuring deals using royalty agreements and earn-outs rather than relying solely on traditional bank loans (SBA).
- [ ] **Identify Undervalued Service Niches:** Research large, under-recognized service industries (like domestic language services) that are essential to critical sectors (healthcare, government) but lack professionalization or consolidation.
- [ ] **Consult M&A Specialists:** Connect with firms like Bison Business for a complimentary evaluation if considering selling an existing business, or research the valuation of your own specialized skills/services against market rates.
- [ ] **Focus on Passion Over Profit (Initial Motivation):** When starting a venture, ensure that the core motivation is intrinsic enjoyment and problem-solving rather than solely financial gain, as this sustains long-term effort through difficult scaling phases.
