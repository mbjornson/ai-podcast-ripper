# Casey McDaniel - Pest Control & Crazy Exit Multiples — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode centers on the business of pest control, using the guest's company as a case study to discuss high-value, recurring revenue service businesses and their potential valuation. Casey McDaniel, the owner, details how his company built itself from scratch over the last three and a half years, focusing heavily on residential services. The core business model relies on quarterly preventative maintenance (spraying foundations, treating nests), which generates "sticky recurring revenue"—the most valuable asset in the industry.

The discussion quickly pivots from operational details to financial valuation. The hosts analyze McDaniel's impressive growth trajectory, noting that the company has roughly doubled its revenue year over year. The main thesis is that by building a predictable, high-margin service business with strong recurring billing, the company can achieve massive enterprise value, positioning it as an ideal acquisition target for private equity and institutional players.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Recurring Revenue:** The majority of the revenue (90%+) comes from quarterly service contracts (spraying foundations, etc.). This predictable, recurring billing is the primary driver of high valuation multiples.
- **Aggressive Growth Trajectory:** The company started in March 2022, generating $180K in the first nine months. They hit $1M in revenue in Year 3 and are on pace to reach $2M this year, demonstrating a rapid, near-doubling annual growth rate.
- **Valuation Multiples:** The market for well-structured service businesses is currently "bifurcated," meaning institutional players and private equity are aggressively buying up deals. The potential valuation is extremely high: achieving $6M in revenue could lead to $2M in EBITDA, placing the enterprise value in the **$15M to $25M** range.
- **Building vs. Selling Tension:** The discussion highlights the inherent tension for business owners: the choice between cashing out now for a solid number, or continuing to grow the business to maximize the eventual exit multiple.

## Tools & Resources
- **Eversight:** Mentioned as a digital marketing sponsor. They offer a subscription model for website design, SEO, and unlimited updates, contrasting with the traditional high upfront fees ($10K–$20K) and hourly charges of competitors.

## Quotable
> "If you build it right, where you've got those quarterly billings, those sticky recurring revenue, you've got that sales machine dialed in, and you can be a bolt on to one of these bigger platforms, tremendous competition."

> "Multiples have just been driven up like crazy among the private equity and institutional players."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Focus on Recurring Revenue:** Structure any service-based business to maximize the percentage of revenue derived from predictable, contract-based, quarterly, or annual maintenance services.
- [ ] **Systematize Growth:** Treat the business like a machine that can be scaled through repeatable processes (e.g., door-to-door sales teams, standardized service delivery) rather than relying on individual effort.
- [ ] **Understand Exit Multiples:** When building a business, always keep the exit multiple in mind. Focus on maximizing EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) and maintaining high margins, as this directly dictates the potential sale price.
- [ ] **Review Digital Marketing Spend:** If building a website or needing ongoing digital marketing, investigate subscription-based models (like Eversight) that offer predictable costs and unlimited updates, avoiding large upfront fees.
