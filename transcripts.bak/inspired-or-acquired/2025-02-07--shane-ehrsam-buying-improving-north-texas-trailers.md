# Shane Ehrsam - Buying & Improving North Texas Trailers — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Shane Ehrsam, owner of North Texas Trailers, details his transition from a high-level corporate management role to becoming a business owner through acquisition. He spent 11 years climbing the corporate ladder, managing a $125 million P&L and 750 employees, but ultimately found the lack of autonomy and control frustrating. This realization, coupled with a layoff in Fall 2021, prompted him to pursue the path of buying cash-flowing assets. His goal was not just financial stability, but achieving true ownership and work-life balance, a stark contrast to the "golden handcuffs" of corporate life.

His acquisition journey involved deep research, listening to podcasts, and joining resources like the Acquisition Lab. While initially targeting predictable B2B service models (like landscaping or pest control), he was open to unique niches. This openness led him to North Texas Trailers, a seemingly unexpected retail dealership. The discussion highlights the strategic importance of networking with brokers and being willing to consider industries outside of one's comfort zone.

## Key Points
- **The Corporate Exit Signal:** The primary driver for the pivot was the desire for autonomy and ownership, not just financial necessity. The "golden handcuffs" of corporate life are a major psychological hurdle for high-achieving professionals.
- **Strategic Financial Forecasting:** When evaluating the business, he avoided simply using the peak COVID-era numbers ($9.5M revenue in 2021). Instead, he used a "three-year blended average" with a heavier weight toward higher numbers, recognizing that the underlying cost structure (manufacturer inputs) had permanently increased.
- **Inflationary Cost Structure:** The cost of a trailer is reported to be 60-70% higher than pre-COVID levels, a structural change that increases the average revenue per unit across the industry.
- **Maximizing Ownership via Financing:** He initially believed his financing capacity was limited to $2.5 million (based on standard SBA 10% down/liquidity requirements). To acquire a $4 million business while maintaining 100% ownership, he structured a highly leveraged deal using a full standby seller note, a standard seller note, and a partial standby, resulting in a 95% leveraged deal.
- **Warning on ROBS:** He expressed skepticism regarding the Rollovers as Business Startups (ROBS) program, noting that the tax consequences and restrictions often make liquidating a 401k through a short-term loan (like the one provided by his father) a simpler, more advantageous alternative.

## Tools & Resources

- **Walker Diebel:** Mentioned as a key resource/figure in the acquisition space.
- **Bison Business:** M&A firm specializing in helping companies in the $1M–$20M revenue range get acquired.
- **Apple Tree Business:** Provides nationwide quality accounting and tax services for businesses.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop the "Autonomy Metric":** When evaluating a career or business, prioritize the degree of operational control and decision-making power over salary potential.
- [ ] **Network with Brokers:** Actively engage with local business brokers (not just for listings, but for advice on niche industries) and be prepared to consider unexpected sectors.
- [ ] **Master Blended Financial Analysis:** When analyzing a target business, do not rely solely on peak revenue years; calculate a weighted, multi-year average that accounts for permanent shifts in cost inputs (inflation, supply chain changes).
- [ ] **Study Advanced Deal Structuring:** Research alternative financing methods beyond standard SBA loans (e.g., standby seller notes, partial standby notes) to maximize leverage while preserving 100% equity ownership.
- [ ] **Consult Tax Experts on 401k Liquidation:** Before considering ROBS or liquidating retirement funds for a business purchase, consult a tax professional to compare the penalty/tax implications versus alternative short-term loan structures.
