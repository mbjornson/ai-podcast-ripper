# Brendan Dawson - What buyers might pay the most for Accident Plan? — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode features Brendan Dawson, founder of Accident Plan, a specialized risk management software platform designed for North American commercial trucking companies. The discussion centers on the high stakes and inherent legal risks associated with commercial auto crashes, which often result in multi-million dollar claims. Accident Plan addresses this by providing drivers with a step-by-step guide at the scene of an accident, coaching them on what to say, what not to say, and how to systematically gather all necessary evidence (insurance certificates, law enforcement reports, etc.). The data collected automatically feeds into a central network, allowing claims and risk departments to make real-time, educated decisions.

Dawson's credibility stems from his deep expertise in the trucking and claims investigation space, having spent 20 years in private investigations, often called in by plaintiff attorneys. He frames Accident Plan not just as software, but as a tool for "defensive trucking." From a business standpoint, he details his current financials ($126k annual revenue, 40% margins) and his strategic focus on penetrating the niche market of insurance captives—groups of companies that manage their own risk by forming private insurance pools.

## Key Points
- **Niche Market Focus (Insurance Captives):** Dawson's primary business development target is fleet operators who belong to insurance captives. These groups are highly motivated to manage risk internally, making them ideal clients for a risk mitigation product like Accident Plan.
- **The Value of Defensibility:** The core value proposition is creating "defensibility" for the carrier. In a litigious environment, the ability to prove due diligence and collect structured data immediately at the scene is worth far more than the cost of the software ($4.99 per driver/month).
- **High-Margin, Low-Overhead Model:** The company operates with strong margins (40%) while maintaining a minimal operational footprint, with Dawson being the only working member and utilizing contractors for development.
- **IP Strategy vs. Patents:** Dawson notes that while patents are expensive, the company's defensibility comes from its proprietary, integrated intellectual property (IP), including the mobile application, integration with major telematic systems (like Geotab), and its unique educational content (Skidmore Safely).
- **The Problem of Human Error:** The business solves a fundamental human failure point: in a high-stress accident scenario, the driver's "cognitive thought processes go out the window," making external, structured guidance essential.

## Tools & Resources
- **Accident Plan:** Risk management platform for commercial trucking, providing real-time guidance to drivers at the scene of an accident.
- **Geotab:** Existing telematic system that Accident Plan integrates with, demonstrating the importance of API integration in the industry.
- **Bison Business:** M&A firm specializing in helping individuals buy and sell profitable businesses in the $1M to $20M revenue range.
- **Apple Tree Business Services:** Provides nationwide quality accounting and tax services for businesses looking to operate or acquire.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Identify a High-Risk, Regulated Industry:** Instead of building a general SaaS product, focus on a specific, high-stakes industry (e.g., construction, specialized logistics, healthcare transport) where human error or regulatory failure leads to massive financial liability.
- [ ] **Develop a "Defensive" Digital Solution:** Build a low-cost, mandatory digital tool that guides the user through the critical, high-stress moments of an incident, ensuring standardized data collection and compliance.
- [ ] **Target Insurance Captives:** Research and approach groups of companies that pool their own risk (insurance captives) as they represent the most motivated and financially capable buyers for risk mitigation technology.
- [ ] **Focus on IP Integration:** Prioritize building proprietary integrations with existing, industry-standard hardware or software (like telematic systems) rather than spending heavily on patents.
- [ ] **Optimize for Minimal Overhead:** Structure the business model to maintain high margins (40%+) by keeping the core operational team small and utilizing contractors for development.
