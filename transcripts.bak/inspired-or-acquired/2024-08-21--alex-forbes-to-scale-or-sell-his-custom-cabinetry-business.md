# Alex Forbes - To Scale? Or Sell His Custom Cabinetry Business? — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Alex Forbes runs Lane 17 Cabinet Company, a custom cabinetry business that designs, engineers, and manufactures high-end custom storage solutions. With eight years of operation, Alex has built a company with a strong focus on quality customization, serving both local trade partners (designers, builders) and direct homeowners. The core discussion revolves around the classic small business dilemma: whether to sell the established entity or continue scaling. While the interviewer suggests that due to the current market climate and industry type, an immediate sale would yield low multiples ($500k-$700k), Alex argues that selling now would be "selling ourselves a bit short." He believes the company is only just getting started in the high-ticket custom cabinetry niche and requires significant growth before its true value can be realized.

The conversation highlights the critical importance of market positioning and strategic scaling. Instead of competing on volume, Alex has successfully pivoted to catering exclusively to the very high end of the market—full house packages—which provides insulation from general macroeconomic swings. His current strategy involves aggressively expanding his out-of-state presence by building relationships with specialized trade professionals in new markets like upstate New York and San Francisco, demonstrating a clear path toward reaching a multi-million dollar revenue plateau necessary to justify the existing equipment debt and operational overhead.

## Key Points
- **The Scaling Plateau:** The company requires reaching a $3M–$5M revenue plateau to fully extract the capacity value from its purchased equipment and overcome current debt levels, indicating that immediate valuation is artificially low due to size constraints.
- **High-End Niche Focus:** By focusing on full house packages for the very high end of the market, the business has achieved a degree of insulation from general macroeconomic downturns, allowing it to double its revenue in the last 24 months despite industry headwinds.
- **Trade Partner Leverage:** The primary driver of recent growth is not local demand but increased out-of-state presence and repeat business generated through specialized trade partners (designers/builders). This suggests a scalable B2B model over relying solely on direct consumer sales.
- **Valuation Gap:** There is a significant discrepancy between the suggested immediate sale value ($500k–$700k) and the company's actual earning power and future potential, which Alex correctly identifies as an "asset sale" rather than capturing true goodwill or growth value.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Niche Specialization:** Focus on moving upmarket to high-ticket, complex service packages (e.g., full house remodels) rather than competing on volume or basic commodity goods.
- [ ] **Systematize Processes:** Dedicate resources to standardizing internal manufacturing and operational processes to support rapid scaling beyond the founder's direct involvement.
- [ ] **Geographic Expansion via B2B:** Develop a systematic strategy for expanding out-of-state presence by establishing deep, repeatable partnerships with specialized trade professionals (designers/builders) in target markets.
- [ ] **Financial Modeling:** Model future revenue goals ($3M–$5M) to determine the necessary milestones required to justify current equipment debt and operational overhead.
