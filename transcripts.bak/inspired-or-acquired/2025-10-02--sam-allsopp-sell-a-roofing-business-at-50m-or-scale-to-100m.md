# Sam Allsopp - Sell A Roofing Business at $50M? Or Scale to $100M? — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode features Sam Allsopp, the owner of a rapidly scaling residential roofing and waterproofing company in South Florida. Sam's background is highly relevant to the discussion, having started in marketing and sales, previously running lead generation for home service companies before building his own successful niche business. His credibility is built on his ability to transition from a pure marketing/lead generation role to owning and scaling a complex physical service business, demonstrating a deep understanding of both the tech stack (CRM, sales process) and the operational side of the industry.

The core discussion revolves around business valuation and the strategic choice between selling a successful, high-growth company or continuing to scale it into a larger "platform" enterprise. Sam's company has achieved significant growth, with impressive trailing 12-month (TTM) financials. While the valuation suggests a potential sale price of $45M to $60M, Sam is hesitant to sell because he sees the potential for further exponential growth through strategic acquisitions (M&A).

Ultimately, Sam views his company not just as a profitable business to be sold, but as the foundational "platform" from which he intends to run multiple acquisitions and build a larger, diversified parent company. The discussion emphasizes that the true value lies in the scalable system and the ability to execute M&A, rather than just the current revenue stream.

## Key Points
- **Valuation Multiples:** Sam estimates a conservative sale valuation range of $45M to $60M, based on a 7x to 10x multiple applied to his $7.5M trailing 12-month EBITDA.
- **Financial Scale:** The company reported a TTM revenue of $43.5 million and an EBITDA of $7.5 million, demonstrating rapid, sustained growth in the home services sector.
- **The Platform Strategy:** Instead of selling, Sam plans to use his existing company as the "platform"—the model organization—to execute multiple acquisitions (M&A). This strategy involves buying other companies and running them under a parent company structure, allowing the brands to operate independently while benefiting from centralized services (like marketing and accounting).
- **Multiple Arbitrage:** Sam's stated strategy is to buy companies at a lower multiple (e.g., 5x to 9x) and then increase the overall value through operational improvements and scaling, effectively performing "multiple arbitrage."
- **Niche Advantage:** The business successfully leveraged the direct-to-consumer residential market, which is currently viewed as a hot, high-growth sector, attracting interest from private equity.
