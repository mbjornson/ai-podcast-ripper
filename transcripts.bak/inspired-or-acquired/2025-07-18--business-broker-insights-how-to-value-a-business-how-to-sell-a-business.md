# Business Broker Insights - How To Value A Business & How To Sell A Business — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode provides an educational deep dive into the mechanics of business valuation, focusing on what happens *before* a business hits a marketplace. The discussion, led by co-hosts with expertise in business brokerage and accounting, demystifies the process of determining a business's true worth and preparing it for sale. The core message is that selling a business is not a simple transaction; it requires rigorous qualification, financial cleanup, and strategic accounting adjustments.

The conversation heavily emphasizes the difference between a business's reported tax profit and its true earning potential. The experts explain that the valuation process involves "reverse engineering" the owner's tax strategy to calculate the Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE). This process allows brokers and buyers to see the maximum sustainable income the business could generate if it were run by a professional buyer, rather than being inflated by owner-specific tax deductions and benefits.

## Key Points
- **Qualification is the First Step:** Before valuation, a business must be qualified. The biggest disqualifier is usually the size or financial health; the brokers explicitly state they do not handle distressed or turnaround situations (i.e., businesses selling because they are losing money).
- **Minimum Viable Criteria:** For a deal to be taken seriously, the business typically needs a **three-year track record of established earnings of $250,000 or more** in owner cash flow.
- **Understanding SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings):** SDE is the adjusted measure of a business's profitability. It is calculated by taking the reported income and *adding back* owner benefits and non-essential expenses (e.g., personal vehicle depreciation, owner-paid health insurance, home office deductions, and excessive 401k contributions).
- **The Power of Tax Strategy:** Small business owners can legally use tax strategies to reduce taxable income, which often makes their reported financials look less impressive. However, when an accountant "recasts" the financials, they can prove the true, underlying earning stream by removing these owner-specific benefits to arrive at a more accurate SDE.
- **Financial Cleanliness is Paramount:** To maximize SDE and prove earning power to buyers and banks, the business must maintain clean, closed financials every single month. The books must match the tax returns, and all owner benefits must be clearly marked.

- [ ] **Consult a CPA on Tax Strategy:** Schedule a meeting with an accountant specializing in small business sales to identify all legal deductions and owner benefits that can be added back to your income to maximize your SDE.
- [ ] **Implement Monthly Bookkeeping Discipline:** Ensure that your accounting software (e.g., QuickBooks Online) is updated and reconciled *every month* to maintain clean, auditable records that match your tax filings.
