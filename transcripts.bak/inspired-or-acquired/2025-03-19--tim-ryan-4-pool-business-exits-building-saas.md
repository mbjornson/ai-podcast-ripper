# Tim Ryan - 4 Pool Business Exits & Building SaaS — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Tim Ryan details his journey from a blue-collar pool repair technician to a serial business owner and SaaS builder. His background is highly self-made, lacking traditional education, which underscores his operational expertise. He built his initial pool cleaning business from nothing, scaling it to 200 clients and generating $40k in monthly revenue by 2019. His story is a masterclass in leveraging cash flow and the M&A process.

The core of the episode revolves around his pattern of selling successful, cash-flowing service businesses (the "4 exits") to fund and fuel the development of a software company (SaaS). He repeatedly capitalized on the M&A market, securing owner-financed deals and eventually selling his fourth pool company to a rollup for a massive multiple. The discussion highlights the tension between the potential for exponential growth (keeping the business) versus the appeal of predictable, passive income (selling the business).

## Key Points
- **The Power of the Multiple:** When selling service businesses, the multiple (valuation multiple) is critical. Tim notes that while typical multiples are 8-10x revenue, he successfully sold his fourth pool company to a rollup for a **17x revenue multiple**.
- **The Passive Income Trap:** Tim's primary motivation for selling, despite the potential for growth, is the desire for predictable, passive income. He stated, "I would rather not do anything and make money than... be running to make money." This suggests that for some entrepreneurs, the value of freedom and reliable cash flow outweighs the potential for massive operational scale.
- **Operational Excellence through Ruthlessness:** He improved his operations by creating a highly structured, almost clinical hiring process. Instead of simply hiring, they "sat down and listed all the reasons we should fire somebody," effectively treating the hiring process as a system designed to find the best talent and cycle out poor performers quickly.
- **Scaling Speed:** He demonstrated rapid scaling, taking a pool company from 75 accounts to 300 accounts in approximately five months, generating $65k in revenue.
- **Owner Financing Strategy:** He successfully utilized owner-financing deals multiple times, allowing him to keep the cash flow running while building capital for the next venture.
