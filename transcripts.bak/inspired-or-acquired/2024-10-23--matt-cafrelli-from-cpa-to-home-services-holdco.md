# Matt Cafrelli - From CPA To Home Services HoldCo — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Matt Caffrelli transitioned from a career in public accounting (CPA, Big Four) to becoming an acquirer and operator of local home services businesses, including window cleaning, gutter cleaning, and landscaping. His pivot was driven by burnout from the demanding 80-hour weeks typical of tax work. He initially found inspiration through resources like Walker Deibel's book, which guided him into the Estimated Tax Accounting (ETA) space—the world of small business acquisition.

His current strategy involves acquiring subscale businesses in established service industries rather than targeting large, mature operations. This approach allows him to get his "feet wet" and build experience while minimizing initial capital outlay and investor dependency. The discussion highlights that while the transition is demanding (requiring more sweat equity than public accounting), it offers a level of autonomy and enjoyment far removed from corporate life.

## Key Points
- **Acquisition Strategy:** Focus on subscale businesses to minimize risk and entry barriers, rather than aiming for multi-million dollar EBITDA targets often advised by others. The goal is initial market penetration and learning the operational mechanics of service industries.
- **Valuation & Debt Management:** Acquisitions are averaging approximately 2.3 times SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings) on the purchase price. Crucially, he prioritizes avoiding excessive debt, opting for a mix of cash, small seller notes, and private debt to maintain full ownership and control, even if it slightly impacts potential returns.
- **Operational Imperative:** The transition from owner/operator to acquirer requires immediate attention to operational structure. Having an Operations Manager in place is critical for smooth transitions and scaling—a lesson learned the hard way after his second acquisition.
- **Market Arbitrage via Migration:** A significant opportunity exists by identifying areas experiencing massive domestic migration of well-off individuals (e.g., Texas Hill Country). These areas often have a lag in service providers, creating a "gold rush" opportunity for high-end service businesses that meet the standards of affluent newcomers.
- **The Value of Relationships:** In local service industries, personal relationships are paramount. Losing key customer relationships during an exit is a major risk factor, emphasizing the need to build trust and continuity immediately post-acquisition.

## Tools & Resources
- **Walker Deibel's Book:** Mentioned as a foundational resource for those interested in entering the Estimated Tax Accounting (ETA) space or small business acquisition.
- **Local Market Analysis:** The concept of tracking "migratory patterns" of affluent populations is a key strategic tool for identifying underserved service markets.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Operational Management Skills:** Prioritize learning how to structure and manage operations (e.g., hiring, process documentation) immediately upon acquiring any business, viewing an Operations Manager role as non-negotiable from day one.
- [ ] **Conduct Migration Gap Analysis:** Research local areas experiencing rapid influx of high net worth individuals (retirees or remote workers). Identify which essential services are currently underserved by professional providers in these specific geographic pockets.
- [ ] **Model Low-Debt Acquisition Structures:** Practice structuring deals using a mix of cash, seller notes, and private debt to maintain maximum equity ownership and control, even if the immediate return multiple is slightly lower than industry averages.
- [ ] **Network with Local Service Providers:** Identify local businesses in target markets (like high-growth suburban/rural areas) that are ripe for acquisition, focusing on those doing steady, recurring commercial work rather than purely residential services.
