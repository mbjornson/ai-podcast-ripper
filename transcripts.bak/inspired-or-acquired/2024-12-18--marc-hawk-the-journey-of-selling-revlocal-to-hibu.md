# Marc Hawk - The Journey of Selling RevLocal To Hibu — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode details the journey of RevLocal, a digital marketing agency specializing in local search optimization (Google My Business), and the strategic decision to merge with Hibu. The discussion centers not just on the massive growth—from a startup of 4-5 people to a 400-employee operation serving 7-10,000 clients across 40 states—but on the founder's mindset regarding business ownership and exit strategy. The guest, Marc Hawk, emphasizes that successful founders must always view their company through the lens of its eventual sale, treating it like a house that will eventually be resold.

The core narrative revolves around the disciplined process of maintaining control and recognizing market shifts. Hawk outlines a philosophy that success is defined not by the initial idea or "secret sauce," but by the quality of execution. The decision to merge was framed not as a pursuit of maximum financial value, but as a strategic move to be "good stewards" to the employees, investors, and most importantly, the customers, ensuring the best possible future value.

## Key Points
- **The "Open Mind" Exit Strategy:** From the beginning, the founders maintained the mindset that the business would someday be sold. This proactive approach is crucial for avoiding emotional attachment to the company, which can cloud judgment during major decisions.
- **The Three Reasons to Sell:** Hawk identifies three primary triggers for selling a business:
    1.  **Stupid Money:** When the financial opportunity presented is simply too large to refuse.
    2.  **The Coming Train:** Identifying a massive, inevitable market shift or opportunity that others have not yet seen.
    3.  **Exhaustion:** When the founder is simply tired or done with the operational demands of the business.
- **Control is Paramount:** The founders consistently maintained an 80% ownership stake, illustrating the principle that giving up control (selling too much equity) is the greatest risk, even if it means sacrificing immediate capital.
- **Execution Over Idea:** The most critical element of success in the competitive digital marketing space is not the idea or the "secret sauce," but the consistent, disciplined execution of the go-to-market strategy (GTM).
- **Unique GTM Strategy:** RevLocal's competitive advantage was its highly personal, in-person approach—knocking on doors to build relationships and educate business owners on their digital needs, contrasting with competitors who relied solely on PPC or inbound marketing.

## Tools & Resources
- **Bison Business:** A nationwide M&A firm specializing in helping businesses (particularly those in the $1M-$20M revenue range) prepare for sale and acquisition.
- **Apple Tree Business Services:** Provides nationwide accounting and tax services for businesses buying or running companies.

## Quotable
> "If you're building it to sell it, it's like buying a house. You got to keep in mind that you're going to someday sell that house."
>
> "The key is our execution. It's not the idea of what we're doing, it's not our secret sauce that's the key; it's our execution."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Adopt the "Exit Mindset":** When building any venture, constantly evaluate the business as if it were being sold today. This forces disciplined financial and operational clarity.
- [ ] **Prioritize Ownership Control:** Structure deals and investments to maintain maximum control (e.g., keeping at least 80% ownership) to ensure you are never forced into a decision you disagree with.
- [ ] **Audit Your GTM Strategy:** If you are in a service or sales business, evaluate if your current go-to-market strategy is differentiated enough. Focus on building personal relationships (the "door-knocking" model) rather than relying solely on paid ads or digital funnels.
- [ ] **Identify the "Train":** Dedicate time to observing macro-economic trends and industry shifts. The goal is to be the first to spot a massive, inevitable market shift that others are missing.
- [ ] **Consult M&A Experts:** If considering an exit, utilize specialized M&A firms (like Bison Business) for a complimentary evaluation to understand your company's true market value and readiness for sale.
