# Peter Lohmann - Could he be tempted to sell his property management business soon? — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode centers on an in-depth valuation discussion of Peter Lohmann's property management company in Columbus, Ohio. Peter, who started the business from scratch 11 years ago with his co-founder, manages approximately 650 units for about 300 external investors and has generated $2.6 million in revenue. The conversation quickly shifts from simple valuation to complex operational scaling challenges and personal opportunity cost.

The expert notes that property management companies are often valued using a *revenue multiple* rather than an earnings multiple, which is a critical distinction for business owners. While the market suggests a potential sale range of $3M to $5M, Peter reveals his primary hesitation isn't the cash amount—which he admits would be "life-changing"—but the operational intensity and risk associated with the business, leading him to question if selling would sacrifice too much potential growth in other ventures.

## Key Points
- **Valuation Multiple Focus:** Unlike most sectors valued on earnings (EBITDA), property management companies are often more accurately valued using a **revenue multiple**. This is a critical insight for any service business owner considering an exit.
- **Estimated Valuation Range:** Based on $2.6M in revenue, the estimated market value falls between **$3 million and $5 million**, depending on whether the buyer is an individual (SBA loan) or a large institutional platform player.
- **The Operational Plateau Trap:** Scaling service businesses is not linear. Peter described hitting plateaus (e.g., around 700 doors), where growth stalls because the cost of necessary management layers increases disproportionately, eating into profitability until a much larger scale (900–1000 units) can sustain them.
- **Opportunity Cost as the Primary Hurdle:** For Peter, the biggest factor in considering an exit is not the money itself, but the *opportunity cost*—the potential for aggressive growth and development in other projects or businesses if he didn't have this operationally intense business on his plate.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Assess Opportunity Cost:** When evaluating a current business, quantify the potential revenue/growth in 2–3 alternative ventures. Use this comparison to determine if the operational drag of your primary job outweighs its income benefit.
- [ ] **Model Scaling Costs (The Labor Trap):** Before committing to aggressive growth goals, model how much management overhead will be required at different unit/client thresholds (e.g., 500 vs. 750 clients). Ensure the projected revenue increase can sustainably cover the necessary managerial salary increases without sacrificing profitability.
- [ ] **Understand Valuation Multiples:** For service businesses, research whether your industry is primarily valued on *revenue* or *earnings*. Knowing this will help you structure financial reporting to maximize perceived value during an exit process.
- [ ] **Revisit Exit Planning Periodically:** Treat the decision to sell not as a one-time event, but as a recurring strategic review (e.g., every 3–6 months) to maintain rational focus and prevent emotional attachment from derailing growth.
