# Craig Hausz - Are Accounting Firms or Wealth Management Firms More Valuable? — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode features a deep dive into business valuation by discussing the comparative value structures of CPA accounting firms versus wealth management practices. The core thesis challenges the common assumption that higher revenue multiples automatically equate to greater value; instead, the discussion emphasizes that focusing on EBITDA is crucial for accurate market assessment. Craig Hausz, who runs a combined CPA and Wealth Management firm with 18 employees, details his growth trajectory, noting that while he has successfully scaled the business (from $2M to nearly $4M in revenue), the strategic focus needs to shift toward increasing the percentage of revenue derived from wealth management services.

The conversation quickly pivots from operational metrics to exit strategy and owner life cycle planning. The host estimates a potential market valuation for the firm in the neighborhood of $6 million, noting that due to low debt and industry liquidity, the owners could retain significant capital. This discussion serves as a powerful case study on how business owners must reconcile their current cash flow needs with the long-term financial goals associated with retirement or scaling down—a common tension point for successful entrepreneurs.

## Key Points
- **EBITDA vs. Revenue Multiples:** Do not rely solely on revenue multiples (e.g., 1.2x multiple for CPA vs. 2.5x–3x for WM). The true value comparison requires peeling back the EBITDA, as a wealth management firm may have a much higher EBITDA margin than its revenue suggests compared to a CPA firm.
- **Strategic Revenue Shift:** For firms combining accounting and financial services, the ideal long-term goal is increasing the percentage of revenue from the high-margin wealth management side (Craig aims for WM revenue to exceed CPA revenue).
- **The "Accordion Effect" of Growth:** Be wary of non-ideal or temporary revenue boosts (like those generated by ERC or PPP money). True sustainable growth requires pruning these transient sources to create a more stable, profitable operational core.
- **Valuation Timing and Owner Psychology:** The decision to sell is often less about the business's inherent value and more about the owner’s personal life cycle—specifically hitting a milestone (like age 50) that forces a re-evaluation of future income needs versus the headache of continued ownership.
