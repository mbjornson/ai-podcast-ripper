# Johannes Hock - Leaving Private Equity Career To 10x DFW Turf — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode features Johannes Haack, who successfully transitioned from a traditional Private Equity background to becoming an operational business owner through strategic acquisitions. Haunted by the typical "big payday" model of PE funds, Haack and his partner opted for a hands-on approach: acquiring small businesses and scaling them themselves. They initially acquired an artificial turf installation company in Dallas (2022), optimizing its processes from manual methods to a scalable blueprint before rapidly expanding into new markets like Austin, Atlanta, Nashville, and Utah.

Haack's journey demonstrates the power of operational improvement combined with aggressive, multi-location scaling. The discussion centers on his valuation—moving from an initial revenue base of over $5 million (2022) to a projected low $20 million in annual revenue today. This rapid growth, coupled with securing capital through both SBA loans and later, sophisticated family office financing, positions the company for significant future value realization.

The conversation concludes by framing the entire process as an exercise in M&A strategy, emphasizing that while Haack is generating life-changing wealth, he remains strategically far from the "exit button," indicating a continued focus on maximizing growth potential rather than immediate liquidation.

## Key Points
- **Operational Blueprinting:** The initial key move was not just buying the business, but spending the first year optimizing its processes—converting manual operations (whiteboard scheduling, pen and paper) into a scalable blueprint that allowed for rapid multi-city expansion.
- **Growth Trajectory:** The original business has tripled in value/scale within two years of acquisition. Revenue jumped from over $5 million to projected low $20 million on a T-12 basis.
- **Financing Strategy:** Haack utilized a mix of financing: starting with an SBA loan and investor capital, and later refinancing the entire structure by partnering with a family office in New York for "more firepower" toward subsequent acquisitions (e.g., Utah).
- **Valuation Estimate:** Based on low $20M revenue and healthy EBITDA margins (estimated 15-25%), the guest's enterprise value was estimated to be safely north of $20 million, potentially reaching $25–$30 million, depending heavily on the predictability of future growth.
- **Asymmetric Information:** The discussion highlighted a critical M&A concept: the inherent information gap between the buyer and seller. Buyers must discount heavily because they do not know how "real" or predictable the seller's stated growth opportunities are.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Operational Blueprints:** When acquiring any small or growing business, dedicate initial resources to documenting and systematizing every process (sales, scheduling, fulfillment) to ensure scalability beyond the founder's involvement.
- [ ] **Target SBA/Family Office Financing:** Research financing options that move beyond traditional bank loans; investigate how family offices can provide capital for rapid expansion after an initial acquisition.
- [ ] **Focus on Scalable Niches:** Identify service-based businesses (like turf installation) with high local demand but low operational sophistication, making them ripe for professional optimization and multi-location rollout.
- [ ] **Study M&A Valuation Metrics:** Deepen knowledge of EBITDA margins in specific industries to better assess the true enterprise value potential during due diligence.
