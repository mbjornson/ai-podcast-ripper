# Devin Wanzor - Lessons Buying and Selling 10 Businesses — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Devin Wanzor is a serial business buyer who transitioned from a career in commercial banking to becoming an operator and acquirer of small businesses. His background provides a clear narrative arc: moving from a highly regulated, committee-driven corporate environment (banking) to the high-action, decision-making world of entrepreneurship. His initial success involved taking a commercial signage business from $500k in revenue to over $10 million through a combination of acquisitions and organic growth over seven years, all while being a young operator.

The core lesson Wanzor emphasizes is that the most valuable outcome of these early ventures was not necessarily the immediate financial payout, but the rapid, intensive skill acquisition. He details how the demanding nature of running a business—handling books, sales, HR, and production—built a comprehensive skill set that proved more valuable than the initial capital structure of his deals. He then discusses his subsequent acquisition of a liquor store during the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic, highlighting the necessity of strategic purchasing, rigorous demand forecasting, and understanding local regulatory nuances to succeed in a volatile market.

## Key Points
- **The Value of Operational Experience:** Wanzor stresses that the true education came from the "heavy lift" of being an operator—doing all the books, sales, HR, and production—even when a full leadership team was eventually hired. This hands-on experience is the most valuable skill set for future growth and acquisitions.
- **Growth Through Acquisition and Organic Effort:** His first business grew from $500k to over $10 million, demonstrating that sustained growth requires both strategic acquisitions and dedicated organic effort.
- **The Danger of Misaligned Incentives:** He learned that even highly profitable businesses can be hampered by internal conflict, specifically when the lines blur between running a profitable commercial enterprise and running a "quasi charity," which can lead to operational stagnation.
- **Market Timing and Risk Assessment:** Buying a business during a downturn (like early COVID) requires extreme caution. The focus must be on assessing how much "upside is already baked in" and developing robust forecasting models for demand and inventory purchasing.
- **Industry-Specific Operational Mastery:** Success in niche businesses (like liquor stores) is not generic. It requires mastering local regulations (e.g., Oklahoma's specific discount structure) and developing sophisticated forecasting models for inventory purchasing.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Intentionally seek out roles or projects that force you to handle the full cycle of business operations: bookkeeping, sales, HR, and production/fulfillment.
- [ ] **Business Modeling:** When evaluating a potential investment or career pivot, focus less on the initial financial structure and more on the *transferable operational skills* you will acquire.
- [ ] **Market Due Diligence:** When considering a purchase or investment, develop a rigorous forecasting model that accounts for potential market downturns and assesses what "upside is already baked in" versus what needs to be built.
- [ ] **Networking:** Connect with M&A brokers and business service providers (like Bison Business or Apple Tree) to understand the mechanics of buying and selling established small businesses.
