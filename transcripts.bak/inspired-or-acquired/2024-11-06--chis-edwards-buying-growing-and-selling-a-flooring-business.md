# Chis Edwards - Buying, Growing, and Selling A Flooring Business — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Chris Edwards detailed his journey of acquiring, growing, and successfully exiting a small business—Affordable Flooring Warehouse—in Steamboat, Colorado. After quitting his corporate finance job in October 2020, he entered the "entrepreneur through acquisition" world, self-funding the purchase of the flooring company in February 2021. The initial years were challenging, marked by employee turnover and operational turmoil, but Chris successfully transitioned the business from a "prototypical boomer owned" state—characterized by paper-based processes and a broken culture—into a modern, systems-driven operation.

Over the subsequent three and a half years, he significantly scaled the business, successfully doubling EBITDA and growing revenue substantially. The sale process itself was highly efficient, moving quickly from Letter of Intent (LOI) to closing in October. His decision to exit was strategic: while he loved the lifestyle and the location, he realized the market ceiling in Steamboat was too small to support continued massive growth, making monetization and capital deployment into a new venture the logical next step.

The episode provides a detailed blueprint of the M&A lifecycle, from initial self-funded search and due diligence to the final sale. It highlights the importance of operational improvements (systems, culture, technology) as the primary driver of value, demonstrating that a business's internal health is often more critical to a successful exit than its physical location.

## Key Points
- **Value Creation is Operational:** Chris’s primary value add was not just management, but installing systems, implementing new technology stacks, and fixing the underlying culture of the "prototypical boomer owned business."
- **The SBA Financing Structure:** The deal was financed using a common small business structure: 10% equity (self-funded), 10% seller note, and 80% SBA loan.
- **The Market Ceiling Constraint:** A key non-obvious take is recognizing when a geographical market limits growth. Chris realized that even with good management, Steamboat was too small a market to double the business further, necessitating an exit to monetize the built value.
- **The J-Curve of Entrepreneurship:** He accurately described the business journey as following a J-curve, acknowledging that the initial years were marked by struggle, turmoil, and moments of doubt, but persistent effort led to strong, sustained momentum and results.
- **Buyer Qualification is Critical:** When selling in a desirable location like Steamboat, the buyer must be willing to move and live the "mountain life," filtering out many casual "tire kickers" who only like the idea of the location.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Operational Systems:** Focus on documenting and installing repeatable processes (SOPs) in your current business to move it away from reliance on single individuals or paper-based methods.
- [ ] **Analyze Market Saturation:** Conduct a "market ceiling" assessment for your industry/location. Determine if your current geographical market limits your potential for exponential growth.
- [ ] **Build a Financial Model for Exit:** Understand the typical M&A financing structure (e.g., 10% equity, 10% seller note, 80% debt) to prepare for a potential sale.
- [ ] **Network with M&A Specialists:** Connect with business brokers (like Bison Business) early in your growth phase to understand the valuation process and prepare for a potential exit.
- [ ] **Prioritize Lifestyle/Location Fit:** When considering a business acquisition, evaluate not just the financials, but the lifestyle and location fit for both yourself and the future buyer.
