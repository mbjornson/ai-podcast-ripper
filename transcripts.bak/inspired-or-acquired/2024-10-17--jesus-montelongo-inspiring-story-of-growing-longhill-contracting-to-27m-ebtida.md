# Jesus Montelongo - Inspiring Story Of Growing Longhill Contracting to +$2.7M EBTIDA — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode features Jesus Montelongo, founder of Long Hill Contracting, a Denver-based company that started as a specialized drywall service and has successfully scaled into a diversified general contracting sub-contractor. The discussion centers on his rapid growth trajectory—from initial revenue in 2017 to tracking toward record revenues of $22-$24 million—and the financial structure required to sustain this scale. Montelongo’s journey is framed by the need for consistent cash flow, prompting him to leave a stable career in retail banking and build his own company.

The conversation with business broker Clint Fiore focuses heavily on the mechanics of scaling and preparing for an exit. While sub-contracting businesses are notoriously difficult to sell due to their reliance on specific local markets (new construction), Montelongo has taken steps to professionalize the operation, hiring a controller three years ago and building out internal supervision teams. This shift from founder-dependent word-of-mouth growth to structured operations is highlighted as his biggest asset for potential acquisition.

## Key Points
- **The Cash Flow Imperative:** The initial motivation for starting Long Hill was not passion or industry expertise, but the need for reliable, immediate cash flow, contrasting with the delayed paychecks common in large projects like house flipping. This highlights that financial necessity can be a powerful business catalyst.
- **Financial Scaling Metrics:** The company operates with an estimated EBITDA margin of 12%. On years exceeding $20 million in revenue, this translates to over $2.4 million in adjusted earnings (EBITDA), placing the company in a strong financial position for acquisition.
- **The Value of Professionalization:** Montelongo’s ability to transition from an owner-operator model to one with 25 W2 employees (including dedicated accounting and supervision teams) is critical. The broker notes that this professional structure makes the business "transferable" and significantly increases its value compared to a founder-dependent operation.
- **Exit Strategy Leverage:** While a quick valuation estimate was placed around $10 million, the most significant insight is the potential for a much higher deal by structuring the sale with an **earn-out**. By tying future payments to continued performance (putting "skin in the game"), the seller can appeal to more generous buyers.

## Tools & Resources
- **Bison Business:** A nationwide M&A firm specializing in helping businesses (particularly those in the $1M–$20M revenue range) prepare for sale and connect sellers with qualified buyers.
- **Appletree Business Services:** Provides quality accounting and tax services nationwide, useful for both running a business and due diligence during an acquisition process.

## Quotable
> "I started this company for cash flow... I just couldn't really live without a paycheck." (Highlights the fundamental motivation behind building wealth/security.)
>
> "If you're willing to put a little bit of skin in the game on the forward looking performance of the company, you could potentially do better [on valuation]." (A key piece of advice regarding maximizing exit value.)

## Action Items
- [ ] **Professionalize Accounting:** Hire a dedicated controller or outsourced accounting team early in growth. Transitioning from basic bookkeeping to structured financial reporting is essential for scaling and demonstrating transferability.
- [ ] **Structure for Exit Value:** When considering selling or raising capital, proactively structure deals with performance-based earn-outs rather than relying solely on historical EBITDA multiples.
- [ ] **Diversify Service Offerings (The "Halo Effect"):** As a specialized sub-contractor, expand services beyond the core competency (drywall) into adjacent areas (painting, fencing, framing). This increases client stickiness and revenue resilience when one area slows down.
- [ ] **Follow M&A Experts:** Monitor firms like Bison Business for insights on preparing small to mid-sized businesses for sale, focusing specifically on operational documentation and financial cleanup required during due diligence.
