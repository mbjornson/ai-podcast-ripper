# Brett McManus - Building $1M Equity In 1 Year With Apres Inn — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Brett McManus, owner of a boutique motel in Killington, Vermont, details the process of acquiring and renovating a rundown roadside motel into a high-end, boutique operation. The episode serves as a deep dive into the operational realities of the hospitality industry, moving far beyond the myth of "passive income." McManus emphasizes that running a physical business requires intense, hands-on labor, constant problem-solving, and adaptability—a stark contrast to the idealized version often presented online.

The core of the discussion revolves around the financial metrics and the steep learning curve associated with asset acquisition. McManus purchased the property for $1.4 million, achieving a strong initial 16% cap rate. After extensive, unexpected renovations (which cost over $400k), the property is now projected to stabilize at a much higher NOI, significantly increasing its valuation and demonstrating the power of operational improvements and market positioning.

## Key Points
- **Valuation Uplift through Operations:** The property's valuation increased dramatically due to operational improvements. It was purchased with an initial NOI of $272k (implying a 16% cap rate). With projected stabilized NOI of $300k–$350k, the potential valuation increases to $2.7M or more (assuming a 10% cap rate).
- **The Hidden Cost of Renovation:** McManus warns that renovation budgets are highly susceptible to unexpected costs. While they budgeted $300k, the actual cost exceeded $400k, illustrating that physical assets require significant contingency planning and financial flexibility.
- **The Importance of Pro Forma Forecasting:** Successful long-term planning requires a 3-4 year pro forma. While the first year is crucial for building reputation and cash flow, the true financial goal is stabilizing at a target NOI (estimated at $350k) over several years.
- **Dynamic Pricing is Critical:** A major learning curve was realizing that initial, one-off bookings (like those made months in advance) can skew revenue. To maximize profit, owners must constantly adjust pricing to be more competitive with the market, especially during peak periods.
- **Market Differentiation:** The motel transitioned from a basic, subsidized "shelter housing" program (which provided steady, but non-market-rate, income) to a premium, boutique experience, allowing them to capture higher Average Daily Rates (ADR).

## Tools & Resources
- **SBA Loans:** Used for initial purchase and renovation funding.
- **Social Media Marketing:** Documenting the renovation "mess" and journey online (Instagram) was a key marketing asset that generated traction and built brand awareness.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a 3-4 Year Pro Forma:** Do not rely on single-year projections. Model revenue and expenses over multiple years to determine true stabilization goals and long-term asset value.
- [ ] **Stress-Test Renovation Budgets:** Always allocate a minimum 30-40% contingency fund for unexpected structural issues (rewiring, plumbing, etc.) when acquiring older properties.
- [ ] **Implement Dynamic Pricing Strategy:** Track booking patterns to identify when and where the property is underpriced. Use data to adjust rates to maximize revenue during peak demand periods.
- [ ] **Build a Content Marketing Funnel:** Document the operational "mess" and challenges of running the business (e.g., unexpected repairs, OSHA inspections) and use this content to build brand authenticity and market visibility.
- [ ] **Focus on Cap Rate Improvement:** Understand that increasing NOI through operational efficiency and revenue growth is the most direct path to increasing property valuation, even more so than simply increasing the initial purchase price.
