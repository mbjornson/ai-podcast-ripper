# David Ensley - Building vs Buying Orthodontic Practices — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
The episode features Dr. David Ensley, an orthodontist, discussing the complex business of building and operating a dental practice within the structure of a Dental Service Organization (DSO). Ensley's background is highly credible, having served as an Army orthodontist, which gave him experience treating complex surgical cases and, importantly, allowed him to start his civilian practice without student loan debt. He emphasizes the critical difference between a doctor-owned DSO (like his) and those owned by Private Equity (PE), arguing that maintaining doctor ownership is key to organic, sustainable growth.

The core discussion revolves around the financial mechanics of modern dental practices. Ensley details the financial advantages of the industry, noting that banks often finance 100% of the start-up costs, minimizing personal capital risk. More critically, he highlights the necessity of adopting accrual accounting over cash basis. This shift is presented not just as an accounting detail, but as a strategic business move required to accurately represent the company's true, long-term earning power, preventing the need to sell the business to PE firms simply to "clean up" the books.

## Key Points
- **100% Financing Potential:** Dental practices are unique in that banks often loan 100% of the necessary capital to start a build-out, eliminating the need for significant personal down payments (unlike many other industries).
- **The DSO Structure:** A DSO allows a doctor to own their specific practice while paying management fees to a larger, centralized entity. Ensley's DSO is noted as being completely *doctor-owned*, a significant differentiator from PE-backed chains.
- **The Accrual vs. Cash Accounting Trap:** This is the most critical insight. Orthodontics involves monthly payments spread over years. Using **cash basis** accounting makes revenue appear artificially low in any given year. Switching to **accrual basis** provides a true, accurate picture of the company's full earning power and revenue stream, which is vital for valuation and growth.
- **Rapid Growth Curve:** Ensley detailed a strong growth trajectory: 6 months to break even, 18 months to feel comfortable, and achieving $1.5 million in revenue and $740,000 in net profit in the third full year of operation.
- **Valuation Indicator:** Based on the rapid growth, accrual basis revenue, and high net profit margin, the host estimated the business value range at $2 million to $3.5 million.

## Tools & Resources
- **DSO (Dental Service Organization):** Understanding the structure and operational model of DSOs is key for any professional looking to scale a service-based practice.
- **Accrual Accounting:** A fundamental financial concept that must be mastered by business owners to accurately assess their company's true financial health and valuation potential.
