# Mike Botkin - Massive Landscaping Rollup & Exit Within 2 years — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Mike Botkin details his journey from a COO role in real estate private equity to becoming an aggressive small business acquisition specialist, successfully executing a massive "roll-up" of landscaping companies over 23 months. Starting with a modest $780,000 revenue local operation, he acquired six additional businesses across Orlando and Atlanta, culminating in a sale that placed the combined entity among the top 100 in the national industry. His success was not simply due to volume but stemmed from systematically improving profitability and operational efficiency through strategic changes in customer focus and labor management.

Botkin's core thesis is that scaling SMB acquisitions requires more than just capital; it demands a disciplined, data-driven approach to both clientele and internal processes. He highlights the shift from servicing "anyone and everyone" to hyper-focusing on an ideal commercial client profile. Furthermore, he demonstrates how simple logistical changes—like eliminating unnecessary travel time for crews—can dramatically improve direct labor efficiency and EBITDA margins, proving that operational excellence is key to massive scale in service industries.

## Key Points
- **The Power of Hyper-Niche Customer Profiling:** Botkin shifted the business from servicing any residential client to being 100% commercial. He developed a sophisticated profile using 22 data points (e.g., top line revenue, gross margin, number of touchpoints). The strategy was to ruthlessly "kick out" the bottom 10% of customers every quarter, generating significant profitability and allowing them to turn down lucrative contracts that didn't fit their desired client profile.
- **Operational Efficiency through Logistics:** He drastically improved direct labor costs by eliminating property traffic time. Instead of having crews report to a central office or dealing with long commutes, he implemented connex boxes/storage units at the job sites, making crews report directly there. This simple logistical change saved hours of wasted travel and non-value-add time for multiple crew members daily.
- **Strategic Acquisition Criteria:** Initially, Botkin bought businesses from owners who were neglecting them. He later refined his criteria to only acquire companies where the owner was committed to staying involved in the business. He argues that "businesses are mirrors of people," meaning leadership stability and cultural continuity (the small things like remembering birthdays) are critical for long-term success and preventing culture decay post-acquisition.
- **Exponential Growth Model:** The transition from $780,000 revenue to a top 100 national player involved acquiring six additional businesses in under two years, demonstrating an exponential growth curve fueled by systematic operational improvements rather than just brute force sales.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Define Your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP):** Audit your current client base and develop a scoring system using 5-10 key performance indicators (KPIs) beyond just revenue (e.g., gross margin contribution, frequency of touchpoints, lifetime value). Commit to pruning the bottom X% of clients quarterly.
- [ ] **Audit Operational Logistics:** Map out the non-value-add time in your core operations (travel, waiting, internal movement). Implement a system change—even if it requires physical infrastructure like storage units or dedicated staging areas—to eliminate unnecessary commutes and delays for field staff.
- [ ] **Refine Acquisition Strategy:** If considering buying a business, prioritize the owner's commitment to staying involved over the size of the assets. Structure deals that integrate the existing leadership team to protect institutional knowledge and culture.
- [ ] **Focus on EBITDA Margin Improvement:** When scaling service businesses, focus less on top-line revenue growth from every source, and more on optimizing labor costs by increasing density and efficiency at the job site level.
