# Nathan Lindley - HVAC Struggles To Success — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Nathan Lindley, owner of Dunray, operates a residential HVAC roll-up business specializing in acquiring small, local "mom-and-pop" HVAC companies across Dallas and Austin. His core strategy is to professionalize these small businesses—standardizing operations, implementing sales training, and consolidating branding—to create a larger, scalable platform. The company has executed nine acquisitions, with a recent focus on rapidly scaling through multiple deals.

Lindley's background is characterized by a significant pivot: after 15 years running a Christian book publishing company with his father, he experienced burnout and realized the inherent vulnerability of discretionary revenue streams. This led him to the immediate, tangible cash flow potential of acquiring established, essential service businesses, which he views as a superior model for wealth generation compared to publishing.

The episode details his current financial trajectory, projecting over $8 million in revenue and an EBITDA of $2 million for the year. He outlines a sophisticated "level up" strategy: buying assets at lower, Main Street multiples, consolidating them, and growing them into a middle-market platform suitable for eventual sale to private equity or institutional buyers.

## Key Points
- **The Roll-Up Strategy:** The core business model is acquiring small, localized service businesses (HVAC) and professionalizing them through standardization and centralized sales training. This allows for rapid scale and de-risking of individual assets.
- **Targeting Underserved Deals:** Lindley focuses on deals that institutional buyers and private equity often overlook, buying at lower multiples and building value internally until the platform reaches a size attractive to larger capital sources.
- **EBITDA Milestones and Multiples:** The valuation of a platform deal is highly dependent on achieving specific EBITDA milestones. He notes that multiples increase significantly at $1M, $2M, and $5M EBITDA, suggesting that hitting these benchmarks is critical for maximizing exit value.
- **Deal Size and Valuation:** The company has executed deals ranging from under $1.5 million to $3 million in revenue. He estimates the current $10 million valuation is based on the potential of the consolidated platform, not just the sum of the parts.
- **The Importance of Cash Flow:** His pivot from publishing was driven by the realization that books are discretionary purchases, while essential services (like HVAC) provide necessary, non-discretionary cash flow, which is the foundation of his current success.
