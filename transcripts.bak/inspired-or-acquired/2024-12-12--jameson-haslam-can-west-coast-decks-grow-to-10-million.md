# Jameson Haslam -  Can West Coast Decks grow to $10 million? — Inspired Or Acquired

## Summary
Jameson Haslam, founder of West Coast Deck, provides a fascinating case study of pivoting from elite finance to a specialized construction trade. His background is highly credentialed, spanning Stanford, Morgan Stanley, Google, and Bain Capital, giving him a deep understanding of high-level corporate valuation and strategy. After a failed venture in the Airbnb arbitrage space, he transitioned to decking, a niche he now runs in San Diego. The discussion centers on the strategic viability of the decking industry, arguing that it occupies a "sweet spot" in the market that allows for significant revenue growth with manageable operational complexity.

Haslam details West Coast Deck's rapid growth trajectory, moving from $600k in Year 1 to projecting $1.2M–$1.4M in Year 3, achieving a CAGR of roughly 35%. He frames the business not just as a construction service, but as a highly optimized market capture play, capitalizing on the massive post-pandemic shift in consumer discretionary spending toward outdoor living and home improvements.

## Key Points
- **The AOV/Complexity Sweet Spot:** The ideal trade business model, according to Haslam, maximizes the ratio between Average Order Value (AOV) and client decision-making complexity. Decking achieves this balance: AOV is high ($30k–$40k), but the client decision points are limited to 2-3 selections (shape, decking type, railing).
- **Natural Barrier to Entry (Execution Risk):** The complexity of execution (e.g., building a second-story balcony) is high enough that it naturally scares away low-skill, "handyman" contractors. This inherent execution risk acts as a protective barrier, limiting competition and maintaining pricing power.
- **Market Tailwind: Outdoor Living:** The business capitalized on the post-COVID shift in consumer spending, noting that dollars previously allocated to travel were redirected into home renovations and outdoor living spaces (pools, hardscapes, patios).
- **Financial Benchmarking:** The company is tracking toward the revenue levels of successful regional competitors: LA Decks ($7M top line) and the Cascade Fence and Deck (Portland, $12M–$13M top line).
- **The Value of Scale:** He cautions against the "glamour" of small business revenue, noting that earning $1M as an owner is "slim pickings," and that reaching $3M is a critical threshold for taking on more help and scaling operations.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a "Sweet Spot" Business Model:** Analyze your current industry to identify a service that combines a high Average Order Value (AOV) with low client decision-making complexity.
- [ ] **Assess Execution Risk:** Identify a natural barrier to entry in your trade or service that requires specialized skill, making it difficult for general contractors or low-skill labor to replicate.
- [ ] **Monitor Discretionary Spending:** Track macro trends in consumer spending (e.g., post-pandemic shifts) to identify sectors experiencing accelerated demand (e.g., outdoor living, home improvement).
- [ ] **Focus on the $3M Revenue Milestone:** When scaling a service business, set a clear financial goal ($3M+) that represents the point where the business can transition from owner-operator to managing a larger team.
