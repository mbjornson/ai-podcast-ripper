# The Problem with Modern Dating | David O’Connor on Nietzsche, Plato, Christ — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
Professor David O’Connor diagnoses a crisis in modern dating rooted not merely in market dynamics, but in a profound loss of "erotic agency" among young men and women. The discussion frames contemporary issues—such as the rise of incel culture and the normalization of platforms like OnlyFans—as symptoms of deeper anxieties concerning identity, self-worth, and connection within an elite, anxious culture. O’Connor argues that much of the current discourse is driven by intellectualizing victimhood, where suffering becomes a "badge of honor."

The professor utilizes classical philosophical traditions (Plato, Catholic/Platonic resources) to counter modern dating anxieties. He posits that young men are overly fearful about expressing their attraction, having lost confidence in their inherent power to attract and form meaningful couples. The core message is that true connection requires moving beyond the superficial search for validation and embracing a confident sense of self-completeness—a mindset shift necessary for both personal success and romantic fulfillment.

## Key Points
- **Erotic Agency vs. Friendship:** Do not treat friendship and erotic attraction as continuous or interchangeable. Confusing these two diminishes one's power to pursue genuine connection; you must be bold enough to let a woman see your interest in her as an attractive person, not just a friend.
- **The Couple Mindset:** The most powerful energy comes from viewing oneself (and potential partners) as *couples* rather than individuals engaged in a search. This shift is key to maximizing erotic potency and confidence.
- **Incel Culture's Appeal:** The movement appeals because it allows highly intelligent young men to channel their frustration into an intellectual, systemic analysis of failure. They take pride in victimhood by framing dating woes as a political or conspiratorial issue (e.g., "The Chads" vs. the rest).
- **Intellectualization of Suffering:** The attraction to extremist views (whether incel discourse or reading Ayn Rand/Nietzsche) is not just about sex; it's about achieving an identity within an intellectual avant-garde. This sense of belonging provides a powerful, albeit corrupting, form of self-worth.
- **The Anxiety Layer:** Current dating anxiety is often layered on top of broader socio-economic anxieties (like the financial downturn post-2008), which create a generalized fear about the future that permeates personal relationships and confidence.

## Tools & Resources
- **Philosophical Frameworks:** Classical resources from Platonic and Catholic traditions are suggested as counterpoints to modern dating anxiety.
- **Texts/Concepts:** *Notes from Underground* (Dostoevsky) is cited as an example of self-inflicted suffering taken with pride, mirroring the incel mindset.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Erotic Agency:** Practice being overtly bold in expressing interest to women, resisting the urge to default to "friend zone" behavior. Treat attraction as an immediate, powerful force, not something built slowly through platonic stages.
- [ ] **Shift Focus from Individual Search to Partnership:** When thinking about goals (romantic or professional), frame them around *being* a unit or a couple, rather than being in a constant state of individual acquisition or searching for validation.
- [ ] **Deconstruct Victimhood Narratives:** When confronted with failure (in dating, business, or career), resist the urge to intellectualize it into a political conspiracy or systemic problem. Focus instead on internal agency and self-correction.
- [ ] **Study Classical Philosophy:** Explore Platonic and Catholic philosophical traditions to gain grounding in ideas of human nature and relationship structure that contrast with modern anxiety culture.
