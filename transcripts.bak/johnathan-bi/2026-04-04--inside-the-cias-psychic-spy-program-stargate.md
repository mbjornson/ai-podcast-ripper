# Inside the CIA’s Psychic Spy Program | Stargate — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
This podcast episode, hosted by Jonathan Bi, explores the controversial history of the CIA’s “Stargate” program, a psychic spy initiative that utilized remote viewing. The core argument is that despite the program’s termination in 1995 and a reported $20 million investment, compelling evidence suggests it was genuinely effective, particularly in intelligence gathering. The episode’s credibility rests on the presentation of original CIA documents and the interview with the founder of the “Archives of the Impossible,” a repository of these materials. The discussion moves beyond simple claims of psychic ability, delving into philosophical implications – ontology, metaphysics – and the potential for a deeper understanding of reality. The host emphasizes a critical approach, advocating for “imminent critique” – understanding the assumptions of opposing viewpoints – rather than simply dismissing claims based on worldview.

## Key Points
- **The Stargate Program:** The CIA’s Stargate program (1977-1995) involved training and employing individuals to remotely view targets – locations, people, or events – using a standardized worksheet and sketching process.
- **Original Documents & Hits:** The episode highlights the use of actual CIA documents and “hits” – sketches produced by remote viewers that accurately depicted targets – as evidence of the program’s effectiveness. Examples include matching sketches of the Stanford Shopping Center and a nuclear material test site with their respective targets.
- **Temporal Anomalies:** Remote viewers sometimes reported seeing targets at different times – past or future – a phenomenon that challenged conventional understanding of time and space.
- **Cold War Context:** The program’s funding and operation were driven by the Cold War’s need for intelligence advantage, with the U.S. and Soviet Union both investing in this technology.
- **Skepticism & Pragmatism:** The host acknowledges the skepticism surrounding remote viewing but argues that the CIA’s continued funding for 20 years, despite a 1995 review suggesting failure, demonstrates a pragmatic commitment to exploring potentially valuable intelligence sources.
- **Imminent Critique:** The host advocates for a critical approach to evaluating claims, urging participants to understand and engage with the assumptions of opposing viewpoints, rather than simply dismissing them based on personal beliefs.
- **Religious & Philosophical Implications:** The episode explores the philosophical implications of remote viewing, linking it to questions of ontology and metaphysics, and examining the historical use of psychic abilities in various cultures and traditions.

## Tools & Resources

- **Ayahuasca:** Mentioned as a historical example of a substance used for psychic or spiritual exploration, often with potentially negative consequences.
