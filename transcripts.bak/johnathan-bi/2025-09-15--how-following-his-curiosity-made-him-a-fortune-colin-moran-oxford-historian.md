# How Following His Curiosity Made Him a Fortune | Colin Moran, Oxford Historian — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
The episode features Colin Moran, a successful hedge fund manager whose investment philosophy defies traditional financial metrics. Moran's background is highly academic, with degrees from Oxford and Duke, and his fund's team is composed of humanities scholars (Classics, Theology, Music) rather than typical finance professionals. The core thesis is that the most profitable investments are often found by following genuine intellectual curiosity—what Moran calls "following the fun"—rather than by strictly adhering to financial models.

Moran argues that companies should not be viewed merely as financial abstractions, but rather as "human organisms" or "human ecologies." This shift in perspective requires analyzing the company's culture, the underlying human dynamics, and the quality of its leadership. The discussion highlights the paradox that pursuing an interest purely for its own sake can lead to massive, unexpected financial success, as demonstrated by his investment in Shopify.

## Key Points
- **The "Fun" Investment Thesis:** The best money-making opportunities arise from following intellectual curiosity. The process is: follow the thing you want to learn about $\rightarrow$ good money-making opportunities will happen.
- **Company as Human Ecology:** Instead of analyzing balance sheets, analyze the company's culture and human dynamics. The key is to identify *who* the people are and *where* the value is being created, which is often not obvious from the organizational chart.
- **The Shopify Case Study:** Moran was initially attracted to Shopify not for its immediate financial metrics (it was highly valued at a much higher multiple than he was trained to accept), but because of its unusual, dynamic, and compelling culture, which he found "fun to study."
- **The Warshaw Method (Slow Probing):** To decode a company or culture, do not try to get all the answers at once. Instead, slowly "probe the subject" by talking to people who work there and people who use the product. The story will eventually cohere and pixelate.
- **Leadership Vices as Strengths:** Drawing from St. Augustine, Moran notes that sometimes the "distasteful behavior" or vices in a leader—behavior that is not illegal or explicitly improper—can actually be the source of their strength and make them exceptional investment targets.

- [ ] **Practice "Fun-First" Research:** When starting a new project or investment, identify the aspect you are genuinely curious about (the "fun" part) and let that curiosity guide your initial research, rather than starting with a financial goal.
- [ ] **Implement the "Slow Probe" Method:** When trying to understand a complex system (a company, a relationship, a market), do not seek immediate answers. Instead, conduct deep, qualitative interviews with diverse stakeholders (users, employees, etc.) to allow the story to cohere naturally.
