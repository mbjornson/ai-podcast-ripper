# Rebel Oxford Philosopher Declares War on Universities | Michael Gibson — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
Michael Gibson, a philosopher with deep academic credentials (NYU, UChicago, Oxford), argues that modern universities are actively detrimental to innovation and creativity. He frames the entire academic-industrial complex as a "paper belt"—a centralized bureaucracy whose power relies on printing paper (diplomas, laws, money) rather than generating genuine value. Gibson contends that the system is not merely outdated, but actively stifling unique thought and originality.

His core thesis is that the intense, standardized competition for spots in elite schools leads to intellectual homogenization, a concept he links to René Girard's philosophy of imitation. Instead of fostering unique genius, the system forces students into a single, predictable "monomaniacal path to success." He advocates for alternative, non-academic paths for developing talent, citing the success of early-stage, self-funded projects like Ethereum and Figma, which emerged from individuals outside the traditional university structure.

## Key Points
- **The "Paper Belt" Thesis:** Gibson compares the modern university system to the corrupt Catholic Church selling indulgences. The diploma is the modern "indulgence"—a piece of paper that gives people the false sense of security and status, regardless of actual skill or knowledge.
- **The Girardian Principle of Imitation:** The intense societal pressure to attend and graduate from elite universities causes people to become increasingly alike. This homogenization reduces the pool of unique, original thought necessary for true societal innovation.
- **Stagnation in Output:** Gibson argues that the evidence of societal stagnation is visible not just in tech, but in the arts, novels, and philosophy. He claims that the current output lacks the originality and depth of previous eras, suggesting the source of the problem is the educational system.
- **The Value Shift (Selection vs. Cultivation):** He posits that the primary function of elite education may no longer be *imparting knowledge* (cultivation), but rather *selecting people* (identifying those with inherent conscientiousness and ability to execute).
- **The Early Talent Model:** He highlights the success of the Teal Fellowship, which funded and nurtured talent (like Vitalik Buterin) *before* the age of 19 and without requiring formal enrollment, proving that exceptional talent can bypass the traditional academic gatekeepers.
