# The High Integrity Way Great Men Chase Fame | UT Philosopher Thomas Pangle on the American Founding — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
This podcast episode features a conversation between Johnathan Bi and Thomas Pangle, a philosopher specializing in classical political thought, focusing on the concept of “high integrity” and how figures like George Washington pursued fame. Pangle argues that true greatness isn’t simply about being well-known, but about achieving recognition for admirable qualities – a concept rooted in classical philosophy, particularly the ideas of Plato, Nietzsche, and the Stoics. The core argument centers around the idea that a truly great man’s desire for recognition isn’t driven by ego, but by a desire to confirm his own self-perception and live up to his ideals. Pangle contrasts this with a more superficial or self-serving pursuit of glory, exemplified by figures like Napoleon, who sought recognition solely for personal aggrandizement. The episode delves into the complexities of honor, distinguishing between monarchical and republican conceptions, and explores the potential tragedy inherent in the great man’s pursuit of fame.

## Key Points
- **The “High Integrity Way”:** Pangle advocates for a “high integrity way” of chasing fame, defined by seeking recognition for admirable qualities rather than simply being well-known. This aligns with classical ideals of virtue and self-cultivation.
- **Classical Roots:** The discussion draws heavily on classical philosophers like Plato (contrast between reason and passion), Nietzsche (the need for “pathos of distance” to achieve greatness), and the Stoics (the acceptance of fate and the importance of honor).
- **Washington as a Model:** Washington is presented as a prime example of this “high integrity way,” driven by a desire to live up to his own self-image and receive recognition from respected peers, not just public acclaim.
- **The “Ruling Passion” Debate:** Pangle challenges Hamilton’s assertion that the pursuit of fame is a “ruling passion,” arguing that a truly noble mind prioritizes reason over fleeting desires for recognition.
- **The Master-Slave Dialectic:** The episode utilizes Hegel’s master-slave dialectic to illustrate how the great man’s pursuit of recognition can be tragically self-defeating, as the object of his desire (another powerful figure) cannot truly validate his achievements.
- **Egalitarianism and Recognition:** Pangle argues that a truly fulfilled individual needs to allow for the recognition of others, suggesting that egalitarianism is, paradoxically, essential for achieving a sense of self-worth.
- **Machiavelli’s Critique:** The episode incorporates Machiavelli’s observation that ordinary people’s judgments are both vulgar and good, suggesting that a leader should seek the approval of wise, discerning individuals rather than blindly following popular opinion.
- **Cato’s Sacrifice:** The discussion of Cato’s suicide highlights the tension between a rational, stoic acceptance of defeat and the potentially fanatical desire for glory. It’s a case study in the potential pitfalls of pursuing honor at all costs.

## Tools & Resources
- **Plato’s *Republic*:**  Fundamental for understanding the contrast between reason and passion.
- **Nietzsche’s *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*:**  Provides the concept of “pathos of distance” – the necessary detachment to achieve greatness.
- **Montesquieu’s *The Spirit of the Laws*:**  Offers a perspective on monarchical honor and hierarchical societies.
- **Stoic Philosophy (Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus):**  Provides the framework for accepting fate and prioritizing virtue.

## Quotable
> “The great man’s desire for glory is a bit odd because he desires it from usually lesser men.” – Thomas Pangle
> “Honor sort of has two faces. On the one hand, it’s concerned with what other people think of one. But on the other hand, it’s a word for the code that we follow in our lives as the most important defining dimension of our character.” – Thomas Pangle

## Action Items
- **Self-Reflection:**  Examine your motivations for seeking recognition – are they driven by genuine self-improvement or a desire for external validation?
- **Define Your Values:**  Clearly articulate your core values and strive to live in accordance with them, regardless of external approval.
- **Seek Peer Recognition:**  Focus on earning the respect of individuals you admire and consider wise – this is a more meaningful form of recognition than simply seeking public acclaim.
- **Read:** *The Republic* by Plato, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* by Nietzsche, *Meditations* by Marcus Aurelius.
- **Follow:**  Individuals who embody the principles of integrity, virtue, and self-discipline.
- **Practice:** Cultivate self-awareness and regularly assess your actions against your values.
