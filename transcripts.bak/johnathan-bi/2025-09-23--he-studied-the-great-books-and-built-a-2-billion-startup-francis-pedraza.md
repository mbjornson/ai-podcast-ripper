# He Studied the Great Books and Built a $2 Billion Startup | Francis Pedraza — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
The episode features a deep, philosophical conversation between Jonathan B. (founder of Cosmos, an AI venture fund) and Francis Pedraza, a highly successful entrepreneur who built the multi-billion dollar company, Invisible. The discussion centers on the tension between the life of "action" (building companies, making money) and the life of "contemplation" (studying philosophy and the Great Books). Pedraza’s journey—from a failure to a successful founder—is framed as proof that deep philosophical study is not a distraction, but the fundamental source of unique insights and perseverance necessary for extreme success.

The core thesis is that true success is achieved not by choosing between action and contemplation, but by integrating them. Jonathan B. argues that the modern, linear view of "progress" (the Silicon Valley "hockey stick") is fundamentally flawed. Instead, he draws heavily on ancient texts (like the I Ching and the Bhagavad Gita) to argue that human experience and history are cyclical, requiring a mindset that embraces change and non-attachment to outcomes.

## Key Points
- **The Cyclical Nature of History (Anti-Progress Thesis):** The most contrarian take is the rejection of linear progress. Drawing from the *I Ching* (Book of Changes), the speaker argues that life operates in cycles: Failure can lead to Success, and Success can lead to Failure. This challenges the modern belief that technological advancement guarantees an endless, upward trajectory.
- **The Dharma of Action vs. Knowledge:** The discussion references the *Bhagavad Gita*, distinguishing between *Jnana Yoga* (knowledge/contemplation) and *Karma Yoga* (action/duty). The ideal state is not to retreat into the ivory tower, but to apply profound study and knowledge directly into the world to solve real-world problems.
- **Intrinsic Motivation is the Rare Edge:** The conversation highlights that while "mercenary thing is gold" (referring to financial incentives), the truly exceptional person operates beyond simple incentives, driven by intrinsic motivation.
- **The Transcendent Position:** The goal of the modern entrepreneur, according to the philosophy discussed, is not merely wealth accumulation, but achieving a "transcendent position"—a state of inner liberation or truth—where the business success is viewed as a necessary *vehicle* or byproduct of the deeper intellectual quest.

## Tools & Resources
- **Cosmos:** Jonathan B.'s venture fund that funds research, incubates, and invests in AI startups, specifically focusing on the "philosopher builders" ecosystem.
- **The Great Books:** The continuous study of foundational texts (Western and non-Western) to gain a broad, non-specialized understanding of human experience.
- **I Ching (Book of Changes):** An ancient Chinese text used to illustrate the cyclical nature of life and change, challenging the linear concept of progress.
- **Bhagavad Gita:** A foundational Hindu text that provides a framework for duty (*dharma*) and action (*karma*).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Challenge Linear Thinking:** When faced with a problem or a business model, consciously analyze it through a cyclical lens (e.g., what was the peak, and what is the inevitable trough?). Do not assume perpetual growth.
- [ ] **Integrate Study and Action:** Dedicate time to deep, non-specialized learning (reading non-Western texts, philosophy, history) and immediately identify a practical way to apply that knowledge to a current professional challenge.
- [ ] **Cultivate Intrinsic Motivation:** Identify the core, non-monetary purpose (the "why") behind your professional endeavors. Use this internal drive as your primary metric for success, rather than external incentives.
- [ ] **Explore the "Philosopher Builder" Model:** If interested in the intersection of deep thought and technology, research communities or funds (like Cosmos) that explicitly fund research and philosophical inquiry alongside AI development.
