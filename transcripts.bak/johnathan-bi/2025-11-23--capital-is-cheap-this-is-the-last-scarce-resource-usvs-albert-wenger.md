# Capital is Cheap, This is The Last Scarce Resource | USV’s Albert Wenger — Johnathan Bi

## Summary
The episode features Albert Wenger, a partner at USV and early investor in Twitter, who presents a deeply philosophical take on the relationship between technological progress, privacy, and human society. Wenger argues that the current technological trajectory—characterized by rapid, permissionless innovation—is fundamentally incompatible with traditional notions of privacy. He posits that the asymmetry between our ability to construct things (which is difficult) and our ability to destroy them (which is increasingly easy) is driving humanity toward a critical inflection point.

Wenger’s core thesis is that we are facing a choice: either implement massive government control over technology (which requires ever-increasing state power) or accept a radical shift away from absolute privacy. He reframes the concept of privacy, arguing that the value lies not in keeping information secret, but in ensuring that when information *is* known, it cannot be used against you. He suggests that true progress requires a parallel focus on "moral progress" to match the pace of technological advancement.

## Key Points
- **The Asymmetry of Destruction:** Technological progress is fundamentally driven by the fact that our ability to destroy (e.g., with advanced weaponry or viruses) grows faster than our ability to construct. This necessitates a radical rethinking of societal controls.
- **Privacy is a False Dichotomy:** Wenger rejects the idea that one can have both unfettered, permissionless innovation *and* absolute privacy. The trade-off has shifted dramatically; while digital life makes information harder to secure physically, it makes it easier to get multiple opinions (e.g., second medical opinions from AI).
- **The Level Playing Field of Knowledge:** He uses the example of public tax records in Sweden and Norway. When everyone's wealth is known, the information is less threatening because it creates a "level playing field," suggesting that collective transparency can mitigate the power imbalance of private knowledge.
- **The Need for Moral Progress:** Technological progress is inherently good, but it is currently outpacing our moral and ethical development. He argues that without converging on a universal moral core, humanity risks self-destruction, citing the historical failures of the Agricultural and Industrial Revolutions.
- **The AI Race Imperative:** The current geopolitical and market forces are "flooring the AI race," making a pause or "braking" on technology impossible. Therefore, the focus must shift to building the necessary moral and philosophical guardrails *concurrently* with the technological buildout.
