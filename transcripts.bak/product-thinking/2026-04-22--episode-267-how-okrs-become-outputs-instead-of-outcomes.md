# Episode 267: How OKRs Become Outputs Instead of Outcomes — Product Thinking

## Summary
This episode of Product Thinking delves into the common pitfalls of using OKRs and offers a pragmatic approach to their implementation. The core argument is that OKRs too often become simply renamed roadmaps or performance metrics, losing their potential to drive strategic alignment and focus. The episode features a panel of experts – Hugo Froese (OLX), Jeff Goethelf & Josh Seiden (Sense & Respond Learning), and Anish Bhimani (JP Morgan Chase) – who collectively emphasize the importance of framing key results as qualitative objectives that reflect genuine behavioral change, rather than just quantifiable outputs. A key takeaway is the need to break down OKRs into distinct categories – discovery, build, and outcome – to foster flexibility and avoid the trap of treating them as rigid, time-bound commitments. The discussion highlights the critical distinction between aspirational goals and measurable key results, arguing that key results should reflect how the team is impacting the customer’s behavior.

## Key Points
- **OKRs as Roadmaps vs. Strategic Tools:** The episode argues that many organizations treat OKRs as simply a more sophisticated roadmap, leading to a lack of focus and a tendency to prioritize outputs over outcomes.
- **Discovery, Build, Outcome OKRs:** Hugo Froese introduces a framework for categorizing OKRs into discovery, build, and outcome types. This approach promotes flexibility and ensures teams are actively exploring, building, and measuring the impact of their work.
- **Meaningful Key Results – Behavioral Focus:** Jeff Goethelf and Josh Seiden stress that key results should be defined as qualitative objectives that reflect changes in human behavior. They advocate for key results that measure how the team is positively impacting the customer.
- **Cascading OKRs – Critical Thinking:** The panelists strongly discourage simply cascading OKRs down from higher levels. They advocate for a critical thinking exercise, ensuring that team-level key results directly contribute to the organization’s overarching objectives.
- **Avoiding Frankenstein's Monsters:** Anish Bhimani shares a real-world example of JP Morgan Chase, where mapping their key results revealed 341 of them, leading to a shift towards more focused, impactful results. He warns against creating overly ambitious or disconnected key results that ultimately derail progress.
- **Output vs. Outcome Trap:** The conversation repeatedly highlights the danger of focusing on outputs (e.g., a feature built) rather than outcomes (e.g., increased user engagement).
