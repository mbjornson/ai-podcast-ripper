# Episode 221: Balancing Strategy and Execution at Scale with Kristin Dorsett — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into scaling product development, emphasizing that great products are not just about engineering but require systemic support across an entire organization. The discussion highlights the tension between ambitious growth opportunities and the necessity of disciplined focus. A key theme is moving from scattered, custom solutions to standardized, scalable processes while maintaining a strong customer-centric view.

The guest, Kristen Dorsett (COO at Viator), exemplifies this holistic approach by overseeing all customer-facing functions—including product, design, marketing channels, and customer service. Her role demonstrates that true leadership in scale requires viewing the business as one continuous user journey, from initial marketing touchpoint to post-trip support. The advice provided is highly strategic, focusing on how leaders must manage complexity and prioritize efforts by tying every decision back to core business metrics like revenue and cost.

## Key Points
- **Prioritization via "Big Bets":** When scaling, structure product initiatives into two categories: 1) Top-down mandates ("big bets")—the three major company problems to solve; and 2) Funded pods/charters for smaller, defined problem spaces (e.g., "make logins easier"). This structured approach prevents resource dilution across too many high-ROI opportunities.
- **The Configuration Trap:** When dealing with enterprise clients who demand customization, do not build custom solutions internally. Instead, define the core differentiation and either: a) Create a partnership model where external vendors handle the configuration work (like Salesforce), or b) Make it easier for customers to configure it themselves using self-service tools.
- **Leadership Communication:** When presenting complex product needs to leadership, do not frame the discussion from a "building" or "supporting" perspective. Instead, adopt a business hat and structure your argument by analyzing what contributes to revenue and what contributes to costs. This financial lens is what gets executive buy-in.
- **Holistic Customer Journey Mapping:** For large marketplaces (multi-sided platforms), success requires mapping the entire customer experience across *all* touchpoints—from the initial marketing channel messaging, through the landing page, into the app, and finally to the support agent's ability to solve problems.
- **The Art of Letting Go:** As complexity grows, a leader cannot be a Subject Matter Expert (SME) on every function. The focus must shift to identifying and mastering the *most critical user journeys* that define the core value proposition.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit your current product roadmap:** Categorize all planned features into "Big Bets" (top-down mandates) and smaller, defined "Pods/Charters." Commit to fewer focus areas this quarter.
- [ ] **Reframe internal presentations:** When presenting a new feature or process improvement to leadership, structure the pitch around financial impact: *How does this increase revenue?* or *How does this decrease operational cost?* (Do not lead with technical feasibility.)
- [ ] **Map your core user journey:** Select one critical customer path and map every single touchpoint—from the first ad they see to the final support interaction. Identify points of friction that require cross-functional alignment.
- [ ] **Identify "Non-Negotiable" Standardization Points:** For any configurable product, determine which elements *must* be standardized (the core value) versus those that can be outsourced or self-service configured.
