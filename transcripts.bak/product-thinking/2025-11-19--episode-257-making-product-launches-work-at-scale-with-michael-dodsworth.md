# Episode 257: Making Product Launches Work at Scale with Michael Dodsworth — Product Thinking

## Summary
This episode features Michael Dodsworth, founder and CEO of Fanfare, a company specializing in immersive product launch experiences. Dodsworth’s background is rooted in engineering leadership roles at Salesforce and Optimizely, transitioning to a focus on customer-centric storytelling and rallying teams around a shared vision. The conversation centers around the critical importance of understanding your target audience and evoking the desired emotional response during product launches. Dodsworth emphasizes that a successful launch isn’t just about the initial event, but about building a sustained relationship with customers and fostering trust. He stresses the need for nimble thinking, continuous learning, and a willingness to adapt to changing market conditions, advocating for staying ahead of the curve by constantly listening to customer needs and weaving compelling narratives.

## Key Points
- **Customer-Centric Launches:** Successful product launches aren't just about the initial event; they're part of a longer customer journey. Companies need to understand their target audience's demographics and desired emotional response.
- **Trust is Paramount:** Destroying customer trust through a poor launch can be incredibly damaging. Delivering on promises and building a strong relationship are crucial.
- **Storytelling as a Core Skill:** Dodsworth argues that storytelling is a fundamental skill for product managers, particularly in selling a vision and rallying teams. He highlights Nathan Hubbard’s approach at Ticketmaster as a prime example.
- **Components of a Good Story:** Key elements of a compelling story include identifying 2-3 core points that resonate with the audience, personalizing the narrative with relatable examples, and repeatedly emphasizing those key elements.
- **Active Listening is Crucial:**  The early stages of product development and launch require deep listening to understand customer pain points and weave them into a cohesive narrative. The "Mom Test" book is recommended for effective questioning.
- **Critical Launch Stages:** The most important time for storytelling is during the initial design and thesis-building phase, where mistakes can be identified and a groundswell of support generated.
- **Launch Anatomy:** A successful product launch requires a clear understanding of the launch’s purpose, involving the right teams (product, marketing, etc.), and consistently delivering on the promised experience.

## Tools & Resources
- **The Mom Test:** A book by Rob Fitzpatrick that provides a framework for asking effective questions and gathering valuable customer feedback.
- **Fanfare:** Michael Dodsworth’s company, specializing in immersive product launch experiences. (Website: [https://fanfare.com/](https://fanfare.com/))

## Quotable

## Action Items
- **[ ] Define Your Target Audience:** Clearly articulate your ideal customer’s demographics, motivations, and pain points.
- **[ ] Craft a Compelling Narrative:** Develop a story that resonates with your target audience, highlighting the problem you’re solving and the desired emotional response.
- **[ ] Practice Active Listening:**  Utilize techniques like "The Mom Test" to gather genuine customer feedback and understand their needs.
- **[ ] Build a Cross-Functional Team:**  Ensure alignment and collaboration between product, marketing, and other relevant teams throughout the launch process.
- **[ ] Focus on Trust-Building:** Prioritize delivering on promises and building a strong, positive relationship with your customers.
