# Episode 204: Building Women's Health Products: From Validation to Scale with Inessa Lurye — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode frames product development not merely as feature delivery but as an organizational system—encompassing processes, culture, and internal tools that allow a company to deliver value. The discussion highlights the necessity of thinking like a full-spectrum product leader who can optimize for both external customers and internal employees. Anissa Lurye, former Head of Women's Health at Hinge Health (a $6B digital clinic), provides deep expertise in zero-to-one product development, particularly within complex, high-stakes areas like women’s health and public policy. Her background includes using technology to solve massive social problems, such as coordinating housing and care for chronically homeless individuals in D.C., demonstrating the power of tech when applied to intractable societal challenges.

A significant portion of the discussion addresses the often-overlooked area of internal products (B2E/Employee Experience). The core argument is that treating internal tools with less rigor than customer-facing products is a critical mistake. Poorly designed back-end systems create massive operational friction, leading directly to employee dissatisfaction and high churn risk—a business problem as severe as any external product failure.

## Key Points
- **Internal Products Require External Rigor:** The philosophy for building internal organizational tools (B2E) must be identical to that used for B2C/B2B products. Internal friction points are not exempt from UX design, discovery, or iteration cycles.
- **Operational Friction is a Business Risk:** Poorly designed internal tools can cause significant operational slowdowns and employee dissatisfaction ("causing people to consider if they wanted to leave their job"). This makes the internal product experience a critical component of business continuity and retention.
- **Product Scope is Systemic:** Great product leadership requires thinking about the entire organizational system—the processes, cultural support, and tools—that enable value delivery, not just the final user interface or feature set.
- **De-risking Internal Tools:** While there may be more "wiggle room" in terms of regulatory stage gates for internal launches, the principles of de-risking remain paramount. Failure to test and iterate on internal tools can cause massive operational problems (e.g., duplication of effort, inefficient workflows).
- **The Power of Tech in Social Impact:** Technology is a critical enabler for solving complex social issues. The ability to coordinate disparate data points (case management status, housing availability, paperwork) through tech was essential to achieving dramatic reductions in street homelessness.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Internal Processes:** Identify 2-3 core operational workflows in your current role (e.g., reporting, data entry, content uploading) and treat them as if they were external products needing a UX overhaul.
- [ ] **Adopt the "Internal Product" Mindset:** When building or recommending any internal tool/system, mandate that it undergoes formal discovery, user interviews with employees, and measurable outcome tracking (e.g., time saved, error rate reduced).
- [ ] **Study Systemic Thinking:** Focus on how technology connects disparate human processes (like housing, care management, and policy) to solve a single complex problem, rather than focusing only on the tech itself.
- [ ] **Follow Product Leaders in Health Tech/Policy:** Research companies or initiatives that use data and product thinking to tackle public health crises (e.g., women's pelvic health technology).
