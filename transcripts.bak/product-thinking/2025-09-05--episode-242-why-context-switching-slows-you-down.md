# Episode 242: Why Context Switching Slows You Down — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode addresses a common organizational bottleneck: multiple product managers (PMs) attempting to manage and access a single, limited development team. The core thesis is that this setup is not merely a product management issue, but a severe organizational efficiency problem driven by "context switching." The guest, Melissa Perry, explains that when developers are constantly rotating between different product areas, they lose massive amounts of time and cognitive energy simply re-understanding the domain, codebase, and requirements. This constant switching degrades quality, slows delivery, and ultimately makes the company appear incapable of delivering value.

Perry argues that the root cause is often a mismatch between ambition and resources. She strongly advises that PMs must shift their focus from simply owning the work to owning the *system* and the *process* that delivers the work. The most critical takeaway is that the PM:Developer ratio (3:7 in the example) is fundamentally unsustainable. The solution requires PMs to use data—tracking cycle time, bug rates, and delays—to negotiate with leadership, forcing a choice between hiring more developers or drastically reducing the scope of work.

## Key Points
- **Context Switching is a Major Productivity Killer:** Every time a developer switches domains, they must "reboot" or re-understand the code base and requirements. This cognitive load is enormous, comparable to asking a writer to switch between a romance novel, a technical manual, and a legal brief.
- **Quantified Loss:** Research suggests it can take 15 to 25 minutes to fully refocus after switching tasks. In a development context, this overhead can easily cost 20% to 30% of the team's total productivity.
- **The Ideal State is Dedicated Ownership:** To maximize efficiency, developers should be dedicated to specific areas for extended periods (ideally a year, or at least a quarter in a startup) to build deep product thinking and ownership.
- **The PM:Dev Ratio is Critical:** The ratio of 3 PMs to 7 developers is flagged as "insane" and unsustainable. Industry standards typically suggest a ratio of 1 PM to 5-7 developers, or splitting the PMs into two highly related, smaller teams.
- **Data-Driven Negotiation:** PMs must track and measure the real cost of their current operations (cycle time, bug rates, delivery delays) to build a data-backed case to leadership, forcing them to either hire more people or re-scope the product roadmap.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Measure Context Switching Cost:** Document how long it takes your team to get productive every time they switch tasks or domains. Use this metric to quantify the overhead loss.
- [ ] **Audit PM:Dev Ratio:** Calculate your current PM to developer ratio and compare it against industry benchmarks (1:5 to 1:7). Use this data point to challenge leadership assumptions.
- [ ] **Establish Ownership Zones:** Work with your engineering lead to dedicate specific developers (even if only 2-3) to specific product areas for extended periods (at least one quarter) to build deep expertise and accountability.
- [ ] **Improve Product Context:** Ensure developers have a deep understanding of the *users* and the *strategic reasoning* behind features, rather than just the technical requirements, to reduce the need for constant hand-holding.
- [ ] **Build the Business Case:** Compile a report showing the current cycle time, bug rates, and delays. Present this data to leadership, forcing them to choose between hiring more resources or significantly reducing the scope of work.
