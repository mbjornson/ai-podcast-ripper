# Episode 240: Mastering Product Craft as a New VP — Product Thinking

## Summary
This episode addresses the common challenge faced by high-level industry experts (like a former Chief People Officer) who transition into a VP of Product role for the first time. The core message is that while deep industry experience provides invaluable empathy and understanding of customer pain points, product management itself is a specialized *craft*—a blend of art, science, and psychology—that requires dedicated learning and adherence to specific methodologies.

The guest advises that the new leader must shift their focus from being a domain expert who dictates solutions to being a strategic leader who defines the *right problems* to solve. Success hinges on mastering the operational frameworks of product development, building internal scaffolding, and maintaining a disciplined approach to problem definition, rather than simply relying on past industry solutions.

## Key Points
- **PM is a Craft, Not Just Expertise:** Product management is a codified discipline with its own methodologies and ways of thinking. A leader must proactively learn these fundamentals (e.g., Product Market Fit, user journeys, feature prioritization methods) to gain credibility with the team.
- **The VP's Role is Strategy, Not Specification:** The leader's job is to set the high-level strategy by identifying major industry problems and pushes. They must then scope this problem space to allow product managers the autonomy to investigate and propose solutions, rather than dictating specific features.
- **Build the Scaffolding:** As a VP, you are responsible for building the operational infrastructure for the PM team. This means implementing and standardizing strategic frameworks (like OKRs or strategic intent models) and establishing clear processes for how the team interacts with tech and business stakeholders.
- **Combat the "We've Always Done It This Way" Bias:** While deep industry knowledge is a superpower, it can create a powerful bias toward existing solutions. To innovate, the leader must actively encourage the team to look at patterns, user experiences, and solutions from *outside* their industry.
- **Strategic Mentorship:** If the team lacks a senior PM expert, the leader should either hire one or identify an internal senior PM to act as a partner, helping to define and refine the operational "craft" of product management.

## Tools & Resources
- **Frameworks:** OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) and Strategic Intents (a similar framework to OKRs).
- **Concepts:** Product Market Fit (PMF), User Journeys, Feature Prioritization Methods.
- **Platform Mention:** Monday Dev (for customizable, real-time development workflows).

## Quotable
> "Product management... is a craft. It is part art, part science, part psychology. It's figuring out the intersection of business, technology, customers, problems, and creatively coming up with solutions."
>
> "Your job as a leader here too is not to just tell people what to build. It's to concentrate them on the right problems to solve."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Dedicate time to learning core product fundamentals (PMF, user journeys, prioritization matrices) to speak the language of product development.
- [ ] **Career Move:** Proactively network with other VPs of Product to understand how they scope strategy and what frameworks they implement.
- [ ] **Team Building:** Identify or hire a senior, highly skilled Product Manager to serve as a strategic partner and help build the operational scaffolding for the PM team.
- [ ] **Mindset Practice:** When identifying problems, force yourself to look for analogous solutions, patterns, and UX improvements from industries completely outside your own domain.
- [ ] **Leadership Shift:** When presenting a problem to your team, focus 90% of your effort on defining the *Why* (the core problem) and 10% on the *What* (the desired outcome), resisting the urge to dictate the solution.
