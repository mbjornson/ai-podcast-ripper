# Episode 265: How Marketplace Teams Decide What to Build — Product Thinking

## Summary
This episode of Product Thinking features a deep dive into how product teams, particularly in marketplace environments, make decisions and prioritize features. The conversation centers around practical strategies for aligning teams and focusing efforts, moving beyond simply generating a long list of ideas. The episode highlights the importance of frameworks like OKRs, product principles, and flywheels. The guest, Kristin Dorsett (formerly CPO at Viator), and Craig Saldanha (CPO at Yelp), offer valuable insights gleaned from their experience building large, complex products. A key takeaway is the need for disciplined focus, recognizing that attempting to do too much at once inevitably leads to diluted efforts and missed opportunities. The episode emphasizes a shift from reactive, feature-driven development to a proactive, growth-oriented approach.

## Key Points
- **Focus is a Discipline:**  The core message is that excessive “big bet” thinking is detrimental. Dorsett recounts a progression from 30 to 6 to 3 big bets, illustrating the need for prioritization and a reduction in scope to achieve meaningful progress.
- **OKRs as a Framework:** Dorsett initially used bottoms-up OKRs, which resulted in overwhelming numbers of ideas.  Transitioning to a more structured approach with fewer, clearly defined OKRs proved far more effective.
- **Product Principles for Alignment:** Craig Saldanha advocates for establishing a set of product principles to guide decision-making and ensure team alignment. He developed five tenets at Yelp, which became the foundation for their product strategy.
- **Two-Sided Marketplace Dynamics:** Saldanha emphasizes the critical need to understand and balance the needs of both sides of a two-sided marketplace (e.g., consumers and businesses). He highlights the importance of a clear mental model for resolving conflicts between these groups.
- **The Flywheel Concept:** Saldanha introduces the flywheel concept – a self-sustaining growth mechanism – as a crucial framework for product managers, particularly in marketplaces.  Focusing on metrics that drive this flywheel (e.g., connections between consumers and businesses) is paramount.
- **Shadowing & Context:** Saldanha's experience shadowing the CEO at Yelp demonstrated the value of gaining a holistic understanding of the company's culture and priorities, especially in a remote environment.

## Tools & Resources
- **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results):** A goal-setting framework for aligning teams and tracking progress.
- **Product Principles:**  A set of guiding principles that define a product’s strategy and influence decision-making.
- **Flywheel:** A visual representation of a self-sustaining growth mechanism.
- **Jeremy (CEO & Founder of Yelp):**  A key figure in shaping Yelp's product culture through direct feedback and debate.

## Quotable
> “Trying to do way too many big bets… it’s been a journey over several years. It was where probably four or five years ago, we started doing OKRs at the company level, period.” - Kristin Dorsett
> “We want to be consistent, scalable, and self-service.” - Craig Saldanha

## Action Items
- **Prioritization Exercise:**  Conduct a prioritization exercise within your team, using frameworks like the Eisenhower Matrix (urgent/important) to identify the most impactful initiatives.
- **Define Product Principles:**  Develop a set of 3-5 product principles that will guide your team’s decision-making.
- **Map Your Flywheel:**  Identify the key stages of your product’s flywheel and the levers you can pull to accelerate growth.
- **Seek Context:**  When joining a new company or team, proactively seek to understand the company’s culture, strategy, and key priorities – even if it means “shadowing” key stakeholders.
- **Focus on Connections:**  For marketplace products, prioritize metrics that drive connections between users (e.g., Yelp's focus on connections between consumers and local businesses).
