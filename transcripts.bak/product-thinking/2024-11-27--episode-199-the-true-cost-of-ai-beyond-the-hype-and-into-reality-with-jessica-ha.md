# Episode 199: The True Cost of AI: Beyond the Hype and Into Reality with Jessica Hall — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode provides deep insights into modern product leadership, shifting the focus from individual PM tasks to the systemic processes, cultures, and organizational support required for successful product delivery. The discussion features Jessica Hall, Chief Product Officer at Just Eat, who details her varied career path—from developer to CPO—emphasizing that broad exposure is key to high-level strategic thinking.

A significant portion of the episode addresses the common confusion surrounding technical roles (PM vs. Technical PM vs. Project Manager). The core message delivered by the host is that regardless of whether a product is user-facing or an internal workflow, all Product Managers must maintain a focus on reducing failure risk across three dimensions: Feasibility, Desirability, and Viability.

The discussion strongly cautions against assuming that because a tool is internal (e.g., for fraud detection or compliance), the principles of good UX and product thinking can be ignored. The most valuable signal is the reminder that even employees are "customers" who will resist poorly designed systems if they have the option to leave.

## Key Points
- **The Three Pillars of Product Risk:** Every PM, regardless of domain (internal or external), must assess three risks: 1) **Feasibility** (Can we build it?), 2) **Desirability** (Do users/employees actually want this and will they use it?), and 3) **Viability** (Does it work for the business?).
- **Internal Tools are Not Exempt:** The biggest trap in product development is losing the "product mindset" when building internal tools. Do not assume employees *have* to use a solution; treat them as customers who can leave if the experience is poor.
- **Technical PMs Still Need UX Thinking:** Technical Product Managers overseeing back-end systems (like fraud or compliance) must be creative problem solvers, not just workflow implementers. The goal should be protecting the business better, making processes more accurate, and improving intervention ease—not simply automating a mandated process.
- **The Power of Sideways Moves:** For career growth, Jessica Hall highlights that taking "sideways moves" and saying yes to steep learning curves in different departments is the most impactful way to build a broad view and connect disparate professional dots necessary for executive roles.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a 'Desirability Filter':** When designing any workflow or internal tool (even if mandated by compliance/operations), pause and ask: "If the user hates this system, what is their alternative action?" This forces consideration of employee resistance.
- [ ] **Reframe Technical Roles:** If your role involves building back-end systems, shift your focus from *implementing* a workflow to *optimizing* risk mitigation (e.g., How can we make fraud detection more accurate or intervention easier?).
- [ ] **Seek Cross-Functional Exposure:** Intentionally seek out projects that force you into adjacent departments (e.g., if you are in tech, spend time with sales operations; if you are in marketing, work on internal data infrastructure). This builds the "broad view" required for executive roles.
- [ ] **Study Product Risk Frameworks:** Review and apply the Feasibility/Desirability/Viability framework to every project idea, regardless of how small or technical it seems.
