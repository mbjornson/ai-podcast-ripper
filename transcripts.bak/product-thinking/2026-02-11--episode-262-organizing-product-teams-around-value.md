# Episode 262: Organizing Product Teams Around Value — Product Thinking

## Summary
This episode of Product Thinking delves into the critical topic of organizing product teams around value, featuring a conversation with Jose Casada, VP of Product Management at American Express. Casada emphasizes a shift from focusing on solutions to anchoring product development around clearly defined business outcomes, advocating for a more experimental and psychologically safe approach to innovation. The episode highlights the importance of timing, data-driven hypothesis formation, and aligning teams around a shared vision. A secondary segment incorporates insights from Melissa Perry’s previous episode on value streams, further reinforcing the need for a customer-centric, outcome-oriented approach to product development and scaling.

## Key Points
- **Outcome-First Thinking:** The core message is to prioritize defining desired business outcomes over simply generating ideas or features. Casada argues that starting with the “why” – the desired outcome – drives the subsequent strategy and execution.
- **Timing is Crucial:** Transformation efforts are not universally successful and depend heavily on the context and timing. It’s vital to assess whether the current environment is receptive to change before embarking on ambitious initiatives.
- **Psychological Safety for Experimentation:**  Casada champions a culture of experimentation where teams are encouraged to make mistakes and learn from them. He actively encourages his team to run experiments, even if they initially fail, viewing these as essential for reducing uncertainty.
- **Value Stream Organization:** The episode introduces the concept of organizing product teams around value streams, rather than channels or architecture. This approach promotes better ownership, faster delivery, and a focus on customer needs.
- **Team Structure & Ownership:**  American Express utilizes a structure with journey-based teams focused on value streams across two channels (mobile and web), alongside a horizontal strategy team for long-term product vision.
- **Alignment & Co-creation:**  Successful product development requires alignment between product teams and business partners, facilitated through OKRs and collaborative co-creation.
- **Architecture as a Trap:** The episode cautions against organizing teams around technical architecture, arguing that this can lead to teams optimizing for irrelevant components and creating unnecessary busywork.
- **Product Operations as a Strategic Role:** Matthew Skelton’s insights emphasize the role of product operations – focusing on user needs, value delivery, and governance – as a critical function, particularly at scale, often overlooked by CPOs.

## Tools & Resources
- **American Express’s Journey-Based Teams:** A specific example of a successful organizational structure focused on value streams.
- **Team Topologies:** (Mentioned in the clip) A framework for structuring product teams for optimal performance and scaling.
- **Value Streams:** The concept of mapping customer journeys and value delivery processes.
- **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results):** A framework for setting and tracking measurable goals.
- **Conflux:** (Mentioned in the clip) A platform for product discovery and collaboration.

## Quotable

## Action Items
- **]**  **Define Clear Outcomes:** Start every product initiative with a clearly defined business outcome – what are you trying to achieve?
- **]**  **Embrace Experimentation:** Create a culture where teams are encouraged to experiment, learn from failures, and iterate quickly.
- **]**  **Map Value Streams:**  Analyze your customer journeys and identify opportunities to optimize value delivery.
- **]**  **Build a Product Operations Function:**  Invest in a dedicated team or individual responsible for product governance, process optimization, and user research.
- **]**  **Read:** *Team Topologies* by Matthew Skelton, *Inspired: How to Create Products Customers Love* by Marty Cagan.
