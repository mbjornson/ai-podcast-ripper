# Episode 202: Transforming Product Teams into Investment Partners with Fabrice des Mazery — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into elevating product management from a tactical function of "building" features to a strategic discipline that acts as an investment partner for the entire organization. Fabrice de Mazery, CPO at The Fork (part of TripAdvisor), grounds this philosophy in his experience leading massive transformations, notably shifting the company's model from simple fee-based transactions to a sustainable marketplace. His core thesis is that product leaders must adopt an investor’s mindset—meaning they must quantify business value and demonstrate ROI rather than simply executing development sprints.

The discussion also touches on large-scale organizational change management, particularly when implementing models like Agile or Product Operating Models. The advice here emphasizes that transformation is not merely a process change (like adopting Scrum); it requires deep consideration of roles, budgeting, governance, and people upskilling. Ultimately, both segments stress that successful product leadership hinges on defining clear, measurable goals *before* any major implementation begins, allowing the business to track success over time and justify further investment.

## Key Points
- **Product as an Investment, Not a Cost Center:** Product leaders must shift their internal narrative from being "builders" (executing requests) to strategic investors who define opportunity and measure return on capital/effort.
- **The Importance of Pure Success Metrics:** When building marketplaces or services, focus on metrics that represent genuine user success (e.g., actual paying customers/people), rather than vanity metrics like raw leads or simple transactions. This is critical for proving value to stakeholders.
- **Defining the True Competitive Landscape:** Don't assume competitors are limited to your immediate geographic area or industry niche. A strategic product thinker must analyze who users compare you against when solving a problem, which often requires deep data analysis.
- **Transformation Requires Change Management (The 3 Pillars):** Large organizational shifts require managing three interconnected areas: 1) Role definitions and upskilling; 2) Process changes (e.g., how work is planned); and 3) Governance/Budgeting structures. Ignoring any of these leads to failure, regardless of the product quality.
- **The Goal-First Approach:** Any transformation must start by asking: "What do we think this transformation is actually going to do?" Success metrics (profitability increase, X achieved, Y reduced) must be defined and tracked *before* implementation begins. This process can take years, requiring patience and iterative measurement.

- [x] **Map the Full Transformation Scope:** If leading a major change, create an explicit plan detailing how roles, budgeting processes, and governance structures will change, recognizing that people/process changes are often harder than technical ones.
