# Episode 238: Managing Success Beyond the Build — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode tackles a common organizational challenge: how to practice high-level product management when a company relies heavily on purchasing and implementing third-party SaaS tools, rather than building products in-house. The discussion addresses a listener who works in a large enterprise using purchased software and whose team is being relegated to a "feature factory" because their Product Owners lack formal PM training.

The core thesis is that product management is fundamentally a process of strategic discovery, not an exclusive function of the development team. Regardless of whether the tool is built internally or bought externally, the PM's job remains the same: to own the success of the product, define the problem, and ensure the solution is fit for purpose and strategically aligned with the company's market position. The host guides the listener on how to reframe the conversation with leadership, proving that strategic product thinking is necessary even when the company is primarily a service provider utilizing purchased technology.

## Key Points
- **PM is Process, Not Ownership:** The most critical takeaway is that product management is about "discovering the right solutions to the right problems." This function persists even when the company does not own the code or the tool.
- **The Buy vs. Build Framework Still Applies:** When evaluating purchased tools, the PM must still conduct a rigorous assessment: Is the tool solving the *right* problem? Does it provide a competitive advantage? Should the company extend the tool's functionality, or should they build custom parts to integrate with it?
- **The PM Lifecycle (Universal):** The core PM cycle remains constant, regardless of the source of the tool: 1) Set goals, 2) Understand users/customers, 3) Define the problem, 4) Determine the solution (buy/build/extend), 5) Experiment/Test, 6) Measure success, 7) Iterate/Change course.
- **The Strategic Value of PM:** PM should be positioned as a strategic investment that prevents costly mistakes. The host cites an example where developers threatened to quit after a poorly implemented tool, demonstrating that PM oversight is crucial for employee retention and business continuity.
- **Addressing Leadership Skepticism:** To convince leadership, focus on the basic principles of PM. Frame the discussion around the "Buy" decision: "Is this the right solution for us? If not, what can we do to make it better?"

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Strategic Framing:** When presenting to leadership, shift the conversation from "features" (what the tool does) to "problems" (what the business needs to solve).
- [ ] **Implement a "Fit-for-Purpose" Audit:** For every purchased SaaS tool, create a formal audit that answers: 1) What is the core problem we are solving? 2) Is this tool the *best* solution, or just the easiest? 3) Where must we build custom integrations to gain a competitive advantage?
- [ ] **Master the Build vs. Buy Argument:** When recommending a solution, do not simply recommend "buying." Instead, present a matrix comparing the cost/time/risk of buying vs. the strategic advantage/cost of building, always tying the decision back to the company's core mission.
- [ ] **Study the PM Lifecycle:** Use the 7-step PM cycle (Goal Setting $\rightarrow$ User Understanding $\rightarrow$ Problem Definition $\rightarrow$ Solution Determination $\rightarrow$ Testing $\rightarrow$ Measurement $\rightarrow$ Iteration) as a repeatable framework to structure all product discussions, regardless of ownership.
