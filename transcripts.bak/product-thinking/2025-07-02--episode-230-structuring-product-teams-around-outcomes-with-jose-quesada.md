# Episode 230: Structuring Product Teams Around Outcomes with Jose Quesada — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into the complexities of product leadership, focusing heavily on the necessity of strategic timing and organizational buy-in for successful digital transformation. The discussion, particularly the "Dear Melissa" segment, addresses a common, high-stakes scenario: a CPO taking over a product organization in a PE-backed company that is under pressure for a quick exit (3-4 years) but lacks product discipline. The core message is that transformation is not purely about building a perfect long-term strategy; it requires navigating political constraints, managing conflicting stakeholder expectations (CEO vs. Board), and earning trust through visible, immediate wins.

The guest, Jose Quesada (VP of Product Management at American Express), provides credibility by detailing his 15+ year journey, specifically his role in scaling the Amex mobile app from inception to maturity. His experience reinforces the idea that modern product leadership must evolve beyond mere continuous delivery to incorporate robust, forward-looking product strategy that supports global, multi-channel growth.

## Key Points
- **The Importance of Timing and Failure:** Transformation requires good timing. Product discovery isn't just about knowing what to build, but understanding what *not* to build. Teams must be encouraged to experiment and make mistakes, as this is the primary mechanism for growth and learning.
- **The Trust-Building Strategy (The "Quick Win" Principle):** When entering a challenging organization, especially one under investor pressure, do not dismiss the need for quick wins. Strategic concessions—doing something meaningful, even if it's not the "perfect" long-term solution—are critical to garnering goodwill and building the trust necessary to later implement a rigorous, data-driven strategy.
- **Working Backwards from the Exit:** In PE-backed companies with a defined exit timeline (e.g., 3-4 years), the product strategy must be constrained by the financial goals. The CPO must work backward: determine what the business needs to look like to achieve the expected multiple, and then build the product roadmap to bridge the gap.
- **Soft Skills Over Hard Skills:** The speaker emphasizes that soft skills (stakeholder management, communication, empathy) are significantly more important than technical or hard product skills for an executive product leader.
- **The Goal of Product Leadership:** Great product creation is not limited to PMs and developers; it is about the entire organizational system, processes, and culture that enables the delivery of value to the customer.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Stakeholders:** Identify the key decision-makers (CEO, Board, Sales Head) and determine what small, visible "quick win" concession you can make *now* to build goodwill, even if it deviates from your ideal long-term plan.
- [ ] **Map the Exit:** If you are in a company with a defined financial exit goal, work backward. Determine the required metrics (e.g., revenue multiple, EBITDA) and build your product strategy to hit those milestones, rather than building a purely ideal product.
- [ ] **Institutionalize Failure:** When leading a team, explicitly frame failures and mistakes as necessary, budgeted experiments. Reward the *learning* derived from a failed test, not just the successful outcome.
- [ ] **Prioritize Soft Skills:** Dedicate time to practicing executive communication and stakeholder negotiation. View these skills as your primary product output, as they unlock the ability to execute the product strategy.
- [ ] **Enroll in Product Strategy Training:** Check out Product Institute for structured learning on product strategy development.
