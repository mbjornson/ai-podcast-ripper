# Episode 248: Escaping the Reactive Trap in Product Teams — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode addresses the common organizational failure point where product teams become trapped in a reactive cycle, constantly addressing the loudest customer requests rather than executing strategic product vision. The discussion emphasizes that building great products requires not just PM effort, but robust systems, processes, and cultural alignment across the entire organization. The core argument is that overwhelming backlogs are not merely a prioritization problem, but a systemic expectation management issue that requires a fundamental process overhaul.

The host guides listeners through establishing structured methods to filter, categorize, and communicate product development efforts. The advice moves beyond simple backlog grooming, focusing instead on using data to refine the company's target market, defining clear internal communication rhythms, and protecting the time necessary for deep, strategic discovery work.

## Key Points
- **Systemic vs. Tactical:** The problem is not the volume of requests, but the lack of a process to handle them. The goal is to shift from being a reactive service provider (consultancy) to a strategic product leader.
- **The Triage Framework (The 3 Lenses):** When reviewing incoming requests, do not just prioritize by urgency. Segment requests using three dimensions:
    1. **Impact:** (High/Low) The consequence of not solving the issue (e.g., potential churn, SLA breach).
    2. **Effort:** The resources required to implement the fix.
    3. **Customer Type:** Crucially, segment by customer tier (Enterprise, Key Accounts, General Users) to determine if the request aligns with the *intended* strategic market.
- **The Communication Funnel:** To regain control, establish a regular, transparent cadence for backlog review (weekly/biweekly). This process must communicate not only what *is* being worked on, but also what *is not* being worked on, and the strategic reasoning behind that decision.
- **Root Cause Analysis over Band-Aids:** When identifying "quick wins" (low effort, high visibility), do not treat individual symptoms. Instead, look for underlying patterns across multiple tickets. The strategic focus must be on fixing the root cause that generates the pattern.
- **Non-Negotiable Discovery Time:** Strategic thinking and user research must be treated like any other meeting—they must be blocked out on the calendar and protected. This time is non-negotiable, and learning to say "no" to other requests is a critical skill for product leaders.

## Tools & Resources

- **Centralized Ticketing System:** Essential for maintaining open lines of communication and visibility between Product, Sales, Account Management, and Customer Support.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement a 3-Dimensional Triage System:** Create a structured process for reviewing all incoming requests, scoring them by Impact, Effort, and alignment with the target Customer Type (e.g., Enterprise).
- [ ] **Schedule a Stakeholder Reset Meeting:** Hold a meeting with internal stakeholders (Sales, AM, Support) to transparently explain the new, strategic approach and why reactive firefighting is unsustainable.
- [ ] **Establish a Communication Cadence:** Institute a bi-weekly "Backlog Review" meeting and publish a simple status update that explicitly states what is *not* being worked on and the strategic rationale.
- [ ] **Block Discovery Time:** Immediately block 1-2 hours per week on your calendar, labeling it "Discovery/Strategic Thinking," and treat it as a mandatory, non-negotiable meeting.
- [ ] **Conduct Pattern Analysis:** Instead of fixing the top 5 most requested features, analyze the top 5 *patterns* of requests to identify the single underlying root cause that needs to be solved.
