# Episode 227: Inside LinkedIn’s Product Strategy Culture with Monica Lewis — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into modern product strategy, emphasizing that successful product development is not merely a function of PMs and developers, but rather a systemic effort involving organizational culture, processes, and cross-functional alignment. The discussion highlights that great products require a cohesive system where Product, Design, and Engineering are all "cost-functional partners."

The conversation features Monica Lewis, VP of Product at LinkedIn, who specializes in LinkedIn Sales Solutions—a billion-dollar business leveraging AI to revolutionize enterprise sales. Monica discusses how AI and LLMs are being used to solve repetitive, high-value problems for sales teams, such as research and insight generation. The core takeaway across both segments is that regardless of the technology (AI, etc.), product strategy must always be grounded in a deep, empathetic understanding of the customer's specific problem or need.

## Key Points
- **The Value of Cross-Functional Systems:** Creating great products requires more than just PM-to-developer interaction; it demands a healthy organizational system where all departments (Product, Design, Engineering, etc.) are aligned and support the product as a whole.
- **The "Customer Problem" Anchor:** When facing any product challenge, the most critical question to ask—and the one that often gets overlooked—is: "What customer problem are you solving?" This question must guide every decision, especially when integrating powerful new technology like AI.
- **The 360° Approach to New Industries (Consulting Skill):** When entering a new industry (e.g., healthcare), do not rely on a single perspective. Map out all contributing roles (doctors, nurses, insurance, billing) and understand the value proposition for *each* role.
- **Leveraging Internal Stakeholders for Discovery:** To quickly get up to speed on a product or industry, systematically interview internal teams:
    1. **Sales Team:** Ask them to run a presentation for you as if you were a skeptical customer.
    2. **Customer Support/User Researchers:** Listen to actual customer calls to understand pain points in real-time.
    3. **Product Managers:** Review existing workflows and user flows.
- **Setting Client Expectations (Consulting Warning):** When moving into consulting, always set clear boundaries and expectations upfront. Do not allow clients to bypass the discovery phase ("Just build me the right thing")—this is the fastest way to fail.
- **AI Strategy Grounding:** When incorporating AI, the process must be: 1) Deeply understand the customer's need/problem. 2) Determine the most effective, trustworthy, and efficient way to solve it. AI is a solution, not a starting point.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Discovery Process:** When starting a new project, dedicate 30% of your initial time to mapping out all user roles and workflows, rather than jumping into feature ideation.
- [ ] **Conduct a "Sales Presentation" Simulation:** Ask a senior colleague or sales team member to walk you through a product pitch, but insist they act as a skeptical, non-technical customer. Use their presentation to identify gaps in your current understanding.
- [ ] **Review Competitor Pain Points:** Spend an hour on G2 (or a similar review site) researching a competitor product. Do not look at the features; instead, focus only on the recurring complaints and failure points mentioned by users.
- [ ] **Define Your Scope Contractually:** If you are in a consulting or client-facing role, draft a "Discovery Phase Scope Document" that explicitly outlines the required time, deliverables, and client participation needed *before* any development work begins.
- [ ] **Focus on the "Why":** Before proposing any solution or feature, write down the answer to the question: "What specific, measurable value does this provide to the customer's daily workflow?"
