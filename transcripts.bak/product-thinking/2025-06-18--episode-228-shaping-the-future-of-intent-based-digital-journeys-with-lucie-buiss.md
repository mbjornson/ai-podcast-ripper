# Episode 228: Shaping the Future of Intent-Based Digital Journeys with Lucie Buisson — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode centers on the evolution of digital product development, emphasizing that modern user experience must be "intent-based"—meaning the product must understand and help the user achieve their specific goals, rather than simply relying on demographics. The discussion highlights the critical shift in product strategy from being purely a technical or development challenge to a complex organizational system involving processes, culture, and leadership.

A significant portion of the conversation addresses the common misconception in product management regarding the allocation of time between discovery/design and development. The guest, Lucy Buisson, provides a case study of scaling a company (ContentSquare) from a small consulting practice to a large, global SaaS operation, illustrating the necessary pivot from serving highly expert users to building intuitive, self-service platforms for a broader, less expert customer base.

## Key Points
- **Intent-Based Journeys:** The next level of digital experience requires products to understand and act upon a user's *intent* (what they are trying to achieve), not just their profile (age, location, etc.). Failure to do this results in inefficient digital experiences.
- **Risk-Based Discovery Investment:** The common belief that discovery/design should take less time than development is a "big misconception." The time invested upfront must be *commensurate* with the project's uncertainty and potential business risk.
- **The Cost of Building Wrong:** When tackling high-stakes projects, the investment in upfront research and design is not just about time; it's about mitigating the massive opportunity cost and reputational damage of "building the wrong thing."
- **The SaaS Pivot:** Successful scaling requires a fundamental shift in product focus. When moving from a consulting model (where expert users are paid to use the product) to a SaaS model, the product must become dramatically easier and faster for general users to adopt and use independently.
- **Productivity Leverage:** Utilizing AI tools can provide massive productivity gains (up to 100% daily), freeing up significant time (e.g., 8 hours per week) that should be reinvested into strategic customer thinking and experience transformation.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Re-evaluate Project Timelines:** When proposing a new feature or product, do not accept the assumption that discovery must be quick. Instead, quantify the project's uncertainty and risk, and propose a discovery timeline commensurate with that risk.
- [ ] **Frame Discussions in Terms of Risk:** When discussing product strategy with stakeholders, shift the conversation from "how long will it take to build?" to "how much are we willing to invest (time/money) to prove this is the right thing to build?"
- [ ] **Audit Your Workflow for AI Leverage:** Identify 2-3 repetitive, time-consuming tasks in your current role (e.g., reporting, initial drafting, data aggregation) and research how AI tools can automate or boost your productivity in those areas.
- [ ] **Study the SaaS Transition:** If you are in a B2B or consulting space, analyze how your product currently serves the "expert user" versus how it would need to be simplified and made self-service for a general, non-expert user.
