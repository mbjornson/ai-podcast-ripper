# Episode 241: Mastering AI Strategy in Enterprise Teams with Maryam Ashoori — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into mastering AI strategy, arguing that the current AI hype cycle requires a strategic shift from focusing on *technology* to focusing on *the problem* being solved. Dr. Miriam Ashoori, a Vice President of Product and Engineering at IBM WatsonX, brings a unique and highly valuable background spanning two decades across AI research, system design, engineering, and product leadership. Her expertise is centered on building AI systems that are not only fast (velocity) but also trustworthy and responsible by design.

A central theme is the evolution of AI capabilities. While early Generative AI (GenAI) focused on content generation and summarization, the current frontier involves AI Agents. These agents are intelligent systems with reasoning and planning capabilities that can automatically make decisions and execute actions. Ashoori emphasizes that successful enterprise AI implementation requires connecting the LLM's knowledge base to proprietary, structured data (like databases) and external APIs, allowing the system to move beyond mere text generation into automated, multi-step business processes.

## Key Points
- **The Problem-First Mandate:** Do not be distracted by the latest AI technology. Instead, take a step back and identify the core business problem you are trying to solve. Use this problem as the primary "frame and lens" for evaluating all potential technologies.
- **Evolving Role Boundaries:** The traditional ratio of Product Management to Engineers (historically 1:6 to 1:10) is changing. Leaders are now discussing a ratio closer to **1:0.5**, indicating that the ability to correctly identify and frame the right problem is becoming the most critical skill, surpassing mere role labeling.
- **AI Agents vs. LLMs:** LLMs (like ChatGPT or Claude) are generative models—they are one type of AI. AI Agents are the next evolution; they are systems with **reasoning and planning capabilities** that can automatically take actions (tool calling/action calling).
- **The Data Gap:** LLMs are limited to their training data. To solve real-world business problems, agents must be able to connect to and query external, proprietary data sources (e.g., databases). This involves converting natural language queries into executable code (like SQL) automatically.
- **The Multi-Step Process (RAG + Action):** A sophisticated agent doesn't just answer a question; it executes a pipeline. For example, in customer service, it first uses Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) on internal manuals. If that fails, it automatically triggers an external search (API calling). If that fails, it escalates the issue to a human partner for validation, demonstrating programmed discretion.

## Tools & Resources
- **AI Agents:** Intelligent systems designed for reasoning, planning, and automated action execution.
- **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG):** A technique used to ground an LLM's response by retrieving information from a specific, proprietary body of knowledge (e.g., internal manuals) to prevent hallucination.
- **Tool Calling/Function Calling:** The mechanism by which an AI agent connects to external APIs or tools, allowing it to execute actions (e.g., querying a database, running a search).
- **Open/Closed LLMs:** The market includes both open-source models (e.g., Hugging Face) and proprietary cloud services (e.g., Anthropic, OpenAI). The application layer (the agent framework) is what connects them to the business.

## Quotable
> "I go to the business problems and I question, is this problem as well suited to be tackled by AI? And if so, what are the advantages? What are the risks?"

> "It's about how an organization supports products as a whole, the systems, the processes, and cultures in place that help companies deliver value to their customers."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Adopt the Reverse Engineering Mindset:** When facing a business challenge, do not start by researching the newest AI technology. Instead, start by defining the problem, and then ask: "Is this problem suitable for AI?"
- [ ] **Focus on System Design:** Shift your product thinking from merely building a feature (the product) to designing the entire organizational system, including processes, culture, and data flow, that supports the product's value delivery.
- [ ] **Prioritize Agent Architecture:** When building AI solutions, aim for an agent framework that can perform multi-step reasoning (e.g., check internal knowledge $\rightarrow$ if fail, check external API $\rightarrow$ if fail, escalate to human).
- [ ] **Master Data Connection:** Learn how to connect LLMs to structured data sources (databases) by designing pipelines that convert natural language queries into executable code (e.g., SQL).
- [ ] **Study the 1:0.5 Ratio:** Recognize that the ability to define the problem space (Product Strategy) is now disproportionately more valuable than the technical execution role (Engineering).
