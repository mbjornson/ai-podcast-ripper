# Episode 216: Getting OKRs Right: Planning with Impact at OLX with Hugo Froes — Product Thinking

## Summary
The episode focuses on Product Operations, a critical function that moves beyond traditional product management silos by addressing systemic organizational health and process gaps. The guest, Hugo Froese, Head of Operations at OLX, brings a unique background, having transitioned from graphic design through UX and service design before specializing in Product Ops. His credibility stems from his ability to bridge creative (design) thinking with measurable business outcomes, making him an expert in holistic system development rather than just feature delivery.

The core discussion critiques the misuse of frameworks like OKRs, warning that hitting arbitrary metrics without maintaining long-term product value results in a "Frankenstein's monster." Product Ops, as demonstrated by Hugo’s experience, is not merely about process governance; it is about building the organizational systems and cultures required for sustained value delivery. The central thesis is that true product leadership requires viewing the entire company—including research, data, and design—as an interconnected system.

## Key Points
- **Product Ops as a System Integrator:** Product Operations' primary function is to act as the "bridge" between traditionally siloed teams (e.g., UX Research, Data Insights, Marketing Strategy). The goal is not to own these domains but to ensure their outputs are accessible and actionable for product development and management.
- **The Liaison Role:** When faced with multiple specialized teams (like 7,000 dashboards or dedicated research units), the Product Ops leader must figure out *how* to connect them to streamline workflow, rather than trying to own the data or research itself. This requires integrating tools for seamless data flow and advocating across departmental lines.
- **Critique of OKRs:** Be wary of using Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) as an "excuse to aspire." Success measured only by hitting set numbers without considering long-term value risks creating a product that is technically functional but fundamentally broken or unviable.
- **The Power of Design Thinking in Ops:** The best Product Ops professionals often have roots in UX/Service Design because this background inherently trains them to look at the entire "machine and system" (the user journey, the internal workflow) rather than just one component.
- **Organizational Dynamics:** Historically, design teams were more naturally inclined to hold cross-functional discussions (retreats, workshops) about systemic improvements than product teams, which often remained in functional silos ("I'm swamp").

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a Systemic View:** When analyzing any current project or department, map the entire value chain (from initial idea/research through deployment) to identify process gaps that cross departmental lines.
- [ ] **Practice Cross-Functional Bridging:** If you work in Product Management, schedule "data flow mapping" sessions with your data science and research teams. The goal is not to request reports, but to understand *how* their insights can be integrated into PM tools (e.g., Jira, Notion) for immediate use.
- [ ] **Lead a Process Audit:** Propose an internal audit focused on "handoff friction points" between two major departments (e.g., Marketing $\rightarrow$ Product; Research $\rightarrow$ Design). Use this to define a standardized operational process that minimizes manual data transfer and communication gaps.
- [ ] **Study Service Design Principles:** To enhance your strategic thinking, read resources or take courses focused on service design and co-design methodologies, as these teach you to view the entire user/business experience as an interconnected system.
