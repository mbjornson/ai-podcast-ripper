# Episode 258: Treating Internal Tools with Equal Rigor — Product Thinking

## Summary
This episode of Product Thinking tackles a surprisingly nuanced point: the often-misunderstood distinction between inbound and outbound product management. Melissa Perry argues against rigidly separating these approaches, asserting that a consistent product management process – strategy, discovery, and delivery – is paramount regardless of the user base. The core argument centers on the idea that treating internal tools with the same rigor as external-facing products is crucial for delivering true value to customers. Perry’s credibility stems from her extensive experience in product leadership and a pragmatic approach to problem-solving, emphasizing a systems-level view of product development. The episode leans heavily into illustrating the interconnectedness of product development efforts, highlighting the potential pitfalls of siloed thinking.

## Key Points
- **Inbound vs. Outbound Distinction:** The episode clarifies the common, but often overly simplistic, division between inbound product management (tools for internal employees) and outbound product management (tools for external customers). The key differentiator isn't the *process* but the *context* and potential impact.
- **Shared Rigor:** Perry’s central thesis is that both types of product management require the same fundamental elements: strategy, discovery, and delivery. The tools and processes may differ based on the user base, but the underlying principles remain constant.
- **Risk Mitigation:** The episode highlights the danger of neglecting the potential impact of internal tools on external customers. The example of algorithms denying insurance claims illustrates how seemingly internal development decisions can have significant customer-facing consequences.
- **Value Stream Thinking:** Perry advocates for a “value stream” approach, recognizing that product development isn’t linear but involves a complex flow of information and processes impacting both internal and external stakeholders.
- **AI Context:** The discussion extends to the implications of AI, emphasizing the need for robust evaluation and data structuring regardless of the product type.
