# You are what you pretend to be — Derek Sivers

## Summary
Derek Sivers explores the power of deliberate self-construction through the concept of “pretending.” He argues that our outward behavior significantly shapes our internal reality, and that consciously adopting a desired persona—even a temporary one—can lead to genuine transformation. Sivers draws heavily on his own experiences, particularly his approach to fatherhood, as a core example, illustrating how strategically embodying a role, regardless of initial feelings, can yield profound and lasting results. The episode isn’t a philosophical treatise, but rather a practical, almost blunt, instruction on leveraging the malleability of self-perception.

The central argument revolves around the idea that we don’t need to *become* a certain person; we simply need to *pretend* to be that person. This isn’t about deception, but about triggering a corresponding shift in our internal state. Sivers frames this as a technique for overcoming inertia and pushing beyond self-limiting beliefs. He emphasizes the importance of short bursts of action, highlighting that consistent, even if artificial, behavior can eventually reshape our identity.

Sivers’ credibility stems from his history as a prolific internet entrepreneur and artist, known for his unconventional approach to business and creative endeavors. He’s a master of self-experimentation and applying seemingly counterintuitive ideas, lending significant weight to his advice. The episode is delivered with his characteristic directness and a lack of polished presentation, which aligns with his brand.



## Key Points
- **The Power of Simulated Behavior:** The core concept is that our actions, even if initially feigned, fundamentally alter our internal experience and, over time, shape our identity. This isn’t about lying; it’s about priming the system.
- **The “El Tigre” Effect:** Sivers uses the “El Tigre” (The Tiger) example – his negotiation persona – to illustrate how adopting a distinct role can unlock performance capabilities that wouldn’t otherwise be accessible. This suggests a psychological shortcut to achieving desired outcomes.
- **Short Bursts of Action:** The episode stresses the importance of *short* periods of deliberate behavior. The father example highlights that consistent, even if artificial, action—a “few minutes or hours”—can build a lasting change in relationship dynamics.
- **Overcoming Inertia:** Pretending provides a mechanism to bypass internal resistance and initiate action. It’s a way to “trick” yourself into doing what you need to do.
