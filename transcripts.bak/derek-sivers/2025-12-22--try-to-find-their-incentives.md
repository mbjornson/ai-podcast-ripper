# Try to find their incentives — Derek Sivers

## Summary
The episode segment introduces a powerful psychological framework for navigating disagreement and misunderstanding, centered on identifying underlying human incentives. The core premise is that initial confusion or alarm triggers a primal, defensive, and suspicious reaction—a natural "tribal" state. However, this defensiveness can be overcome by seeking understanding. Instead of judging someone's beliefs or actions as inherently wrong or crazy, the listener is taught to assume there is an underlying purpose or incentive driving that belief from the person's point of view. This shift in perspective allows for empathy and connection, separating the individual from their current set of held beliefs.

## Key Points
- **The Alarm/Explanation Cycle:** Human reaction follows a pattern: Confusion/Alarm $\rightarrow$ Assume Worst (Defensive) $\rightarrow$ Explanation/Understanding $\rightarrow$ Relaxation/Empathy. This mechanism applies to everything from physical danger to ideological disagreement.
- **Incentive Mapping:** When encountering a belief or action that seems irrational, the immediate cognitive task is not judgment, but inquiry: "What incentives from their point of view make this belief useful?" Understanding the *utility* (the purpose) helps defuse defensiveness.
- **Separating Person from Belief:** The goal is to treat beliefs as temporary tools ("something they're currently holding for a purpose") rather than defining characteristics of the person. This prevents emotional escalation and allows for objective analysis.

## Quotable
> "When someone believes something that seems crazy to you, consider what incentives from their point of view make that belief useful."
> "It helps you separate the person and the actions to see their beliefs not as who they are, but as something they're currently holding for a purpose."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Practice Incentive Mapping:** When faced with strong disagreement (in business or personal life), pause your defensive reaction. Instead of arguing against the belief itself, ask: "What core need, fear, or incentive is this person trying to satisfy by holding this belief?"
- [ ] **Shift from Judgment to Function:** Consciously reframe judgmental thoughts ("That's stupid") into functional questions ("What purpose does that thought serve for them right now?").
- [ ] **Observe the "Alarm State":** Recognize when your own defensiveness is kicking in. Acknowledging this primal, tribal reaction allows you to consciously choose empathy over suspicion.
