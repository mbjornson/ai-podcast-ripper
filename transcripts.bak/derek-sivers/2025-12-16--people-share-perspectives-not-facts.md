# People share perspectives, not facts — Derek Sivers

## Summary
The episode argues that human communication is fundamentally driven by social and emotional needs, not by the objective exchange of facts. Derek Sivers posits that when people communicate, they are primarily attempting to "commune"—to bond and establish shared identity—which means their goal is validation and alignment rather than accuracy. The speaker emphasizes that judgments, opinions, and ethics serve as powerful signaling mechanisms because they allow individuals to signal which group they belong to or whose side they are on.

The core takeaway is a shift in understanding communication's purpose: facts are considered emotionally inert ("boring as dirt") and lack the necessary social charge for connection. Instead, people have a strong incentive to share viewpoints—even if those views are inaccurate—because doing so successfully builds social capital and confirms that their perspective has been acknowledged and justified by others.

## Key Points
- **Communication is Bonding:** The root purpose of communication is social bonding ("commune"), not data transfer.
- **Opinions as Signaling Tools:** Judgments, ethics, and opinions are used for "signaling." They allow individuals to signal their group affiliation or moral alignment, which is far more potent than any objective fact.
- **Validation Over Truth:** The primary emotional goal in conversation is validation—the desire for others to acknowledge that one's viewpoint is justified. This makes people highly motivated to share perspectives rather than verifiable facts.
- **The Low Signal of Facts:** Objective facts are inherently less engaging and therefore rarely shared because they do not contribute to the social or emotional connection necessary for bonding.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Communication Strategy:** When presenting ideas (in business or tech), lead with a shared value proposition or an emotional hook rather than starting with raw data. Build rapport and perceived alignment before introducing objective truth.
- [ ] **Interpersonal Awareness:** Be aware that when others are communicating, they may be seeking validation for their *perspective* rather than engaging in a genuine search for facts. Adjust your response to address the underlying need for acknowledgment first.
- [ ] **Content Creation:** If creating content, structure it around "why" (the emotional/social impact) rather than just "what" (the objective data). Use strong opinions or controversial takes to maximize engagement and signaling.
