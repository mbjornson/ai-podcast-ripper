# 769: How to Connect Better with Remote Colleagues, with Charles Duhigg — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode explores the critical shift in human communication, moving from a predominantly in-person world to a remote-first, digital environment. Charles Duhigg, a Pulitzer Prize-winning investigative journalist and author of *The Power of Habit*, provides expert insights into how communication rules change based on the medium—be it a phone call, a Zoom meeting, or a text message. Duhigg argues that just as we learned new rules for using the telephone a century ago, we are still actively learning the unspoken protocols for digital connection. The core message is that effective remote leadership requires conscious awareness of these channel-specific rules, particularly focusing on politeness, emotional signaling, and structured conversational turn-taking.

The discussion emphasizes that connection is not automatic in a digital setting; it must be intentionally engineered. Duhigg outlines specific, actionable techniques for leaders to improve virtual meetings, moving beyond simply "having a call" to creating structured interactions that mimic the natural flow of an in-person room. The advice is highly practical, offering measurable ways to improve group dynamics and emotional intelligence in a virtual setting.

## Key Points
- **Communication is Channel-Dependent:** The rules for effective communication change drastically based on the medium. For example, when using the phone, people subconsciously over-enunciate words by about **30%** and inject **20%** more emotion into their voice because visual cues are lost.
- **Politeness is a High-Leverage Online Tool:** In digital communication, politeness matters significantly more than in real life. A study of Wikipedia editors found that the simple inclusion of "please" and "thank you" by just one person could lower the overall emotional temperature of a dispute by as much as **40%**.
- **Sarcasm is High-Risk Online:** Sarcasm relies heavily on non-verbal cues (tone, facial expressions). When communicating online, these cues are often missing, leading to the risk that sarcastic comments are taken literally and cause offense.
- **The Three Pillars of a Good Meeting (Remote Replication):** To replicate the success of an in-person meeting online, leaders must ensure three things:
    1. **Pre-Meeting "Grease":** Dedicate time for casual, directionless chat before the meeting starts to build connection.
    2. **Equality in Conversational Turn-Taking:** Ensure that every participant feels they have something to say and are actively participating.
    3. **Ostentatious Listening:** The leader must model active listening by explicitly restating or referencing what others have said ("What I hear you say is...") to validate contributions and increase group engagement.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Practice "Ostentatious Listening":** In your next virtual meeting, make a conscious effort to restate or summarize a colleague's point (e.g., "To circle back to your point, John, you mentioned...") to validate their contribution and model active listening.
- [ ] **Schedule "Grease Time":** For all virtual team meetings, allocate the first 5 minutes to non-work-related, casual conversation to build rapport before diving into the agenda.
- [ ] **Audit Online Communication Tone:** When writing emails or Slack messages, intentionally increase the use of explicit politeness markers ("Please," "Thank you," "I appreciate your time") to manage the emotional temperature of the conversation.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Recognize that communication rules are not fixed; they are learned protocols that must be consciously applied and adapted for every new channel (Zoom, phone, email, etc.).
