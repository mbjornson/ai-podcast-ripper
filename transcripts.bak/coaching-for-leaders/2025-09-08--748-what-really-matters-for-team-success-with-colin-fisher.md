# 748: What Really Matters for Team Success, with Colin Fisher — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode challenges the common leadership assumption that continuous coaching and process improvement are the most critical drivers of team success. Colin Fisher, an Associate Professor of Organizations and Innovation at UCL School of Management, argues that this focus is misplaced. Drawing on research from pioneers like Richard Hackman and Ruth Wageman, Fisher posits that only a small fraction of group effectiveness comes from moment-to-moment coaching; the vast majority (90%) is determined by the underlying *structure* of the team and the "game" it plays.

Fisher uses the analogy of playing in a casino: no matter how brilliant your strategy or process, if the game itself is rigged (i.e., the structure is flawed), you are destined to lose. Therefore, leaders must shift their energy from tactical intervention (coaching) to strategic design—focusing on team composition, clear goals, defined tasks, and established norms *before* the work begins.

## Key Points
- **The 90/10 Rule:** Only about 10% of a team's performance variance is attributable to ongoing coaching, support, or motivation (the process). The remaining 90% is dictated by structural elements.
- **Structural Elements (The Game):** To build an effective structure, leaders must define four core areas: 1) Composition of the group (who is on it), 2) Clear goals (what success looks like), 3) Tasks given (the scope of work), and 4) Interaction norms (how they are expected to behave).
- **The 60-30-10 Rule:** This rule provides a framework for where leadership attention should be focused:
    *   **60% (Structure):** Determined by pre-work—the hiring, goal setting, and task definition that happens *before* the team meets.
    *   **30% (Launch Meeting):** The initial kickoff meeting is highly influential because norms established early are "sticky." Leaders must use this time to articulate the purpose and challenge of the mission clearly.
    *   **10% (Coaching/Process):** Ongoing guidance, support, or motivation provides a significant boost but cannot overcome fundamental structural flaws.
- **Structure Trumps Process:** Research shows that if a team is poorly structured, good coaching has minimal effect. Conversely, well-structured teams benefit immensely from even moderate coaching, while bad coaching hurts poor structures the most (the "rich getting richer" dynamic).

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *The Collective Edge: Unlocking the Secret Power of Groups* by Colin Fisher.
- **Academic Frameworks/People to Follow:** Richard Hackman and Ruth Wageman (pioneers in organizational structure research).
- **Concept:** The 60-30-10 Rule (A framework for allocating leadership attention based on performance impact).

## Quotable
> "If you're playing a rigged game, coming up with the best strategy isn't going to help you because eventually the structure of that game is going to win."
>
> "The 60% of team performance is determined by structure... The 30% is determined in the launch meeting... and only the remaining 10% is real-time coaching."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Team Structure:** Before launching any new project, map out the four structural elements: Who are the members (composition)? What is the single, measurable goal? What specific tasks must be completed? What are the explicit norms for communication and conflict resolution?
- [ ] **Overhaul Kickoff Meetings:** Treat the first meeting of a team as mission-critical. Dedicate time to articulating not just *what* the team needs to do, but *why* it matters and what challenges they will face (the purpose).
- [ ] **Shift Leadership Focus:** Reallocate 90% of your leadership energy away from "fixing behavior" or "motivating people" during execution phases. Instead, focus on improving the foundational structure—refining goals, adjusting roles, or changing processes *before* the next cycle begins.
- [ ] **Read/Research:** Check out Colin Fisher's book, *The Collective Edge*, for deeper dives into group dynamics and structural design.
