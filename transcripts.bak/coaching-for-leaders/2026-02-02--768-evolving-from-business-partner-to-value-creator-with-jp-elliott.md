# 768: Evolving from Business Partner to Value Creator, with JP Elliott — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the critical professional challenge faced by leaders in support functions (HR, IT, Marketing, Finance): how to evolve from being perceived as mere "business partners" (executing tasks) to becoming indispensable "value creators" (shaping strategy and driving growth). The guest, JP Elliott, brings decades of experience as a talent and HR executive at major corporations like Taco Bell, McAfee, and Lenovo. His core message is that support leaders must fundamentally change their mindset from process-oriented thinking to financial and strategic thinking.

Elliott emphasizes that true strategy is not a plan or a set of tactics; it is the process of defining how an organization will create a competitive advantage—a "moat"—around itself. To achieve this, support leaders must master "business acumen," which means understanding how strategic decisions translate directly into core financial metrics like EBITDA, gross profit, and margin. The ultimate goal is to ensure every initiative can be framed in terms of revenue generation and measurable business impact.

## Key Points
- **The Failure of Soft Answers:** Support leaders often fail when they respond to executive questions with "soft answers" (e.g., "it will improve retention" or "it will help our leaders be better"). CEOs and P&L owners require answers tied directly to revenue growth and sales increases.
- **Strategy vs. Tactics:** Strategy is the *definition* of how the business will be different or better, not the execution plan. It must first establish the competitive advantage and the path to higher margins.
- **The Business Acumen Mandate:** Support leaders must operate as "business leaders with HR expertise" (or equivalent). This requires fluency in financial metrics (EBITDA, margin, gross profit) to understand where the organization is going and what it financially needs.
- **The Four CEO Questions:** Leaders must proactively frame their initiatives around the CEO's core concerns. The first, and most critical, is: "Are we focused on the right strategic imperatives?" This forces the leader to look forward and ensure the organization is heading in the correct direction, quarter over quarter.
- **The Alignment Test:** Every initiative, especially leadership development programs, must be tested against the business imperative. The guiding question must be: "Leadership for what?" The answer must be framed by the business need, not the HR function's capability.

## Tools & Resources
- **The Business Acumen Framework:** Focus on understanding how competitive advantage translates into financial metrics (EBITDA, margin, gross profit).
- **The Four CEO Questions:** A framework for re-framing all support function initiatives to prove strategic alignment.
- **Future of HR Podcast:** (Mentioned by JP Elliott) A resource for HR professionals aiming to increase their business impact.

## Quotable
> "How does this drive revenue? How will this help us get same source sales growth? Why would I invest in this if it's not going to help the business?"
>
> "Strategy isn't just about goals; it's about having the right perspective... it's about creating competitive advantage with our strategies."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Financial Fluency:** Dedicate time to understanding core financial statements (P&L, Balance Sheet) and how metrics like EBITDA and gross profit are calculated.
- [ ] **Audit Your Initiatives:** For your next three major projects, force yourself to answer the question: "How does this directly move the business forward in terms of revenue or cost reduction?"
- [ ] **Shift Your Mindset:** When preparing for executive meetings, stop thinking about *what* your function does (e.g., "We run training") and start thinking about *why* the business needs it (e.g., "We need to upskill managers to reduce turnover costs by X%").
- [ ] **Practice Strategic Framing:** When discussing any initiative, lead with the CEO's perspective: "To ensure we are focused on the right strategic imperatives, we recommend X because it addresses the market trend of Y."
