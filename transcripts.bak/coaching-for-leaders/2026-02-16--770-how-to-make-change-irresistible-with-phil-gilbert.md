# 770: How to Make Change Irresistible, with Phil Gilbert — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode explores the critical challenge of leading organizational change, arguing that traditional "mandated" change initiatives often fail because they treat adoption as a compliance issue rather than a value proposition. Phil Gilbert, an expert in large-scale corporate transformation best known for his work at IBM, posits that the most effective way to implement change is to reframe it entirely: it must be treated and managed like a high-value-add product. This shift moves the focus from forcing compliance to creating genuine, irresistible user demand.

Gilbert’s background provides significant credibility, having guided major transformations in massive, bureaucratic organizations. He emphasizes that successful change requires a deep, user-centric approach—rooted in Design Thinking—that focuses on the human problem, not just the technology solution. The core message is that true leadership in the modern era is less about issuing orders and more about becoming a market creator, capable of seeding a new idea and building organic adoption from the ground up.

The discussion highlights a crucial distinction often missed by leaders: the difference between achieving a temporary "quick win" (improving immediate competency) and architecting for "sustained cultural adoption." To make change stick, the entire program must be designed to embed the new way of working into the organizational DNA, making the change feel like a natural, valuable evolution rather than a forced mandate.

## Key Points
- **Change as a Product:** Do not treat change as a directive or a mandate. Instead, approach it like launching a new product into a market. This requires identifying the "customer" (the employee/team), understanding their existing pain points, and making the change so valuable that they willingly adopt it.
- **The Shift in Responsibility:** When change is mandated, the responsibility for failure falls on the employee. When change is treated as a product, the responsibility shifts entirely to the leader/change architect. If teams resist, the leader must figure out *why* the product isn't working, rather than assuming the employees "just don't get it."
- **Focus on Sustained Adoption over Quick Wins:** Most change programs fail because they focus on achieving a visible, immediate "quick win" (e.g., "We used AI for this project, and it was better"). This only proves temporary competency. True success requires architecting the change program around the mission of *sustained cultural adoption*, ensuring the new behavior becomes the default, permanent way of working.
- **The Power of Empathy (Design Thinking):** The foundation of irresistible change is gaining deep empathy with the people being served. The focus must always be on the user's problem and use case, not on the technology or process itself.
- **The Inertia Force:** Recognize that inertia (the tendency to stick with the old way) is a powerful force. To overcome it, you must create a compelling, visible market for the new idea and get early adopters to tell their stories to their peers.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Irresistible Change: A Blueprint for Earning Buy-In and Breakout Success* by Phil Gilbert.
- **Framework:** Design Thinking (A methodology centered on empathy, prototyping, and user-centric problem-solving, pioneered by groups like IDEO and David Kelley).
- **Concept:** Treating change initiatives as a "product" that must be marketed, sold, and adopted.

## Quotable
> "If there's one thing you take away from this book, I hope it's this: at every critical decision point, the change program must be architected on the mission of sustained cultural adoption of the thing, as opposed to improving immediate competency in the thing."

> "When people resist, it's like, 'Well, they just don't get it.' That was forbidden, because if they didn't get it, that wasn't their fault, it was our fault, and we had to figure out why."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Conduct a "Change Product" Audit:** Before launching any major initiative, map out the change as if it were a consumer product. Identify the "customer" (the employee), the "pain point" (the current struggle), and the "value proposition" (how the new way makes their life demonstrably better).
- [ ] **Shift Language:** Eliminate phrases like "They just don't get it" or "This is mandatory." Replace them with questions like, "What barriers are we creating that prevent adoption?" or "What assumptions are we making about the user?"
- [ ] **Prioritize Adoption over Implementation:** When designing a change program, dedicate 70% of the effort to marketing, communication, and building internal champions (early adopters), and only 30% to the technical implementation.
- [ ] **Deepen Empathy:** Before making any decision, conduct user interviews focused purely on the user's current workflow and frustrations, rather than presenting a solution.
- [ ] **Read:** *Irresistible Change: A Blueprint for Earning Buy-In and Breakout Success* by Phil Gilbert.
