# 777: How to Help Employees Handle Tough Moments, with Anthony Klotz — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode discusses "jolts"—significant, often unexpected events (personal or professional) that force an individual to critically re-evaluate their relationship with work. Anthony Klotz, an organizational psychologist and professor at the UCL School of Management, provides a framework for understanding how to navigate these moments for maximum career success. Klotz is a leading voice on the future of work, notably having predicted and coined the term "The Great Resignation," and is the author of *Jolted*.

The core argument is that while jolts are powerful wake-up calls, they can also lead to poor decisions if the individual focuses too narrowly on the immediate trigger. For leaders, the stakes are higher, as they are uniquely positioned to predict jolts and manage the organizational response. Klotz emphasizes that the way an organization communicates the *why* behind a change is more critical than the change itself, as poor communication erodes trust and leads to negative, uncontrolled "sense-making" among employees.

## Key Points
- **The Nature of a Jolt:** A jolt is not just an event, but an event that forces a critical, holistic re-evaluation of one's entire relationship with work, preventing over-weighting of a single deficient element.
- **Managerial Power:** Managers are uniquely significant because they have the power to buffer the negative effects of jolts or amplify the positive ones through open communication and making necessary adjustments (e.g., adjusting tasks or schedules).
- **Predicting Jolts:** Unlike individual experiences, leaders can often predict jolts because they are responsible for the major organizational decisions (mergers, changes, etc.) that will affect a percentage of the workforce.
- **The Critical Importance of "The Why":** When an organization undergoes a major change, the clarity and transparency of the *reason* (the "why") are paramount. If the "why" is deficient or perceived as withheld, trust is lost, leading to uncontrolled, negative "back channeling" (rumination and gossip).
- **Rumination vs. Clarity:** When the source of a jolt is confusing or unknown, individuals enter a "rumination zone," leading to negative conclusions. Understanding the source, even if difficult, allows for constructive processing.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Jolted: Why We Quit, When to Stay, and Why It Matters* (Anthony Klotz)
- **Concept:** The "Jolt Toolkit" (A framework for responding to career jolts to ensure they lead to higher career success).
- **Skill:** Holistic View of Work (The ability to step back from a single trigger and assess the entire job/career relationship).

## Quotable
> "If an organization gives us a really solid just why behind a big organizational change, we still might talk about it, but we'll talk about that why."
>
> "Leaders are arguably in the best position to insulate workers from the negative effects, and amplify the positive effects of jolts before, during, and after they occur."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop a "Why" Communication Protocol:** When proposing any organizational change (business or personal), preemptively articulate the core *why* (the purpose, the necessity, the ultimate benefit) to build trust and prevent rumor-fueled resistance.
- [ ] **Practice the Holistic View:** When facing a career setback or frustration (a potential "jolt"), force yourself to step back and assess the *entire* relationship with your job, rather than focusing solely on the element that caused the immediate pain point.
- [ ] **Proactive Managerial Communication:** If you are in a leadership role, proactively identify which employees or teams are most likely to be "jolted" by upcoming changes and schedule one-on-one conversations to address their specific concerns and provide the full context (the "why").
- [ ] **Read:** *Jolted: Why We Quit, When to Stay, and Why It Matters* by Anthony Klotz.
