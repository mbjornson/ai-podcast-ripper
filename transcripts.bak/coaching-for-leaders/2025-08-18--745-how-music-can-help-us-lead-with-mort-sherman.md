# 745: How Music Can Help Us Lead, with Mort Sherman — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode explores how music—a tool often relegated to personal or recreational life—can be strategically leveraged in professional settings to enhance leadership and organizational culture. The guest, Mort Sherman, is a retired superintendent and author of *Resonant: The Transformative Power of Music*, bringing deep experience in visionary leadership and education reform. He argues that effective leadership moves beyond simply providing answers; it requires creating an emotional frequency—a "resonance"—that empowers every team member to feel like a co-conductor of their own professional life.

Sherman introduces the concept of **Resonance** as the fundamental connection built through deep listening, acknowledging the agency and voice of others. This resonance is practically manifested as a collective **Groove** or **Emotional Frequency**. The core thesis is that leaders must intentionally curate this environment, using music (or sound design) not just to set a mood, but to orchestrate focus, build community, and guide collaboration in ways that prevent employees from feeling like mere "automatons."

## Key Points
- **Resonance vs. Authority:** Great leadership is defined by resonance—the ability to connect with and understand those you serve (citing historical examples like Lincoln or FDR). This requires listening deeply rather than acting as the "sage on the stage" who has all the answers.
- **The Power of Intentional Sound:** Background music (like elevator music) is passive and low signal. For music to be effective in a professional setting, it must be *intentional*—deliberately chosen to create community, focus, or shift emotional frequency (e.g., at a ball game or product launch).
- **The Shift from Command to Choreography:** The most advanced form of leadership involves giving agency and voice to the team regarding the environment's tone. Instead of unilaterally setting the mood, leaders should involve groups in curating the "playlist" for meetings or projects.
- **Music as a Stress Regulator (High Signal Example):** In high-stress environments (like professional kitchens), music is not used to pump up energy, but rather to *calm* and focus people. It acts as an orchestration tool, choreographing every movement from cutting vegetables to plating a dish, thereby maintaining intentionality and reducing pressure.
- **Neuroscientific Basis:** The connection between resonance and "groove" suggests that collective rhythm (whether through music or shared purpose) creates alignment and trust, moving individuals beyond rote compliance toward active participation.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Resonant: The Transformative Power of Music* (Co-authored by Mort Sherman and Sarah Leila Sherman).
- **Concept/Framework:** Resonance (The deep connection built through listening and shared understanding).
- **Concept/Framework:** Emotional Frequency / Groove (The collective state of alignment, trust, and rhythm within a group).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Meetings:** Stop relying on silence or generic background noise. Before a major meeting or presentation, involve the team in curating the "soundscape" (music/audio) to build buy-in and establish an intentional emotional frequency.
- [ ] **Shift Leadership Role:** Practice moving from being the "sage on the stage" (the person with all the answers) to being the conductor who facilitates collective input, thereby giving team members agency.
- [ ] **Apply Environmental Orchestration:** Identify a high-stress area in your professional life (e.g., product development sprints, client negotiations). Experiment with using specific sounds or music not to motivate *more* work, but to intentionally calm and focus the group's energy.
- [ ] **Read/Study:** Check out *Resonant: The Transformative Power of Music* for deeper understanding of these principles.
