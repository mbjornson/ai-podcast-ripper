# 736: How High Performers Land New Business, with Matt Dixon — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode provides a deep dive into how modern high performers are successfully landing new business, arguing that traditional sales and professional service models are fundamentally outdated. The discussion centers on the shift in power from the vendor/consultant to the buyer, who is now highly educated and empowered. Matt Dixon, a founding partner at DCM Insights and author of bestsellers like *The Challenger Sale* and *The Activator Advantage*, establishes credibility by detailing years of research into how professionals—from lawyers and bankers to consultants—must adapt their approach when the client no longer relies on institutional trust.

Dixon argues that success today requires understanding three major shifts: customers are doing most of their due diligence before they ever speak to a salesperson; purchases are made by increasingly large committees; and the old assumption of automatic professional loyalty is breaking down. The core message is that professionals must transition from simply delivering great work to actively guiding, educating, and competing within complex buying groups.

## Key Points
- **The "Sales" Myth:** Dixon reframes sales not as an icky act of convincing a counterparty, but as a fundamental human endeavor—a skill required by virtually everyone (e.g., selling ideas to your boss, or even selling vegetables to your kids).
- **Self-Education is the Norm:** The average business purchaser today completes approximately **60%** of their learning and due diligence journey *before* they ever talk to a salesperson. Buyers are creating shortlists and approaching vendors with significant pre-existing knowledge.
- **The Buying Committee Explosion:** Consensus-based purchasing has replaced unilateral decision-making. While the average buying committee was tracked at 5.5 stakeholders in 2013, it is now upwards of **20 stakeholders** for even modest business purchases.
- **Eroding Professional Loyalty:** The "social compact" that once guaranteed repeat business based on good service is failing. Five years ago, nearly three quarters (75%) of C-level executives stated they would return to a firm after a good experience; today, that figure has dropped closer to **50%**.
- **The Activator Mindset:** High performers view the loss of guaranteed loyalty not as bad news, but as an opportunity. The goal is no longer relying on incumbent advantage, but actively competing and winning market share in an open field.

## Tools & Resources
- ***The Challenger Sale*** (Book): Dixon's foundational work detailing how successful sellers teach clients something new about their own business problems.
- ***The Activator Advantage: What Today's Rainmakers Do Differently*** (Book): The latest framework focusing on the modern behaviors and approaches of high-performing professionals in a volatile market.
- ***To Sell is Human*** (Book by Dan Pink): A resource suggesting that selling/persuading is an inherent, universal human skill.

## Quotable
> "The access upon which business has spun for a very long time has been the belief that if you do good work for your client and... they will automatically come back to you." (This highlights the core assumption that is now broken.)

## Action Items
- [ ] **Shift Focus from Selling to Educating:** Stop assuming the client needs you to tell them what they need. Instead, focus on identifying and teaching them about problems they didn't know existed (The Challenger approach).
- [ ] **Map the Buying Committee:** When starting a new deal, do not assume the decision maker is the only stakeholder. Proactively identify all potential stakeholders and understand their individual concerns to manage consensus buying.
- [ ] **Build Pre-Sale Content Assets:** Develop high-quality educational content (white papers, research reports) that addresses industry pain points *before* a prospect reaches out. This positions you as a thought leader rather than just a vendor.
- [ ] **Reframe Relationship Building:** Stop aiming for "loyalty" and start aiming for "relevance." Your value proposition must be continually updated to compete against every other option, even those the client hasn't considered yet.
