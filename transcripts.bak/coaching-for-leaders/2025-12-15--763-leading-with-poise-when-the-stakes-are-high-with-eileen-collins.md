# 763: Leading with Poise When the Stakes are High, with Eileen Collins — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode features Colonel Eileen Collins, a highly decorated astronaut and the first American woman to command a US Space mission, discussing the critical skill of maintaining poise and focus when the stakes are at their highest. Collins's background provides a unique lens on high-stakes performance, drawing parallels between the controlled environment of space travel and the unpredictable pressures of executive leadership. She emphasizes that true capability is often revealed only when tested, citing her own early life struggles with shyness and her family's navigation of addiction as formative experiences that built resilience.

The core discussion revolves around defining and executing a clear "Mission" or "Bigger Purpose." Collins argues that when faced with panic, distraction, or intense media scrutiny, the ability to transcend personal anxiety by focusing on a larger, external objective is paramount. She provides actionable frameworks for both professional life (defining company/personal missions) and personal life (managing emotional chaos and setting boundaries).

## Key Points
- **The Power of the Mission (The "Why"):** When under pressure (e.g., speaking in front of national media), the strategy is to shift focus from the self ("Eileen") to the mission ("Space Shuttle Commander"). This external, nobler purpose provides a framework for composure and execution.
- **Proactive Training for Stress:** Collins notes that while she trained extensively for technical maneuvers, she realized she had *not* trained for the media spotlight. This highlights that leadership requires anticipating and practicing for non-technical, high-visibility scenarios (like public speaking or crisis communication).
- **The "What's Next?" Mindset:** In high-performance fields (like flying), continuous focus is mandatory. Leaders must maintain a constant, forward-thinking state of readiness, always anticipating the next challenge, which requires both physical and mental preparedness.
- **Controlling the Controllable:** Drawing from her experience with addiction recovery (AA), Collins stresses the importance of the Serenity Prayer principle: distinguishing between what you can control (your actions, your focus, your response) and what you cannot (other people's behavior, external crises).
- **Setting Boundaries in the Spotlight:** Being successful requires the ability to say "No" to flattering requests (like a visit from the First Lady) if doing so compromises the primary mission or safety protocol.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Through the Glass Ceiling to the Stars: The Story of the First American Woman to Command a Space Mission* (Eileen Collins)
- **Concept/Framework:** The 12-Step Process / Serenity Prayer (Tools for managing anxiety and defining personal control).

## Quotable

>
> "You have to be constantly thinking... You've got to be in good physical shape and good mental shape to be able to stay focused and thinking about what's coming next."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Define Your Mission Statement:** Articulate a clear, overarching "Why" for your career or current project. When facing distractions or crises, mentally pivot back to this core mission to regain focus.
- [ ] **Practice "Mission-Centric" Responses:** When preparing for high-stakes presentations or meetings, practice shifting the focus from personal achievement ("I did this") to the collective goal ("We achieved this because of the mission").
- [ ] **Audit Your Focus:** Identify and eliminate low-value distractions (e.g., unnecessary phone notifications, non-essential meetings) that pull focus away from your primary mission.
- [ ] **Implement the "What's Next?" Drill:** Incorporate a habit of constantly asking yourself, "What is the next potential challenge or step?" to maintain a state of proactive readiness, rather than reacting to immediate stimuli.
- [ ] **Review Boundaries:** Identify one area in your professional or personal life where you tend to overcommit or say "Yes" out of flattery or obligation, and practice saying "No" to protect your core mission.
