# 753: The Key Norm of a High-Performing Team, with Vanessa Druskat — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode fundamentally challenges the conventional wisdom that a team's success is determined by assembling the smartest individuals or focusing solely on individual emotional intelligence (EQ). The discussion, featuring Vanessa Druskat, argues that raw talent and even high interpersonal skills are secondary to **team norms**—the established routines and habits of how members interact. Druskat emphasizes that true performance emerges from a shared sense of belongingness, which she defines as an innate, involuntary social need rooted in evolutionary tribal behavior.

Druskat's core thesis is that team success is not about the leader managing or influencing individual members; rather, it is determined by the quality and frequency of *member-to-member* interactions. She reframes belongingness from a personal deficiency to be "satisfied" into a systemic environment that must be built collaboratively. The discussion provides a powerful framework for leaders to shift their focus from optimizing individual skills (like EQ) to actively designing the social system that allows those skills to flourish naturally.

## Key Points
- **Norms Trump Talent:** Team performance is not predicted by the raw talent or even the high interpersonal skills of its members, but rather by the established team norms and routines (e.g., checking in on one another, how feedback is given).
- **Belonging is Innate and Systemic:** Belongingness is an involuntary, fundamental human need—the "social need that rules them all." Treating it as a personal deficiency that individuals must solve for themselves is a critical leadership trap.
- **The Leader's Paradoxical Role:** While leaders often feel the highest status and are thus less acutely aware of the pang of rejection (and may therefore neglect belonging), they bear the responsibility of setting the foundational tone for psychological safety and inclusion for *all* members.
- **The Cost of Exclusion (Motivated Tacticians):** When an individual does not feel a sense of belonging or status, they become "motivated tacticians." This behavior involves guarding information, being overly cautious, and withholding valuable insights to protect themselves, which directly lowers overall team performance.
- **Interaction is the System:** Since every interaction generates emotion, negative or dismissive interactions are contagious and actively degrade the team's emotional climate, regardless of the individual skills present in the room.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *The Emotionally Intelligent Team: Building Collaborative Groups that Outperform the Rest* by Vanessa Druskat. (Focuses on building collaborative group systems).
- **Academic Background:** Peter T. Paul College of Business and Economics at the University of New Hampshire.
- **Framework Concept:** The shift from focusing on "composition" (who is in the room) to "construction" (how the people interact).

## Quotable
> "Team success is not about the interaction between the leader and the member. It's about the members interactions with one another."
>

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Team Norms:** Identify 3 specific routines (e.g., meeting start/end procedures, feedback delivery) and redesign them to explicitly reinforce mutual support and inclusion, rather than just task completion.
- [ ] **Shift Focus from Skills to Systems:** When evaluating team members or processes, ask: "What norms are we reinforcing here?" instead of asking, "Does this person have the right skills?"
- [ ] **Practice Active Belonging Check-ins:** In meetings, dedicate time for non-work related check-ins that require vulnerability (e.g., "What was your biggest win outside of work last week?"). This signals value beyond professional output.
- [ ] **Identify Your Status Blind Spots:** Reflect on moments where you assumed a colleague's silence meant disengagement. Instead, assume they are being a "motivated tactician" and proactively ask for their input to draw out guarded information.
