# 761: Notice Disruption and Innovate Through It, with Steve Blank — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode uses historical case studies to teach leaders how to anticipate and navigate major industry disruptions, arguing that the failure to adapt often stems from focusing too narrowly on the current product rather than the underlying customer need. Steve Blank, a renowned expert in entrepreneurship and the co-founder of the Lean Startup movement, frames disruption not as a sudden event, but as a pattern that can be observed by shifting focus from the "what" (the product) to the "why" (the core business model).

Blank's central thesis is illustrated by the decline of the carriage and wagon industry in the early 20th century. While 4,000+ established companies were built around horse-drawn mobility, the arrival of the automobile—initially loud, unreliable, and inferior—caused mass obsolescence. The key lesson is that the companies that survived, like Studebaker, were those that understood that their true business asset was not the carriage, but the concept of *mobility* itself.

This framework provides a powerful lens for modern leaders: when facing disruptive technologies (like AI), the focus must shift from the specific technology or product form to the fundamental "job" the customer is trying to get done. Companies that successfully pivot are those that maintain a holistic view of the ecosystem and the core human need being addressed.

## Key Points
- **The Product vs. Business Model Trap:** The most critical insight is that incumbents often mistake their product for their business model. Studebaker survived because they realized their core competency was "mobility and transportation," not "carriage manufacturing."
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD):** The customer is not buying a product; they are "hiring" a solution to perform a task (e.g., getting from Point A to Point B). Leaders must identify the underlying job the customer is hiring the service for, rather than optimizing the existing product.
- **The Nature of Disruption:** Disruption often begins with inferior, noisy, and laughably primitive products (the "innovator's dilemma"). Incumbents tend to dismiss these early attempts, which is precisely when the market is learning and the foundational shift is occurring.
- **The "Gradual vs. Sudden" Warning:** Disruption rarely happens overnight. Leaders must be aware of the subtle, accumulating signs of change, recognizing that the signs are often present long before the industry collapses.

## Tools & Resources
- **The Four Steps to the Epiphany:** (Book by Steve Blank) A foundational text on the startup process and customer discovery.
- **Lean Startup Methodology:** A framework for building and testing products quickly with minimal resources, emphasizing rapid iteration and validated learning.
- **Model Canvas:** (Associated with Alexander Osterwalder) A tool for visualizing and iterating on a business model, forcing a holistic view beyond just the product.
- **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Framework:** A strategic framework for understanding customer needs by focusing on the functional, emotional, and social jobs they are trying to accomplish.

## Quotable
> "The key part was that they realized their business model was not the carriage, that their business model was mobility and transportation."
>
> "The job the customer was hiring them to do, get me from one place to the other."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Business Model:** Stop asking, "What product do we sell?" and start asking, "What fundamental job are we solving for the customer?"
- [ ] **Identify Adjacent Ecosystems:** Look outside your core industry. What are the adjacent technologies or services that are changing the *means* of delivery, even if they don't seem related to your current product?
- [ ] **Practice "Inferior Product" Thinking:** When analyzing a disruptive competitor, assume their initial product is loud, unreliable, and laughably primitive. This forces you to focus on the underlying potential value, not the current polish.
- [ ] **Read:** *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* by Steve Blank.
- [ ] **Develop:** A structured process for mapping out the "Job" (the customer need) before mapping out the "Product" (the solution).
