# 756: When It Feels Like You Don’t Belong, with Muriel Wilkins — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the common and deeply disorienting experience of feeling like you don't belong, even after achieving a significant leadership role. Muriel Wilkins, a highly credentialed C-suite advisor, executive coach, and author, guides listeners through the psychological and professional dynamics of this feeling, which often manifests as a form of Imposter Syndrome. She frames this feeling not as a personal failing, but as a predictable reaction to specific professional circumstances.

Wilkins identifies two primary triggers for this sense of displacement: first, undergoing significant professional change (like a promotion or stretch assignment); and second, being part of an underrepresented group (e.g., women or people of color in executive roles). The core message is a powerful shift in locus of control: instead of focusing on external validation or the behavior of others (which can be influenced by systemic bias or exclusion), the leader must focus entirely on the internal beliefs and narratives they choose to adopt.

## Key Points
- **The Two Triggers of Disconnection:** The feeling of not belonging typically appears when a leader is experiencing **(1) professional change** (promotion, stretch assignment) or when they are **(2) in an underrepresented group** (e.g., women or people of color in executive ranks).
- **The Vicious Cycle:** The feeling of disconnection leads to disengagement (pulling back, not asserting oneself). This lack of visible participation is then misinterpreted by others, reinforcing the original belief that the individual does not belong, thus perpetuating the cycle.
- **Belonging vs. Acceptance (The Critical Distinction):** These are not the same. You *belong* simply by virtue of your physical presence or being invited to the table. *Acceptance* is the validation you receive from others. Many leaders mistakenly believe that achieving acceptance is necessary to feel worthy or belong, which is a dangerous trap.
- **The Power of Internal Choice:** The most powerful lever for change is recognizing that every dynamic is co-created. Regardless of whether the environment is exclusionary or biased, the individual has the choice to *experience* the situation differently, thereby putting power back in their hands.
- **Uniformity is Not Required:** The belief that you must conform to fit in is false. Difference and unique perspectives are sources of value; striving for uniformity is counterproductive to professional growth.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** When feeling excluded or undervalued, immediately shift focus from "What are others doing to me?" to "What is in my control?" (Focus on internal narrative and response, not external action).
- [ ] **Self-Assessment:** Identify if your current feeling of "not belonging" is triggered by a major professional change (e.g., new role, new team) or by systemic/group dynamics (underrepresentation).
- [ ] **Communication Practice:** When in a meeting, practice speaking up not to *prove* your belonging, but to *add value* through a unique perspective, thereby making your difference the source of the discussion's strength.
- [ ] **Reading/Listening:** Check out Muriel Wilkins' work, particularly *Own the Room*, to deepen understanding of executive presence and self-belief.
