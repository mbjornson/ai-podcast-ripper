# 765: How to See What’s Holding You Back, with Marty Dubin — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode explores the concept of "blind spots"—the internal limitations or misalignments that prevent leaders from moving forward, even when external success appears stable. The guest, Marty Dubin, is a clinical psychologist, serial entrepreneur, and business coach, author of *Blindspotting: How To See What's Holding You Back as a Leader*. Dubin grounds his advice in the psychological concept of identity mismatch, arguing that professional failure often stems not from lack of skill, but from a misalignment between one's core self-identity and one's current professional role.

Dubin details how individuals often cling to an identity derived from their initial training or past successes (e.g., remaining "the psychologist" even when operating as a CEO). This mismatch causes subtle erosion of performance, leading to poor decision-making, such as signing bad contracts because the focus remains on the *mental health* aspect rather than the *actuarial data* required of a business leader. The discussion emphasizes that recognizing and addressing this psychological gap is critical for career advancement and organizational health.

## Key Points
- **The Identity Blind Spot:** The most common and dangerous blind spot is the mismatch between your professional role and your core identity. If your identity is rooted in your past training (e.g., "I am an engineer"), you will subconsciously filter your attention and focus on those aspects, even when your current role requires a different skill set (e.g., "I am a manager").
- **The Psychological Cost of Change:** Promotions and role shifts are not merely skill-based; they are a psychological process. Moving down a professional identity level (e.g., from "leader" to "manager") can trigger feelings of grief and loss, which must be acknowledged and managed for successful transition.
- **The Value of "The Glue":** People often define themselves by their function rather than their title. The most valuable contributors are those who define themselves by the problems they solve—the "invisible glue"—rather than the formal role they occupy.
- **Listening for Self-Perception:** As a trained psychologist, Dubin advises paying close attention to *how* people introduce themselves (the first things they say) and the last things they say in interactions. This reveals what the person genuinely values and how they perceive their own importance within the system.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Blindspotting: How To See What's Holding You Back as a Leader* by Marty Dubin.
- **Professional Focus:** Developing self-awareness regarding the gap between one's professional role and core identity.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Identity:** Write down your professional title, your core skill set, and your deepest self-identity. Identify any significant gaps or mismatches between the three.
- [ ] **Practice Self-Interrogation:** When making a major professional decision (e.g., signing a contract, accepting a promotion), pause and ask: "Am I approaching this decision through the lens of my *current role* or my *historical identity*?"
- [ ] **Analyze Introductions:** In your next three professional meetings, actively listen for how people introduce themselves. Note the first things they say, as this reveals their perceived value and priorities.
- [ ] **Acknowledge Grief:** If you are in a role transition or promotion, proactively recognize that the change is not just a skill acquisition but a psychological process that may involve feelings of loss or grief.
- [ ] **Define Your Value Function:** Instead of describing your role by your title, practice defining your value by the problems you solve (e.g., "I am the person who streamlines cross-departmental communication" rather than "I am the Project Manager").
