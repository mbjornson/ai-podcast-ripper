# 779: How to Address Bad Behavior, with Nilofer Merchant — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the critical leadership challenge of how to effectively address bad behavior and prevent negative actions from becoming accepted cultural norms within an organization. The guest, Nilofer Merchant, is a highly credible thought leader, ranked among the world's top management thinkers by Thinkers50, and the founder of The Intangible Labs. She shifts the focus from blaming "bad apples" (individuals) to examining the systemic and cultural norms that allow bad behavior to persist. Using the example of an idea theft, Merchant argues that the problem isn't the individual perpetrator or even the direct manager, but the organizational culture that excuses such misconduct.

Merchant outlines the significant cost of ignoring these issues, citing data that 80% of employees are disengaged and that 60-70% of ideas are never heard. She posits that when organizations fail to nourish the "field" of innovation—by devaluing the contributions of its people—the talent will inevitably leave. The core takeaway is that true, sustainable change must be initiated at the relational level, not just by top-down mandates or individual willpower.

## Key Points
- **The Cost of Norms:** Bad behavior is not an isolated incident; it is a symptom of a larger organizational norm that is implicitly accepting the misconduct. Ignoring it allows the negative behavior to become the default, draining the company's intellectual capital.
- **The Field vs. The Flower:** Leaders must shift focus from celebrating the immediate, visible "flower" (the successful idea or product) to nourishing the underlying "field" (the culture, the process, and the people who generated the idea). If the field is neglected, the talent leaves, and the innovation pipeline dries up.
- **The Three Levels of Change:** Change management is often mistakenly focused on the Macro (systemic/cultural) or Micro (individual) level. Merchant identifies the critical **Meso (Relational/Interactional) layer** as the pivot point. By changing the norms between people—by initiating a new, positive behavior—you simultaneously influence both individual actions and the overall culture.
- **Talent Mobility:** The relationship between talent and recognition is transactional. If an organization consistently tells talent that their ideas or contributions don't matter, that talent will not beg for recognition; they will simply move to an environment where they are valued.

## Tools & Resources

-***Our Best Work: Break Free from the 24 Invisible Norms That Limit Us***: Merchant's book, which provides frameworks for identifying and challenging the persistent, unexamined norms that stifle innovation and performance.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Organizational Norms:** Identify 3-5 "invisible norms" in your team or company (e.g., "We always work late," "Ideas get stolen," "We don't question the status quo"). Question whether these norms are truly serving innovation or merely maintaining comfort.
- [ ] **Focus on the Meso Layer:** Instead of waiting for a formal policy change (Macro) or fixing your personal habits (Micro), initiate a visible, positive change in your immediate team interactions. Be the person who starts the "salsa dance" by modeling the desired behavior.
- [ ] **Practice "Field Thinking":** When celebrating a success (the "flower"), explicitly dedicate time to acknowledging the processes, the foundational work, and the people who sustained the effort (the "field"). This reinforces the value of contribution over just the outcome.
- [ ] **Read/Follow:** Check out Nilofer Merchant's work, particularly *Our Best Work*, to understand the mechanics of organizational norms and change management.
