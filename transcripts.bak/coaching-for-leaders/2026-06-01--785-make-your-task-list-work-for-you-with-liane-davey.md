# 785: Make Your Task List Work for You, with Liane Davey — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the common struggle with task lists, arguing that traditional to-do lists are counterproductive, creating anxiety and failing to guide focus. The core argument is that the modern professional is not primarily limited by "workload," but by "thoughtload"—an invisible tax on performance. Liane Davey, an organizational psychologist and author of *Thoughtload*, defines this thoughtload as a combination of increasing cognitive demands, emotional burdens, and finite energy reserves.

To combat this, the discussion pivots away from managing attention based on *activity* (the things on the list) or even *output* (the things to finish). Instead, the speaker advocates for a radical shift to managing attention based on *outcomes*—the measurable ways you are trying to change the world. This strategic shift requires abandoning the single, overwhelming master list in favor of three distinct, intentionally managed categories of tasks.

## Key Points
- **The Thoughtload Concept:** Thoughtload is defined as the invisible tax on performance, stemming from a "treacherous triad": 1) Increasing cognitive demands, 2) Emotional burdens, and 3) Limited energy reserves. Recognizing this triad is the first step to managing energy, not just time.
- **The Flaw of Traditional Lists:** To-do lists are "terrible" because they force the user to confront a daunting, undifferentiated backlog, which is detrimental to managing attention, anxiety, and energy.
- **The Strategic Shift (Activity $\rightarrow$ Output $\rightarrow$ Outcome):** Leaders must move their focus from *activity* (what I have to do) to *output* (what I have to finish), and ultimately, to *outcomes* (what I am trying to change in the world).
- **The Three Separate Lists:** Tasks must be categorized into three distinct lists, each serving a different strategic purpose:
    1.  **List 1 (Self-Directed Outcomes):** Tasks that move the needle on outcomes most important to your own role or job description. (Focus: *Impact*).
    2.  **List 2 (Contribution/Value):** Tasks where you are contributing to or adding value toward *someone else's* very important and meaningful outcome. (Focus: *Leverage*).
    3.  **List 3 (Maintenance/Side Quests):** Necessary administrative tasks required to keep the organization or personal life running (e.g., invoicing, booking travel). (Focus: *Necessity*).

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Thoughtload: Manage the Madness and Free Your Team to Do Great Work* by Liane Davey.
- **Concept:** The framework of separating tasks into the three distinct lists (Self-Directed, Contribution, Maintenance).

## Quotable
> "We need to go one notch further and get to what are the outcomes? What are the ways I'm trying to change the world?"
> "To-do lists are terrible for managing your attention, not to mention managing your anxiety or maintaining your energy, because they force you to confront your daunting backlog all in one place with no help in discerning what to focus on first."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Lists:** Immediately stop using a single master to-do list. Create three separate lists for the coming week: Self-Directed Outcomes, Contribution/Value, and Maintenance/Side Quests.
- [ ] **Define "Outcome":** Before listing tasks, define the single most important, measurable outcome you want to achieve this quarter. All tasks on List 1 must directly serve this outcome.
- [ ] **Practice External Framing:** When planning, start by asking external questions about your industry or the world (e.g., "How is this global trend changing the value my company needs me to add?") rather than reviewing your internal backlog.
- [ ] **Read:** *Thoughtload: Manage the Madness and Free Your Team to Do Great Work* by Liane Davey.
