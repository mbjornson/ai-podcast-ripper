# 752: How to Start the Top Job, with Scott Keller — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode discusses the highly complex transition of stepping into a top leadership role (CEO, President, etc.), noting that it is a period prone to significant challenges, with estimates suggesting 1/3 to 1/2 of new CEOs fail within 18 months. Scott Keller, a senior partner at McKinsey and author of *A CEO for All Seasons*, frames this transition not just as a job change, but as a profound "institutional renewal" that requires balancing personal growth with organizational necessity.

Keller introduces the concept of leadership in four "seasons": Spring (candidate phase), Summer (the actual transition/starting strong), Fall (staying ahead and avoiding complacency), and Winter (graceful exit). The core message is that success depends on recognizing this cyclical nature, where a CEO's legacy is tied not only to their tenure but also to the success of their successor.

The discussion emphasizes the dual challenge: managing the intense personal spotlight and "reality distortion" while simultaneously leveraging the institutional opportunity presented by the transition itself—a moment when an organization naturally enters an "unfreezing" state, creating a massive window for change.

## Key Points
- **The CEO Transition is an Unfreezing Event:** Using Kurt Lewin's model (Frozen $\rightarrow$ Unfreezing $\rightarrow$ Refreezing), Keller argues that a new CEO arrival is inherently an unfreezing event. This creates a unique institutional opportunity to drive massive, necessary change, which savvy leaders must leverage.
- **The Dual Challenge of the New CEO:** The role presents two simultaneous pressures: 1) A deep personal transition (feeling like becoming a parent or falling in love); and 2) Being accountable for everything within an institution's broader process. This combination creates intense scrutiny ("spotlight that burns more brightly than one could ever imagine").
- **The Distinction Between Change and Transition:** Citing William Bridges, Keller highlights that *change* is what happens to people (external events like a merger or crisis), but *transition* is internal—it's the mental process of adjusting to those changes. A new CEO must master this internal psychological work.
- **Guard Against "Reality Distortion":** New leaders are prone to an inflated sense of self-importance ("CEO centric view"). The best CEOs guard against this by shifting their focus from "What legacy am I going to leave?" (self-focused) to "What organizational purpose do I serve?" (system/people focused).
- **The Importance of the Successor:** A CEO's ultimate success is pinned as much on how successful their successor is, as it is on their own tenure. This requires building institutional capacity and preparing for continuity.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *A CEO for All Seasons: Mastering the Cycles of Leadership* (By Scott Keller et al.)
- **Framework:** The Four Seasons of a CEO's Career (Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter).
- **Model:** Kurt Lewin’s Change Model (Frozen $\rightarrow$ Unfreezing $\rightarrow$ Refreezing).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Develop Self-Awareness:** When entering a new role, consciously track your personal emotional transition alongside the institutional needs. Recognize that feeling overwhelmed or overly self-focused is normal (the "reality distortion").
- [ ] **Shift Focus from Legacy to Purpose:** Instead of framing goals around personal achievement ("What legacy am I going to leave?"), reframe them around serving a clear organizational purpose ("How are we putting in place the conditions for others to succeed?").
- [ ] **Study Organizational Change Models:** Familiarize yourself with Lewin's Unfreezing $\rightarrow$ Refreezing cycle. When leading change, identify if your organization is ripe for transformation (unfrozen) and act decisively during that window.
- [ ] **Read/Research:** *A CEO for All Seasons: Mastering the Cycles of Leadership* by Scott Keller et al.
