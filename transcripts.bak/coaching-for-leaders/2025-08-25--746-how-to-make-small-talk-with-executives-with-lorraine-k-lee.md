# 746: How to Make Small Talk with Executives, with Lorraine K. Lee — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the universally awkward yet critically important skill of making small talk with powerful executives and stakeholders—a conversation that is essential for career visibility and advancement. Lorraine K. Lee, a LinkedIn Top Voice in workplace communication who spent over a decade working on core products at major tech firms like LinkedIn, provides actionable strategies to transform small talk from an uncomfortable obligation into a strategic networking tool. She emphasizes that mastering this skill allows professionals not only to advance their own careers but also to elevate the visibility of their teams and organizations.

The discussion pivots away from generic advice, focusing instead on structured techniques for intentional communication. Lorraine introduces the concept of "Conversational Threading," which is a framework designed to move conversations beyond superficial autopilot exchanges ("How are you? I'm good."). By implementing this technique, professionals learn how to ask questions that elicit multiple follow-up points (threads) and how to answer in a way that gives the other person several hooks to pull on, ensuring sustained engagement.

Ultimately, the episode reframes small talk not as filler time, but as the crucial "avenue toward medium and big talk." The core message is that preparation—whether through researching the context or practicing specific conversational structures—is key to making these high-stakes interactions memorable and impactful.

## Key Points
- **The Strategic Value of Small Talk:** Small talk should be viewed not as a social chore, but as the primary mechanism for building connections and creating opportunities ("the avenue toward medium and big talk"). Failing to engage well can mean losing visibility in a crowded professional environment.
- **Preparation is Non-Negotiable:** The most critical mistake when speaking with executives is lack of preparation (illustrated by the story of shadowing a studio president). Always research the person, the company, or the event topic beforehand to ensure you have substantive points ready beyond general pleasantries.
- **Avoid Autopilot Conversation:** Recognize and eliminate "autopilot small talk" (e.g., "How are you? I'm good."). These exchanges provide no value for either party and fail to build meaningful connections.
- **Implement Conversational Threading:** This is a structured communication technique involving two parts:
    1.  **Asking Threads:** Asking open-ended, specific questions that require more than a one-word answer (e.g., instead of "How was your weekend?" ask, "What was the highlight of your weekend?").
    2.  **Giving Threads:** When responding, providing multiple pieces of information or topics for the question asker to follow up on (e.g., discussing both going to a spa *and* running three miles).

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Unforgettable Presence: Get Seen, Gain Influence, and Catapult Your Career* by Lorraine K. Lee.
- **Framework:** Conversational Threading (A method for structuring questions and answers to ensure sustained, deep conversation).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Practice Threading:** In your next three interactions, consciously shift from answering with single statements ("It was good.") to providing 2-3 distinct details or topics (e.g., "I did X, and I also managed to do Y").
- [ ] **Elevate Questions:** Replace generic questions like "How are you?" with specific prompts that demand a narrative answer, such as: "What's been the most interesting challenge your team has faced lately?" or "What was the highlight of your week so far?"
- [ ] **Pre-Game Research:** Before attending any major event (conference, all-hands meeting), identify 2-3 key people you might encounter and research a specific, non-obvious point about their work or company to use as an opening topic.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Reframe small talk from being a social obligation into a strategic professional activity designed solely to build visibility for yourself and your team.
