# 742: The Problem Leaders Have With Authenticity, with Sabina Nawaz — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode challenges the widely accepted notion that "authenticity" is a sufficient or even desirable goal for leaders. Sabina Nawaz, an executive coach with extensive experience advising C-level executives at Fortune 500 companies and government agencies, argues that equating leadership success with simply "being true to yourself" is a fallacy. She posits that authenticity is neither singular nor static; rather, it is context-dependent and role-based. The core discussion shifts the focus from *who* you are (authenticity) to *how* your actions align with your stated goals (integrity). Nawaz argues that leaders often misuse "authenticity" as an excuse to avoid the difficult work of calibrating their behavior toward a specific organizational purpose or intention.

The conversation highlights two major pitfalls: first, the conversational stopper "Yeah, but," which kills generative dialogue; and second, the misunderstanding that one's values are absolute truths. She uses extreme examples (like prioritizing love over honesty) to demonstrate that value conflicts are inevitable, requiring leaders to make conscious trade-offs based on their primary purpose or goal. The ultimate message is that effective leadership requires recognizing your roles, understanding your limitations, and deliberately choosing actions that serve a higher intention, even if those actions feel inauthentic to a single "self."

## Key Points
- **The Danger of "Yeah, But":** This phrase is cited as the primary blocker of generative conversation—the kind of dialogue that builds upon ideas. It shuts down team members and prevents managers from hearing the full truth because people know it will be met with resistance.
- **Authenticity is Plural, Not Singular:** The concept of a single "true self" is rejected. Instead, we are shaped by our roles (partner, manager, friend) and external context. A leader's behavior must be viewed as *role-dependent* and *context-dependent*.
- **Integrity Requires Calibration:** True leadership integrity does not mean being perpetually honest or transparent; it means making conscious trade-offs between conflicting values based on your overarching purpose (e.g., prioritizing the life of a child over the value of honesty).
- **The Improvisational Shift: Yes, And:** To counteract the limiting nature of "Yeah, but," Nawaz recommends adopting the improvisational practice of "Yes, and..." This framework forces participants to build upon an idea rather than negate it.
- **Focus on Intention Over Instinct:** Leaders must assess whether their natural, primal instincts are actually serving their present-day intentions or goals. The goal is not simply to be true to oneself, but to align one's actions with a desired outcome.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *You're Boss: Become the Manager You Want to Be (and Others Need)* by Sabina Nawaz.
- **Skill/Practice:** Improvisational practice using "Yes, and..." This technique is useful for improving communication in high-stakes meetings or creative brainstorming sessions.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Communication Skill:** When in a meeting or giving feedback, actively replace the phrase "Yeah, but" with an open-ended question or a validating statement starting with "Yes, and..." to encourage generative conversation.
- [ ] **Leadership Mindset:** Identify 3 distinct roles you play (e.g., manager, partner, employee). For each role, define what your *primary goal* is, recognizing that the ideal behavior for Role A may conflict with Role B.
- [ ] **Self-Assessment:** When facing a difficult decision where two of your core values clash (e.g., honesty vs. loyalty), write down which value you are prioritizing and explicitly state why it serves your overarching purpose or goal in that specific context.
- [ ] **Reading/Learning:** Review *You're Boss* by Sabina Nawaz to deepen understanding of executive coaching principles and leadership calibration.
