# How to Get Better at Listening, with Bill Mayo — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode features Bill Mayo, an alum of the Coaching for Leaders Academy and a plant foreman in the natural gas industry. Bill discusses his journey to improving his leadership skills after receiving critical feedback from his company's Vice President, who noted his lack of leadership depth. His initial goal was simply to acquire "more leadership skills," but he quickly zeroed in on listening as the most critical area for improvement.

Bill explains that his previous approach to communication was merely "listening just well enough to respond," which was insufficient for true leadership. He realized that genuine listening—the act of truly understanding the speaker's perspective—fundamentally changes the conversation's dynamic and the quality of the connection. The episode details his commitment to making listening an active, measurable skill, moving beyond passive hearing to intentional inquiry.

## Key Points
- **Shift from Responding to Understanding:** The fundamental difference in effective leadership listening is moving from merely formulating a response to genuinely absorbing and understanding the speaker's point of view.
- **The Power of the Second and Third Question:** Asking follow-up questions (the second or third question) is not just an act of curiosity; it is a mechanism that forces the speaker to elaborate, providing deeper, more nuanced information that would otherwise be "trite or kind of transactional."
- **The "Tell Me More" Technique:** This broad, open-ended question is highlighted as a highly effective tool. It acts as an open invitation, encouraging people to open up and share tangential information they might otherwise forget to mention.
- **Listening as a Counter to Micromanagement:** Bill notes that before his commitment, he tended to be a micromanager, asking enough questions to know what was going on and then immediately telling people what to do. By prioritizing listening, he learned to ask questions and then *be quiet*, allowing the team to fully articulate their version of events before offering guidance.
- **Improved Decision-Making:** By committing to better listening, Bill found that his snap decisions were often wrong. The time spent asking questions and gathering comprehensive information revealed that the reality was often "totally different" from his initial assumption.

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement the 2-3 Question Rule:** In your next three professional conversations, commit to asking at least two follow-up questions (beyond the initial open-ended question) to ensure you have gathered maximum detail and context.
- [ ] **Practice the "Tell Me More" Prompt:** When someone presents a topic or idea, use the phrase, "Tell me more about that," to open the door for deeper, unprompted information.
- [ ] **Adopt the "Listen-Then-Wait" Protocol:** When receiving information or feedback, resist the urge to immediately offer a solution or correction. Instead, practice asking a clarifying question and then remaining silent to allow the speaker to fill the void.
- [ ] **Set a 60-Day Listening Commitment:** Treat improving your listening skills like a defined, measurable project, committing to practicing the techniques daily for 60 days.
