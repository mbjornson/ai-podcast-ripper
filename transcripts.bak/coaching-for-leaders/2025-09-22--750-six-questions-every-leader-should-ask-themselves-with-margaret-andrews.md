# 750: Six Questions Every Leader Should Ask Themselves, with Margaret Andrews — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode establishes that effective leadership is fundamentally rooted in self-mastery; one must first learn to lead oneself before successfully leading others. The guest, Margaret Andrews, is a highly credible expert—a seasoned executive and academic leader whose program, *Manage Yourself to Lead Others*, is noted as the most popular professional development course at Harvard. She argues that true leadership wisdom begins with deep self-understanding, which allows an individual not only to understand their own strengths but also to manage personal growth across different career stages.

The core discussion revolves around how understanding oneself enables resilience, improves decision-making, and enhances one's ability to develop others. Andrews introduces a framework of six key questions designed to guide this self-discovery process. The methodology emphasizes that answering these questions is not a quick task; it requires dedicated time, reflection, and revisiting the answers over months or even years for maximum impact.

## Key Points
- **The Interpersonal Skill Dominance:** Margaret Andrews' "Best Boss Exercise" reveals that when people identify their best boss, the overwhelming majority of reasons (85%) relate to interpersonal skills (soft skills, emotional intelligence, relationship skills). Only 15% is split between IQ and technical/functional expertise.
- **Leadership Hierarchy:** While high IQ or specialized technical skills may be what gets a person *promoted*, it is the mastery of interpersonal skills that defines who is a truly *great* leader or boss.
- **Self-Understanding as Foundational:** Understanding yourself allows you to recognize your unique features (rather than viewing them as flaws) and enables continuous self-management—meaning, adapting your "self" to meet the demands of each new level in your career.
- **The Process is Iterative:** The six questions are not answered in a single sitting. To be effective, the process requires initial freehand writing (5–10 minutes), followed by revisiting and adding to those answers over weeks, months, or even years.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Manage Yourself to Lead Others: Why Great Leadership Begins with Self-Understanding* (By Margaret Andrews)
- **Program/Course:** Milo Manage Yourself to Lead Others (A professional development program popular at Harvard)
