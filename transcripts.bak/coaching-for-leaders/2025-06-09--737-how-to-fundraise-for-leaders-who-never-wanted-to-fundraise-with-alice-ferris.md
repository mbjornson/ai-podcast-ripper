# 737: How to Fundraise for Leaders Who Never Wanted to Fundraise, with Alice Ferris — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode, featuring Alice Ferris of Goalbusters, reframes fundraising from a dreaded financial transaction into a sophisticated relationship development skill critical for any leader or organization seeking resources. While the topic is specific to non-profits, the underlying principles—stakeholder management, sustained communication, and strategic timing—are universally applicable in business leadership. Alice emphasizes that most people view "the ask" (the donation) as the core of fundraising, but she argues this is a massive misunderstanding.

Alice details a structured process for successful resource acquisition, drawing heavily on an analogy to dating. She outlines distinct phases: identifying potential prospects, making initial contact, cultivating the relationship through dialogue, executing the ask, and finally, stewardship (following up). Her background includes over 30 years of specialized experience in strategic planning, board training, and executive coaching, lending significant credibility to her process-oriented approach.

The core message is that success hinges on the preparatory work—the sustained effort to build trust and mutual understanding—rather than the moment of asking for money or resources. She also cautions against setting unachievable goals based purely on budget shortfalls, advocating instead for a strategic view of resource needs.

## Key Points
- **The 90/10 Rule:** Only 5–10% of fundraising is "the ask." The remaining 90–95% involves the preparatory work: relationship development (getting to know each other) and stewardship (showing up for the date).
- **The Prospect Funnel:** Fundraising must be approached systematically. This involves three stages: 1) Identifying a broad pool of potential prospects; 2) Winnowing this down to *qualified* prospects who are genuinely interested in the cause; and 3) Making initial contact with those qualified individuals.
- **Cultivation is Dialogue, Not Monologue:** During relationship development (cultivation), leaders must avoid talking only about their own organization or cause. The process requires a two-way dialogue where the potential donor/stakeholder gets to know the leader as well as the leader gets to know them.
- **The Ask Must Be Adjusted:** Rejection is rarely personal failure; it usually means the ask was mistimed, too big, or not relevant to the current stage of the relationship. The process requires iteratively adjusting the request until the right timing and scope are found.
- **Goal Setting Trap:** A common organizational mistake is setting fundraising goals simply to cover a budget shortfall. This approach is demoralizing; strategic goal-setting must be rooted in defining desired outcomes first, then calculating necessary resources.

## Tools & Resources
- **Goalbusters:** The firm founded by Alice Ferris, specializing in practical strategies for small and medium-sized fundraising teams (planning, teaching skills, hands-on work).
- **Dating Analogy Framework:** A simple mental model to understand the relationship development cycle: Pool $\rightarrow$ Qualified Prospects $\rightarrow$ Initial Contact $\rightarrow$ Cultivation $\rightarrow$ The Ask $\rightarrow$ Stewardship.

## Quotable
> "The ask is the smallest percentage of the process."
>
> "It's not that you've been rejected outright, it's that you just need to adjust the ask... Maybe it's too big of an ask."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement a Relationship Audit:** For any major goal (business or personal), map out the stakeholders and identify what percentage of your effort is spent on "the ask" versus "relationship development/cultivation." Aim to shift 80% of your focus to relationship building.
- [ ] **Practice Two-Way Dialogue:** When speaking with key partners, clients, or mentors, consciously dedicate time to asking deep questions about *their* challenges and interests before presenting your own needs or solutions.
- [ ] **Develop a Prospecting Funnel:** Formalize the process of identifying potential supporters/investors by first creating a broad pool, then applying specific "moral filters" (criteria) to narrow it down to only qualified prospects.
- [ ] **Reframe Failure as Timing:** When facing rejection or setbacks in negotiations, immediately analyze if the failure was due to the *scope* or *timing* of your request, rather than assuming a fundamental disagreement.
