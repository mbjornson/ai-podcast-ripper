# 739: What Leaders Should Learn from Taylor Swift, with Kevin Evers — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode uses Taylor Swift’s career as a deep-dive case study to extract strategic lessons for modern leaders and businesspeople. The discussion, led by Kevin Evers—a senior editor at Harvard Business Review and author of *There's Nothing Like This: The Strategic Genius of Taylor Swift*—argues that her success is not merely artistic but rooted in masterful business strategy.

The core framework used is the "Job-to-be-Done" theory, which posits that customers don't just buy a product; they hire it to solve a specific problem or fulfill an emotional need. Evers argues that Swift excels because she understands her fans' deeper needs—connection, vulnerability, and intimacy—rather than simply selling songs. This deep understanding allows her to create experiences (concerts, music, social media interactions) that are highly personalized and difficult for competitors to replicate.

## Key Points
- **Job-to-be-Done (JTBD):** Customers "hire" products/services to fulfill a need. Swift understands her fans don't just want music; they want *connection* and *vulnerability*. By addressing these emotional needs, she creates an uncopyable value proposition.
- **Blue Ocean Strategy:** Instead of competing in established genres (like country music), Swift zeroed in on an underserved demographic—teenage girls—a group the industry had written off or taken lightly. She proved that this niche audience was highly valuable and loyal.
- **The Power of Persistence & Vision:** Despite facing skepticism from the traditional music establishment, Swift persisted by focusing intensely on her specific vision (writing for teenage girls). This persistence allowed her to make bold, counter-intuitive decisions.
- **Prioritizing People Over Product (Skin in the Game):** When faced with a major label development deal (RCA), she made the strategic choice to leave it and sign with Scott Bruschetta's new, underfunded label. She chose someone who *believed* in her vision ("who") rather than accepting the established structure ("what").
- **Intimacy as a Marketing Tool:** Her concert experience is described not as a massive spectacle but as an "intimate affair," despite 70,000 attendees. This suggests that maintaining a perceived closeness and personal connection (e.g., constant Jumbotron visibility) is key to fan loyalty and emotional investment.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *There's Nothing Like This: The Strategic Genius of Taylor Swift* by Kevin Evers
- **Framework:** Job-to-be-Done Theory (Clayton Christensen)
- **Strategy:** Blue Ocean Strategy

## Quotable
> "For products or companies that really understand the job to be done, they're able to create products and experiences that can't be copied and in some cases can't be comprehended."
>
> "She ended up going with the who instead of the what... she needed someone who had skin in the game, who could hustle on her behalf."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Analyze your market through a JTBD lens:** Stop asking "What product do I sell?" and start asking, "What core emotional need am I helping my customer *hire* my service to fulfill?" (e.g., security, belonging, validation).
- [ ] **Identify an underserved niche (Blue Ocean):** Look for demographics or market segments that established players have dismissed or ignored. Develop a specialized offering tailored specifically to their unique needs and language.
- [ ] **Vet potential partners based on belief:** When making major career/business decisions, prioritize working with people who are deeply invested in your vision ("skin in the game") over those who simply offer the largest resources or most established credentials.
- [ ] **Cultivate a clear, unwavering personal vision:** Like Swift at age 13, maintain an extremely clear understanding of your desired outcome and target audience from an early stage to guide bold decision-making.
