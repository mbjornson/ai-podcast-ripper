# 787: Better Leadership Through Humor, with Chris Duffy — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode reframes humor from being a mere "nice-to-have" leadership competency into an essential and powerful *tool* that facilitates all other difficult leadership skills. Guest Chris Duffy, an award-winning podcaster, comedian, and author of *Humor Me*, argues that the biggest barrier to deep connection, vulnerability, or presence is often the effort required to perform those traits. Humor acts as a "shortcut," allowing leaders to achieve these goals in a way that feels joyful and effortless rather than like mandatory work.

Duffy emphasizes that true humor in leadership isn't about being the center of attention or telling jokes; it’s fundamentally about *paying deep attention* and practicing generosity. The most powerful form of workplace humor is self-deprecating—the ability to laugh at mistakes, technical difficulties, or absurdities when they happen. By doing this, a leader signals that imperfections are acceptable, which dramatically builds trust and connection within the team.

## Key Points
- **Humor as an Enabler:** Humor is not a primary competency but a "magical tool" that makes it easier to practice core leadership skills like deep connection, vulnerability, and presence without them feeling like difficult homework assignments.
- **The Power of Acknowledging Failure:** Instead of hiding mistakes (e.g., typos on client gifts, technical difficulties during a keynote), the leader who laughs about the mishap creates an opportunity for culture building. This signals that imperfections can be addressed rather than having to be hidden.
- **Humor is Inherently Generous:** The core function of humor is creating delight for others and making the mundane feel joyful (e.g., turning a simple chore reminder into a funny, absurd sign). It’s about giving emotional energy to others, not seeking applause.
- **The Value of Self-Deprecation:** Being willing to point out your own ridiculousness or flaws reduces intimidation among colleagues. When leaders model this vulnerability, it makes the team feel more comfortable and connected because they realize they don't have to be perfect.
- **Empirical Evidence (Job Interview Study):** A psychological study demonstrated that job candidates who were equally qualified but handled a mishap (like spilling coffee) with humor and grace were rated highly by interviewers—sometimes even higher than the perfectly polished candidates.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Humor Me: How Laughing More Can Make You Present Creative, Connected, and Happy* by Chris Duffy.
- **Podcast/Talk:** Chris Duffy's comedic TED Talk (available online) on finding laughter anywhere.
- **Concept:** The practice of "finding the absurd" in everyday life or professional failures to generate connection.

## Quotable
> "The important part is noticing the strange and unusual and the absurd and being willing to laugh about it, even if it is in yourself."

> "It's about creating delight for other people... It's a real generosity in creating that feeling for other people."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Practice "Mistake Humor":** The next time something goes wrong at work (a typo, a technical glitch, a minor failure), do not pretend it isn't happening. Acknowledge it with a lighthearted comment or laugh to defuse tension and build connection.
- [ ] **Identify the Mundane:** Look for everyday tasks or processes in your team/business that feel routine or stressful. Brainstorm ways to inject absurdity or delight into them (e.g., creating funny, non-serious reminders).
- [ ] **Model Vulnerability:** Intentionally share a minor, harmless personal flaw or mistake with colleagues this week. This models the behavior of self-deprecation and lowers the perceived pressure for perfection in your team.
- [ ] **Observe Generosity:** When interacting with others, shift your focus from what you need to achieve (the outcome) to how you can create delight or joy for them (the feeling).
