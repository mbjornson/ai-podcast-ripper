# 764: Stop Solving Your Team’s Problems, with Elizabeth Lotardo — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the common leadership trap of "solving your team's problems" out of excessive compassion. While servant leadership is a genuine strength, the discussion highlights that when this instinct is over-indexed, it creates a cycle of burnout for the leader and profound disempowerment for the team. Elizabeth Lotardo, a consultant and writer for Harvard Business Review, argues that the short-term satisfaction of "everyone winning" through rescue ultimately costs both parties long-term growth and energy.

The core message is not to abandon compassion, but to redirect it. Leaders must learn to shift from being problem-solvers to being problem-enablers. This requires a deliberate, often uncomfortable, change in behavior that forces the team to take ownership of their challenges. The conversation provides specific, actionable questioning frameworks designed to build belief in the team's capacity while maintaining a supportive, guiding presence.

## Key Points
- **The Cost of Over-Compassion:** The impulse to "remove roadblocks" is rooted in good intentions but leads to unsustainable dynamics. For the leader, it causes burnout; for the employee, it results in a lack of empowerment and dependency, especially in complex, matrixed organizations.
- **The Danger of Extremes:** Leadership requires balancing two extremes: 1) Over-solving (The rescue instinct) and 2) Absence of compassion (The "come to me with solutions" mandate). The sweet spot is compassionate redirection.
- **The Power of "What Have You Tried?":** This question is highly effective because it carries two assumptions: 1) It assumes the employee has the ability to act, and 2) It is an invitation for the employee to say "nothing," which is a safe entry point for those who feel disempowered.
- **Interpreting the "Awkward Blank Stare":** When a leader asks these redirecting questions and is met with awkwardness, this is not a sign of failure. It is *reinforcement* that the team feels disempowered and needs the leader to continue asking the question.
- **The Joint Action Sequence:** To move the team from dependency to independence, the suggested sequence is: 1) "What have you tried?" (To build belief) $\rightarrow$ 2) "What can we try together?" (To establish joint action) $\rightarrow$ 3) Employee action (To build autonomy).

## Tools & Resources
- **Books/Articles:** *Leading Yourself: Find More Joy, Meaning and Opportunities in the Job You Already Have* (Author: Elizabeth Lotardo); HBR article "Stop Solving Your Team's Problems for Them."
- **Framework:** The three-step questioning sequence for redirection: "What have you tried?" $\rightarrow$ "What can we try together?" $\rightarrow$ (Employee action).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Skill Development:** Practice shifting from giving answers to asking questions. Specifically, use the "What have you tried?" question in your next three one-on-one meetings.
- [ ] **Mindset Shift:** Reframe the "awkward blank stare" as a positive signal that your redirection efforts are hitting the right nerve, rather than a sign of failure.
- [ ] **Career Move:** When faced with a team member presenting a problem, immediately use the three-step sequence: 1. "What have you tried?" 2. "What can we try together?" 3. Allow the employee to own the next step.
- [ ] **Reading:** Check out Elizabeth Lotardo's work, particularly her articles on emotional engagement and leadership, to deepen understanding of compassionate boundaries.
