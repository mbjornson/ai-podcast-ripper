# 730: How to Take Initiative, with Tom Henschel — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the common struggle of knowing how and when to take initiative in a professional setting. The guest, Tom Henschel, an expert in workplace communication and executive coaching, introduces the critical concept that "initiative is in the eye of the beholder." This means that while your intentions may be helpful (e.g., sending an informative email), the actual impact—or how it lands with management—is what matters most. The discussion emphasizes that simply doing *more* work isn't enough; leaders must develop a new "lens" to identify actions that are truly bold and significantly move the organization's needle, rather than just minor process improvements.

Henschel outlines a structured approach for developing initiative, moving beyond vague advice. He posits that true self-discovery regarding professional opportunities requires actively articulating your ideas—either through conversation or dedicated reflection. The overall message is that taking initiative is not about having brilliant ideas; it's about adopting the right perspective and committing to execution.

## Key Points
- **Initiative = Impact, Not Intention:** Your effort must be judged by its visible outcome. An action taken with good intentions can fail if the recipient doesn't perceive its value or necessity.
- **The Blind Spot of Perspective:** Many professionals are trapped in their own perspective (the "I" view), causing them to miss opportunities that require a bolder, more strategic viewpoint. The goal is to shift this lens to see what matters to the organization.
- **Three Steps to Initiative (Tom Henschel's Model):**
    1.  **Think/Talk:** Dedicate time to actively thinking about your work and discussing it with others. This process of articulation forces ideas to surface that you couldn't see otherwise.
    2.  **Execute:** Once the idea is surfaced, the critical step is to *do* it. Hesitation or over-planning can stall progress.
    3.  (Implicit) **Communicate/Align:** While not a formal third step, communication is crucial for ensuring your actions are received well and don't "step off a cliff."
- **The Waiting Signal:** If you frequently find yourself feeling like you are "waiting for someone else" to make a decision or take action, this may be an indicator that the person waiting is not going to happen. This feeling should prompt *you* to consider taking the first step.

## Tools & Resources
- **Tom Henschel (Essential Communications):** Executive coach and expert in workplace communications and self-presentation.
- **The Look and Sound of Leadership:** Tom Henschel's podcast, recommended for deeper leadership wisdom.

## Quotable
> "Initiative is in the eye of the beholder... your action shows up, the initiative you took shows up, but your intention does not."
> "If I find myself noticing myself waiting on other people a lot, that may be an indicator coming back to me... that maybe I should be the one taking the first step on this."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Schedule Reflection Time:** Dedicate specific time (even 15 minutes) each week to think about your work and articulate what is happening—without the goal of "taking initiative," but simply for the sake of surfacing ideas.
- [ ] **Practice Externalizing Ideas:** Initiate conversations with peers or mentors specifically asking, "What do you see that I am missing?" This forces you out of your own perspective (the "I" view).
- [ ] **Identify Your "Waiting Signal":** When feeling blocked by others' decisions, pause and ask: *Is this a policy decision, or is this a pattern?* If it's a pattern, treat the lack of action as an indicator that you should move first.
- [ ] **Focus on Bold Impact:** When considering taking initiative, mentally filter your ideas through the question: "Will this significantly move the needle for the organization?" (Moving beyond minor process fixes).
