# From Command and Control to Serve and Support, with Marisol Bello — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode features Marisol Bello, Executive Director of the Housing Narrative Lab, who discusses the critical transition from a "command and control" leadership style to one rooted in "serve and support." Marisol's background is highly relevant to the target audience, having transitioned from a journalist to a strategic communicator and narrative strategist. Her work focuses on using research-driven storytelling to build public understanding and support for complex social issues, such as housing solutions.

The core discussion revolves around the difficulty of internal leadership development. Marisol shares that her journey required moving beyond the traditional idea of having a "roadmap" or a mentor. Instead, she found that true growth came from committing to continuous, often difficult, internal work—specifically, developing self-awareness and practicing new behaviors in real-time, even when facing organizational chaos.

## Key Points
- **The Achiever vs. Leader Shift:** The discussion frames leadership development as a transition from being an "achiever" (focused on winning and execution) to a supportive leader. This shift requires a fundamental change in self-perception and how one interacts with others.
- **Focus on Micro-Actions:** The most powerful lesson is that large, abstract goals (like "being a trusted leader") are overwhelming. Instead, effective development requires identifying small, practical, immediate behavioral changes—"what can you do in five minutes?"
- **The "Stop, Breathe, Listen" Protocol:** Marisol adopted a variation of the "Stop, Breathe, and is it worth it?" framework. She translated this into a physical commitment to pausing and actively listening before speaking. This counteracts the natural tendency to "jump in" and "add value" by taking control of the conversation.
- **The Danger of Unfiltered Input:** A critical insight shared by the cohort was that "our whispers sound like yells and our suggestions sound like orders." This highlights the need for extreme behavioral calibration, especially for creative or brainstorming-minded individuals, who must learn to distinguish between a casual idea and a directive.

## Tools & Resources
- **The Yellow Post-it Note:** A physical reminder used by Marisol to enforce the "Stop, Breathe, Listen" protocol during conversations.
- **The Academy (Conceptual):** Represents a structured, intensive learning environment focused on practical, behavioral commitments rather than theoretical knowledge.

## Quotable
> "The great thing about the academy... wasn't about, you are going to be a great big trusted leader... It was really, okay, well, how, what can you do in five minutes? What could be one practice?"
>
> "Our whispers sound like yells and our suggestions sound like orders."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement the "Stop, Breathe, Listen" Rule:** Before responding in any meeting or conversation, physically pause for three seconds. Use this time to actively listen to the speaker's full point before formulating a response.
- [ ] **Identify One Behavioral Commitment:** Instead of setting a massive career goal, identify one specific, measurable, and small behavioral change (e.g., "In every meeting this week, I will ask two clarifying questions before offering my own opinion").
- [ ] **Practice "Listening as a Skill":** When in a conversation, make it a goal to resist the urge to "add value" or jump in with solutions. Focus solely on summarizing what the other person said to ensure understanding.
- [ ] **Self-Audit for "Commanding":** When you find yourself formulating a suggestion, pause and ask: "Is this a suggestion, or is this an order?" This helps calibrate your communication style to be supportive rather than directive.
