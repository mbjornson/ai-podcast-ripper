# 772: How to Measure Your Meeting’s Success, with Rebecca Hinds — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the pervasive organizational problem of poorly run meetings, arguing that while we spend massive amounts of time in them, we rarely measure their actual success or value. Rebecca Hinds, a leading expert on organizational behavior who founded and led the Work Innovation Lab at Asana and the Work AI Institute at Glean, frames meetings not as necessary evils, but as the most important, yet least optimized, "product" within an organization. She emphasizes that successful meeting design requires moving beyond anecdotal complaints and adopting rigorous, product-management-style metrics.

Hinds guides the listener away from common, misleading metrics like simply calculating the "cost" of meetings (salaries x time). She argues that focusing solely on cost can incentivize detrimental, hyper-efficient behaviors and fails to capture the true value of a meeting (e.g., triggering a new product line or reducing lawsuits). Instead, she introduces a more sophisticated, value-based metric designed to force accountability and continuous improvement.

## Key Points
- **Meetings as a Product:** The core premise is that meetings must be treated with the same discipline as a product in development—they need to be measured, refined, and iterated upon.
- **The Pitfall of Cost Metrics:** While quantifying the cost of meetings (salaries in the room / time) can be useful for "jolting" an organization out of inertia, it is insufficient. Over-indexing on cost incentivizes the wrong behaviors and fails to correlate cost with actual value.
- **The "Meeting Suck Reflex":** People are socially conditioned to believe that meetings are inherently bad. This visceral negative reaction means that public feedback on meetings is often inaccurate and biased, making objective measurement crucial.
- **Return on Time Invested (ROTI):** This is the recommended, actionable metric. Instead of asking "Was this meeting good?", the organizer should ask attendees to rate the meeting on a simple 0 to 5 scale for "was this meeting worth the time invested?"
- **Actionable Feedback Loop:** Crucially, the ROTI measurement must be paired with a qualitative question: "What could I do differently as the organizer to boost this score by 1 point?" This shifts the focus from blame to actionable improvement.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *Your Best Meeting Ever: 7 Principles for Designing Meetings That Get Things Done* (By Rebecca Hinds)
- **People/Concepts:** Steven Rogelberg (Collaborator who conducted research on public vs. private meeting ratings); Bob Sutton (Mentioned in relation to the "bad is stronger than good" principle).

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement ROTI:** For the next 10% of meetings you organize, implement a mandatory, quick survey asking attendees to rate the meeting on a 0-5 scale for "worth the time invested," and to suggest one specific action to improve the score.
- [ ] **Shift Mindset:** Stop viewing meetings as a necessary expense and start viewing them as a product that requires continuous optimization and iteration.
- [ ] **Challenge Cost Metrics:** When discussing meeting efficiency, pivot the conversation away from simple salary cost calculations and toward value-based outcomes (e.g., "How did this meeting reduce risk?" or "What new idea did this meeting trigger?").
