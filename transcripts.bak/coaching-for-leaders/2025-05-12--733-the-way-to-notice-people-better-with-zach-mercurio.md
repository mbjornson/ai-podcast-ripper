# 733: The Way to Notice People Better, with Zach Mercurio — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode explores the concept of "mattering"—the profound human need to feel seen, supported, and significant—and how leaders can intentionally build a culture around this principle. Zach Mercurio, an author and researcher specializing in purposeful leadership and organizational psychology, argues that professional success is rarely driven by tangible rewards (bonuses, awards) but rather by small moments of genuine recognition and connection. His work centers on shifting the corporate mindset from transactional exchanges to deep, human interactions.

Mercurio posits that most workplace communication defaults to "update fests"—transacting information—which fails to address the emotional needs of employees. He emphasizes that a leader's behavior is critical; research shows a leader’s actions account for nearly 50% of whether an employee feels meaningfulness at work. The core message is that leaders must become the "secure base" for their teams, optimizing existing interactions rather than implementing new programs or platforms to foster psychological safety and value.

## Key Points
- **The Shift from Transactional to Interactional:** Most organizational meetings are merely transacting information ("Here's what I need"). True connection requires making space and time for deep check-ins that cannot be resolved via email, such as discussing personal struggles or conflicts in collaboration.
- **Mattering is a Leading Indicator:** Feeling valued (mattering) is not an outcome of engagement; it is the *predictor* of engagement. The three core predictors of employee engagement are psychological meaningfulness ("Does what I do matter?"), psychological safety ("Can I speak up without fear?"), and availability ("Is there someone to support me?").
- **The Value Precedes Performance Model:** Contrary to the traditional industrial model (where performance earns recognition), people need to feel valued *first* in order to perform. This creates a virtuous cycle: feeling valued builds self-esteem and self-efficacy, which then drives higher value output.
- **Data Gap Warning:** Despite massive investment in HR tech, only 39% of employees reported feeling that someone at work cared about them as a person, and only 30% felt that someone knew and invested in their unique gifts. This signals a critical failure point in modern corporate culture.

## Tools & Resources
- **Book:** *The Power of Mattering: How Leaders Can Create a Culture of Significance* by Zach Mercurio (Highly recommended for understanding the framework).
- **Concept/Skill:** Optimizing interactions rather than implementing new programs or platforms.

## Quotable
> "When people feel like they matter, they act like they matter."
> "Remember my name and say thank you."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Audit Your Interactions:** For one week, track your professional conversations. Calculate the ratio of time spent on *information exchange* (transactional) versus *understanding how the person is doing* (deep check-in). Aim to shift more energy toward understanding.
- [ ] **Practice "The Deep Check-In":** When speaking with a team member, dedicate 5 minutes to asking questions that require emotional vulnerability and cannot be answered with a bullet point update (e.g., "What's the biggest non-work stressor you are dealing with right now?" or "How is your energy level this week?").
- [ ] **Reframe Feedback:** When giving feedback, start by affirming the person's value *before* discussing their performance gaps. Focus on building self-esteem and self-efficacy first.
- [ ] **Read/Study:** Seek out resources related to "secure attachment" in adult professional settings to understand how leaders can function as emotional safe bases for their teams.
