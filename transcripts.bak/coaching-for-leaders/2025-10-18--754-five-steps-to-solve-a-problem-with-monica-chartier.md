# 754: Five Steps to Solve a Problem, with Monica Chartier — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
This episode introduces a structured, user-centric framework for solving organizational problems, drawing heavily from Design Thinking and Agile methodologies. The discussion, led by Monica Chartier, focuses on moving beyond intuition and assumptions when tackling complex issues within a team or community. Monica, a Product Manager in the technology industry, brings expertise in supporting global product experiences and applying these principles to leadership challenges.

The core message is that effective problem-solving requires external perspective and deep user immersion. The framework emphasizes that leaders must actively gather data and listen to the people most impacted by the problem—the "users"—rather than relying on their own internal knowledge or best judgment. The hosts demonstrate the framework's power by detailing how they applied it to restructure and improve the post-academy experience for the "Coaching for Leaders" community, moving from initial assumptions to a data-driven solution.

## Key Points
- **The Foundational Principle:** Problem-solving must be guided by the perspective of the people being affected, not the people solving the problem. Leaders must actively resist the urge to assume they know the answer because they are "too close" to the situation.
- **The Framework Origin:** The methodology is rooted in Design Thinking, which is a proven process for developing products and solutions by centering the user experience.
- **Step 1: Empathy (The Starting Point):** Do not start with solutions. Start by talking directly to the people impacted by the problem. The goal is to understand their feelings, their context, and what they genuinely need, which ensures they feel "bought in" to the solution.
- **Step 2: Defining the Problem (Data Collection):** After initial empathy, the next step is to gather broad, quantitative data. This involves using tools like surveys and speaking to a wider group to validate the problem and ensure the scope is accurately defined before any solutioning begins.
- **The Value of External Challenge:** The process benefits greatly from external input. Being challenged by peers or experts (like a fellow in the community) helps leaders break out of their own established thinking patterns and biases.

## Tools & Resources
- **Design Thinking:** A structured methodology (originating partly at Stanford Design) for creative problem-solving that emphasizes empathy and iterative prototyping.
- **Agile Methodology:** The principles of Agile (used in software development) are highly relevant, emphasizing continuous feedback loops and user input over rigid planning.
- **Community Debriefing:** Utilizing a peer group or mentor to challenge assumptions and provide objective feedback is a critical resource for leaders.

## Quotable
> "We fundamentally are not the folks in those shoes. And in a lot of times we're just so close to the problem or to, you know, the offering... it takes a little bit of different thinking to adjust that."

> "The key tenants are tenants from design thinking... really looking at what is the problem that we're solving for our key customers and letting the customers... really help lead and guide the idea."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Implement the Empathy Interview:** When faced with an organizational problem, schedule 5-10 deep-dive conversations with the people most impacted. Focus solely on listening to their experience ("How did you get into this situation?") rather than offering solutions.
- [ ] **Structure Feedback Loops:** When developing a new process or product, mandate a formal data collection phase (surveys, interviews) *before* finalizing the solution.
- [ ] **Seek External Challenge:** Identify a peer or mentor who is outside of your immediate project/department and regularly debrief with them to challenge your assumptions and gain objective perspective.
- [ ] **Read:** Research the core principles of Design Thinking (e.g., books by IDEO or articles on the Stanford d.school website).
