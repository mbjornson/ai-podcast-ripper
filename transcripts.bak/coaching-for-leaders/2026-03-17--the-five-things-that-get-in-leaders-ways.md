# The Five Things That Get in Leaders’ Ways — Coaching for Leaders

## Summary
The episode addresses the critical question of what truly differentiates thriving leaders, arguing that the issue is rarely a lack of knowledge or resources. Instead, the speaker outlines "The Five Things" that most commonly impede a leader's ability to improve. The core thesis is that growth requires transcending these psychological and behavioral stopping points. The speaker draws heavily on personal experience, detailing how a failure to ask for help early in his career led to a significant professional misstep, illustrating the high cost of perceived vulnerability. The discussion emphasizes that true leadership development is less about accumulating information and more about implementing behavioral change through safe, structured practice.

## Key Points
- **The Danger of Silence (Not Asking for Help):** The primary barrier is the fear of appearing incompetent or struggling, which prevents leaders from seeking advice. The speaker notes that this fear is often compounded by organizational politics, making it feel unsafe to admit a knowledge gap.
- **Knowledge $\neq$ Behavior:** Leadership skills are not acquired merely by reading or passing an exam. Knowledge is the starting point, but it is insufficient. The analogy of learning to drive highlights that true mastery requires experiential practice (getting behind the wheel) rather than just classroom theory.
- **The Power of Practice Over Acquisition:** When stuck, do not seek a new book or course. Instead, take a concept you already know and apply it immediately in a low-stakes, real-world scenario with your team. This shifts focus from *input* (knowledge) to *output* (behavior).
- **Distinguishing Outcomes from Tactics:** While leaders should set extremely high standards for their *outcomes* (vision, goals), they must intentionally lower the bar for their *tactics* (daily behaviors). This makes the necessary behavioral changes feel manageable and less overwhelming.
- **Building a Safe Network:** To overcome the reluctance to ask for help, leaders should seek out mentors or peers who are in a similar role but are *not* invested in the immediate politics or success of your current organization.

## Tools & Resources
- **Coaching for Leaders Academy:** A structured program designed to help leaders work through the five stopping points by focusing heavily on behavioral practice rather than traditional curriculum.
- **"How can we help today?" Prompt:** A specific, actionable phrase suggested for initiating conversations, designed to normalize the act of asking for help and immediately open the conversation to mutual support.

## Quotable
> "Knowledge is an essential starting point, but it's just insufficient on its own."
> "The irony is if i had asked for help i think they would have actually respected me more but it didn't feel safe at the time to do that."

## Action Items
- [ ] **Identify a Safe Peer Mentor:** Find a professional mentor or peer in a different industry who is at a similar career stage but is not connected to your current organization's politics.
- [ ] **Practice Vulnerability:** When facing a professional challenge, intentionally use the phrase, "I'm struggling with X, and I'd appreciate your perspective on Y," to normalize asking for help.
- [ ] **Implement the Practice Loop:** Instead of researching a new skill, identify one skill you already know and commit to applying it in a specific, measurable way with a team member this week.
- [ ] **Lower the Tactical Bar:** When setting goals, consciously separate the "Vision/Outcome" (high standard) from the "Daily Behavior/Tactic" (low, manageable standard) to reduce feelings of overwhelm.
